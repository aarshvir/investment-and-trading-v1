### Kill-criteria status
None fired. (1) Net debt/adj. EBITDA >3.0x: 30 Jun net debt $15.4bn (company non-GAAP, per DA8 correction) / FY26 guide midpoint $8.3bn = 1.86x. ### Strongest bear case
What the market may know: (a) CRH is paying 11.5x EBITDA for Arcosa while its own stock trades at roughly 8.4x EV/EBITDA (EV ~$70bn = $54.6bn market cap + $15.4bn net debt, over $8.3bn). Issuing leverage at a 3-turn premium to its own multiple is how acquirers de-rate; the stock fell after announcement and is near its 52-week low while three brokers cut targets in the last two weeks. (b) Cost inflation: Q2 commentary flagged higher haulage/diesel costs needing surcharges, and Citi cut Q3 construction-materials estimates on diesel; the dossier does not mention fuel at all. (c) Cement volume -2% and price -1% in Q2; guidance was reaffirmed rather than raised despite a beat, which suggests limited H2 cushion. (d) The 'cheap' call rests on EV/EBITDA versus VMC/MLM with no peer multiples actually quoted in the dossier and no reverse DCF (the dossier says no V1 row exists), so 'implied_vs_base = below' is asserted, not demonstrated. (e) CRH's growth is bought: ~$4.1bn of FY25 bolt-on M&A plus $1.7bn growth capex, so free cash flow is not free. My own rough reverse DCF on EV $70bn, $5.6bn pre-interest FCF, 8-9% WACC and 3% terminal growth gives implied FCF growth of about -3% to -1%; with only $4.5bn of FCF left after growth spend it is about -0.6% to +1.7%. Either way the price implies little growth, which supports 'cheap' only if the M&A engine keeps compounding at acceptable returns.

### Single breaking assumption
That CRH can deliver its guided ~$8.3bn FY26 adjusted EBITDA (and flat-to-up EBITDA after Arcosa) while holding leverage near 2.4x. If US non-residential/infrastructure volumes soften (cement already -2%) or fuel/haulage costs are not fully recovered, EBITDA slips, the 2.4x pro forma drifts toward 3.0x, and the M&A-funded growth story becomes a de-leveraging story at a lower multiple.

### Watch

Any HSR Second Request or divestiture demand on Arcosa (a close beyond Q1 2027 fires kill criterion 2). Pro forma net debt/EBITDA versus 2.4x guided. Price versus the $80.81 52-week low.

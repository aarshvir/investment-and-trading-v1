## 3. Loop-2-specific judgments (per the persona-file notes)

**a) Were Loop 1's must-fix items really fixed?** Yes, on direct inspection of the underlying files rather than the narrative claiming so — see the criterion table above and the recomputations in §4. None was fixed only in prose without a matching data change.

**b) Are sleeve-rule amendments (a)–(f) justified or result-chasing?** Judged **justified**. Each amendment closes a specific, nameable internal inconsistency rather than tuning toward a better-looking outcome: (a)/(c) make a missing or incomplete valuation ineligible — fixing a data-pipeline timing bug that let RL in; (b) makes a negative valuation base case ineligible — enforcing that a "fair" verdict cannot coexist with a projected loss (excludes MPC, HAS); (e) applies the same logic to the analyst's own reconciled dossier view, symmetric with (a) (excludes JBHT); (d) halves the cap for half-conviction names and routes unfillable capacity to the index fund rather than concentrating it further in fewer stocks — this is the most "portfolio-shaping" amendment, but it routes money to the index (the report's own stated zero-alpha instrument), which cuts against, not for, flattering the stock-picking result. (f) is the strongest evidence against result-chasing: V2R's from-filings NAREIT-FFO rebuild for REITs is a genuine calculation-error fix (V1's proxy counted one-off property-sale gains as recurring FFO) that made HST and FRT look *more* expensive, not less — it downgraded FRT from INCLUDE to INCLUDE-SMALL. A rule change that only ever tightens eligibility can, by construction, only remove or downgrade names, never add ones that wouldn't otherwise qualify; that is exactly the pattern observed (RL, MPC, HAS, DXCM, JBHT out; FRT downgraded; the growth from 15 to 20 names between Loop 1 and Loop 2 came from extending diligence coverage to previously-undiligenced quant-eligible names, not from loosening any of these six rules).

**c) Research-only scope constraint — can a non-technical reader convert percentages to dollars himself?** Yes, and this is the loop's biggest concrete improvement (criterion 7 above). The per-$10,000 table, the formula, the $80,000 worked example, and the "cash quantity" execution shortcut together let a reader go from "my total capital" to "dollars per layer, dollars per stock, units per tranche" with at most one division (or zero, using cash-quantity), while the deliverable correctly continues to decline to guess the reader's actual balance or place any order itself. This is the right resolution of a genuine scope constraint, not a gap.

**d) Is the Codex v005 reconciliation fair?** Yes, on independent verification. I read v005's own `INVESTMENT_DECISION_MEMO.md` directly (not only v4's summary of it) and cross-checked roughly a dozen of FINAL_REPORT §13's specific claims against it: the tail-shock loss (18.0% at 60/75/2% shocks), AMP/PAYX entry ceilings and withholding-adjusted IRRs (12.8%/12.2%, exact), HIG/RL/AIZ/NTAP entry and reconsideration prices (all exact), and v005's own hedge language ("not a claim to have found the best fourteen investments in the entire market") — quoted verbatim and correctly attributed.

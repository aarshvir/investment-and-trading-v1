# A2 audit — loop 0 baseline (v3 deliverable)

**Auditor:** A2 — former portfolio manager / investment-committee member, top-tier fundamental long-only fund (company diligence, valuation, portfolio construction specialist). **Focus weighting per brief:** criteria 4, 5, 6, 7.

## 1. Total score and verdict

**Total: 42/100.**

**Verdict:** This would not survive five minutes in front of a real investment committee. The delivered weight table breaches its own stated ≤10%/≤25% caps (RL and ABNB at 10.63%, three sectors at 26.25%), the cap set is mathematically infeasible for the chosen 14 names (max feasible investment is 95%, not 100%), the headline "-26.2% max drawdown" is a month-end artifact that hides a -36.2% daily/rebalanced drawdown against a client who said he tolerates 15-20%, the only out-of-sample test of the stock-picking method underperforms its own universe, and the flagship "90% five-year odds" is a simulation output re-centred to an assumed 10.7% market drift, not a data-derived probability. Facts checked against primary sources were **not fabricated** — that is a genuine, verified strength — but the analytical machinery built on top of those facts (valuation horizon, expected-return formula, constraint solving, drawdown reporting, lens independence) is unreliable enough that none of the four focus criteria (4-7) clears "material gaps."

## 3. Must-fix to reach ≥95 (ranked by score impact)

1. **Re-solve the portfolio under verified-feasible constraints** (criterion 6, currently the lowest score). The stated ≤10%/≤25% caps cannot be met by 14 names in 4 sectors (max feasible = 95%). Either raise a cap, add an eligible name/sector, or explicitly hold ~5% cash — and check and publish the solver's `success` flag and residuals, not a post-hoc clipped renormalization.
2. **Report the daily, monthly-rebalanced drawdown (-36%), not the month-end figure (-26%)**, and explicitly reconcile it against the investor's stated 15-20% tolerance instead of presenting a figure that happens to look compliant only because of the reporting convention.
3. **Declare one consistent NTM valuation horizon and re-screen on it.** The current FY0/FY1 mix changes the "cheap" narrative by 15-45% per name (NVDA 14.3x → 16.6x true NTM; MSFT 21.0x → 24.1x) and this is exactly the kind of number a diligence-focused IC will catch first.
4. **Replace the expected-return formula** (drop the flat 10%×beta market leg for rf+β·ERP; stop adding buyback yield on top of per-share EPS growth) and re-run selection — two of fourteen names change under the fix, so the current 14 are not robust to a defensible methodology choice.
5. **Add adverse facts and one measurable kill criterion to every finalist dossier**, and fix the Financials-bucket balance-sheet test so a 2.7x-levered payments company (CPAY) cannot pass on ROE alone.
6. **Deliver the order sheet, a weekly review checklist, and a true (non-survivorship-biased) stress test** to close the mandate-fit and monitoring gaps.
7. **Pre-register the next model version before any further backtesting on 2023-25**, disclose the drift-sensitivity of the "90%" headline in the main body (not a footnote), and record the bootstrap's seed and library versions.

# WY — Weyerhaeuser Company — Diligence Dossier (Agent F153, Wave-8 Standard)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (thesis horizon 24-48 months). Quality assets (largest private US timberland owner, about 10 million acres) but the 25 Sep 2026 close of $20.01 prices a housing/lumber recovery that has not yet shown up in cash: TTM free cash flow is about $22M against a roughly $606M annual dividend, and net debt is 5.0x LTM Adjusted EBITDA. The quick triage (Q14: advance=false, "near-zero FCF yield") is confirmed on primary-source work. Condition that would change it: Adjusted EBITDA run-rate above about $1.3B with Adjusted FAD covering base dividends for two consecutive quarters, or a price near $15-16 (implied 10-year FCF growth falling toward my base).

## 2. Business in plain English
Weyerhaeuser owns timberland and sells logs (Timberlands), sells land for development, conservation and carbon projects (Strategic Land Solutions, renamed from Real Estate, Energy & Natural Resources in Q1 2026) and makes lumber, OSB and engineered wood for homebuilders (Wood Products). In Q2'26 Timberlands produced $123M, Strategic Land Solutions $129M and Wood Products $129M of Adjusted EBITDA (unallocated items -$71M); Wood Products swings with lumber and OSB prices (company sensitivity: "$10/MBF≈ $50 Million EBITDA Annually" for lumber, Ex-99.1 to 8-K 0001193125-26-400727). It is a REIT, so earnings are tied to US housing starts and repair/remodel activity, mortgage rates and Canadian lumber trade policy.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and is it durable?
b1_live_scores.csv ranks it on valuation/sentiment: street target $31 (55% above the price), forward P/E 29.8x on a depressed EPS base. The "cheapness" is an artefact of trough earnings: the 25 Sep price implies recovery in lumber, not a mispriced steady state. d4 FCF $13.6M is consistent with my TTM calculation ($22M, below). Not durable as a reason on its own.

## 8. Bull and bear
**Bull:** (1) Lumber and OSB leverage is large and the stock is a cheap call on it: each $10/MBF lumber = about $50M EBITDA, and Q2 lumber realizations rose 15% q/q; (2) timberland base, Climate Solutions (conservation easement $94M in Q1), the Monticello facility (start-up goal 2027) and the stated $1.5B EBITDA add by 2030 against a 2024 baseline are real optionality; (3) investment-grade balance sheet (BBB per S&P, 85% fixed rate, no maturity cliff, revolver undrawn).
**Bear:** (1) FCF of $22M TTM (consolidated) does not cover a $606M dividend; the dividend is being funded by asset sales ($306M timberland proceeds H1'26) and debt; (2) leverage rose from 1.0x (2022) to 5.0x as EBITDA fell; Q3 Wood Products outlook was cut on Sep 24 and lumber has given back about $30/MBF from the Q3 QTD level; (3) 2025 EBITDA about $1.0B vs $1.3B, with Canadian softwood duties and mortgage-rate uncertainty outside management's control.

## 9. Key risks and kill criteria
1. Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDA (LTM, consolidated, company table "NET DEBT TO ADJUSTED EBITDA RECONCILIATION") above 5.5x at any quarter-end (Jun 30 2026: 5.0x).
2. Base dividend cut below $0.21 per share per quarter, or Adjusted FAD below base dividends in four consecutive quarters (Q1'26 FAD -$58M, Q2'26 +$323M).
3. Wood Products Adjusted EBITDA below $50M in any quarter (Q1'26 $71M, Q2'26 $129M).
4. LTM consolidated Adjusted EBITDA below $850M at two consecutive quarter-ends (about $0.98B now).
5. Monticello engineered wood facility start-up slips beyond 2027 or 2026 capex exceeds the $400-450M range plus $300M Monticello stated in the Q2 10-Q.

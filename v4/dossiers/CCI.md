## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL.** Reservation: **elevated leverage (~6.4x net debt/EBITDA) and a still-unresolved organic-growth drag from Sprint/DISH tower non-renewals**, on top of a recent, real (not cosmetic) balance-sheet transition. Thesis horizon **12–24 months** (shorter than standard, given the AFFO guide is still being reset quarter to quarter as the divestiture beds in). One-sentence reason: a genuine, completed de-risking event (the May 2026 fiber/small-cell sale, debt paydown and buyback) plus a 6.3% dividend yield and a reverse-DCF that is close to fair value rather than clearly cheap — attractive, but with less margin of safety and more moving parts than the two distributors in this batch.

## 2. Business in plain English

Crown Castle owns and leases roughly 40,000 US cell towers to wireless carriers (Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile) under long-term (5–15 year), contracted, escalator-bearing leases — a REIT with a toll-road-like economic model: it does not operate the network, it rents the vertical real estate the network sits on. Until May 2026 it also owned a large fiber/small-cell business; that business (~$8.4bn) was sold, and the company is now a **pure-play US tower REIT**. Revenue and cash flow are earned from tenant rent, escalators (contractual annual increases) and new-tenant "co-location" additions to existing towers; the main demand driver is carrier network densification (5G, capacity), and the main headwind of the last several years has been the T-Mobile/Sprint merger (redundant Sprint-leased sites being non-renewed) and DISH Network's build-out slowdown/exit.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and whether that's durable

Triage's framing (a genuine, completed de-risking catalyst forced by activist pressure; the revenue decline is divestiture noise, not organic decline; 6.3% yield/39% street upside) is **substantially confirmed by primary filings**, with one correction: the CEO change triage flagged as a governance red flag is verified via the company's own press release (Ex-99.1, filed 2025-08-04) as a **completed, board-run search process** — Christian Hillabrant, a tower-industry veteran (former CEO of Vantage Towers AG, ~86,000 sites across 10 countries; prior COO of Tillman Infrastructure), was appointed President & CEO effective **2025-09-15** — not an ongoing instability signal, though the underlying pattern of leadership change alongside activist pressure is worth weighing on its own. The **durable** part: the fiber/small-cell sale closed, ~$7bn of debt was repaid and $1bn of shares repurchased in the following quarter (both confirmed in the Q2 2026 release), and the balance sheet is now 100% fixed-rate debt with a ~7-year weighted average maturity and ~$4.5bn of revolver availability. The **less durable / still-adjusting** part: organic tower growth excluding DISH terminations and Sprint cancellations is running **~3.5–4.2%** (Q2 2026 actual 3.9% headline, 4.2% ex-DISH-base; FY2026 outlook ~3.4–3.6%) — a real but modest growth rate, and FY2026 AFFO guidance has needed **three separate revisions in seven months** (initial Feb 2026 guide, a special update concurrent with the May 1 divestiture close, then the Q2 raise) as the company re-based the business post-sale — evidence the current guidance is still settling, not yet a multi-year established trend.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (REIT metric set — see playbook note in the header)

- **Entity scope**: all figures are **consolidated** Crown Castle Inc., the REIT parent (10-Q balance sheet, 2026-06-30).
- **Leverage**: Net Debt (company-defined, non-GAAP) = **$17,099m** at Q2 2026 (per the earnings-release non-GAAP schedule), down from an estimated ~$27bn gross-debt-less-minimal-cash position at 2025-12-31 (XBRL: long-term debt $24.34bn + current debt $2.78bn, cash only $99m) — confirming the triage's "$7bn+ debt paydown" figure from divestiture proceeds. Against FY2026 guided Adjusted EBITDA of $2,665–$2,715m (midpoint $2,690m), **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 6.4x** — moderate-to-high for the tower REIT sector (towers typically run 5–7x) and a level that materially constrains further buyback/dividend growth until it declines.
- **Debt structure**: 100% fixed-rate debt, weighted-average maturity ~7 years, ~$4.5bn revolver availability at Q2 2026 (per the earnings release) — a genuinely de-risked maturity/rate profile versus a leveraged floating-rate REIT.
- **Dividend coverage**: the quarterly dividend has been held flat at **$1.0625/share** ($4.25/share annualized) in every dividend-declaration 8-K checked from August 2025 through August 2026 (five consecutive declarations, no cut and no raise observed in this window) — against FY2026 guided AFFO/share of $4.53–$4.65 (midpoint $4.59), the **AFFO payout ratio is ≈92.6%** — tight, funds the dividend but leaves little room for dividend growth or incremental buybacks without net debt reduction or AFFO growth.
- **Capital allocation since the divestiture**: ~$1bn of shares repurchased and ~$7bn of debt repaid in the period immediately following the May 2026 sale close (company's own release) — capital was directed to deleveraging and buybacks, not dividend growth, consistent with the leverage constraint above.
- **Organic growth quality**: reported organic contribution to site rental billings is disclosed **both including and excluding** DISH terminations and Sprint cancellations (3.9% headline, 4.2% ex-DISH-base for Q2 2026) — a transparent disclosure that lets the reader see the churn drag explicitly rather than a blended number; FY2026 guide is 3.4–3.6% on the same ex-churn basis.

**Reverse DCF** (2-stage, per-share AFFO, terminal growth 3.0% given towers' structural data-demand tailwind, FY2026 guided AFFO/share midpoint $4.59 as the starting cash-flow proxy): given the elevated leverage (6.4x) and the still-settling post-divestiture guidance, a **higher cost of equity (9.0–9.5%) is more appropriate here than for the two distributors** in this batch. At that range, the **$67.84 price implies roughly +1.0% to +2.1% annualized 10-year AFFO/share growth** — modest, and closer to fair value than clearly cheap. **Analyst base case: 3–5%/yr 10-year AFFO/share growth** (organic tower growth of ~3.5–4% net of the fading Sprint/DISH churn drag, plus modest buyback accretion once leverage normalizes, partly offset by refinancing risk as fixed-rate debt eventually rolls) — **above** the ~1–2% implied at a conservative discount rate, but the gap is materially narrower than for CAH or COR, and **flips to roughly in-line if a lower (8.0–8.5%) discount rate is used** (implied growth would then be −1.2% to −0.1%, i.e., cheap) or to expensive-looking territory only if the base case growth undershoots. Given this sensitivity, **valuation_view_vs_v1.implied_vs_base = "below"** on the analyst's central discount-rate assumption (9.0–9.5%, justified by the leverage), but flagged as the least confident "below" call in this batch — a genuinely close one, not a clear-cut cheap stock.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull**: (1) The fiber/small-cell divestiture is a real, completed simplification — $7bn of debt repaid, $1bn bought back, 100% fixed-rate debt with a 7-year weighted maturity — removing years of "which segment is actually working" ambiguity. (2) A 6.3% dividend yield backed by AFFO coverage of ~93% (tight but funded), with an experienced tower-industry CEO now in place for a full year. (3) Organic tower growth (ex-churn) of 3.5–4%+ with the Sprint-cancellation and DISH-termination drags disclosed transparently and, by nature, mechanically fading as the comparison base rolls off.

**Bear**: (1) Net debt/EBITDA of ~6.4x is genuinely elevated, and the ~93% AFFO payout ratio leaves little buffer if AFFO disappoints — a REIT in this position has less room to absorb a bad quarter than CAH or COR. (2) FY2026 guidance has been revised three times in seven months as the post-divestiture business is re-based; the "clean beat-and-raise" pattern seen at the two distributors is less established here. (3) The reverse DCF is genuinely close to fair value rather than cheap once a leverage-appropriate discount rate is used — this is a name where the margin of safety depends heavily on the discount-rate assumption, not a screening artefact.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. The quarterly dividend is cut from $1.0625/share (breaking the flat-since-at-least-Aug-2025 record and signaling AFFO coverage has failed).
2. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 7.0x (from ~6.4x today) without a disclosed deleveraging plan.
3. Organic contribution to site rental billings (ex-DISH/Sprint) falls below 2.5% for two consecutive quarters.
4. FY AFFO/share guidance is cut at any quarterly release (breaking the current raise/reaffirm pattern).
5. A second CEO or CFO departure within 24 months of Hillabrant's September 2025 appointment (a repeat of the leadership-churn pattern the triage flagged).

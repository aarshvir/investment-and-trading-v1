# BALL — Ball Corporation — Diligence Dossier (Agent F72, Wave 4)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** — 24–36 month horizon. Ball is a clean, single-business (aluminum beverage-can) story with a consistent double-digit comparable-EPS-growth algorithm, a manageable balance sheet, and — on a reverse DCF — a price that does not require heroic growth assumptions. The main reservation, not severe enough to downgrade to INCLUDE-SMALL, is that FY2026 guidance (10%+ EPS growth, FCF >$900m) is a deceleration from FY2025's delivered 12–15% growth and record FCF, so the growth algorithm needs re-verification each quarter rather than assumed.

## 2. Business in plain English
Ball makes aluminum cans and bottles for beverage companies (Coca-Cola, AB InBev, PepsiCo-type customers) across North & Central America, EMEA and South America, plus a smaller aerosol/personal-care aluminum packaging business. It sold its aerospace division in February 2024 and is now a pure-play aluminum packaging company. It earns money on a largely pass-through-priced, volume-and-mix basis: aluminum and other input costs are contractually passed through to customers, so profit tracks unit volumes, price/mix and operating efficiency rather than commodity price levels. Its edge is being one of a small number of scaled global can-makers, with long-term supply agreements and a structural tailwind from sustainability-driven substitution away from plastic and glass.

## 5. Guidance track record (last 4 releases; source: same Ex-99.1 releases)

- **Q4'25 / FY2025 actual (Feb 2026):** delivered FY2025 comparable EPS **$3.57**, ~14% growth — **within the guided range, at the upper-middle**. Also delivered "record adjusted free cash flow of $956m." **This guidance was met, not missed** — a clean track record entering FY2026.
- **FY2026 initial guide (given with Q4'25 release, Feb 2026):** comparable diluted EPS growth **"10-plus percent,"** FCF **"greater than $900 million."** Note this is a step-down from the 12–15% delivered in FY2025 — management is guiding to a slower growth rate, not maintaining the prior pace.
- **Q1'26 (May 2026) and Q2'26 (Aug 2026):** both **reaffirmed** the same FY2026 guide verbatim ("10-plus percent" EPS growth, FCF >$900m). H1 2026 comparable EPS growth (+22.1% Q1, +14.4% Q2) is running well ahead of the 10%+ floor, so the guide currently looks conservative rather than at risk — but it has not yet been raised despite two quarters of double-digit outperformance, which is itself informative (management is not chasing the guide up on two data points).

A reasonable firm-level base case (global aluminum-can volume growth of 2–4%/year plus modest margin/mix improvement) is roughly **4–5%/year**, i.e. the implied 3.8% sits **at or slightly below** that base case → **"below."** This is a more modest margin of safety than WM's or AMCR's reverse-DCF read, but it is not a case of paying up for unrealistic growth.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Structural, multi-year substitution tailwind from plastic/glass to aluminum on sustainability/recyclability grounds, showing up as positive volume growth in every reported geographic segment for six consecutive quarters. (2) A clean FY2025 track record: guided 12–15% comparable EPS growth, delivered ~14%, plus record FCF — management guidance has been credible, in contrast to AMCR's FY2026 FCF miss. (3) Meaningful, sustained share buybacks (13% reduction in diluted count over 1.5 years) directly compound per-share value independent of the reverse-DCF's firm-level growth assumption.
**Bear:** (1) FY2026 guidance (10%+ EPS growth) is a deliberate deceleration from FY2025's delivered 12–15% — the easy comps and volume recovery of 2025 may not repeat, and two quarters of 14–22% growth have not (yet) prompted a guidance raise, which could mean management sees tougher comparisons ahead in H2 2026. (2) Aluminum input-cost/tariff exposure is real and not fully resolved: a September 2025 U.S. Customs and Border Protection challenge to Ball's tariff classification of aluminum imports is unresolved, unreserved, and the company itself says it is "unable to develop a reasonable estimate of loss." (3) Capital returns ($1.54bn in FY2025) have exceeded free cash flow in at least one recent year, funded by balance-sheet capacity — sustainable only if FCF continues to grow toward the buyback/dividend run-rate.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (each, if it occurs, would invalidate the INCLUDE thesis)
1. Global aluminum packaging shipment growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters (currently +4.3% in Q2'26 — a clean, quantifiable proxy for the substitution thesis losing steam).
2. Net debt/comparable EBITDA (company's own next disclosed figure) exceeds **3.5x** at any year-end or half-year disclosure (currently ≈2.8x at Dec 2025, ≈3.2x on my own approximation for June 2026).
3. FY2026 comparable diluted EPS growth guidance (currently "10-plus percent") is cut below 10% at any quarterly update.
4. The U.S. CBP aluminum-tariff-classification matter (disclosed Sept 2025, unreserved) results in a quantified assessment exceeding **$100 million**.
5. Full-year free cash flow for 2026 comes in below the guided **$900 million** floor at year-end.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **Unresolved, unreserved contingency:** September 2025 U.S. Customs and Border Protection notice challenging the tariff classification and duty rate of certain aluminum imports, asserting additional duties/tariffs payable and challenging Ball's use of certain exemptions. Ball "intends to vigorously defend" the matter, calls a loss "reasonably possible," and states it "is unable to develop a reasonable estimate of loss" — **no reserve recorded** (FY2025 10-K, Note 22). This is a real, quantifiable-but-unquantified tail risk, not yet a red flag on the numbers but worth tracking every filing.
- **Closed, favorable litigation:** a long-running patent dispute with Crown Technology Holding (beverage-can-end patents) was affirmed in Ball's favor by the Federal Circuit on June 30, 2025 and is now closed — cited to show the company does disclose litigation outcomes both ways, a positive signal on disclosure quality.
- Ordinary-course Brazil tax assessments (goodwill deductibility, transfer-pricing-type claims) — routine for a multinational manufacturer with Brazilian operations, not flagged as material.
- **No material weakness**: FY2025 10-K states "As of December 31, 2025, the company had no material weaknesses," and management's ICFR assessment and the PwC audit opinion were both clean.
- No PFAS liability quantified; PFAS is disclosed as a forward risk (U.S. states and the EU restricting PFAS-containing coatings), and Ball states it is already working with suppliers to remove PFAS-containing coatings — a proactive, not yet costed, mitigation.

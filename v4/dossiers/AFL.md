# Aflac Incorporated (AFL) - Fundamental Diligence Dossier (F128, standard depth)

**Verdict: WATCH**, 12-36 month horizon. Reason: a high-quality, well-capitalised supplemental insurer (adjusted return on equity excluding foreign-currency remeasurement 16.6%, Japanese solvency ratio 226%) that is priced for about +3% a year earnings growth when my base case is +1%; per-share growth in 2026 comes from buying back about 7% of the company a year with cash that exceeds earnings. Change condition: price near $98 (base-case present value, about 13% below 113.72), or evidence that Aflac Japan's underlying premiums stop shrinking and the yen strengthens. Triage (Q05, not advanced, "shrinking core Japan block") is confirmed on price, not on quality, and its -18.6% forward-EPS figure is largely a comparison artefact (below).

## 1. Business in plain English
Aflac sells supplemental health and life insurance (cancer, medical, accident, savings-type life) to individuals through agents and brokers. About two-thirds of adjusted pre-tax profit comes from Aflac Japan (premiums in yen, with persistency above 92%); the US business sells workplace voluntary benefits, plus group life, disability, dental and vision that are scaling. Earnings come from premiums less claims and expenses plus investment income on a $103B portfolio. Moat is agency distribution, brand and the locked-in block of in-force policies; the drag is that Japan's in-force premiums are shrinking (limited-pay policies reaching paid-up, reinsurance) and a weak yen (159.45 per dollar in Q2 2026 vs 144.60) cuts reported dollar earnings.

## 4. Guidance track record (verbatim)

(prior 2025 Japan range not opened). FY2025 segment outcomes were not retrieved.
- Q4 2025 call (0001628280-26-005458), 2026 outlook: "For Aflac Japan, we expect underlying earned premiums to decline 1% to 2% in 2026, and we also expect the expense ratio to be in the 20% to 23% range. However, we expect the benefit ratio in Japan will be in the 60% to 63% range and the pretax profit margin to be in the 33% to 36% range." and "In the U.S., we continue to expect net earned premium growth to be at the lower end of the 3% to 6% range ... benefit ratio for 2026 to be in the 48% to 52% range and the expense ratio to be in the 36% to 39% range ... pretax margin for 2026 to be in the range of 17% to 20%." Japan's margin range is 2 points lower than 2025's 35%-38%: a guided step-down.
- Q1 2026 call (0001628280-26-028396): no change to these ranges was stated in the prepared remarks (I searched the text).
- Q2 2026 call (0001628280-26-054333): "we now expect to be at the high end of our guidance range of 60% to 63% for the full year of 2026, excluding the annual actuarial assumption review in Q3" (Japan benefit ratio: a worse outlook, year-to-date 63.4%) and "We expect our net earned premium growth rate for 2026 to be just below our guidance range of 3% to 6%, versus previous guidance for the low end of this range" (a trim).
Assessment: two trims in Q2 2026 (Japan benefit ratio to the high end, US premium growth below the range); nothing raised. H1 2026 Japan margin of 34.6% is inside 33%-36%.

## 7. Bull and bear
Bull: (1) capital is strong (ESR 226%, RBC above 600%, leverage 21.8%) and management is deploying it ("$983 million" of repurchases in Q2); (2) new Japan products are selling (H1 sales +7.0% to ¥37.3B, Miraito cancer, Tsumitasu savings life, Anshin Palette medical) and Japan's margin is up 270 basis points in H1; (3) a yen that strengthens from 159 lifts reported dollar earnings, and the reinsurance target widened to "up to 30% of FSA reserves" should raise ROE.
Bear: (1) Japan underlying earned premiums are guided to fall 1%-2% a year and persistency slipped to 92.7% from 93.7%; (2) two unresolved cyber incidents (US, 12 Jun 2025, "pending disputes" with regulators; Japan, 25 Jun 2026, systems suspended, loss "cannot be reasonably estimated"); (3) distributions exceed earnings, adjusted book ex-foreign-currency remeasurement is falling 4%, and the loan-loss allowance and securities-lending liabilities are rising.

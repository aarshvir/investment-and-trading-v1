# DG — Dollar General Corporation

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — thesis horizon 12–36 months. Statistically rare valuation (NTM P/E at the 7th percentile of its own 175-month trading history) combined with a primary-source-verified, broad-based operating recovery (four consecutive quarterly guidance *raises*, six consecutive quarters of positive comps in all four merchandise categories) justifies a position, but a recurring workplace-safety compliance problem, unresolved shareholder securities litigation, and a guidance raise that is partly non-repeating (tariff refunds, a lower tax-rate assumption) are specific, named reservations against taking a full-weight position today.

## 2. Business in plain English

Dollar General is the largest small-box discount retailer in the US: 21,148 stores (Dollar General, DG Market, DGX, pOpshelf, plus a small Mexico pilot) as of July 31, 2026, mostly in rural and small-town America, selling consumables (food, health/beauty, cleaning — ~82% of sales), seasonal goods, home products and apparel at everyday low prices within a few minutes' drive of its customers. It makes money on thin per-unit margins (gross margin ~30–33%) applied to very high inventory turns and a low-cost, no-frills real-estate/labor model; its moat is store density and proximity in markets too small for Walmart or Target to bother with. It competes on convenience and price against Dollar Tree/Family Dollar, Walmart, and increasingly grocery and drug chains.

## 3. Why the model likes it — and is it durable?

B1 composite score is in the top decile (live_rank 18/~2,300; composite percentile 96.5; **gates: the composite itself carries no disqualifying-red-flag gate — the red-flag/legal check is done separately in §11 below, not netted into this score**). Family drivers: **Value (fam_V ≈0.88, very high)** — cheap on earnings yield, FCF yield, EBIT/EV and book/price; **Quality (fam_Q ≈0.63)** — solid gross-profit/assets and low accruals; **Earnings momentum (fam_S ≈0.84)** — SUE has been strongly positive as EPS beat-and-raise repeated; **Momentum (fam_M ≈0.70)** — the stock has rallied off a 2024 low as the recovery took hold.

Durability check: the Value signal is real and verifiable — DG trades at 15.2x NTM EPS against a 175-month own history whose 7th percentile this is, i.e., DG has traded above today's multiple in ~93% of the months since 2012. The earnings-momentum signal is **partly an artefact**: of the ~$0.60 increase in the FY2026 diluted-EPS guidance midpoint between the Q1 (Jun-2026) and Q2 (Aug-2026) releases, management explicitly attributes ~$0.25 (≈40%) to a one-time tariff-refund benefit it says will **not** recur in H2, and part of the earlier Q4→Q1 raise was a lower effective-tax-rate assumption (25.0%→24.5%) rather than operating improvement. The comp/traffic recovery itself (6 straight quarters of positive comps in every category, 5 straight quarters of positive traffic) is corroborated by the primary-source press releases and is the more durable part of the story.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **FCF conversion:** H1 FY26 FCF/NI = $738.3m / $994.4m = 0.74x, down from 1.40x in H1 FY25 — driven by the working-capital swing in §4, not by a change in underlying profitability.
- **SBC:** immaterial, 0.10%–0.34% of quarterly net sales across the last 8 quarters — not a distorting factor for this name.
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** DG reports clean GAAP with limited non-GAAP adjustment; the main "quality" question is the tariff-refund/tax-rate composition of the EPS raise (see §3/§5), which management itself discloses and quantifies — a genuine positive for transparency.
- **Balance sheet (2026-07-31 vs 2025-08-01, from the 10-Q balance sheet):** cash $1,589.6m (was $1,284.6m, +23.7%); merchandise inventory $6,552.8m (was $6,609.7m, −0.9% gross, "−2.7% per store" per the release — real inventory discipline against store-count growth); total debt (LT obligations + current portion) **$4,570.8m, down 20.4% YoY from $5,745.1m** — meaningful deleveraging, which is the direct cause of the 25.7% YoY drop in net interest expense; shareholders' equity $9,288.6m (+15.9% YoY). Operating lease liabilities (current + long-term) ≈$11.2bn — large but flat YoY and, being US GAAP (ASC 842), already sit as a P&L opex line rather than mechanically inflating EBITDA the way IFRS 16 lessees' would (per the sector playbook's US/IFRS distinction) — DG's margins are **not** artificially flattered by lease accounting the way a European retail peer's might be.
- **Net debt/EBITDA:** using V1's inputs (net debt $2,981m, TTM EBIT $2,440m + TTM D&A $1,085m ≈ TTM EBITDA $3,525m) ≈ **0.85x — very low**, leaving real balance-sheet capacity.
- **Share count:** diluted shares essentially flat (~220–222m) over the period; buybacks only resumed in FY2026 so have not yet meaningfully reduced the count.
- **M&A:** none pending or recent.

## 7. Valuation snapshot — reconciliation with V1

**V1 verdict: attractive** (score 0.80/1.0). NTM P/E 15.2x vs DG's own 175-month history — **7th percentile** (DG has traded richer than this in ~93% of months since 2012) — and vs peer median 16.1x (COST/DLTR/TGT/WMT). Reverse-DCF implied 10-year growth is **−2.9%** (the market is pricing gradual *decline*), against consensus FY1 growth of +4.0% and delivered 5-year/10-year CAGRs of +5.3%/+7.6% — i.e., the price already assumes something worse than the depressed recent past, not a return to form. V1 base case: revenue growth 7.6%/yr (in line with the 10-year delivered rate — not aggressive), margin reaching only 5.8% by year 3 (below the FY22 peak of 8.79% and roughly in line with what FY2026 guidance already implies for the current year — not aggressive), exit multiple 19.0x (below DG's own historical norm, which a 7th-percentile ranking implies was routinely higher) → **base annualised 3-yr return +34.7%**; bear −19.7%/bull +50.9%×wait, bear scenario is stated as an *annualised return of +19.7%* (V1's "bear" scenario is still a positive return here, reflecting how depressed the starting multiple already is) and bull +50.9%. Street 12-month target range $90–$176 (mean $140.76); V1's base 3-year value of ~$305/share exceeds the Street's 12-month high, which V1 itself flags as a sanity-check flag (`street_flag`) — a reasonable flag given the difference in horizon (3-year vs 12-month), not evidence the model is broken.

**My view: consistent = true, dossier_view = cheap.** I agree the stock is statistically cheap on both an absolute (own-history) and relative (implied-vs-delivered-growth) basis, and the base case's margin/multiple assumptions are, if anything, conservative relative to what has already been guided and delivered. My reservation is not with the valuation math but with two things V1's arithmetic cannot see: (i) the durability of the recovery is not yet proven across a full year without one-off tailwinds (tariff refunds explicitly flagged by management as non-repeating), and (ii) the legal/compliance overhang in §9 is a real tail risk to the "clean execution" assumption embedded in continuing to re-rate toward 19x.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull (3):**
1. Six consecutive quarters of positive comps across *all four* merchandise categories, with positive traffic for five straight quarters — a broad, primary-source-verified demand recovery, not a one-category fluke.
2. Deleveraging is real and already showing up (net interest expense −25.7% YoY) with net debt/EBITDA ≈0.85x, giving room to keep resuming buybacks (guided up to $700m in FY26, versus none in FY25) while continuing store growth (~460 new stores planned FY26).
3. Valuation sits at the 7th percentile of its own 13+ year history — a rare entry point if the recovery merely continues rather than accelerates.

**Bear (3):**
1. Roughly 40% of the most recent guidance raise is a one-time tariff-refund benefit management itself says will not repeat in H2 FY2026 — strip it out and the "beat" is smaller than headline.
2. H1 FY2026 operating cash flow fell 17.5% YoY and free cash flow fell 34.1% YoY even as net income rose — a genuine, unexplained-by-one-offs working-capital drag that bears watching for a second consecutive half.
3. A corporate-wide OSHA safety settlement ($12m, July 2024, on top of >$15m in cumulative penalties since 2017) and an unresolved shareholder securities class action (dismissed without prejudice June 2025; plaintiffs seeking leave to amend as of Aug 2025) both point to unresolved execution/governance friction in a 21,000-plus-store, high-labor-turnover business.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. Same-store sales growth turns negative for **two consecutive quarters** (from +3.5% currently).
2. Full-year diluted-EPS guidance is **cut** versus the prior quarter's range (four straight raises to date; a cut would break the trend).
3. Net debt / TTM EBITDA rises above **2.5x** (currently ≈0.85x).
4. The Board suspends or fails to execute materially against the "**up to $700 million**" FY2026 buyback authorization.
5. A new OSHA penalty or DOJ/regulatory safety action exceeding **$5 million**, or the securities class action is reinstated with an expanded class/damages theory.

## 11. Red-flag scan

- **OSHA settlement (July 2024):** $12m corporate-wide penalty resolving allegations of blocked emergency exits, blocked electrical panels/fire extinguishers and unsafe storage across (ex-pOpshelf) stores; DG has paid **>$15m in cumulative OSHA penalties since 2017** — a recurring, structural safety-compliance issue, not a one-off (source: US DOL/OSHA press release, 2024-07-11; Grocery Dive, CBS News).
- **Shareholder securities class action:** *Washtenaw County Employees' Retirement System v. Dollar General Corp.* (M.D. Tenn., No. 3:23-cv-01250), alleging DG and officers misrepresented the impact of store labor, inventory and pricing practices on results (putative class period May 2020–Aug 2024). Court granted defendants' motion to dismiss the second consolidated amended complaint **without prejudice** on 2026-06-23; lead plaintiffs filed a motion for leave to amend on 2025-08-25. **Status as of this dossier: dismissed but not fully resolved** — monitor.
- **Store-portfolio charge:** FY2024 (ended Jan-2025) results included a ~$0.81/share negative impact "related to the store portfolio"; separately, ~45 pOpshelf stores were closed and 6 converted to Dollar General banners with associated impairment in the same window.
- **Executive change:** EVP & General Counsel Rhonda Taylor announced retirement 2026-08-25 (Item 5.02 8-K); internal successor (Kelly Collier) named, long transition through April 2027 — routine succession, not a red flag on its own.
- No auditor change, no restatement, no going-concern language identified in the filings reviewed.

# PKG — Packaging Corporation of America

**1. Verdict: WATCH.** Best-in-class containerboard/corrugated operator, but the 25 Sep 2026 close ($237.18) already
prices in the cyclical price/volume recovery; EV/EBIT (TTM) is ~24x vs a historical mid-cycle range of ~10–14x for
integrated containerboard producers. Horizon: 12–24 months (re-look after Q3'26 print and outage/capex trajectory
clarify).

**2. Business in plain English.** PKG is the third-largest US containerboard/corrugated packaging producer: it grows
and buys wood, makes containerboard at its own mills, and converts most of it internally into corrugated boxes sold
to food, beverage and e-commerce shippers. It also has a smaller paper (office/printing) segment. Moat = integrated
mill network, freight-advantaged regional plants, and consistently the highest packaging-segment margins in the
sector (management discipline on price-over-volume).

**3. Why the model likes it (b1).** Composite decile 2/quintile 1 (bottom of the live universe overall, driven by
weak Momentum 0.35 pctile and Earnings-momentum 0.10 pctile) but Quality 0.46 and Value 0.45 pctile are mid-pack;
d4/triage flags 75.5% consensus forward EPS growth — this is a cyclical snap-back (price increases + Greif
containerboard-mill acquisition), not a durable growth rate. **Not durable at that magnitude; base case should use
mid-cycle margins, not the trough-to-guide delta.**

**5. Guidance track record.** Q3'26 guide (8-K, 2026-07-23, quoted in Seeking Alpha/company PR): **$2.91/sh
excluding special items**, up from $2.35 realized in Q2'26 — driven by two price increases flowing through, one
extra shipping day, lower planned-outage impact, and Greif accretion; offset by elevated freight and recycled-fiber
costs. This is a sequential guide, not a raise/cut vs a prior FY26 range (PKG does not give full-year EPS guidance).
CapEx guide: $840–870M for FY2026 (up from $828.9M actual FY2025) — heavy new-mill/capacity spend continuing.

only **$432.95M**, implying a ~$310M gap likely from financing-lease/other investing items not captured in the
simple OCF-capex proxy — **flagged as a data_conflict; use the SEC-sourced $742M as primary.** Either way, FCF yield
is thin (2.0–3.5% of $21.1B market cap) because of the heavy new-mill capex program. Consolidated long-term debt
(non-current, consolidated balance sheet) roughly **doubled** from $2.47B (2025-06-30) to $3.97B (2025-09-30
onward) — the step-up coincides with the Greif mill acquisition financing (consolidated figures, CIK 0000075677).
Consolidated net debt ≈$3.8B / TTM EBITDA ≈$2.0B ⇒ **~1.9x net debt/EBITDA** — moderate, but rising from a low base. Cash fell from $685M (FY24) to $442.8M (Q2'26).

**8. Bull case:** (1) Two 2026 price increases plus e-commerce-driven box volume give genuine 2H26 EPS
acceleration ($2.91 Q3 guide vs $2.15 Q2 actual). (2) Greif mill acquisition adds scale/synergies at a
reasonable multiple. (3) Best operating margins in the sector historically support premium valuation.
**Bear case:** (1) EV/EBIT ~24x already prices peak-cycle pricing; a demand slowdown or a competitor
capacity add reverses pricing gains quickly (containerboard is a commodity input). (2) Elevated freight/recycled-
fiber costs and heavy ($840–870M) capex compress FCF yield to ~2–3.5% even in a good year. (3) Leverage roughly
doubled post-Greif; a cyclical downturn would hit net debt/EBITDA from a higher base.

**9. Kill criteria:** (1) Containerboard price index (AF&PA / RISI) falls for two consecutive months. (2) Consolidated
net debt/EBITDA exceeds 2.5x. (3) Quarterly consolidated FCF (OCF−capex, SEC-sourced) below
$100M for two consecutive quarters. (4) Q3'26 adjusted EPS misses the $2.91 guide by >10%. (5) A domestic
containerboard capacity addition (new mill announcement by IP/WestRock/Georgia-Pacific) is announced.

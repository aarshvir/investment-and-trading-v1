# Garmin Ltd. (GRMN) - Fundamental Diligence Dossier (F147, standard depth)

**Verdict: WATCH** - 12-36 month horizon. A debt-free, high-margin consumer-electronics franchise with real momentum, but the 25 Sep 2026 close ($294.14) already prices roughly 11% a year of free-cash-flow growth for ten years versus my evidence-based base case of about 6%, so it is a great business priced for more than it can deliver. The condition that changes this: a price near $210-240 (implied growth 6-8%), or evidence that the Fitness upcycle is structural rather than a launch-cycle peak.

**Triage reconciliation:** Q02 triage did not advance GRMN ("Great business, priced for it (2.7% upside)"). Primary-source work confirms that view; the b1 model ranks it 336 of ~340 (composite 0.32, decile 4), and the price-implied growth is the binding issue, not quality.

## 1. Business in plain English
Garmin sells GPS and sensor-based devices in five segments: Fitness (running/cycling watches, wearables), Outdoor (adventure watches, golf), Aviation (cockpit avionics, OEM and aftermarket), Marine (chartplotters, sonar, radios) and Auto OEM (domain controllers). Customers are consumers and aircraft/boat/vehicle manufacturers. It makes money on hardware with about 61% gross margin and almost no debt. The moat is vertical integration (in-house design and manufacturing), brand trust in sport and aviation, and certified avionics with long switching cycles; subscriptions (Connect+, inReach) are still small.

## 5. Growth drivers and moat
Fitness wearables (Forerunner 70/170, new CIRQA screenless band) and Marine are the engines; Aviation grows high single digit on OEM plus aftermarket (AXIS flight displays); TrainingPeaks/TrainHeroic acquired 20 Jul 2026 ("effect ... not material", 10-Q) adds software. Outdoor (-2%) is pressured in consumer auto and adventure watches. No price-tier or scale risk is visible: segment gross margins run 61-75%.

## 8. Bull and bear
Bull: (1) wearables share gains plus subscription attach lift Fitness to a structurally higher base; (2) 62% gross margin holds as mix improves, with unrecognised tariff refunds as extra upside; (3) net cash of $4.4B funds tuck-ins such as TrainingPeaks and a growing dividend.
Bear: (1) H1 growth is launch-cycle driven, Outdoor is already shrinking and H2 guide implies +7% EPS growth; (2) margin expansion contains one-offs (tariff refunds, about 100 bps of Q2 gross margin); (3) at 29x earnings a return to 22-24x with 8% growth gives roughly flat returns.

## 9. Key risks and kill criteria
1. Consolidated revenue growth below 5% YoY for two consecutive quarters (Q2'26: +11%).
2. Fitness segment revenue down YoY in any quarter (Q2'26: +25%).
3. Consolidated gross margin (ex tariff refunds, per 10-Q MD&A) below 57.0% for two quarters (Q2'26 ex-refund about 61.4%).
4. FY26 pro forma EPS guidance cut below the $10.00 set on 29 Jul 2026.
5. Inventories above 30% of trailing-twelve-month revenue at any quarter end (27 Jun 2026: $1,966M / TTM revenue $7.67B = 25.6%).

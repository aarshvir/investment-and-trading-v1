# WEC Energy Group (WEC) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F94, Wave 6, standard depth)

**Recency gate:** Most recent period incorporated: Q2 2026 10-Q (period end 2026-06-30, filed 2026-08-04, accession 0000783325-26-000087). Checked for events to 2026-09-25 close (price $101.45, per b1_live_scores.csv 2026-09-25 snapshot).

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE.** Horizon 24–36 months. A regulated Wisconsin utility with a rare, real large-load (data-center) growth pipeline layered on top of the normal rate-base growth story, guiding 7–8% EPS growth through 2030 off a record $37.5B five-year capital plan — and the reverse DCF implies the market is pricing in materially *less* growth than that guide. The main watch-item is financing (heavy equity issuance), not the demand story.

## 2. Business in plain English
WEC Energy Group is a regulated electric and natural-gas utility holding company serving Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan and Minnesota (main subsidiaries: We Energies, Wisconsin Public Service, Peoples Gas). It earns a regulator-approved return on its rate base (the capital it invests in poles, wires, pipes, generation and transmission), so growth comes from capital investment approved into rates, not from competitive market share.

## 7. Valuation snapshot
Price $101.45 (2026-09-25). 2026 EPS guide midpoint $5.56 → **P/E ≈ 18.2x**, in line with large regulated-utility peers (typically 17–20x in the current rate environment) — not a bargain multiple, but not stretched either given the visible capital-plan growth.

**Reverse DCF (qualitative):** two-stage model, EPS₀=$5.56, cost of equity 7.5%, terminal growth 3.5%, 10-year explicit period → **implied 10-year EPS growth ≈ −0.8%/year** to justify the current price (i.e., at these discount-rate/terminal-growth assumptions, the terminal value alone very nearly covers the price, meaning almost no explicit-period growth is being priced in). This is *materially below* our base case of ~7.5%/year (management's own guided 7–8% EPS growth through 2030). **Implied vs. base: BELOW**, with a wide margin — the largest gap of the three names in this batch. Caveat: this result is sensitive to the discount-rate/terminal-growth spread (only 400bps here); at a more conventional 8.5% cost of equity / 3% terminal growth, implied growth rises to a still-comfortably-below-base ~3–4%/year. Either way, the direction (implied below base) holds, which is what drives the INCLUDE verdict, but the formal valuation agent should re-run this with a tighter discount-rate assumption before finalizing sizing.

## 8. Bull case
1. The data-center large-load pipeline (Microsoft, Vantage) is contracted/announced, not speculative, and adds a genuine second growth engine on top of normal regulated rate-base growth, unlike most utility "growth stories" that are just capex reclassification.
2. 7–8% guided EPS growth through 2030 is well above the typical 5–7% regulated-utility range, and the reverse DCF says the market isn't paying up for even the low end of that.
3. A-/Baa1-area credit profile (if confirmed) with explicit FFO/debt targets signals management is managing the balance sheet proactively rather than reactively, reducing the odds of a forced, dilutive equity raise beyond the planned $5.3–5.7B.

## 9. Bear case
1. The financing plan requires ~$1.1B/year of equity issuance at a fairly modest ~18x P/E — if the stock re-rates down for any reason, equity becomes more dilutive per dollar raised, creating a negative feedback loop common to capital-intensive utility growth stories.
2. Large-load/data-center demand forecasts (2.6 GW, 3.5 GW, etc.) are 2030-horizon planning figures from the company's own investor materials; data-center buildout plans nationally have seen delays and cancellations at other utilities and hyperscalers when power availability, cost, or AI-capex priorities shift — this is a forecast, not a signed, fully in-service load.
3. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA (~6x, though not the ideal metric for this sector) and the sheer size of the incremental debt load ($14.2–14.8B over five years) raise the stakes on execution; a rate-case setback in any of the four state jurisdictions would compress the return on the capital plan.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Full-year EPS guidance is **cut** (vs. maintained/raised) at any quarterly release.
2. Announced large-load (data-center) capacity commitments are **reduced or cancelled** by Microsoft, Vantage, or another named large customer.
3. A rating agency **downgrades** WEC or a material subsidiary below the current A-/Baa1-area profile, or FFO/debt falls below the stated 15% (S&P) / 16% (Moody's, CFO-pre-WC basis) targets for two consecutive reporting periods.
4. Common-equity issuance in a given year exceeds the guided $5.3–5.7B five-year total pace by >25%, signalling the capital plan is outrunning organic financing capacity.
5. Any adverse rate-case order in Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan, or Mininois [sic Minnesota] that reduces the authorized ROE below ~9.5% or disallows a material portion of planned capex.

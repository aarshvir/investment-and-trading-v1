## 2. Business in plain English
Humana is a large Medicare Advantage insurer: it collects fixed premiums from CMS to cover seniors' medical costs and keeps the spread. It also owns CenterWell (primary-care clinics, pharmacy, home health), the faster-growing profit engine, and is expanding into state Medicaid contracts. The moat is scale and clinic integration; the weakness is concentration in one government-set payment system.

## 8. Bull and bear
Bull: (1) 2026 membership +25% creates a larger base whose margin should normalise; (2) CenterWell guided to $1.3B-$1.8B GAAP operating income, a growing non-premium profit stream; (3) Illinois Medicaid statewide award (go-live January 2027). Bear: (1) insurance segment guided to about breakeven for FY26 while paying 44x earnings; (2) new members run at a higher benefit ratio (10-Q/release), so margin repair is not automatic; (3) every CMS rate or risk-adjustment change hits a concentrated book.

## 9. Key risks and kill criteria
1. Insurance segment GAAP benefit ratio above 93.0% for FY26 (guide: 92.75% plus or minus 25 bps) or any 2026 quarterly print above 92.0% outside the normal Q3/Q4 seasonal step-up.
2. BY2027 Star Ratings (CMS release expected Oct 2026) show less than 25% of Humana MA members in 4-star-or-higher contracts, i.e. no recovery of the 2025 loss.
3. FY26 adjusted EPS guidance cut below the current 'at least $9.00'.
4. Debt-to-total capitalization (revolving credit agreement definition; 42.7% at 30 Jun 2026) above 50%, or parent company cash and short-term investments below $500 million.
5. A CMS RADV extrapolated recovery or DOJ risk-adjustment action with a disclosed estimated loss above $1 billion.

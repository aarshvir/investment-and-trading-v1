# ULTA — Ulta Beauty, Inc. (F107, agent F107)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — thesis horizon 12–24 months. Reservation: comp-sales growth has structurally
decelerated from the ~10%+ era to mid-single digits as Sephora (inside Kohl's, now 1,100+ doors, run-rating >$2bn/yr)
takes share in mass-prestige overlap categories; the current re-acceleration (Q1 FY26 comps +5.3%) needs one more
quarter to confirm it is durable rather than a promotional/loyalty-relaunch pop.

## 2. Business in plain English
Ulta Beauty is the largest U.S. specialty beauty retailer (~1,450+ stores plus e-commerce), selling prestige and
mass cosmetics, skincare, haircare, fragrance and salon services under one roof, monetizing through merchandise
margin, a large loyalty program (Ultamate Rewards) and in-store salon fees. It just closed the Space NK acquisition
(UK prestige beauty retailer), its first material international/inorganic move. Competitive position: category
leader by store count and loyalty base, but facing a structural challenger in Sephora's expansion inside Kohl's,
which gives Sephora suburban/off-mall reach it never had.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
b1_live_scores.csv (as of 2026-08-27 filing lag 55 days): composite percentile decile 9 (top decile), ROE 45.8%,
earnings yield 5.2%, FCF yield 4.9%, EBIT/EV 6.9%, book/price 11.3%, momentum 12-1m **−1.8%** (negative — the
market has been lukewarm), SUE 1.42 (strong recent earnings surprise). The pull is Quality (very high ROE, capital-light
model) and Value/earnings-yield, not Momentum — consistent with a name whose growth story is questioned even as
current-quarter execution beats. **Durability judgment:** the high ROE is a real structural feature (asset-light
specialty retail, high inventory turns, loyalty-driven repeat purchase) not an accounting artefact, but the growth
rate that justified past multiples is not durable at the old pace — Sephora share loss is real and ongoing, so the
quality score should not be read as guaranteeing double-digit growth resumes.

## 5. Guidance track record (last 3 releases; FY26 outlook, vs prior range, quoted verbatim)
- **Q1 FY26 release (2026-06-02):** "Updated Fiscal 2026 Outlook — no change" to initial guide: net sales growth
  6–7%, comp sales growth 2.5–3.5%, operating income growth 6.5–9%. **Maintained**, not raised, despite a strong
  Q1 beat (comps +5.3% vs. 2.5–3.5% full-year guide) — management chose not to flow through a beat into the full-year
  range this early, a conservative but non-trivial signal.
- **Q2 FY26 release (2026-08-27):** guidance **raised**: net sales growth 6–7% → 6.7–7.2%; comp sales growth
  2.5–3.5% → 3.2–3.7%; operating income growth 6.5–9% → 8.3–9.3%; diluted EPS $28.36–28.80 → **$28.70–29.00**; capex
  $400–450M unchanged. Buyback authorization usage plan increased from $1.5bn to $1.8bn for FY26.
- Company gives one formal annual guide, updated quarterly; no separate "beat-and-raise vs Street" figure disclosed
  beyond the ranges above (both quoted from the filed EX-99.1 releases, same URLs as §4).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF conversion:** FY25 OCF $1,502.8M vs GAAP NI $1,153.5M → FCF/NI ≈ 130% (favorable working-capital and D&A
  add-back dynamics typical of a mature retailer); FY25 FCF (OCF − capex) ≈ $1,068M.
- **SBC:** not separately broken out in the releases reviewed; would need the 10-K cash-flow statement note (not
  pulled this pass — **limitation**, flag for next cycle).
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** ULTA does not present a non-GAAP adjusted EPS bridge in its earnings releases — GAAP diluted
  EPS is the headline metric throughout, so there is no adjusted/GAAP gap to reconcile (a genuine positive for
  transparency, unlike many retail peers).
- **Balance sheet, consolidated (10-Q as of 2026-08-01, filed 2026-08-27):** cash & equivalents **$158.5M**
  (down from $424.2M at FY25-end, 2026-01-31, driven by buybacks), short-term investments $55.0M, **short-term debt
  $339.6M** (company states: "primarily to support working capital needs and ongoing capital allocation priorities,
  including share repurchases"), consolidated stockholders' equity **$2,643.7M**. The company carries essentially no
  long-term funded debt on the balance sheet (the only `LongTermDebtNoncurrent` XBRL tag on file dates to a $800M
  2020-era facility that has since rolled off/been reclassified); its principal long-dated liability is operating
  lease obligations from its store footprint, not funded debt — net-debt/EBITDA framing does not apply the way it
  would to a levered retailer. All figures above are **consolidated** (Ulta Beauty, Inc. and subsidiaries; single
  reporting entity, no meaningful minority interests).
- **Share count / buybacks:** repurchased $791.1M in H1 FY26 (1.4M shares); $1.0bn remained under the $3.0bn
  program as of 2026-08-01, and management now plans to exhaust that $1.0bn balance by FYE26 — an aggressive,
  EPS-accretive capital-return pace that explains why diluted EPS growth (+13–16%) outpaces operating-income growth
  (+10–12%).
- **M&A:** Space NK acquisition closed and is now consolidated (drove ~2pp of net-sales growth and diluted gross
  margin by ~10bp in Q2 FY26 per company commentary); no other pending deals disclosed.

- **V1 systematic valuation:** no row exists yet for ULTA in `v1_valuation_table.csv` / `v1_valuation.json` (wave 6

- **Reverse-DCF sense (my own, qualitative-quantitative, since V1 has not run):** using FCF yield ~4.6%, a retail-sector
  WACC of ~8.5%, and the simple Gordon relationship (yield ≈ WACC − g), the price implies roughly **4–5% perpetual
  FCF/EPS growth**. That sits *below* both the company's FY26 guided operating-income growth (8.3–9.3%) and its own
  historical growth rate, but is *roughly in line with or a little above* what mid-single-digit comps plus modest
  buyback accretion can plausibly deliver over 10 years once Sephora/Kohl's share losses are netted out. My base
  case for 10-year FCF/EPS growth is **5–7%** (3–4% comps + 1–2pp margin/mix + buyback accretion), i.e. **at or
  slightly above** what the price implies — this is the borderline case the addendum flags: a good business, not
  egregiously priced for perfection, but not obviously cheap either.
- 3 closest peers for context (not independently re-priced this pass): e.l.f. Beauty, Sally Beauty, and the Sephora
  business embedded in LVMH — none is a clean apples-to-apples public comp; peer comparison is directional only.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Q1 FY26 comp reacceleration (+5.3%, ticket **and** traffic both positive) shows the "Ulta Beauty Unleashed"
   strategy (loyalty relaunch, brand newness, omnichannel) is working, not just lapping easy compares.
2. Guidance has been raised, not cut, in the two most recent quarters — the opposite of the deceleration story
   priced in a year ago.
3. Space NK gives a first credible international growth vector and diversifies away from single-market (US) beauty
   retail concentration.

**Bear:**
1. Comp growth still decelerated sequentially within FY26 (5.3% → 3.8%), and Sephora's Kohl's footprint (>1,100
   doors, run-rating >$2bn) is a structural, not cyclical, share-donor dynamic that management does not control.
2. 12-month price momentum is negative (−1.8% per b1 data) despite the earnings beats — the market is not yet
   rewarding the re-acceleration, which could mean it sees it as unsustainable (promotional, loyalty-relaunch
   sugar-high) rather than durable.
3. Buyback-driven EPS growth (13–16%) well above operating-income growth (10–12%) means a meaningful share of the
   "beat and raise" is capital allocation, not organic execution — if buybacks slow (cash is down to $158.5M and
   the company is drawing $339.6M of short-term debt to help fund them), EPS growth decelerates further.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Comparable sales growth below **2.0%** for two consecutive quarters (below the low end of the raised FY26
   guide range).
2. FY26 diluted EPS guidance **cut** from the current $28.70–29.00 range at any subsequent quarterly release.
3. Gross margin below **38.5%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. 39.1–40.1% in the last four quarters shown above).
4. Net new Sephora-at-Kohl's door count growth re-accelerates materially (company/press disclosure) alongside a
   renewed comp deceleration below 3%, confirming share loss rather than a temporary lapse.
5. Short-term debt balance grows beyond **$500M** while cash stays below $200M, signalling buybacks are being
   funded by leverage rather than FCF.

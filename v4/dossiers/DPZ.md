# DPZ — Domino's Pizza, Inc.

**1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 24–36 months. High-quality, asset-light franchisor trading near the low end of its own valuation history, but FY2026 guidance was already cut once this year, U.S. same-store sales are barely positive, and the buyback-funded, negative-equity capital structure leaves less room for error than the "cheap franchise" framing suggests.

**2. Business in plain English.** Domino's is the world's largest pizza company by sales, operating almost entirely through franchisees (only ~2% of the U.S. system is company-owned). It earns royalties on franchisee retail sales (~5.5–7% of sales) plus supply-chain margin from selling food, boxes and equipment to its own U.S. franchisees, and collects a smaller royalty from ~100 international master franchisees who run the stores abroad. Competitive position: #1 or #2 pizza QSR share in most markets it operates in, built on delivery speed/technology (10–30 minute promise, app/AI ordering) and the lowest menu prices in category.

**3. Why the model likes it.** b1 factor percentiles (as of the live snapshot): quality composite 96th percentile (gp/assets 99th, OCF/assets 99th, accruals 90th — very clean, capital-light earnings), value 62nd percentile driven by earnings/FCF yield (ep 87th, fcfp 66th), but momentum only 1st percentile (stock down ~17% over the year per triage) and book/price 1st percentile (negative tangible book from buybacks, not informative here). The quality signal is durable — it reflects the franchise royalty model, not a one-off — but the momentum/value combination is the market pricing in the deceleration confirmed below, not simply "value in a franchise-quality name" as triage's reason stated.

Revenue growth decelerated from ~6%+ YoY in FY2025 to +3.5–4.3% in H1 2026. Operating margin has been stable-to-slightly-improving (18.4%→20.0%), so the deceleration is a top-line (order/comp) story, not a cost-control problem. **5. Guidance track record (last 4 releases; primary source: 10-Q/8-K earnings releases and reported call commentary).**
- **Q4/FY2025 release (2026-02-23):** initial FY2026 guidance ~8% operating income growth ex-FX/refranchising.
- **Q1 2026 release (2026-04-27):** **CUT.** U.S. and international same-store sales guidance lowered to positive low-single-digit; operating income growth guidance lowered to mid-to-high-single-digit (from ~8%). Management cited "increased macro pressure" and "a challenging start to the year."
- **Q2 2026 release (2026-07-20):** **MAINTAINED** the Q1-revised ranges (U.S. SSS low-single-digit; international SSS low-single-digit, helped by the 2026 World Cup; global retail sales mid-single-digit; operating income growth mid-to-high-single-digit), but trimmed U.S. net store growth to ~175 stores from "175-plus."
- Net: guidance was cut once (Q1'26) and held flat since (Q2'26), with a minor unit-growth trim. This is a materially different picture from a simple "guidance raised" narrative and is flagged as a correction to that pattern in v3/triage-era commentary.

**6. Earnings quality & balance sheet.** FCF conversion is strong and clean: FCF (OCF−capex) ran $147–176mm/quarter in the last five quarters, funding dividends (~$0.6–1.5mm→$60–68mm/quarter as the payout ratio rose) and large buybacks ($75–235mm/quarter). SBC is not separately broken out in these tags but is modest for the sector (asset-light model, few employees relative to system sales). **Capital structure is the central feature to underwrite, not a footnote:** Domino's funds itself via whole-business securitization notes against franchise royalty cash flows, and stockholders' equity has been **negative since at least 2024** (−$3.98bn at 2026-06-14, from −$3.96bn a year earlier) because buybacks have been funded partly with debt against a cash-generative but non-owned store base. Debt + capital-lease obligations were $4.82bn at FY2025-end (2025-12-28), down slightly from $4.98bn a year earlier — i.e., leverage is high (triage's flagged ~4.8x is consistent with debt/EBITDA-proxy math here) but *stable-to-declining*, and this is a structural feature of the franchisor model (shared by Wendy's, Restaurant Brands, Planet Fitness), not distress. The risk is that continued buybacks at the current pace (~$800mm–$1bn/yr annualized against a $9.66bn market cap) require the securitized royalty pool to keep growing; a same-store-sales stall for several quarters would test that assumption. Share count has fallen ~2.9%/yr, which is currently the single largest driver of EPS growth.

**7. Valuation snapshot.** Price $292.14 (2026-09-25 close); market cap $9.66bn; EV $14.65bn. TTM diluted EPS $17.63 (sum of last 4 quarters) → TTM P/E 16.6x (cross-checks to Yahoo's 16.56x). Forward (consensus NTM) EPS $20.83 → forward P/E 14.0x — **not independently recalendarized**, so per the lead's known flaw (mixed fiscal-year forward P/E), treat this as directional only. Own 10-year history: Domino's traded 20–30x P/E during its 2015–2019 high-growth phase and has compressed to the mid-teens as U.S./international comp growth slowed; 14–16.6x sits toward the low end (cheap-looking) of that range. **No V1 row exists for DPZ** (outside the top-70 quant universe that got systematic valuation coverage), so `v1_verdict` is null and there is nothing to reconcile. A simple reverse Gordon-growth back-out (14x forward P/E, ~8.5% assumed cost of equity given beta 0.95) implies the market is pricing roughly **1–2% perpetual growth** — below even a conservative base case (see §8). That gap is the basis for the INCLUDE-SMALL call, but it should be read as a rough sense-check, not a formal DCF (the valuation agent's V1/V1X model does not cover this name).

**8. Bull case / Bear case.**
*Bull:* (1) International recovers with 2026 World Cup tailwind and DPE stabilization; (2) buyback-driven EPS compounding continues at high-single/low-double-digit even with modest comp growth; (3) multiple re-rates back toward its 18–20x mid-cycle range as growth reaccelerates.
*Bear:* (1) U.S. comps stay near-flat (Q2'26 U.S. SSS +0.1%, weakest in over a year, missed consensus of +0.62%) as QSR competition and value-menu wars intensify; (2) leverage keeps rising to fund buybacks without matching royalty-pool growth, forcing a buyback slowdown that removes the main EPS-growth lever; (3) the DPE securities litigation (below) signals the international master-franchisee model has real execution risk, not just a one-off news item.

**9. Kill criteria (measurable) -- what would break the thesis.**
1. U.S. same-store sales negative for two consecutive quarters.
2. FY operating-income-growth guidance cut again below the current mid-to-high-single-digit range.
3. Debt + capital-lease obligations / EBITDA-proxy rises above 6.0x (from ~4.8x currently).
4. International same-store sales (ex-FX) negative for two consecutive quarters.
5. Quarterly buyback pace cut by more than half from the trailing-4-quarter average (signal of FCF/leverage stress in the securitization structure).

**10. Catalysts & calendar.** Next earnings ~2026-10-13 (Q3 2026, per vendor estimate; not yet confirmed by company press release as of 2026-09-25). World Cup 2026 (June–July, hosted across the U.S./Mexico/Canada) is an explicit FY2026 international-SSS tailwind management is guiding to.

**11. Red-flag scan.**
- **Ongoing securities class action** (filed 2024, status confirmed ONGOING as of 2025-06-11, Hon. Linda V. Parker; no 2026 resolution found in this search): alleges Domino's failed to disclose that Domino's Pizza Enterprises (DPE), its largest master franchisee (~19% of global store count), was struggling with new-store openings and closures, such that Domino's was unlikely to hit its long-term global net-store-growth guidance. Class period 2023-12-07 to 2024-07-17. This is directly relevant to §9's international kill criterion.
- **Class action filed 2026-02-26** (N.D. Cal.) alleging undisclosed "junk fees" mislabeled as tax on receipts at certain California stores — consumer, not securities, litigation; financial exposure appears immaterial to date.
- No auditor changes, restatements, or going-concern language found in the filings reviewed.
- No material insider-selling pattern found; insider ownership is minimal (0.5%), typical for a long-public franchisor.

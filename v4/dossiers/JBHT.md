# J.B. Hunt Transport Services (JBHT) — Diligence Dossier

**Recency gate:** Most recent period incorporated: Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended 2026-06-30, 10-Q filed 2026-07-24; earnings released 2026-07-15). Checked for events to 2026-09-25 close. Data-quality note: SEC EDGAR's Archives document server was in a maintenance outage for part of this research session; XBRL structured data (data.sec.gov) and the FY2025 10-K full text were both obtained once it came back up. All figures below are GAAP unless marked "(non-GAAP)"; JBHT discloses very few non-GAAP adjustments (mainly "revenue excluding fuel surcharge," used for volume/mix commentary, with no formal reconciliation table published).

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL (half weight).** Thesis horizon: 12–24 months. JBHT is a genuinely high-quality, low-leverage, non-union logistics franchise showing real evidence of a freight-cycle inflection (Q2 FY26 revenue +19% YoY, intermodal operating income +58%), but the stock's 31x trailing / ~24x NTM P/E already prices a durable recovery that is one quarter old, management itself declined to extrapolate ("careful in ensuring Intermodal is the correct long-term answer" rather than chasing volume — CFO/COO on the Q2'26 call), and two of five segments (Final Mile, Truckload) are shrinking or losing money. Half-weight reflects strong conviction on business quality, low conviction on the entry price.

## 2. Business in plain English
J.B. Hunt is a US surface-transportation and logistics company operating through five segments: Intermodal (JBI — the largest, moving freight in containers via rail plus company-owned drayage trucks), Dedicated Contract Services (DCS — trucks and drivers committed to a single customer's network), Integrated Capacity Solutions (ICS — an asset-light freight brokerage matching shippers with third-party carriers), Final Mile Services (big-and-bulky home delivery), and Truckload (JBT — traditional over-the-road trucking). It makes money by charging shippers (retailers, manufacturers, e-commerce companies) for capacity and network reliability, competing on service and price against rail-owned intermodal competitors, other asset-based truckers, and brokers.

## 3. Why the model likes it
From the preliminary quant screen (`lead_prelim_rank.csv`, families Q/V/M; official point-in-time scores not yet live): JBHT ranks **#12 of ~500** S&P names, prelim composite 0.814, driven by **top-decile Quality (0.968)** and **top-decile Momentum (0.978)**, with only middling Value (0.495 — it is not cheap). The Quality score is durable and evidenced independently here: ROE ~16–19%, ROIC ~13.5–15%, net debt/EBITDA ~1.0x, zero unionized labor, strong buybacks funded from internally generated cash (see §6). The Momentum score is largely an **artefact of one quarter** — FY2025 segment revenue was essentially flat (Intermodal +0.3%, DCS -0.6%, Final Mile -9.5%) and the acceleration is entirely a 2026 phenomenon (Q1'26 revenue +4.6% YoY, Q2'26 +19.4% YoY) tied to a specific, checkable cause: industry-wide truckload capacity tightening plus "road-to-rail" conversion, not a structural re-rating of the whole business. Whether this is durable is precisely the open question this dossier flags as the central risk.

## 5. Guidance track record
JBHT **does not issue formal quantitative revenue or EPS guidance** — consistent across all of the last four releases (Q3'25, Q4'25/FY25, Q1'26, Q2'26 earnings calls) — so there is no "raised/maintained/cut vs. prior range" table to build for the headline numbers, and no prior report should have claimed otherwise. The one quantified, trackable guidance item is the **effective tax rate**: full-year 2026 guidance was narrowed on the Q2'26 call to **24.0%–24.5%**, versus an effective rate of ~25.4% realized in Q2'26 itself and ~24.8% for FY2025 — a modest tightening, not a raise or cut of substance. Beyond that, management gives only qualitative color: on Q2'26 it guided to demand "remain[ing] strong" through H2 2026 and a peak season "similar" in timing/shape to 2025, explicitly declining to quantify the intermodal volume outlook when pressed by analysts (Evercore's Jon Chappell asked directly whether June's growth rate could hold; management would not commit to a number).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
**FCF conversion:** FY2025 FCF/NI = 947.6/598.3 = 158% (capex well below D&A in a low-investment year); TTM-through-Q2'26 conversion is even higher given the capex pullback — but see the OCF-decline point in §4, which is the more important quality signal than the FCF/NI ratio in isolation. **SBC:** not separately broken out in the sources reviewed here — flagged as a gap; recommend the valuation/quant team pull `ShareBasedCompensation` from XBRL directly. **GAAP vs. adjusted:** minimal gap — JBHT does not report a separate adjusted EPS; the only routinely-cited non-GAAP figure is "revenue excluding fuel surcharge" for volume/mix analysis. **Leverage:** gross debt $1,227.7M (current maturities + long-term), cash just $4.2M (JBHT runs a deliberately lean cash balance and sweeps excess cash to debt paydown and buybacks — confirmed by the CFO's Q2'26 comment that consolidated debt fell to $1.15B at 2026-06-30 from $1.72B a year earlier); **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 1.0x on FY2025 EBITDA, improving toward ~0.7–1.0x on the TTM run-rate** — comfortable, and interest coverage is very strong (EBIT/interest ≈ 13x on FY2025 interest expense of $75.2M). **Debt composition:** senior notes issued March 2019 and March 2025 plus a revolving credit facility (exact coupons/maturities not extracted from the excerpt reviewed — recommend a follow-up XBRL pull of the debt footnote if precision is required for the credit view). **M&A:** none identified in 2025–2026. **Share count:** buybacks are substantial and ongoing — $1B authorization (Aug 2024) plus a further $1B (effective Oct 2025); $968M remaining after Q4'25 (843k shares at avg. $166.02); $791M remaining after Q2'26 (392k shares at avg. ~$250/share — notably *above* today's $225.46, i.e. some recent buybacks were done at a higher price than today's, a minor capital-allocation-timing demerit, not a red flag). Dividend: modest (0.78% trailing yield, 25% payout ratio), well covered, room to grow.

## 7. Valuation snapshot
NTM P/E (calendarized) **24.1x**; trailing P/E **31.4x** (via `valuation.py` EV bridge: EV $22.39B on TTM EBITDA $1.67B → **EV/EBITDA 13.4x**, EV/EBIT 23.4x); FCF yield 4.3–4.5%. Against its own history, JBHT has historically traded in a wide range (high-single-digit to mid-20s trailing P/E, richest at earnings troughs and cheapest at peaks — a cyclical inversion the sector playbook explicitly warns about), so today's multiple sits at the rich end even before judging the recovery's durability. Against peers, JBHT is actually the **cheapest of the large-cap truckers** on forward P/E — Old Dominion Freight Line ~35–39x forward, Knight-Swift ~34–36x forward, versus JBHT's ~24–30x depending on source — but "cheapest of an expensive peer group" is not the same as cheap. **Reverse-DCF** (via `valuation.py`, WACC 8.5%, terminal growth 3%): the current EV already implies **~6% per-year FCF growth for a full decade** — a plausible but non-trivial hurdle for a historically cyclical trucker whose FY2025 revenue was flat. A probability-weighted bear/base/bull scenario (bear $5.50 EPS/16x, base $7.20/22x, bull $8.50/27x) centers on **$155/share, ~31% below the current price** — i.e. the market is already underwriting meaningfully more than this dossier's base case.

**Valuation reconciliation with V1 (added 2026-09-26):** V1 rates JBHT **"fair"** (score −0.03, essentially neutral), with a base-case 3-year annualized return of **+6.4%** — implying a price near $266 in three years versus $225.46 spot — and notes this stays inside the Street's 12-month target range ($182–370, so no internal street-flag caution is raised). This dossier's own probability-weighted bear/base/bull scenario above centers on **$155/share, about 31% below the current price**, and §3/§7 state plainly that the stock's 31.4x trailing / 24.1x NTM P/E already "prices a durable recovery that is one quarter old" and that "the market is already underwriting meaningfully more than this dossier's base case." The two are not reconcilable as a rounding difference: V1 sees modest further upside, this dossier sees material downside risk to fair value. **This dossier's more cautious reading is better supported**, because it rests on current, concrete facts a trailing-multiple model does not see: the freight-cycle inflection is one quarter old and management itself declined to extrapolate it (§3/§9); two of five segments (Truckload, Final Mile) are shrinking or unprofitable (§4/§9); and H1 FY26 operating cash flow fell year-over-year despite higher earnings (§4/§9). V1's own-history percentile (36.7%, on NTM P/E) is not itself a red flag, so the two models are not flatly contradictory on every input — but on the bottom-line valuation call, the dossier's downside-weighted scenario is the better-evidenced one, consistent with this dossier's smaller, INCLUDE-SMALL sizing.

## 8. Bull case
1. **Verified, real freight-cycle inflection**, not just a comp effect: Q2'26 intermodal load volume +10% (units, not just price) alongside operating leverage (JBI operating income +58% on +22% segment revenue), and management cites industry-wide capacity tightening (safety enforcement, driver-market tightness) as the structural driver — a classic late-cycle-to-recovery signal for a bellwether name.
2. **Balance sheet and capital discipline are genuinely excellent**: net debt/EBITDA ~1x, zero union exposure, debt down ~$570M YoY, and $135M of structural cost already removed with "additional runway" cited by management — this is a company that used the freight downturn to get leaner, not just survive it.
3. **Record dedicated-fleet sales pipeline** ("record level in terms of number of trucks," per DCS president, exceeding COVID-era peaks) and record "mini-bid" frequency across the network suggest share-of-wallet gains are still to come through 2027, independent of the broader freight cycle.

## 9. Bear case
1. **One good quarter does not confirm a cycle turn**, and management itself would not commit to extrapolating June's growth rate — the stock has already re-rated as if it has. A reversion of intermodal pricing/volume in H2'26 (management flagged rail-service "moderation" as volumes accelerated, a potential constraint) would be a straightforward disappointment against a 24x NTM multiple.
2. **Two of five segments are structurally weak right now**: Truckload is losing money on purchased-transportation inflation despite 35% revenue growth (a genuine earnings-quality flag — growth funded by higher-cost outsourced capacity, not owned assets), and Final Mile revenue is shrinking by management's own choice. A "sum of the parts" investor would not pay a premium multiple for either.
3. **OCF fell year-over-year in H1'26** even as earnings rose — working-capital consumption from the volume surge is a real, current adverse fact, not a hypothetical risk, and the FCF improvement it's paired with was capex-driven (capex -64% YoY) rather than earnings-driven; capex cannot fall indefinitely if volume growth is to be sustained.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
These are the specific, observable, time-bound conditions that would **invalidate** the INCLUDE-SMALL thesis above:
- Intermodal revenue growth (ex-fuel) decelerates back below ~5% YoY for two consecutive quarters — the core recovery thesis fails.
- Truckload (JBT) segment operating losses persist for more than two more quarters without a clear path to breakeven from pricing actions.
- Net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.0x (would indicate the buyback/capex-cut strategy has outrun cash generation).
- OCF conversion (OCF/NI) stays below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters, signaling the working-capital drag is structural rather than transitory.
- Any loss of the "record" DCS sales pipeline translating to signed, revenue-generating fleet growth within the stated 12–18 month cycle.

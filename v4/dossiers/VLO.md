# VLO - Valero Energy Corporation (agent F119, standard depth, information cutoff 2026-09-25 close)

**1. Verdict: WATCH** (horizon 12-36 months). Best-in-class refiner with a strong balance sheet, but the $387.18 close (25 Sep 2026) prices a refining-margin spike (Q2 2026 margin $23.62/bbl vs $12.29/bbl for FY2025) as if it were durable. Triage (Q04_triage.json: advance=false, "negative street upside... fully reflected") is confirmed on valuation, not on quality. Condition that would change it: price near or below ~$240 (about 12x a mid-cycle ~$20 EPS), or four consecutive quarters of refining margin at or above ~$17/bbl.

**2. Business in plain English.** Valero turns crude oil into gasoline, diesel and jet fuel in 14 refineries (about 3.0 million barrels/day capacity), and also owns 12 ethanol plants and half of the Diamond Green Diesel (DGD) renewable-diesel joint venture (8-K EX-99.01, acc. 0001628280-26-050822). It is a price taker: profit equals the spread between product prices and crude cost times volume. Advantages are scale, Gulf Coast complexity and low costs, not pricing power.

**3. Why the model likes it, and whether it is durable.** Quant (b1/d4; filings: 10-K acc. 0001628280-26-011499, 10-Q acc. 0001628280-26-050937): trailing GAAP EPS $23.97 (computed: FY2025 $7.57 + 1H26 $16.78 - 1H25 $0.37) and forward EPS $38.04 give a 10.2x forward P/E, 16.2x trailing, 12-month momentum strong. That is an artefact: the sector playbook (oil-gas.md) warns cyclicals print their lowest P/E at peak earnings. Refining margin per barrel of throughput (consolidated, release tables): Q3 2025 $13.14, Q4 2025 $13.61, Q1 2026 $14.90, Q2 2026 $23.62. The 10-Q outlook attributes the spike to Middle East/Russia refining and export outages and low product inventories, i.e. disruption that can reverse.

**8. Bull case and bear case.** Bull: (1) global refined-product inventories stay low and capacity (Benicia, other closures) keeps tight, sustaining $18-20 margins; (2) renewable diesel demand supported by higher 2026-27 RVOs ("Renewable diesel demand is expected to remain strong", 10-Q outlook); (3) $7.9bn cash funds buybacks above the 59% payout. Bear: (1) margin reverts to 2024-25 range ($10.6-12.3) and EPS falls to about $7-11, implying a stock in the $100s; (2) disruption-driven spreads (Middle East, Hormuz routing) reverse with a ceasefire or SPR releases; (3) buybacks executed at $251 and possible further buying at peak prices destroy value.

# NWS / NWSA — News Corporation — Diligence Dossier (Agent F89, STANDARD depth)
Covers both share classes: **NWSA** (Class A, non-voting-ish, widely held) and **NWS** (Class B, voting, ~35% insider-held per Yahoo cross-check) — one company, one 10-K/10-Q set. Prices differ (NWSA $28.49 / NWS $31.75, 2026-09-25 close) for the usual voting-premium reason.

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** for both NWSA and NWS. Thesis horizon: 12–36 months. Reason: a real digital-transformation story (Dow Jones data/subscriptions, Move/realtor.com, HarperCollins) growing segment EBITDA faster than revenue, at a valuation that implies far less growth than the business is delivering — but the dual-class structure (Murdoch family voting control) is a specific, named governance reservation that caps this at half weight, not full.

## 2. Business in plain English
News Corp is a diversified media and information-services company: Dow Jones (WSJ, Barron's, Factiva/risk-and-compliance data products — increasingly subscription/data, not print-ad dependent), Move Inc. (realtor.com), News Media (Australian/UK/US newspapers — The Sun, The Times, New York Post, Australian mastheads), Book Publishing (HarperCollins) and a residual stake in DAZN (post the April-2025 Foxtel sale). It makes money from digital subscriptions, real-estate lead-generation fees, advertising and book sales.

## 3. Why the model likes it / is it durable?
Triage's headline (~$45% guided EPS growth at ~21x NTM P/E") **could not be verified as stated and is corrected here.** Consensus EPS growth from D4 (Yahoo-sourced, cross-checked against the primary release) is **+11.8% (FY0) / +14.6% (FY1)**, not 45% — flagged as a **data conflict** with the prior triage note; whatever the 45% figure referenced (possibly a specific segment or an EPS-growth calc contaminated by the FY26 GAAP net-income comp, see §4) it is not supported by consensus estimates or the primary release. What *is* durable and well-supported: FY2026 (ended 2026-06-30) revenue +7% to $9.03B, and **Total Segment EBITDA +15% to >$1.6B**, with margin expanding to 18% from 16.7% — genuine operating leverage from Dow Jones digital subscriptions (total Dow Jones subscriptions +7% to 6.7M, WSJ digital-only +8% to ~4.5M, alongside a digital-subscription price increase from $39.99 to $44.99) and Move/realtor.com (Q4 revenue +13%, its seventh consecutive quarter of growth).

No News Corp row exists in `v1_valuation_table.csv` / `v1_valuation.json` (neither NWS nor NWSA) → **v1_verdict = null**. My own simplified reverse-DCF (Gordon-growth on FCF yield, same method and caveat as the NDSN dossier):

- WACC ≈ 8.2% (beta 0.92, rf 5.17%, ERP 4.14% → cost of equity 9.0%; ~14% debt weight at ~5% pretax cost).
- Implied perpetual FCF growth ≈ 8.2% − 6.1% ≈ **2.3%/yr**.
- Base case (my own): Total Segment EBITDA growth of 15% in FY2026 will not repeat every year, but a mid-single-to-low-double-digit medium-term growth rate (digital subscription price increases + Move's seven-consecutive-quarter growth streak, offset by legacy print/News Media decline) is a reasonable **base ≈ 6–8%/yr**.
- **implied_vs_base = "below"** — the ~2.3%/yr the market is pricing in is comfortably below the 6–8%/yr base case, consistent with the INCLUDE-SMALL call.

## 8. Bull / Bear
**Bull:** (1) Dow Jones digital subscription growth + price increases (WSJ full price now $44.99) is a high-margin, recurring-revenue flywheel; (2) Move/realtor.com has now grown seven consecutive quarters even through a soft US housing market; (3) net-cash balance sheet gives buyback/M&A optionality without leverage risk.
**Bear:** (1) dual-class structure means minority (Class A) holders cannot force capital-allocation or governance changes regardless of performance; (2) legacy print/News Media segments remain a structural drag that could offset digital gains if ad markets weaken; (3) the FY25-vs-FY26 GAAP net-income comparison shows how easily one-off gains (Foxtel) can flatter a single year's headline number — investors reading only the trailing P/E without adjusting for this would misprice the stock.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Total Segment EBITDA growth (company's own primary metric) turns negative for two consecutive quarters.
2. Dow Jones digital-only subscriber count (WSJ) declines YoY for two consecutive quarters.
3. Consolidated net debt (per the 10-K/10-Q balance sheet) exceeds $1.5B net (vs. today's ~$106M net cash) without a value-accretive acquisition to show for it.
4. Move/realtor.com segment revenue growth turns negative (breaking the seven-quarter growth streak) for two consecutive quarters.
5. Any transaction that increases Murdoch-family voting concentration or entrenchment beyond current levels without a minority-shareholder vote.

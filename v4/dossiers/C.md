**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Horizon: 24–36 months.
One-sentence reason: the Jane Fraser turnaround is now showing up unambiguously in the numbers (RoTCE 13.0% in Q2

## 2. Business in plain English
Citigroup is a global, systemically important bank organized around five core businesses: Services (transaction
banking/treasury and trade solutions and securities services — its crown jewel and highest-return unit), Markets
(fixed income and equities trading), Banking (investment banking, corporate lending), Wealth (private bank/wealth
management), and U.S. Personal Banking (branded cards, retail banking, Banamex in Mexico, being separated). It earns
net interest income on loans/deposits/trading books plus fees on payments, custody, trading and advisory. Under CEO
Jane Fraser (since 2021) it has been simplifying the structure (exiting most international consumer franchises,
separating Banamex) and cutting costs to raise a chronically sub-peer ROE.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Q04 triage: 39% forward EPS growth and a cheap 10.8x NTM P/E reflect "real turnaround progress" (32% momentum), but a
weak statistical-model rank (418/500) and sub-par 8.5% ROE (still below peers) triggered full diligence. The Q1/Q2
2026 prints (below) confirm the EPS-growth read is not a one-off: diluted EPS accelerated from $1.96 (Q2-25) to $3.15
(Q2-26), and RoTCE improved 430bps YoY with "significant improvement in returns across every single business"
(company Q2 2026 results). This looks durable rather than an artefact — it is broad-based (Services, Markets and
Banking all improved), not a single trading-desk beat — though the ROE base remains below JPM/BAC-level peers.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Broad-based, accelerating operating leverage (Services, Markets, Banking all improving) with RoTCE up 430bp YoY —
   the turnaround is showing in every segment, not just one trading quarter.
2. Capital return: CET1 at 12.8% (120bp above minimum) gives room for continued buybacks even as the bank grows the
   balance sheet, directly supporting EPS growth on top of the operating improvement.
3. Banamex separation, once it clears (IPO now targeted with a >$3bn deal size being marketed as of Sept 2026), removes
   a historically low-return, capital-intensive legacy business and should be a further ROE tailwind on completion.

**Bear:**
1. Banamex timeline has slipped repeatedly (2025 → 2026 → now "early 2027" deconsolidation, IPO beyond that) — this is
   the single most-delayed part of the turnaround story and remains a source of execution risk and headline risk.
2. RoTCE of 13% is still below top-tier US bank peers; if the Q1/Q2 2026 acceleration was partly cyclical (strong
   Markets revenue, benign credit costs) rather than structural, mean-reversion in a weaker macro quarter would hit
   the multiple hard given how much of the recent re-rating is tied to the "13% is durable" read.
3. It's a G-SIB: regulatory capital requirements, stress-test (CCAR) outcomes, and any adverse action on the Banamex
   IPO structure from either US or Mexican regulators are risks largely outside management's control.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. RoTCE falls below 10% for two consecutive quarters (a clear reversal of the current trend, not just quarterly
   noise).
2. CET1 ratio falls below 12.0% (within 20bp of an involuntary buyback pause under the bank's own stated glide path).
3. Banamex deconsolidation is not completed by end-2027 (a further, third slip past the "early 2027" target now
   guided).
4. Net interest income growth turns negative YoY for two consecutive quarters (a rates/credit-cycle warning sign for
   a bank whose recent beat has been partly NII-driven).
5. Any regulatory enforcement action or material adverse finding tied to the long-running consent orders on data/risk
   infrastructure (monitor 8-K filings for this specifically; none identified in this pass, but not exhaustively
   searched).

# Labcorp Holdings Inc. (LH) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F111, Wave 7)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** (18–36 month horizon). Reason: a duopoly clinical-lab franchise (with Quest) is compounding revenue at mid-single digits with margin expansion and raised guidance two quarters running, and the reverse-DCF-implied growth is below both my base case and management's own guided algorithm.

## 2. Business in plain English
Labcorp runs the diagnostic-testing infrastructure much of the US healthcare system relies on: patient service centers and central labs that run blood work, genetic tests and other diagnostics for physicians, hospitals and health plans (Diagnostics Laboratories segment), plus a Biopharma Laboratory Services segment that runs central-lab testing for pharma clinical trials. It earns fees per test/requisition; scale and a national logistics network are the moat, shared with Quest Diagnostics in a durable two-player structure for most of the US market.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Q07 triage: "15.9x NTM P/E with 11% street upside and accelerating forward EPS growth is a reasonable growth-at-a-fair-price case in a duopoly industry," no red flags listed. My read: durable — the 2Q'26 print showed diluted EPS +28.5% YoY ($2.84→$3.64) and adjusted EPS +14.9% YoY ($4.35→$4.99) on 5.8% enterprise revenue growth, i.e. margin expansion on top of organic growth, not a one-off. Management explicitly raised full-year guidance on the back of this (Section 5), which is a stronger signal than a single beat.

## 8. Bull case
1. Two consecutive quarters of raised full-year guidance (confirmed via the Previous/Updated table above), not a single beat.
2. GAAP EPS growing faster than adjusted EPS (+28.5% vs +14.9% YoY) — margin/quality of earnings improving, not just non-GAAP add-backs doing the work.
3. Biopharma backlog $8.73bn with 1.03x book-to-bill signals forward revenue visibility beyond the guided year.

## 9. Bear case
1. Company is a repeat subject of **qui tam False Claims Act suits** (FY2025 10-K, filed 2026-02-24, accession 0000920148-26-000111: "named from time to time in suits brought under the qui tam provisions of the False Claims Act") and received a **DOJ subpoena (served June 27, 2022) requiring production of documents related to urine drug testing**, with which "the Company is cooperating" per the same filing — an open, named legal exposure, not resolved as of the 10-K's filing.
2. Cash fell from $981.1m to $141.8m and long-term debt rose ~$773m in a single quarter (2Q'26) — a capital-structure move I could not fully explain from primary sources this pass.
3. Reimbursement-rate and Medicaid billing-practice risk is structural to the lab-testing model (same 10-K notes "inquiries, including subpoenas and other civil investigative demands, from governmental agencies, Medicare or Medicaid payers").

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

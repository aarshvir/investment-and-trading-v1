# AMCR — Amcor plc — Diligence Dossier (Agent F72, Wave 4)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — 24–36 month horizon, reservation named: leverage. AMCR (Jersey-incorporated, NYSE/ASX-listed) is now the combined Amcor + Berry Global consumer-packaging group following the April 30, 2025 all-stock merger. The combined business is cheap on a reverse-DCF basis (§7) and synergy delivery has so far tracked at the "upper end" of plan every quarter, but FY2026 free cash flow **missed even its own cut guidance** (§5) and leverage (~3.5x) is still above the company's own comfort zone one year post-close. This is a name to hold at reduced size while the deleveraging path proves out, not a full-conviction position.

## 2. Business in plain English
Amcor makes the packaging that wraps, bottles or caps food, beverages, healthcare and personal-care products — flexible films, rigid containers, closures and cartons — for large consumer-goods customers worldwide. It earns money on a cost-plus-margin basis, largely passing through resin/aluminum/paper input costs and keeping a processing margin; growth comes from volume, price/mix, and (since April 2025) the roughly $8bn of acquired Berry Global revenue plus disclosed cost synergies. Its competitive position rests on manufacturing scale, customer switching costs (packaging lines are qualified into a customer's production process) and, since the merger, the largest global footprint in flexible + rigid consumer packaging.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and is it durable?
b1 scores: composite 0.685 (decile 7, the highest-ranked of F72's three names on the pre-registered model), driven almost entirely by fam_S (earnings momentum, SUE 2.40 — the highest in the peer set) and fam_M (12-1m momentum +17.3%), with fam_Q very weak (0.071 — the model's quality family penalizes AMCR's post-merger leverage and net share issuance). **This is exactly the pattern to be suspicious of**: the SUE/momentum signal is mechanically produced by the merger's revenue and EPS step-change (accounting-acquirer comparisons vs a much smaller pre-merger base), not by an organic acceleration in the underlying business. Triage (Q13) correctly flagged this ("83% forward EPS growth reflects merger synergy ramp") and the leverage/integration risk. The durable part of the thesis is the synergy program (tracking ahead of plan every quarter reported) and category leadership; the non-durable part is the momentum/SUE score, which will normalize once merger-anniversary comparisons roll off in Q4 FY2026 (the quarter ended June 30, 2026 was already the last quarter with a meaningfully smaller prior-year base).

## 5. Guidance track record (last 4 releases; source: same Ex-99.1 releases, post-split terms)
- **Q1 FY26 (Nov 2025):** reaffirmed FY2026 guide — adjusted EPS equivalent to **$4.00–4.15** post-split (originally stated 80–83 cents pre-split), FCF **$1.8–1.9bn**.
- **Q2 FY26 (Feb 2026, post-split):** reaffirmed — adjusted EPS $4.00–4.15.
- **Q3 FY26 (May 2026): CUT.** Adjusted EPS narrowed/lowered to $3.98–4.03 ("mitigating impact of Middle East conflict"), and **free cash flow guidance was cut to $1.5–1.6bn** from the original $1.8–1.9bn — a real, quantified guidance cut, not a rounding change.
- **Q4 FY26 / actual (Aug 2026):** delivered adjusted EPS **$4.02** (within the revised range, and within the original $4.00–4.15 range too) — but delivered FCF of only **$1,303m, below even the cut $1.5–1.6bn range**, after funding ~$290m of net transaction/restructuring/integration costs. **This is the single most important adverse fact in this dossier**: FCF missed a guidance range that had already been cut once.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance ssheet (consolidated GAAP; source: FY2026 10-K, filed 2026-08-14, and the Q4 FY26 earnings release)
- **Net debt:** $12,897m at June 30, 2026 (total debt ~$14,012m − cash $1,115m). **Leverage** (company's own definition: net debt ÷ [LTM adjusted EBITDA + share-based comp]) is targeted at **3.5x–3.6x by December 31, 2026** (the end of the forthcoming six-month "Transition Period," see below) — i.e. management itself is guiding to *stay* elevated through year-end, not to delever meaningfully from the current level. On simple net debt/TTM adjusted EBITDA ($3,673m), leverage is ≈**3.5x** today — matching the triage flag almost exactly.
- **Fiscal year change:** Amcor is transitioning its fiscal year end from June 30 to December 31. It will report a standalone **six-month "Transition Period" (July 1–December 31, 2026)**, guided to adjusted EPS of ~$1.80–1.90 — meaning the next scheduled results release is not a normal quarterly comparison and the Yahoo-sourced `next_earnings_date` of 2026-11-05 in the quant snapshot is very likely stale/wrong (flagged as a data conflict, §"data_conflicts" in the summary JSON).
- **Cash conversion:** FY2026 operating cash flow $2,151m vs net income $1,106m (OCF/NI ≈ 1.9x) — reasonable, though FCF (OCF − capex) of $1,303m implies capex of ~$848m, and the $290m of integration/transaction cash costs embedded in that FCF figure means underlying, steady-state FCF is likely higher once integration spend rolls off (a real but not-yet-delivered upside).

But since FY2026's FCF is depressed by ~$290m of one-off integration costs, I re-ran the same EV against a **normalized base FCF of ~$1.6bn** (adding back the disclosed one-off integration cash costs): implied growth falls to **~3.1%/year** — a modest bar given Amcor's own targeted run-rate synergies and mid-single-digit combined-entity volume/price algorithm. **On the normalized basis, implied growth is below a plausible 5–6% base case → "below."** On the unadjusted (depressed-FCF) basis it would read "in line." I use the normalized view as more representative of steady-state cash generation, consistent with the earnings-quality principle of not crediitng one-off costs into a permanent lower base — but flag this as a real judgement call and the single biggest swing factor in the valuation read.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Synergy capture has beaten or matched the upper end of guidance every quarter disclosed (Q1 FY26 synergies "at upper end," Q2 "at upper end and targets reaffirmed," Q3 "at upper end of expectations") — execution risk on the stated synergy number specifically looks low so far. (2) At 8.9x EV/EBITDA and a 6.1% dividend yield, the combined entity is priced well below typical global packaging peer multiples (sector norms are typically low-double-digits), consistent with triage's "cheap 10x NTM" read. (3) The combined flexible + rigid packaging portfolio gives genuine category leadership and cross-sell opportunity that neither legacy company had alone.

**Bear:** (1) Free cash flow missed its own (already-cut) guidance in the first full post-merger fiscal year — a real, quantified execution shortfall, not a rounding miss. (2) Leverage (~3.5x) is targeted to stay at 3.5–3.6x through December 2026 by management's own admission — this is a business that will not meaningfully delever for at least another 12 months, leaving little room for a demand shock or a further input-cost spike. (3) The b1 model's bullish score is substantially a momentum/SUE artefact of merger accounting, not organic acceleration; once FY2026 comparisons fully lap (from Q1 FY2027/Transition Period), the earnings-momentum signal that got this name triaged forward is likely to fade mechanically.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (each, if it occurs, would invalidate the INCLUDE-SMALL thesis)
1. Leverage (net debt ÷ [LTM adjusted EBITDA + SBC], company's own metric) exceeds **3.8x** at any quarter-end (vs the 3.5–3.6x management is itself targeting for Dec-2026).
2. Free cash flow for the six-month Transition Period (Jul–Dec 2026) comes in below the low end of whatever range management guides at the next release — a second consecutive FCF miss would be a much stronger signal than the first.
3. Quarterly dividend is cut or held flat for more than 2 consecutive quarters after being raised in FY2026 (a de facto signal management judges coverage to be tight).
4. Disclosed cost synergies fall below the "upper end of expectations" pace management has claimed every quarter to date (i.e., synergy realization guidance itself is cut).
5. The Brazil tax litigation reasonably-possible-loss estimate (currently $27m above the $14m accrual) is revised materially upward in a future filing.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- No auditor changes, no material weakness, no going-concern language in the FY2026 10-K (audit opinion clean per the reviewed text).
- No SEC/DOJ investigation disclosed.
- Ordinary-course Brazil tax litigation and small environmental PRP accruals only (§6); nothing rises to "material" per the company's own SEC-threshold test ($1m disclosure threshold, no environmental matters required to be disclosed for FY2026).
- PFAS is disclosed as a forward-looking risk-factor item (potential future compliance costs from U.S. state and EU restrictions on PFAS in packaging materials) but not as a currently quantified liability — a watch item, not a present red flag.
- **Reverse stock split (Jan 14, 2026):** a 1-for-5 reverse split retroactively restated all share/EPS figures. This is a mechanical, disclosed corporate action (raising par value, reducing authorized shares) rather than a governance red flag, but it is a genuine trap for anyone comparing pre- and post-January-2026 EPS/share-count data without checking which basis is in use — several public data feeds (including this program's own d4/b1 snapshots, which report per-share figures consistent with the post-split basis) need this reconciliation done explicitly, which this dossier has done in §4.

# LEN - Lennar Corporation (diligence agent F164, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (12-36 months). A strong-balance-sheet homebuilder at 0.9x book, but every operating line is moving the wrong way: three straight full-year delivery-target cuts, gross margin down 170 bps y/y, orders -9%, negative operating cash flow, and net homebuilding debt up about $2.5B in nine months. Price implies slightly less growth than my base case, yet my base 3-year return (+3.1% a year) is below any sensible hurdle. Condition that would change it: two quarters of orders at or above deliveries with gross margin at or above 16%, or net homebuilding debt back below $2B.
Triage (Q02_triage.json: "no", 2/1/2, "Rev -8.7%, negative street upside") is confirmed on direction; I add that the valuation is not demanding, so this is WATCH rather than REJECT.

## 2. Business in plain English
Lennar builds and sells single-family homes and townhomes to first-time, move-up and active-adult buyers in about 1,700 communities, and earns extra money from mortgage and title services (Financial Services). Strategy is "even flow": hold volume steady and cut costs, even if that means price cuts. The company says incentives are "approximately 12.0%" of price and average sales price is $372,000 (Q3 FY26 release). It owns fewer than 2.5% of its roughly 488,000 homesites (the rest is options and land banks, including Millrose Properties).

## 8. Bull and bear
Bull: (1) cost per square foot down 14% since Q4 2023 and cycle time at a record 116 days give operating leverage if rates ease; (2) asset-light land (under 2.5% owned) and 16.6% homebuilding leverage protect the balance sheet; (3) shares at 0.92x book with $1.0B of stock bought back in nine months.
Bear: (1) price cuts are the volume strategy: 12% incentives, ASP -3%, margin 15.8%; (2) mortgage rate "approximately 6.8% at quarter end and even higher since" with consumer confidence falling; (3) cash burn: net homebuilding debt $0.6B -> $3.1B in nine months while inventory grew.

## 9. Key risks and kill criteria
1. Gross margin on home sales below 15.0% in any two consecutive quarters (Q3 FY26: 15.8%).
2. New orders below 19,500 in Q4 FY26 (low end of guide), or orders below deliveries by more than 10% for two consecutive quarters.
3. Homebuilding debt to total capital above 22% (Homebuilding segment, Supplemental Data table; 31 Aug 2026: 16.6%) or net homebuilding debt above $5.0B.
4. FY2027 delivery target below 78,000 homes when first given (FY26 target now 80,000-81,000).
5. Two consecutive quarters in which both gross margin and SG&A ratio miss the guided range.

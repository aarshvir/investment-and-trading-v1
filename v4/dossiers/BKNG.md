# BKNG — Booking Holdings Inc. — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — 24–36 month horizon. The world's largest online travel agent is statistically as cheap versus its own history as it has ever been, operating income has grown in every comparable quarter for four years, and leverage on a cash-flow basis is very low. I agree with V1's directional "attractive" call. Named reservation: **V1's +39.3%/yr "base case" is not a credible base case** — it depends on the NTM P/E more than doubling (13.8x→30.1x), an assumption management's own guidance does not support — so size this as attractive-but-not-39%/yr-attractive, and be aware of two unresolved items: a rapidly deepening negative-equity/rising-debt position, and a live EU regulatory/litigation overhang.

## 2. Business in plain English
Booking Holdings is an online travel agent (Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, Kayak, OpenTable) that connects travelers with hotels, alternative accommodations, rental cars and flights, earning a commission (agency model) or a margin on room revenue it collects and remits (merchant model — now ~73% of gross bookings, +4pts YoY). It makes money on the volume of "room nights" booked and the take rate per booking, funded by heavy performance-marketing spend to acquire direct and paid traffic. Its competitive position is the largest global OTA by gross bookings, competing chiefly with Expedia, Airbnb, and direct hotel-chain booking channels, with Booking.com itself now designated an EU "gatekeeper" platform.

**Note on share count/EPS: Booking Holdings executed a 25-for-1 forward stock split effective April 2, 2026** (record date 2026-03-06; traded split-adjusted from 2026-04-06). All EPS and share-count figures below are presented on a **split-adjusted** basis for comparability; pre-split GAAP releases (e.g., Q2 2025's originally-reported $27.43 EPS) are divided by 25 throughout ($27.43⁄25=$1.10, matching the figure below).

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
B1 ranks BKNG #63 (composite 87.3rd percentile) on strong **Value** (earnings yield 87.3rd percentile, book/price 78.6th, EBIT/EV 91.6th) and reasonable **Quality**, with **weak recent Momentum** (50.6th percentile, unremarkable) and **weak Earnings-momentum/SUE** (11.3rd percentile — the SUE-based earnings-momentum factor is actually a *negative* contributor, worth noting since it cuts against the "attractive" quant read). ## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Unresolved GAAP earnings-quality flag**: GAAP net income fell sharply in **both** Q1 2025 (−57% YoY, $776M→$333M) and Q2 2025 (−41% YoY, $1,521M→$895M) even though **operating income grew in both quarters** (Q1: $791M→$1,062M; Q2: $1,856M→$2,250M). The gap is below the operating line — interest expense, FX/hedging, or a legal/tax item — and was **not fully isolated within this review's time-box**. This matters because it makes 2026's GAAP growth comparisons (e.g., Q2 2026 "+131% YoY" EPS) largely a lapping of that depressed base rather than organic improvement; the ~+15% adjusted EPS growth is the more representative figure. **Flagged as a data_conflict for further diligence** (check the FY2025 10-K "Other income (expense)" note).
- FY2025 GAAP net income $5,404M vs. FY2024 $5,882M (**−8.1%**) despite revenue +13% and adjusted EBITDA +20% to $9.9bn — company attributes this to higher marketing spend (+12%, $7.3bn→$8.2bn) and other "investment costs," which does not fully explain a decline in net income when EBITDA rose sharply; consistent with the same below-the-line pattern above.
- OCF strong and growing: FY2025 $9,409M vs. FY2024 $8,323M (+13.0%); H1 2026 $6,934M vs. H1 2025 $6,484M (+6.9%). Capex low (~2.5% of revenue).
- **Negative equity, use net debt/EBITDA not ROE**: stockholders' equity **−$10,783M** at Q2 2026, worsening from −$8,724M (Q1 2026) and −$6,657M (Q2 2025) — a **$4.67bn deterioration in the last four quarters alone**, the fastest-moving of the three names in this batch. Total debt $20,180M (+9.2% YoY); cash $17,214M (includes merchant/customer funds in transit — not all "excess" cash); **net debt ≈$2,966M**, matching V1 exactly. TTM EBITDA ≈$9.86bn (V1) → **net debt/EBITDA ≈0.30x** — very low leverage on a cash-flow basis despite the alarming-looking equity headline.
- **Buybacks have accelerated sharply**: H1 2026 buybacks **$7,758M vs. H1 2025 $3,668M (+111.5% YoY)**, funded by ~$2.975bn of new debt raised in H1 2026 plus strong OCF. $14.5bn of authorization remains. Quarterly dividend $0.42/share declared for Q3 2026 (dividend only initiated in 2024).

## 7. Valuation snapshot — reconciliation with V1 (task explicitly asks to scrutinise this)
V1 verdict: **attractive** (score +0.993 — among the highest in the entire ~500-name universe). NTM P/E 13.8x — **1.1st percentile of its own ~14.75-year history** (essentially the cheapest it has ever traded) and roughly in line with a peer median of 13.1x (peer set ABNB, CCL, EXPE, HLT and others — **note CCL is a cruise operator with a very different capital/risk profile; peer comparability is imperfect**). Implied 10-year growth from the current price is only +2.3% (i.e. the price itself does not require much growth), but V1's **bear/base/bull 3-year annualised returns are +11.3% / +39.3% / +86.4%** — even the bear case is positive, reflecting how cheap the starting point is.
**I do not accept V1's +39.3%/yr figure as a credible "base case."** Decomposing the scenario: revenue growth assumed 16.8%/yr, and the **NTM P/E exit multiple assumed to expand from 13.8x to 30.1x** — more than doubling — by year 3. That multiple-expansion assumption, not fundamentals, is doing most of the work: management's own FY2026 guidance (high-single-digit bookings/room-nights/revenue, low-to-mid-teens adjusted EPS growth) implies something like 10–15%/yr EPS growth with a flat multiple, not 39%/yr. The model's base-case fair value ($443.57) is **47% above the single highest Wall Street 12-month price target ($301)** — a useful sanity check that the "base" is really more like this model's bull case.
**My view**: the stock is genuinely, statistically cheap (1.1st percentile of own history is a real, checkable signal) and the business is executing (bookings/room-nights growth, raised guidance, very low cash leverage) — I agree with "attractive" as a direction. But I would treat V1's 39.3%/yr as the **upside case**, and underwrite a realistic base case closer to **high-teens-to-low-20s%/yr** (management's own EPS growth guidance plus a partial, not total, re-rating), which is still an attractive risk/reward but materially different position-sizing math than 39%/yr compounded for three years.
**dossier_view: attractive but base case overstated; consistent with V1: no** — I am explicitly flagging the exit-multiple assumption (13.8x→30.1x) as the V1 input I believe is too aggressive to call "base," with the primary-source support being management's own low-to-mid-teens adjusted-EPS guidance and the Street's own $301 high target.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. 13.8x NTM P/E is at roughly the 1st percentile of BKNG's own trading history; Q2 2026 room nights +5%, gross bookings +9% reported (+8% cc), FY2026 guidance just raised to high-single-digit growth plus low-to-mid-teens adjusted EPS growth.
2. Net debt/EBITDA ≈0.3x despite deeply negative book equity — ample capacity funding a rapidly growing capital-return program (H1 2026 buybacks +112% YoY, $14.5bn authorization remaining) that mechanically compresses share count.
3. Operating income has grown in every comparable quarter for four years — the core marketplace economics are intact regardless of GAAP net-income noise below the operating line.

**Bear:**
1. V1's +39.3%/yr "base case" requires the NTM multiple to more than double (13.8x→30.1x) — management's own guidance does not support anything close to that without a multiple re-rating; the base-case fair value already exceeds the Street's highest 12-month target by 47%.
2. Shareholders' equity is deeply and rapidly more negative (−$10.78bn, −$4.67bn in the last four quarters) funded partly by fresh debt (+$2.975bn raised in H1 2026 alone, total debt +9.2% YoY) — low leverage today, but a capital-markets-dependent model.
3. GAAP net income fell sharply in Q1 2025 (−57%) and Q2 2025 (−41%) for reasons not fully isolated in this review, making 2026's headline GAAP growth rates partly a base-effect artefact; separately, the EU Digital Markets Act "gatekeeper" obligations (parity-clause restrictions) took effect November 2024 and remain a live overhang — the European Parliament in April 2026 pressed the Commission to open a formal non-compliance investigation, Italy's AGCM opened its own April 2026 consumer-protection inquiry, and a hotel-industry class action (potentially up to ~18,000 European hotels) is seeking billions in damages for 2004–2024 parity-clause conduct.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Room-night or gross-bookings growth falls below **3% YoY for two consecutive quarters** (currently +5% / +9%).
2. Net debt/EBITDA rises above **1.5x** (currently ≈0.30x).
3. FY2026 guidance (high-single-digit volume/bookings/revenue, low-to-mid-teens adjusted EPS growth) is **cut**.
4. The European Commission opens a **formal DMA non-compliance proceeding** against Booking.com, or the EU hotel class action produces a disclosed material damages award/settlement.
5. Quarterly buyback spend falls by **more than 50% quarter-over-quarter** without a stated capital-return-policy reason — a signal management itself sees less value at the prevailing price.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **EU regulatory**: Booking.com designated a DMA "gatekeeper" (May 13, 2024; compliance required from Nov 14, 2024). As of April 2026, the European Parliament has pushed for a formal Commission non-compliance investigation over continued use of parity clauses; Italy's AGCM opened a related consumer-protection probe the same month. No fine has been levied as of this review, but DMA non-compliance findings can carry fines up to 10% of global annual turnover.
- **Litigation**: Texas Attorney General "junk fees" settlement, $9.5M, August 2025 (immaterial in size, notable for the deceptive-pricing theme). Separately, a European hotel-industry class action (10,000+ hotels filed, potentially representing up to ~18,000) seeks damages for 2004–2024 use of parity clauses; no accrual size could be confirmed in this pass.
- **Insider transactions**: Multiple Form 4 sales by the EVP/General Counsel, CFO, CHRO and two directors in Jul–Sep 2026, all appear to be routine Rule 10b5-1 pre-scheduled sales of modest size relative to holdings — **not** read as an adverse signal, but noted for completeness.
- No 8-K Item 4.01/4.02 (auditor change/restatement) found in the submissions history reviewed.
- **Unresolved**: the Q1–Q2 2025 GAAP net-income air-pocket described in §6 — recommend the audit/valuation team confirm the specific driver before relying on 2026 GAAP EPS growth-rate comparisons.

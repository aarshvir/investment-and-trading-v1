## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon: 24–36 months.
One-sentence reason: best-in-class quality pharma/medtech compounder at a fair-to-slightly-full multiple, held back to half weight by a real, currently *unresolved* talc-litigation tail risk that the prior triage understated.

## 2. Business in plain English
Johnson & Johnson is a ~$653B market-cap health-care company in two segments: **Innovative Medicine** (branded prescription drugs — oncology, immunology, neuroscience) and **MedTech** (surgical devices, orthopedics, vision, electrophysiology). It makes money selling patented drugs and devices to hospitals, physicians and pharmacies worldwide, protected by patents, physician relationships and regulatory approvals. It no longer owns the consumer-health business (spun off as Kenvue in 2023).

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1 factor snapshot (2026-09-25): composite 0.435 (decile 5, not top-30), driven by Quality (ROE 25.8%, high gross-profit/assets) and reasonable earnings yield (3.2%). This is **durable**: ROE is generated by a real, diversified, patent-protected franchise, not a one-off — margins have been stable through the Kenvue separation and the talc-litigation overhang. Momentum (12m 46.6%) has run well ahead of the fundamentals, which is the part of the model's signal I'd treat as less durable and more a re-rating/re-included in this quality bucket.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Innovative Medicine oncology/immunology pipeline (Darzalex, Tremfya, CAR-T assets) keeps growing high-single-digit even as older biologics (Stelara) erode.
2. MedTech recovers procedure-volume growth and orthopedics/robotics share gains (Ottava).
3. AAA-class balance sheet gives capital-allocation optionality (bolt-on M&A, buybacks) that peers lack.

**Bear:**
1. Talc litigation is **not resolved** (see §11) — a large, binary tail liability with no cap after three failed bankruptcy attempts.
2. Innovative Medicine faces a mid-2020s patent-cliff wall (Stelara biosimilars already eroding U.S. sales; Darzalex/Tremfya eventually face the same).
3. Valuation has re-rated up with momentum; multiple compression risk if growth decelerates back toward the mid-single digits guided for FY2026.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Reported/operational sales growth falls below **4%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. the ~5.5–9.9% delivered in H1 2026).
2. Innovative Medicine segment growth falls below **5%** for two consecutive quarters (biosimilar erosion outpacing new launches).
3. A talc bellwether verdict or aggregate reserve increase **>$5B** in a single quarter, or a fourth failed bankruptcy/global-settlement attempt that a court rules abusive.
4. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above **1.5x** (from ~0.5x today) — would signal debt-funded M&A or reserve build-up.
5. FY guidance is **cut** (not just held flat) at either of the next two quarterly releases (2026-10-13 or the following one).

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **Talc litigation — MATERIAL, ACTIVE, NOT RESOLVED.** J&J's subsidiary Red River Talc's Chapter 11 plan (J&J's third bankruptcy attempt to cap talc liability) was rejected by the bankruptcy court as not meeting legal standards; J&J confirmed it will not appeal, and federal MDL litigation has resumed "in earnest" with over 58,600 cases pending. A district judge in Jan 2026 allowed plaintiff-side causation experts to testify (a plaintiff-favorable ruling on Daubert grounds), and in Feb 2026 a separate fraud suit alleging bankruptcy abuse was dismissed (a win for J&J on that narrow point). **This directly contradicts the triage card's framing** ("largely funneled through bankruptcy-trust proceedings") — there is currently no bankruptcy trust in force; the case is back in ordinary litigation with mediation ongoing. This is the single most important adverse fact this dossier adds versus the triage.

# Exelon Corporation (NASDAQ: EXC) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F65, Wave 4)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** — reason: a pure-play, revenue-decoupled regulated T&D utility holding company with guided 5–7% (near top-end)
annual EPS growth through 2029 that the market is pricing below management's own guided growth even under conservative cost-
of-equity assumptions, with two consecutive quarters of guidance re-affirmation and no material red flags found. Thesis
horizon: 24–48 months (utility total-return/compounding thesis, not a re-rating catalyst play).

## 2. Business in plain English
Exelon is a holding company for four regulated electric (and in three cases, gas) distribution/transmission utilities:
ComEd (northern Illinois), PECO (southeastern Pennsylvania), BGE (central Maryland) and PHI (the District of Columbia,
Delaware, Maryland and New Jersey, operating as Pepco, Delmarva Power and Atlantic City Electric). It earns a regulated
return on the capital it invests in wires, substations and pipes ("rate base"), set by state and federal regulators, not by
selling commodities — Exelon spun off its power-generation business (nuclear/renewables) as Constellation Energy in 2022 and
has been a pure "wires" company since. Revenue is largely insulated from weather and usage swings by "decoupling" mechanisms
at most of its utilities.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Entity scope:** the 10-Q is a combined filing for five SEC registrants (Exelon Corporation, ComEd, PECO, BGE and PHI).
  All balance-sheet figures below are **Exelon Corporation consolidated** (CIK 0001109357 XBRL), not any single operating
  company, unless stated. Rate-base and earnings figures by operating company (ComEd, PECO, BGE, PHI) are given separately
  in the earnings releases and are quoted as such in §5/§8 where used.
- **FCF/earnings-quality:** Exelon is a heavy, growing-capex regulated utility — free cash flow is **structurally negative**
  (d4 snapshot: `freeCashflow` −$2.69bn TTM) by design, because it is reinvesting more in rate base than it collects in
  depreciation, funded by debt and equity issuance, not a red flag for this business model (this is normal for a utility in
  a rate-base growth phase) but it means a standard FCF-yield valuation approach does not apply — see §7's dividend-based
  reverse DCF instead.
- **GAAP vs adjusted gap:** narrow and stable — for Q2 2026, GAAP EPS $0.39 vs adjusted operating EPS $0.43 (the company
  states "no adjustments between 2026 projected GAAP earnings and Adjusted... operating earnings currently" for the full
  year, i.e., the FY2026 guidance figure is expected to equal GAAP for the year — a materially cleaner GAAP/adjusted
  relationship than either E&P name in this batch).
- **Balance sheet / leverage:** consolidated long-term debt grew from $41.3bn (2023-12-31) to $44.7bn (2024-12-31) to
  $49.4bn (2025-12-31) to $47.9bn noncurrent portion at 2026-03-31 (SEC XBRL, `LongTermDebt`/`LongTermDebtNoncurrent`) —
  a deliberate, capex-plan-driven increase, not distress; stockholders' equity grew in step, from $26.9bn (2024-12-31) to
  $29.3bn (2026-03-31), and share count grew from 1,005M (2024-12-31) to 1,023M (2026-03-31, +1.8%) as part of the disclosed
  equity financing plan (dilutive, but planned and disclosed, not a surprise).
- **Financing activity (Q2 2026, all primary-source-dated):** ComEd issued $1,425M of First Mortgage Bonds (2026-05-14,
  4.55%/2031 and 5.85%/2056 tranches); BGE issued $925M of notes (2026-05-22, 5.15%/2033 and 6.05%/2056); Pepco issued
  $130M of First Mortgage Bonds (2026-06-17, 5.74%/2056) — all used to repay existing debt or for general corporate
  purposes, consistent with the disclosed financing plan (86% of 2026 planned debt financings executed by Q2 2026).
- **Regulatory/rate-case activity:** BGE filed an electric distribution rate case with the Maryland PSC on 2026-07-02,

  valuation for a business with two consecutive quarters of guidance re-affirmation and no track record of a miss in the
  periods reviewed.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Revenue decoupling at ComEd, BGE and PHI's Maryland/DC operations means distribution earnings are largely insulated from
   weather and usage — a structurally lower-volatility earnings stream than most regulated peers, evidenced by the tight
   GAAP/adjusted EPS gap in §6.
2. Rate base growth of 7.9% over the $41.7bn four-year plan is regulator-approved-in-principle capital spend (transmission
   and distribution investment to serve growing demand, including data-center-adjacent load in the Mid-Atlantic footprint
   per the triage note), which converts fairly mechanically into earnings growth under cost-of-service regulation.
3. Cheapest large-cap utility P/E in its peer set (13.5x NTM per the triage screen) with a ~21% gap to the analyst mean
   target price — a valuation cushion that is not present in many bond-proxy utility peers currently.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Consolidated debt has grown from $41.3bn to $49.4bn in two years to fund the capital plan, with further equity dilution
   (~$3.4bn planned through 2029) — a company that must continually access capital markets on favorable terms; a sustained
   rise in long-term rates would raise financing costs and could pressure the EPS growth algorithm.
2. The BGE Maryland rate case (filed July 2026, $156M ask, 10.40% ROE ask, decision due Q1 2027) is a real near-term
   regulatory event; a materially reduced award (a common regulatory outcome) would be a direct, quantifiable hit to the
   utility's earnings trajectory and a specific data point to re-check at the Q1 2027 decision.
3. "Hold" consensus rating (17 analysts) despite the target-price gap suggests the sell-side is not enthusiastic about
   near-term upside despite acknowledging valuation support — worth understanding why (not resolved in this pass; possible
   read-through is rate risk or the equity-dilution overhang).

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. FY2026 adjusted operating EPS guidance is cut below the current $2.81–$2.91 range.
2. The BGE rate case (decision expected Q1 2027) is settled/decided at less than 70% of the requested $156M revenue
   increase, or with an authorized ROE more than 100bp below the 10.40% ask.
3. Any of Exelon's or its utility subsidiaries' senior unsecured/first-mortgage-bond credit ratings is downgraded by
   Moody's, S&P or Fitch.
4. Consolidated long-term debt grows more than 10% beyond the disclosed $41.7bn four-year capital plan without a
   commensurate, disclosed increase in the rate-base growth guidance (i.e., spending without matching earnings-growth
   justification).
5. Adjusted operating EPS growth (year-over-year) falls below 4% for two consecutive fiscal years (well below the guided
   5–7% "near top end" range).

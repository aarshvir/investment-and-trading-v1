# BRK-B — Berkshire Hathaway Inc. (Agent F79)

1. **Verdict: INCLUDE** (36mo+). Best-in-class diversified balance sheet, a smooth CEO transition to Greg Abel that is now actively re-deploying capital rather than just sitting on it, and no valuation stretch.

2. **Business:** Diversified holding company: insurance/reinsurance float (GEICO, National Indemnity, Berkshire Re), BNSF railroad, Berkshire Hathaway Energy (utilities/pipelines), a large public-equities portfolio (Apple, etc.), and a long tail of wholly owned manufacturing/retail/services businesses. Earns via underwriting profit + float investment income + operating-subsidiary earnings + equity gains.

3. **Why the model likes it:** Q05 triage cites 5/5 quality, float-funded balance sheet strength, and treats the Buffett→Abel transition (completed, Buffett remains chairman) as well-telegraphed and non-disqualifying. This is durable — capital allocation discipline is structural to the business, not a cyclical artefact.

4. **Recent results (primary source: Q2 2026 10-Q, filed SEC EDGAR 2026-08-08, CIK 0001067983; Q2 2026 earnings release, businesswire/berkshirehathaway.com, 2026-08-08):** Operating earnings (GAAP, ex-investment gains/losses) $13.0B in Q2 2026 vs. $11.2B in Q2 2025 (+16% y/y); H1 2026 operating earnings $24.3B vs. $20.8B in H1 2025. Segment detail: manufacturing/service/retailing earnings +24% to $4.47B; Berkshire Hathaway Energy +27% to $891M; BNSF +6% to $1.56B. Cash and equivalents (incl. short-term Treasuries) $365.5B at 30 Jun 2026, down from a record $397.4B at 31 Mar 2026 as capital was deployed.

5. **Guidance:** Berkshire does not issue formal EPS/revenue guidance (standard practice); none to reconcile.

6. **Balance sheet / capital allocation:** Abel's first full quarter as CEO showed a clear shift: buybacks of ~$4.5B in Q2 2026 vs. only ~$235M in Q1 2026, and net equity purchases of ~$20B after 14 consecutive quarters of net selling. This is the single most important data point this quarter — management is signalling that at current prices, deploying the cash pile (still $365.5B, among the largest corporate cash positions globally) beats holding it. Consolidated entity; no segment/subsidiary mixing identified in the figures above (all are consolidated GAAP operating-earnings and segment lines as reported in the release).

7. **Valuation:** Price $505.48 (FMP live quote), market cap ~$1.09T. No V1 systematic row exists for BRK-B in `v1_valuation_table.csv` (v1_verdict = null). Berkshire is not a clean single-multiple story (conglomerate of insurance float + equities + wholly owned operating businesses), so I rely on the capital-allocation signal rather than a formal reverse DCF: management itself, with full information on intrinsic value, chose to accelerate buybacks 19x sequentially and flip from net equity seller to a ~$20B net buyer this quarter. That is a market signal that price is **at or below** intrinsic value — i.e., implied growth **below/in line with** my base case (continued high-single-digit operating-earnings compounding via float growth, BHE capex build-out, and BNSF pricing).

8. **Bull:** (a) Abel is now actively deploying the record cash pile (buybacks + net equity buying) rather than sitting on it, removing the "cash drag" bear case; (b) energy segment earnings +27% y/y show the BHE capex cycle (data-center power demand) is paying off; (c) insurance float plus a diversified, largely non-correlated operating base gives downside ballast in a drawdown.
**Bear:** (a) Succession risk is not zero even if well-telegraphed — capital-allocation judgment concentrated in one new CEO after six decades under Buffett; (b) $365B cash still earns a modest T-bill yield versus what could be higher-return deployment, an opportunity cost if Abel is slow; (c) conglomerate discount can persist or widen regardless of underlying earnings quality.

9. **Kill criteria:** (i) net equity buying reverses to net selling for two consecutive quarters without a stated reason; (ii) buybacks drop back below $1B/quarter while cash exceeds $350B (signals capital-allocation paralysis); (iii) BNSF or BHE operating earnings decline y/y for two consecutive quarters; (iv) an insurance reserve strengthening (adverse development) exceeding $1B in a single quarter; (v) Abel or another key successor departs.

10. **Catalysts:** Q3 2026 10-Q/earnings (est. early Nov 2026); annual shareholder letter (Feb 2027) for Abel's first full-year capital-allocation commentary.

11. **Red flags:** None identified in this pass. No auditor change, restatement, or going-concern language found. Not independently verified against the full 10-Q text; flag as a gap for a deeper pass.

## 1. Verdict

**WATCH** — 12–24 month horizon. A genuinely stronger, more fee-based franchise than five years ago (Wealth
Management now the largest single revenue segment, $10T in client assets), but the current record profitability
is priced as if it is the new steady state rather than partly a cyclical trading/IB peak. Condition to upgrade:
either a pullback in price/multiple, or 2–3 more quarters of ROTCE holding near current levels through a period
of calmer markets (proving durability, not just a hot quarter). No V1 systematic valuation row exists for MS;
`v1_verdict` is null and Section 7 is the analyst's own work using bank/broker-appropriate metrics (P/TBV,
ROTCE, CET1) rather than a generic FCFF DCF, per the stock-analysis skill's banks/brokers convention.

## 2. Business in plain English

Morgan Stanley is a global investment bank and wealth manager. Institutional Securities does M&A advisory,
equity/debt underwriting, and trading (equities and fixed income) for corporations and institutions. Wealth
Management advises individuals ($10T combined client/AUM assets across Wealth + Investment Management) and
earns recurring fee-based revenue plus net interest income on client cash/lending. Investment Management runs
asset-management strategies. It makes money on fees, spreads, trading margins and net interest income; its
competitive position is top-3 in US equity trading/wealth management scale following the 2020 E*TRADE and
Eaton Vance acquisitions.

## 5. Guidance track record

Morgan Stanley does not issue formal quarterly/annual EPS or revenue guidance (typical for large banks/brokers);
management instead sets multi-year targets at investor days (e.g. ROTCE, expense-efficiency-ratio targets) and
discusses quarter-to-quarter drivers on earnings calls. This dossier therefore tracks delivered results and
capital-return actions against the company's own stated efficiency/return targets rather than a point guidance
range: the Q2 2026 **expense efficiency ratio of 65%** (vs 71% a year ago) and **ROTCE of 26.6%** are both well
inside/above the "sub-70% efficiency, 20%+ ROTCE" medium-term targets management has cited in recent investor
communications (secondary-source characterization — not independently verified against a primary investor-day
transcript within this time box; flagged as such).

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse valuation view

TTM P/E: $196.31 / $12.37 TTM diluted EPS ≈ **15.9x**. Price/tangible book value: $196.31 / $53.18 ≈ **3.69x**
TBV — historically rich for MS (which traded 1–2x tangible book for most of the post-2008 decade and re-rated
only as the wealth-management mix shift matured). Triage (Q05) separately cited a ~14.6x forward multiple on
consensus estimates.

**Reverse valuation (Gordon growth on ROTCE, the standard bank/broker method — no generic FCFF DCF, per sector
convention):** P/TBV = (ROTCE − g) / (COE − g). Solving for the ROTCE the market needs Morgan Stanley to sustain
*in perpetuity*, at an assumed cost of equity ≈10% and terminal growth g≈4%: implied sustainable ROTCE ≈
**3.69 × (10%−4%) + 4% ≈ 26.1%** — almost exactly the **26.6% ROTCE MS just posted this quarter**, a quarter the
company itself attributed largely to unusually favorable trading conditions. In other words, **the price is
already assuming the current cyclical-peak ROTCE is the new normal**, not a cyclical high to fade. That is a
materially more demanding ask than a normalized through-cycle ROTCE (management's own medium-term target has
historically been discussed around ~20%, not 26%+) — **implied growth/return is above my base case.**

**3-year scenario returns (annualised, price+dividends, exit-P/E method off TTM EPS $12.37):** bear −9.4%
(EPS reverts to ~$11, P/E to 12x) · base +3.3% (EPS ~$13.5, P/E ~15x) · bull +13.5% (EPS ~$16, P/E re-rates to 17x).

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:** (1) Wealth Management has structurally re-mixed the firm toward recurring, less capital-intensive fee
revenue ($10T client assets, record $148B net new assets in one quarter) — less cyclical than the old
trading-led Morgan Stanley. (2) $20B new buyback authorization plus a 15% dividend raise show management
conviction and shrink the share count into strength. (3) CET1 14.8% and Tier 1 leverage 6.0% leave ample room to
keep returning capital even if credit costs tick up.

**Bear:** (1) Institutional Securities—especially Equity trading (+69% YoY)—is the swing factor behind the
"record" quarter; a normalization of trading/IB activity (which the company's own release attributes to
favorable market conditions, not a structural share gain) would pull ROTCE back toward the high teens/low 20s.
(2) CET1 and Tier 1 leverage ratios are both declining YoY even as buybacks accelerate — a squeeze if credit
costs or a market shock arrive together. (3) Recency of regulatory settlements ($249.4M DOJ/SEC block-trading
settlement, Jan-2024; $15M SEC fine for failing to monitor advisor account transfers, Dec-2024) shows a pattern
of compliance-control gaps that predates but overlaps the current management team's tenure.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. Standardized CET1 ratio falls below **13.0%** (currently 14.8%, down 20bps YoY already).
2. ROTCE falls below **18%** for 2 consecutive quarters (evidence the 26%+ level was cyclical, not structural).
3. Wealth Management net new assets fall below **$50B/quarter** for 2 consecutive quarters (vs the $148.1B
   Q2 2026 record) — a sign the "structural re-mix" story is stalling.
4. A new SEC/DOJ/FINRA enforcement action or fine **>$100M** is announced (given the 2024 pattern above).
5. Provision for credit losses in Institutional Securities exceeds **$300M in a single quarter** (vs $71M in
   Q2 2026) without a matching one-off disclosed cause — a credit-quality deterioration signal.

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (thesis horizon 12-36 months). The quick triage (consolidated figures) said "no" for the wrong reason (it quoted 5.2x net debt/EBITDA and -30.9% FCF yield; neither reproduces from the 10-Q, see section 6). The business is good and 2026 earnings are rising, but at $109 the stock already prices roughly 5% a year of free-cash-flow-per-share growth for ten years against my base case of about 3%, and cash flow is negative. Condition to upgrade: a price near or below $90 (my base-case DCF is about $94), or two more quarters of positive trailing free cash flow with adjusted EPS holding at or above $9.

## 2. Business in plain English
Bunge buys soybeans, wheat, corn and oilseeds from farmers, crushes and refines them into meal, vegetable oils and flour, and ships grain worldwide; it earns a margin per tonne handled and processed, plus trading gains. The July 2025 merger with Viterra roughly doubled its grain origination and shipping network (Q2 2026 net sales $24.0B versus $12.8B a year earlier, with diluted shares up from 136M to 195M). Its edge is scale and logistics in a thin-margin, commodity business; returns swing with crush margins, biofuel policy and trade flows.

## 3. Why the model likes it (b1, d4)
b1 shows a low price-to-earnings (earnings-yield and value percentiles high) and strong recent surprise. The price-to-earnings is cheap only because the multiple is applied to a raised, mid-merger earnings base; the value reading ignores that trailing free cash flow is negative. Durable part: more earnings power after Viterra. Artefact part: crush margins in 2026 are described by the company as "improving market conditions", i.e. cyclical.

## 8. Bull and bear
Bull: (1) US biofuel demand keeps crush margins elevated; FY26 midpoint already raised twice. (2) Viterra synergies plus footprint optimisation lift mid-cycle earnings above $10. (3) Buybacks of $3B remaining authorisation shrink the share count about 3% a year at 11x earnings.
Bear: (1) Margin mean reversion: FY25 adjusted EPS was $7.57 and Q1-26 gross margin fell to 3.5%. (2) Cash flow: free cash flow negative on a trailing basis; inventory and receivables growth absorbs the earnings. (3) Brazil claims, interest cost ($620-660M) and a large GAAP-versus-adjusted gap (mark-to-market) hide volatility.

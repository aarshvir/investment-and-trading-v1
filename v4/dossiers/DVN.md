## 2. Business in plain English
Devon finds and pumps oil, natural gas and natural-gas liquids from US shale, mostly the Delaware Basin (Permian), plus the Anadarko, Eagle Ford, Rockies and, since the merger, Coterra's Marcellus gas. It earns money at the well-head price of oil (guided at 98%-100% of WTI for the full year: "Oil - % of WTI ... 98 % ... 100 %", 8-K acc. 0001193125-26-332824 Ex.99.2), and returns cash through a fixed dividend ($0.32 a quarter), buybacks and debt repayment. It is one of the largest US shale operators after the merger; its edge is acreage quality and scale, not a moat: it is a price-taker.

## 8. Bull and bear
Bull: (1) at today's $92 oil the company would generate about $8.8B of FCF, 16% of market value; (2) synergies of at least $1.0B and an $8.0B buyback at 9.5x earnings are accretive; (3) 400 new top-tier Delaware locations (16,300 net acres, 87.5% NRI) secure inventory.
Bear: (1) oil spike reverses: FY25 average WTI was $64.87; each $10 of WTI is about $2.2B of annual EBITDAX in my model; (2) integration and a pending "asset-by-asset" portfolio review (possible disposals, management turnover: 5 legacy directors resigned at close, Coterra's CFO and CAO took over); (3) $14.1B of acquired unproved acreage plus $2.6B of new leases spent at peak prices, so impairment and low-return capex risk.

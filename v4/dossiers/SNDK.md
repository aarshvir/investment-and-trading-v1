# Sandisk Corporation (NASDAQ: SNDK) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F30, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH**, horizon 12–36 months (re-underwrite when FY2027 guidance clarifies cycle duration). Reason: a genuinely
strong, structurally-improved business (NAND flash for AI-datacenter storage) riding a real, primary-source-verified
demand shortage — but at a price that requires the *current cycle-peak* margins and cash flow to persist and then
compound at a double-digit rate for a decade. That is a higher bar than even the bull case (management itself frames
the shortage as lasting through 2027, not permanently), so per the mandate's own rule, a great business priced for
more than it can deliver is a WATCH, not an INCLUDE — this reconciles with, and independently corroborates, V1's
"excessive" verdict.

## 2. Business in plain English
Sandisk makes NAND flash memory chips and the SSDs/storage systems built from them, sold to hyperscale cloud
providers (AI/datacenter), PC/mobile OEMs, and retail (branded flash drives/cards). It manufactures almost entirely
through the "Flash Ventures" joint venture with Kioxia (~80% of its capacity comes from this JV, per its own 10-K —
it does not fully control its own manufacturing). It was spun off from Western Digital on 24 February 2025 and has
under two years of independent trading history.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (10-Q/10-K through FQ4'26, filed 17 Aug 2026)
- **FCF conversion:** FQ3'26 operating cash flow $3.038B, capex $45M (implied), free cash flow **$2.993B** on net
  income $3.615B — FCF ≈ 83% of NI, healthy and not propped up by working-capital drawdown in this quarter.
- **Balance sheet transformation:** all debt was repaid during FY2026 — long-term debt fell from $1.33B
  (Oct-2025) to **$0** by Apr-2026, and cash built from $1.44B to **$4.76B** by fiscal year-end (Jul-2026). Sandisk
  is now **debt-free and net-cash positive**, a real and verified de-leveraging in under a year, funded by the
  windfall cycle cash flow.
- **Buybacks:** "$4.5 billion of stock repurchased" in FQ4'26 alone (per the earnings release) — a very large,
  cycle-funded capital return; shares outstanding roughly flat (146–148M) over the year despite this, implying the
  buyback offset significant dilution/vesting rather than shrinking the float much — worth re-checking against the
  next 10-K's exact weighted-average share count.
- **Customer concentration:** the FY2026 10-K states **no single customer exceeded 10% of net revenue** in 2024,
  2025 or 2026 — a genuinely reassuring, verified fact for a story that is often assumed to be hyperscaler-concentrated.
- **Structural manufacturing risk:** ~80% of capacity runs through the Flash Ventures JV with Kioxia — SNDK does not
  fully control its own supply, a real structural constraint noted in its own risk factors.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) management's own Q4 disclosure cites the NAND market growing from $300B (CY26) to $500B (CY27) with
bits on allocation beyond 2027 — a primary-source, not analyst-extrapolated, claim of a multi-year shortage. (2) Zero
debt, $4.76B cash, and a demonstrated ability to return $4.5B in a single quarter give real optionality. (3) No
customer concentration risk, unlike the common perception of memory suppliers being hostage to 2–3 hyperscalers.
**Bear:** (1) NAND/DRAM has historically been one of the most brutally cyclical sub-industries in semis — multi-year
up-cycles have every time been followed by sharp price collapses as customers who over-ordered under allocation work
down inventory and new industry capacity comes online; nothing in this cycle's structure (an industry-wide capacity
response is already being discussed in trade press, not verified here) suggests immunity. (2) The stock is up ~13x in
12 months (mom_12_1) — pricing in a large amount of this cycle's persistence already. (3) The reverse DCF, on any
reasonable methodology, requires the current record margins to both persist and keep compounding for a decade — a bar
the bull case's own multi-year (not decade) framing does not clear.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **Extreme earnings volatility is itself the primary flag**: a $1.9B net loss to $6.9B net income swing within four
  quarters is disclosed and verified, not hidden, but it means every "quality" metric the model likes (ROE, margins)
  is a cycle-peak snapshot.
- **Manufacturing concentration**: ~80% of capacity via the Flash Ventures JV with Kioxia — a single-partner
  structural dependency disclosed in the 10-K.
- No customer concentration (>10%) — a genuine mitigant, verified in the 10-K, contrary to a common prior about
  memory-industry customer risk.
- Spin-off complexity: as a company spun off in Feb-2025, historical comparability (pre-2025 numbers) reflects a
  divisional carve-out from Western Digital, not a standalone operating history — flagged as a data-quality caveat
  for any pre-2025 comparison, not itself a red flag.
- No SEC investigation, litigation, short-seller report, auditor change, material weakness, or going-concern language
  found in this session's searches; the 10-K's own Legal Proceedings language is boilerplate ("may at times be
  involved in litigation") with no specific matter disclosed in the excerpt reviewed.
- Insider selling: not separately checked in this session beyond routine Form 4 filings noted in search results
  (disclosed gap).

# KKR & Co. Inc. (KKR) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F111, Wave 7)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (12–36 month horizon). Reason: durable, compounding fee/AUM franchise trading at a valuation that, on a reverse-DCF basis, implies far less growth than KKR has delivered or guides toward — but the Global Atlantic insurance/annuity book adds real earnings volatility and duration risk that the quant model's worst-in-batch rank (per Q04 triage) is at least partly pricing correctly, so full weight is not warranted.

## 2. Business in plain English
KKR is a global alternative-asset manager (private equity, credit, infrastructure, real assets, capital markets) that earns management and performance fees on ~$796bn of assets under management (AUM), plus balance-sheet investment gains. Since 2021 it has also owned 100% of Global Atlantic, a fixed-annuity/life-insurance platform whose "spread" income (investment yield minus policyholder crediting rate) is now folded into KKR's "Insurance Operating Earnings." It makes money from (a) recurring management fees on locked-up capital, (b) performance fees ("carry") when funds exit investments profitably, and (c) insurance float invested into KKR's own credit strategies. Competitive position: top-3 global alternative manager alongside Blackstone and Apollo, differentiated by the insurance-balance-sheet flywheel that funds origination.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Q04 triage flagged 13.6x NTM P/E and strong forward growth, but the **worst statistical-model rank in the batch (495/500)** and negative 12-month momentum (-15%), largely attributable to Global Atlantic's private-credit/annuity exposure and sector-wide insurance-affiliate concerns (flagged alongside Apollo in Mar 2026 press). My read: the low-multiple, high-growth combination is durable at the segment level — Total Operating Earnings (management fees + insurance + strategic holdings; management's preferred "more durable and recurring" metric) rose 19% LTM to $5.5bn (2Q'26 LTM) from $4.7bn (2Q'25 LTM), and 84% of segment earnings now come from these more-recurring sources per the 2Q'26 earnings release. The negative momentum/model rank looks like a market discount for insurance-book opacity, not a break in the fee-earning franchise — but it is not an "artefact" either; it is a real, currently-unresolved risk (Section 6).

TTM ANI/share (2Q'26) = FY2025 ANI/share ($4.87) − 1H2025 ANI/share ($2.33) + 1H2026 ANI/share ($3.02) = **$5.56**.
Reverse DCF (Gordon-growth approximation, cost of equity 9.0%, terminal/perpetual growth solved for): $96.67 = $5.56×(1+g)/(0.09−g) → **implied perpetual growth ≈ 3.1%** (sensitivity: at r=9.5%, implied g≈3.6%; at r=8.5%, implied g≈2.6%).
This is a simplified single-stage approximation (true reverse DCF would model AUM build, fee-rate compression and insurance spread separately); treat as directional, not precise.
**Bear/base/bull ANI/share growth cases (my own, evidence-based):**
- Bear (~4%/yr): private-credit/insurance mark-downs, AUM growth stalls, fee-rate compression continues.
- Base (~10–12%/yr): AUM compounds toward $1.2–1.5tn by mid-2030s on continued fundraising (2Q'26 LTM new capital $4.68bn vs $3.96bn a year earlier), Global Atlantic scales without a credit event.
- Bull (~16–18%/yr): insurance flywheel accelerates origination, carry realizations pick up as rates ease.
Because the reverse-DCF-implied growth (~3%) sits **below** even my bear case, valuation looks **cheap** relative to my own fundamental view — the market appears to be discounting Global Atlantic/insurance risk more than the fee-earning franchise's demonstrated growth would justify, which is consistent with, but does not fully validate, the triage's "worst model rank" concern.

## 8. Bull case
1. Fee Paying AUM +14.7% YoY ($556.2bn→$638.4bn) with LTM new capital raised up 18% YoY ($3.96bn→$4.68bn) — the fundraising engine is accelerating, not decelerating.
2. Total Operating Earnings (the "durable" earnings base) +19% LTM, now 84% of segment earnings — reduces reliance on lumpy performance fees.
3. Reverse-DCF-implied growth (~3%) is well below any plausible base case, leaving room for re-rating if Global Atlantic avoids a credit event.

## 9. Bear case
1. Global Atlantic was, per the FY2025 10-K (filed 2026-02-27, accession 0001404912-26-000007), "the subject of policyholder and agent class action litigation matters and a number of regulatory matters stemming from service disruptions caused by a third-party administrator" — a live, named legal/operational exposure inside the insurance segment.
2. Insurance-book duration/credit risk (private-credit-heavy general account) is opaque from consolidated filings alone; I could not cleanly separate parent-only leverage from insurance-company leverage this pass.
3. KKR gives no forward guidance, so the market must extrapolate from volatile realized-performance-fee quarters (1Q'26 net income $405.2m vs 2Q'26 $700.5m — a 73% sequential swing).

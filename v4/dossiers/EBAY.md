# EBAY — eBay Inc. (F84 diligence dossier, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (12–24 month horizon). A genuine, evidenced re-acceleration (GMV +14–15%, ad revenue +25%, adjusted EPS +41% in Q2 2026) is real, but at ~16.5x NTM P/E the price already implies growth at or above what a conservative base case should assume, and tariff/de-minimis policy risk to its cross-border categories is unresolved.

## 2. Business in plain English
eBay runs an online marketplace connecting buyers and sellers, monetised through final-value/listing fees (take rate on Gross Merchandise Volume) plus a fast-growing first-party advertising business sold to its own sellers. It has been repositioning away from being a generic everything-store toward "focus categories" — collectibles/trading cards, parts & accessories (motors), refurbished/pre-owned electronics, and fashion resale (via its Depop acquisition) — where it has stronger competitive differentiation than against Amazon/Temu/Shein in commodity retail.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
b1 flags EBAY on value (low absolute P/E vs the market and its own history) with improving growth momentum. Q2 2026 results (eBay Inc. 10-Q, SEC EDGAR, CIK 0001065088, period 2026-06-30, and the Q2 2026 press release) show GMV of $22.4bn (+15% as-reported, +14% FX-neutral) beating the company's own prior guidance of $21.3–21.7bn, revenue of $3.13bn beating guidance of $2.97–3.03bn, and non-GAAP operating income of $893m (+16% YoY, 28.5% margin) (Source 1, Source 2). This is a real beat-and-raise, not a one-off comp: over 70% of GMV growth is attributed to the focus-category strategy plus advertising, per management's own attribution (Source 2) — durable if the mix shift continues, but it also means slower-growing "core" categories are a growing drag if focus-category momentum ever stalls.

- Q1 2026 → Q2 2026: Q2 actual beat the guidance given at the Q1 2026 release (GMV $22.4bn vs guided $21.3–21.7bn; revenue $3.13bn vs guided $2.97–3.03bn) (Source 1) — **beaten, and next guide raised.**

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) first-party advertising (+25% YoY) is high-margin and still a small % of GMV, with a long runway versus Amazon's ad take-rate; (2) focus categories (collectibles, motors parts, refurbished, Depop fashion resale) are genuinely differentiated niches where eBay has real competitive moats (inventory depth, trust/authentication), not commodity retail where it loses to Amazon/Temu; (3) capital-light model with high FCF conversion funds continued buybacks even in a soft macro.
**Bear:** (1) tariff and de-minimis policy changes affecting low-value cross-border parcels are a live, unresolved risk to eBay's historically China-sourced seller base (a sector-wide 2025–2026 headwind for cross-border marketplaces; not independently re-verified against eBay's own risk-factor disclosure this pass — **data gap**, flagged rather than asserted); (2) "core" non-focus categories are still large and slower-growing, a drag if the mix shift stalls; (3) leveraged balance sheet (Debt/Equity ~163%, aggregator-sourced) reduces flexibility if a downturn hits GMV.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. FX-neutral GMV growth < 8% for two consecutive quarters (below the low end of the FY2026 outlook range).
2. Advertising revenue growth decelerates below 15% YoY for two consecutive quarters.
3. Consolidated net leverage (net debt/EBITDA) exceeds 3.0x on the next 10-Q.
4. Adverse US or EU regulatory action specifically targeting de-minimis/tariff treatment of cross-border marketplace parcels that eBay quantifies as a material revenue headwind in a filing.
5. Non-GAAP operating margin falls below 26% for two consecutive quarters (guidance/margin miss).

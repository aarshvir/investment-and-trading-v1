## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 24–36 months. Senior-housing demand/supply gap is real and delivering, but the stock has already re-rated to a rich multiple, leverage is above REIT peer medians, and near-term returns depend on the SHOP re-acceleration continuing without a hiccup — position it, don't max it.

## 2. Business in plain English
Ventas is a healthcare-focused REIT that owns and (through operators) manages ~1,400 properties: the largest segment is senior housing (independent/assisted living, largely operated by third parties under RIDEA structures where Ventas participates in NOI upside — the "SHOP" portfolio), plus outpatient medical office/research buildings and a smaller triple-net/skilled-nursing book. It earns money from rents, management fees and its share of senior-housing operating NOI; growth comes from occupancy/rate gains in existing communities plus accretive acquisitions and development.

## 7. Valuation snapshot
Price $86.59 (2026-09-25). Normalized FFO guide midpoint $3.875 → **P/FFO ≈ 22.3x**, well above VTR's pre-2024 historical range (typically mid-to-high teens) and above most healthcare-REIT peers (Welltower, American Healthcare REIT trade at a premium too, given the same SHOP thesis, so the peer set has broadly re-rated together — this is a sector re-rating, not VTR-specific mispricing, which somewhat reduces single-name risk but does not eliminate multiple-compression risk if the SHOP growth deceleration flagged for 2027+ by the sector materializes).

**Reverse DCF (qualitative):** two-stage model, FFO₀=$3.875, cost of equity 8.5%, terminal growth 3.0%, 10-year explicit period → **implied 10-year FFO growth ≈ 5.3%/year** to justify the current price. That is *below* our base-case FFO growth assumption of ~7%/year (guided 8–10% for 2026, moderating as the 2026 investment surge laps). **Implied vs. base: BELOW** — the market is not pricing in as much growth as management's current trajectory suggests, which is what makes this INCLUDE-eligible despite the rich absolute multiple; the margin is not huge, hence INCLUDE-SMALL rather than full weight.

## 8. Bull case
1. SHOP same-store NOI growth (+16% YoY, U.S. +18%) continues near current pace for several more years given genuinely constrained new senior-housing supply (multi-year construction lead times, high construction costs) against accelerating 80+ population growth.
2. Ventas is redeploying at higher, accretive investment volume ($4.5B raised guide) into a still-fragmented senior-housing ownership market, with embedded operating-partner upside as more communities stabilize post-COVID.
3. Balance sheet access is currently open (equity issuance funding growth at a premium valuation is accretive, not dilutive, to per-share FFO when reinvested above the cost of capital).

## 9. Bear case
1. Leverage (5.3x net debt/EBITDA, consolidated) is above peer comfort and rising in dollar terms even as equity issuance keeps the ratio in check; a rate shock or funding-market seizure would hit this business harder than a lower-levered REIT.
2. The 22x P/FFO multiple assumes SHOP NOI growth of the current magnitude persists; occupancy gains are a one-time normalization from COVID-era troughs and mathematically cannot repeat at 16–18% same-store NOI growth indefinitely — deceleration to high-single-digit growth (still good, but a big step down) is the base case for 2027+, and the stock has not obviously priced that in.
3. Share count is growing fast (+13.7% YoY) to fund the investment program; if acquisition cap rates compress further, new deals become less accretive and per-share FFO growth could lag total-company FFO growth.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Same-store SHOP NOI growth (YoY) falls below **8%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. 16% now) — signals the normalization is over.
2. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above **6.0x** (vs. 5.3x now) without a credit-rating affirmation.
3. Normalized FFO guidance is **cut** (not just held) at any quarterly release.
4. Dividend payout ratio (dividends / normalized FFO) exceeds **75%** (vs. ~50% now), signalling the balance sheet is being asked to do more of the growth funding.
5. A public 10-Q/10-K discloses a material weakness, restatement, or going-concern-adjacent language (none currently).

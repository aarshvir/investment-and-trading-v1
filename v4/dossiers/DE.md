## 2. Business in plain English
Deere makes tractors, combines and precision-ag technology (Production & Precision Agriculture), smaller tractors, turf and dairy equipment (Small Ag & Turf), and construction and forestry machines (C&F), sold through dealers. John Deere Financial lends to dealers and farmers. Moat: dealer network, installed base, technology stack and aftermarket, giving above-peer margins.

**Correction (valuation method, item e)**
The reverse DCF used net income ($4.875B, the FY2026 guide midpoint) as the cash-flow base. Net income is not free cash flow: nine-month FY2026 equipment-operations free cash flow was $3,298M (operating cash $4,012M less capex $714M, including $794M of dividends from Financial Services per the original) and stock-based compensation of $116M is a real cost, giving $3,182M after SBC, or 84% of nine-month net income ($3,808M). Applying that conversion to the guide gives FY2026 free cash flow after SBC of about $4,075M. The discount rate of 9% is consistent with 5.17% (10-year Treasury, 25 Sep 2026) plus Blume-adjusted beta 0.94 x 4.14% = 9.06%. Restated on market cap $186.2bn, 3% terminal growth, 10 years:
| Cash-flow base | r 8% | r 9.06% | r 10% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Net income $4,875M (original) | 10.8% | 13.6% | 15.9% |
| FCF after SBC about $4,075M | 13.1% | **16.0%** | 18.3% |
The result is higher than the original and still far above the 9% base: **implied_vs_base stays above; dossier_view stays expensive; verdict stays WATCH.** About $18bn of free cash flow by FY36 would be needed, against FY2023 net income of $10.2bn at the peak.

## 2. Business in plain English
AES owns power plants and utilities: two regulated US electric utilities (AES Indiana and AES Ohio, about 1.1 million customers), a large renewable-energy development business selling contracted solar, wind and storage power (including to technology companies), and energy-infrastructure assets in Latin America. It earns regulated returns at the utilities and long-term contracted prices at the power plants. Its problem is capital: it spends about $6–7B a year on construction, funded with debt and by selling stakes in projects, and cannot fund it from operations.

## 7. Valuation and reverse analysis (price $14.87; V1 has no row for AES)
- **V1:** AES is not in v1_valuation_table.csv (V1 excludes pending-takeover targets), so v1_verdict is null and there is nothing to reconcile.
- **Reverse DCF is not meaningful:** FCFF is negative (-$1.72B TTM), so there is no positive cash-flow base on which to solve a ten-year growth rate; the equity price is pinned to the deal. The informative reverse calculation is market-implied deal probability: with deal value $15.00 and a no-deal reference price of ~$10.7 (the pre-rumour VWAP implied by the 40.3% premium), $14.87 implies about a 97% chance of closing (ignoring time value and dividends).
- **Spread:** 0.87% to $15.00. If it closes Dec 31 2026 (97 days) that is ~3.3% annualised, or ~7.7% annualised including one $0.17595 dividend; for a Mar 31 2027 close (187 days), ~1.7% or ~4.0% including one dividend (dividends after Q2'26 not verified). Versus a 4.24% 3-month risk-free rate (V1 assumption) the excess return is small relative to a ~28% downside tail; break-even break-probability is about 4%.
- **Standalone fundamentals:** EV/2025 Adjusted EBITDA is not computed because the press-release EV ($33.4B) is proportional while the $2,871M Adjusted EBITDA is consolidated (mixed basis). AES is a capital-hungry standalone (negative FCF, equity claims of ~$8B outside AES common), so my standalone base value is below the deal price: implied_vs_base = "above" (price embeds the deal, not fundamentals).
- **3-year scenario returns (annualised, total return):** bear -9.4%, base +4.9%, bull +7.4%. Basis: base = deal closes ~Jan 2027, holder receives $15.00 plus two quarterly dividends of $0.176, proceeds then earn a 4.2% cash rate for the remaining ~2.7 years; bear = deal fails, price ~$10.70 at year 3 plus ~$0.35 dividends (dividend cut); bull = deal fails or is re-bid and the standalone equity reaches ~$17 with four $0.35 dividends. There is no EPS/exit-multiple basis because earnings are not the price driver.

## 8. Bull and bear (for a holder at $14.87)
**Bull:** (1) Shareholders, HSR, CFIUS and the Ohio regulator have all approved, removing the largest regulatory hurdles; financing is committed and not a closing condition; (2) large termination fees protect AES ($100M/$588M from Parent); (3) 2025 adjusted results beat the range "Adjusted EPS of $2.10 to $2.26" ($2.34 actual).
**Bear:** (1) Upside is only $0.13 a share (+0.9%) while a break costs ~$4; (2) NY PSC, FERC and certain foreign approvals remain and "Burdensome Condition" language gives the buyers an exit route; outside date is June 1, 2027 (extendable), so dead money is possible; (3) standalone AES has negative FCF and, per its own board, would have needed a dividend cut or equity issuance.

# Wynn Resorts, Limited (WYNN) — Diligence Dossier

**1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Reservation: leverage is elevated (net debt/EBITDAR ~4x on a pre-rent basis, higher on a post-rent EBITDA basis) and the September 2026 refinancing raises the marginal cost of debt (5.250% notes replaced by 6.875% notes), just as ~$1bn+ more is still to be spent on the unlevered UAE project. Thesis horizon 12–36 months.

**2. Business in plain English.** Wynn Resorts owns and operates luxury casino resorts: Wynn Palace and Wynn Macau in Macau (China), Wynn Las Vegas, and Encore Boston Harbor in Massachusetts. It makes money from casino gaming (VIP and mass-market tables/slots), hotel rooms, and food/beverage/retail at these properties. It is also building **Wynn Al Marjan Island**, a new integrated resort in Ras Al Khaimah, UAE — its first legal gaming property outside the US/Macau, expected to open September 2027.

**3. Why the model likes it.** b1_live_scores.csv: WYNN scores very high on Momentum (pct_mom_12_1 0.81 — 81st percentile) and well on Value (pct_ep 0.58, pct_bp 0.44), live_rank 424/1172, triage price_vs_growth 5/5. This looks durable rather than an artefact: it is underpinned by a real, verifiable Macau mass-market recovery (Wynn Palace mass table win 29.7% in Q2 2026 vs 22.3% a year earlier; +23% Palace revenue in Q1 2026) rather than a one-off VIP swing, which is the higher-quality and less-volatile segment of Macau gaming.

**5. Guidance track record.** WYNN, like most casino operators, does **not issue formal forward revenue/EBITDA guidance** in any of the last four earnings releases reviewed (Q3 2025, Q4/FY2025, Q1 2026, Q2 2026 — no numeric FY range found in any). Management commentary is the closest substitute: CEO Craig Billings reaffirmed the Wynn Al Marjan Island (UAE) **September 2027 opening** in the Q2 2026 release, consistent with prior disclosures — no slippage identified. Absent a numeric guidance range, the reported quarter-over-quarter and YoY property-level trends (§4) are the primary evidence base.

**6. Earnings quality & balance sheet.**
- Adjusted Property EBITDAR is management's standard non-GAAP metric (adds back corporate rent/lease costs); it is not directly comparable to a REIT-adjusted "EBITDA" without a rent add-back reversal. TTM adjusted EBITDAR (Q3'25 + Q4'25 + Q1'26 + Q2'26) is not fully reconstructed here (Q3/Q4 2025 EBITDAR not independently re-pulled at this depth — data gap); using the visible run-rate of ~$550–570M/quarter, TTM is approximately **$2.2–2.3bn**, flagged as an estimate.
- Balance sheet at 2026-06-30: **total debt $10.72bn** (Macau-related $5.76bn, Las Vegas $877.8M, Wynn Resorts Finance (WRF) $3.49bn, retail JV $598.9M), cash $1.57bn + short-term investments $527.4M ≈ **$2.10bn liquid**. Net debt ≈ **$8.6bn**. Against the ~$2.2–2.3bn TTM EBITDAR estimate, net leverage is roughly **3.7–3.9x on a pre-rent (EBITDAR) basis**; the triage's cited "5.7x net debt/EBITDA" is plausibly a post-rent (EBITDA, not EBITDAR) or trailing-different-window measure — both readings show leverage that is elevated for the sector but not covenant-threatening at current cash flow.
- **September 2026 refinancing:** WRF issued $900M of new 6.875% senior notes due 2035 (private placement, 8-K filed 2026-09-10/11) to redeem in full the existing 5.250% senior notes due 2027. This is a **like-for-like maturity extension at a materially higher coupon** (+162.5bp) — a genuine, disclosed increase in run-rate interest expense, not new money for growth capex.
- UAE project: **Wynn Al Marjan Island cumulative cash contributions were $1.06bn as of Q2 2026** (Q1 2026 contribution $100.1M, Q2 2026 $48.1M — pace has slowed quarter over quarter), structured as an unconsolidated joint-venture equity investment (not on WYNN's consolidated capex line); total project cost is not disclosed in either release reviewed (data gap — a material unknown given this is WYNN's first venture into a new, largely unprecedented regulatory jurisdiction for gaming).
- Capital returns: $0.25/share quarterly dividend; share buybacks continuing but modest relative to debt load ($53.8M in Q1 2026, part of a $401–454M remaining authorization) — capital priority is clearly debt/UAE-funding over aggressive buybacks right now.

**8. Bull case / Bear case.**
- Bull: (1) Macau mass-market recovery is broad and verifiable (Wynn Palace mass table win 29.7% vs 22.3% YoY, +23% Palace revenue Q1'26) and is the highest-quality, most durable segment of Macau gaming; (2) Wynn Al Marjan Island (UAE) is a first-mover option on a new, large, currently un-gamed Gulf market with only modest cash commitment so far ($1.06bn of an undisclosed total) relative to WYNN's scale; (3) Las Vegas and Boston remain stable cash generators funding the dividend even while Macau and UAE capital needs are met.
- Bear: (1) leverage (~3.7–4x EBITDAR, refinanced at a higher coupon in Sept 2026) leaves less room for a Macau or Las Vegas downturn without covenant or liquidity stress; (2) UAE total project cost is undisclosed and gaming legality/regulatory framework in the UAE is unprecedented — execution, regulatory-change, and demand risk are all real and unhedged by any comparable operating history; (3) Las Vegas and Encore Boston Harbor EBITDAR both *declined* YoY in Q2 2026 (‑$19.6M and ‑$7.8M respectively) even as Macau grew — a reminder the US segments are not currently a growth driver and could offset Macau strength in a US consumer slowdown.

**9. Key risks & kill criteria.**
1. Net debt / adjusted EBITDAR rises above 4.5x at any quarter-end (from ~3.7–3.9x currently).
2. Wynn Al Marjan Island opening slips beyond March 2028 (6+ months past the reaffirmed September 2027 date) or total project cost is disclosed at a level requiring new external financing beyond the existing revolvers.
3. Las Vegas or Encore Boston Harbor adjusted EBITDAR declines YoY for three consecutive quarters (two already logged in Q2 2026).
4. Macau mass-market table win percentage at either Wynn Palace or Wynn Macau falls below 20% for two consecutive quarters (Wynn Macau was already 15.1–17.1% in the last two quarters — watch closely).
5. Any credit-rating downgrade following the September 2026 refinancing, or a covenant-headroom disclosure in the next 10-Q.

**10. Catalysts & calendar.** Next earnings: **~2026-11-05** (Q3 2026; exact date not yet confirmed by a WYNN press release as of this review — estimated from the prior-year pattern, 2025-11-06). Watch the $900M note settlement/close (expected shortly after the 2026-09-22 8-K/indenture filing) and any further Wynn Al Marjan Island cost updates.

**11. Red-flag scan.** No auditor change, restatement, going-concern language, SEC/DOJ investigation, or short-seller report identified in this review window. The September 2026 debt refinancing at a materially higher coupon (6.875% vs 5.250%) is disclosed and is the single most notable near-term financial event, not concealed but worth flagging as a real cost increase. UAE total project cost remains undisclosed — a genuine information gap rather than a concealment, but material enough that a future disclosure (or lack of one) should be watched. Insider Form-4 pattern not reviewed at standard depth (data gap).

## Independent decision

**I would pause new direct purchases of both AMP and PAYX at the 30 September prices until the exit-multiple assumptions are explicitly approved as underwriting judgments.** The companies are investable businesses, but their existing base-case return hurdles are not supported by earnings growth alone at current prices. The prior AMP ≤$500 and PAYX ≤$101.80 ceilings are valid arithmetic outputs *given* 13x and 20x FY2029 exit P/E respectively; their labels should not imply that these exit multiples are filing facts or a demonstrated market reversion. A narrowly sized AMP starter is more defensible than a PAYX starter if the portfolio committee deliberately accepts that revaluation risk, because AMP's H1 normalized earnings are supported by segment results and capital returns. PAYX's cash conversion has a current unresolved adverse signal. This is a challenge to conviction, not proof that either must fall or that buying them is necessarily wrong.

The four strongest alternatives examined here—ADP, LH, TJX and RSG—also fail a common 12% after-tax/cost hurdle under unchanged 30 September entry P/E and stated growth assumptions. Do **not** replace AMP/PAYX merely to populate the direct-stock sleeve. The 15% index / maximum 10% direct / minimum 75% reserve policy remains the published mandate. If a direct-stock allocation is reduced, the balance can remain in reserve under that policy. The user's 15–20% tolerable drawdown is not a floor or stop-loss guarantee.

## Company-by-company primary-source challenge

**AMP — stronger accounting case, meaningful revaluation bet.** The consolidated Q2 2026 10-Q and earnings exhibit remain the latest filed financial statements; adjusted Q1/Q2 EPS of $11.26/$11.07 produce $22.33 H1, and the prior model normalizes to $44 annually after the one-off Comerica benefit. Wealth-management fee revenue, assets and margins provide operating support, but equity-market levels and advisor flows make earnings cyclical. The issuer's [3 September Q3 considerations PDF](https://s205.q4cdn.com/618704162/files/doc_news/2026/09/Ameriprise-Financial-Provides-an-Update-on-Q3-2026-Considerations.pdf) forecasts roughly $17 million of **sequential pretax** uplift from additional fee/trading days and a 20–22% FY2026 operating tax rate; the day-count uplift is not permanent growth. Its [29 September authorization](https://ir.ameriprise.com/news/news-details/2026/Ameriprise-Financial-Announces-Additional-5-5-Billion-Share-Repurchase-Authorization/default.aspx) permits an additional $5.5 billion of repurchases through September 2028, with $1.1 billion remaining from the prior plan as of June 2026. It is neither an obligation nor an extra $5.5 billion of free equity value. Future share shrinkage must be funded and deducted from aggregate equity/cash; the v011 P/B projection omitted that capital use, as v012 established. The main EPS model counts buyback effects only through future per-share earnings. AMP's inherited $511 discounted sum-of-parts cross-check is only ~4% above the 30 September stock price and uses discretionary multiples. Q3 results are scheduled for [29 October](https://www.ameriprise.com/newsroom), so normalized flows and actual repurchase/capital movements can be tested then. **Challenge verdict:** no automatic add at $492 merely because it is below $500; consider only an explicitly revaluation-dependent small starter if the investor accepts the 13x exit case and its bear path.

**PAYX — recurring franchise, dividend and cash bridge constrain the rerating case.** The consolidated [FY2027 Q1 10-Q](https://investor.paychex.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000723531-26-000006/payx-20260831.htm) reports August-quarter revenue $1,630.5 million, GAAP diluted EPS $1.21 and company-adjusted EPS $1.34. Management Solutions service revenue rose from $1,163.3 million to $1,213.1 million, only ~4.3%; PEO/Insurance revenue grew faster. CFO fell to **$413.5 million from $718.4 million** a year earlier, while cash capex was $56.1 million and dividends paid were $424.1 million. Thus single-quarter CFO less capex of $357.4 million failed to cover the $424.1 million dividend by $66.7 million. One quarter is not proof of an unsustainable annual dividend: payroll-tax/client-fund timing is material, and management's guidance remains in place. It *does* require actual subsequent cash recovery before an add. The previous pack's conservative TTM owner-cash proxy of ~$1.71 billion is only about 1.01x the current $4.76 annualized dividend times ~356 million shares. Corporate cash plus investments of ~$934 million are distinct from client/restricted funds; debt carrying value is ~$4.56 billion. The $6.60 FY2029 EPS at 20x requires both operating delivery and a 19% rise in P/E from the current normalized 16.84x. ADP trades near 23.6x its FY2026 GAAP EPS at $258.06, so 20x for a sound payroll franchise is economically conceivable; PAYX must prove Paycor integration, organic Management Solutions growth and cash coverage for that discount to narrow. **Challenge verdict:** pause a fresh PAYX starter at $98.50 until cash conversion recovers or a materially lower price compensates for the multiple assumption. The prior $101.80 limit had just $0.046 of modeled 12% ceiling room at its old data cutoff; current lower price improves but does not eliminate model risk.

**MSFT — stronger earnings-driven bridge, still near its discretionary limit.** The consolidated [FY2026 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526323660/msft-20260630.htm) reports $331.839 billion revenue and $155.237 billion operating income. Its $500 discretional entry limit remains sensible despite a flat-P/E diagnostic above 12%, because maintaining the current 30.8x earnings valuation is not independently validated. No new financial filing appeared after 25 September in the SEC feed. **Challenge verdict:** WAIT above $500, recheck FY2027 Q1 cloud growth, capex and financing before any entry.

**NVDA — growth can carry returns, but bear loss is severe.** The consolidated [Q2 FY2027 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581026000075/nvda-20260726.htm) and [issuer outlook](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-Financial-Results-for-Second-Quarter-Fiscal-2027/) anchor $96.221 billion quarterly revenue, $63.734 billion operating income and $108 billion ±2% Q3 revenue guide.

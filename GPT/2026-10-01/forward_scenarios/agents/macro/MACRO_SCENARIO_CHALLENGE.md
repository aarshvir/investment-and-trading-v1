# Macro and regime challenge to v014 forward returns

**Prepared:** 1 October 2026. **Portfolio:** `v014_2026-10-01_codex`, 100% individual US-listed stocks, 20 names. **Market anchor:** completed 30 September 2026 US session; 1 October delayed observations are intraday only. This note challenges the macro assumptions underlying forward portfolio scenarios. It does not change v014's weights or claim a calibrated probability distribution.

## Regime paths to use when underwriting returns

These are **illustrative, broad nominal USD total-return stress bands**, not confidence intervals, calibrated odds, or trade instructions. They apply to the 20-stock basket at the 30 September reference prices. They include dividends conceptually but **do not quantify personal tax or FX**; the security-by-security engine should replace them with explicit cash flows, share dilution, exit multiples and after-withholding costs before publication of a house forecast. In every case a one-year outcome is much less predictable than a five-year business result.

| Path | Portfolio total return in 1 year | Annualized return over 3 years | Annualized return over 5 years | What has to happen |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Demand shock / sticky inflation** | **−25% to −40%** | **−8% to +1%** | **−2% to +4%** | Inflation/rates keep discounting harsh while demand/credit deteriorate. Financials and industrials lose earnings, AI orders or cash monetization disappoint, premium software/payment multiples contract. An acute crisis can exceed the 1-year band: v014's hindsight 2007–09 peak-to-trough replay was −44.08%, and a future path could be worse. |
| **Growth persists, valuation normalizes** | **−5% to +15%** | **+4% to +8%** | **+5% to +9%** | GDP stays positive, unemployment/credit roughly stable, inflation eases toward the SEP path, and diversified owner earnings grow mid-single digits to high single digits. Dividend cash contributes modestly but high long rates prevent broad P/E expansion. This is the most defensible *working range*, not a measured expected return. |
| **Productivity and AI cash payback** | **+15% to +30%** | **+10% to +16%** | **+9% to +14%** | AI/cloud spending converts into durable customer earnings and cash, payments/industrials volumes remain resilient, inflation recedes, and rates/risk premiums permit at least stable multiples. Requires multiple favorable developments; do not assign an uncalibrated probability. |

**Arithmetic discipline:** model each security as ending distributable value from **normalized per-share earnings or owner cash × a defensible exit multiple**, plus intervening cash dividends, less costs/tax where known. At the portfolio level, weighted starting valuation and fundamentals are *not* a single P/E: banks, utilities, software and distributors need different denominators. As an illustrative accounting check only, 6% annual per-share fundamental growth plus 1% cash yield and 2% annualized multiple contraction yields roughly 5% nominal annual return before interactions/costs; a 15% one-year multiple contraction can overwhelm the same growth. Those percentages are scenario inputs, **not observed weighted forecasts**.

## Cross-sector transmission and update triggers

- **Inflation/rates:** Longer rates and elevated inflation can compress premium duration stocks (MSFT, NVDA, MA, VEEV, PTC, RSG), raise PEG financing costs and pressure PG/PEP household volume/brand pricing. PEG's first-half CFO less property additions did not cover dividends in the v014 primary audit; do not equate regulated EPS growth with free cash. Use the actual 10-year rate only as a valuation reference, never a non-stock holding recommendation.
- **Recession/credit:** ADP payroll clients and TJX traffic/receipt mix could weaken; AXP card spend and loss provisioning deteriorate together; DOV, BALL, CRH and ALLE depend on industrial/construction orders. RSG, MCK, STE and PG may be comparatively resilient operationally but are not immune to P/E compression or debt costs. These are directional mechanisms, not empirically estimated betas.
- **AI capital cycle:** MSFT's cash conversion versus capex and NVIDIA's commitments/customer demand are first-order tests. Watch delivery lead times, customer funding, receivables, deferred revenue and cash from operations alongside reported revenue; a capacity buildout may initially benefit NVDA but ultimately overbuild customers or crowd out free cash. Do not count a buyback both as cash distribution and share-count growth.
- **Credit/liquidity:** The Fed's May report is four months old and should not be treated as October spread data. Recheck current corporate spreads, AXP monthly delinquencies, and issuer refinancing maturities; a spread shock can coincide with multiple compression even if reported EPS is not yet lower.

**Next hard information dates:** BLS September employment release **2 October**; BEA third-quarter GDP advance estimate and September PCE report **29 October**, subject to official calendar updates. Quarterly issuer releases are security-specific and must be confirmed from each issuer IR calendar before execution. A weekly scenario refresh should record whether each new release changes the demand, inflation, discount-rate or business-cash branch; a price move alone is not evidence of a changed fundamental scenario.

**Limit:** This macro audit covers official macro and selected company primary sources, not full re-underwriting of all 20 companies. The Fed SEP gives conditional official projections; it does not supply odds for this portfolio. The numeric scenario bands above are judgmental and intentionally wide. The v014 report correctly did **not** claim a calibrated portfolio CAGR; no return band should be misrepresented as a guarantee or independently validated alpha estimate.

## Decision I would sign

**Revise the v012 new-money actions on AMP and PAYX from conditional BUY to WAIT at the September 30 prices.** The businesses remain credible candidates, but the proposed 12% three-year return at those prices is *too dependent on a terminal valuation increase* to justify calling the old $500/$101.80 limits high-conviction entries. This is a judgment about expected-return fragility, not a claim that the shares must fall. With both direct purchases suspended, the more coherent **conditional target is 25% broad index and 75% short-duration reserve**, subject to fund eligibility, live price, account-specific tax and liquidity checks. It redeploys the unused direct-stock risk budget into diversified equity without exceeding v012's 25% equity cap. Do not infer a sale: existing holdings and tax lots are unknown, and an owner can rationally hold at a price above a new-buy limit.

**Explicit limit decision: SUSPEND the old $500 AMP and $101.80 PAYX limits as new-buy authorizations.** They may remain archived comparison points, but no live purchase should follow from crossing them. The diagnostic prices and stress ceilings below define what a fresh approval must beat; they are not standing orders. This is a material correction to the v012 decision, rather than a “no change” week.

The prior v012 model is mathematically internally consistent. Its inputs, particularly the exit multiples, need an explicit margin-of-safety challenge. The old limits are not supported as robust entry prices under that challenge. I would **retire them as standing new-buy limits**, rather than silently replace them with a lower unvalidated order. I would prioritize a *re-underwriting review* around **AMP $440** and **PAYX $87** if there is no accompanying thesis deterioration. These are rounded diagnostic prices at which the fixed September 30 observed P/E exit cases reach the 12% hurdle; they are *not* automatic buy prices or intrinsic values. At such lower entries, the exit P/E would itself be above the new entry P/E, so it would be wrong to label those purchase prices “no re-rating.” A true flat-P/E-at-the-price-of-purchase assumption is a different sensitivity and must be recalculated at that date.

If a committee requires a **provisional numerical cap for its watchlist**, I would round below those diagnostic levels to **AMP $435 and PAYX $85**. Those caps clear the unchanged-model joint stress (one exit-P/E turn lower *and* 5% less terminal EPS) and the fixed-September-30-P/E sensitivity at the 12% hurdle, subject to the important limitation that these are still old operating forecasts. Neither cap converts WAIT into a buy without the operating, event, funding and tax gates listed below. The fixed-observed-P/E case has only about a 0.9% price cushion at $435 AMP and 2.4% at $85 PAYX, so a larger error could still erase the hurdle.

## Are other direct stocks better now?

**No funded replacement is demonstrated by the evidence reviewed.** The fresh September 30 dual-feed checks put MSFT at $512.90 versus its published $500 conditional level and NVDA at $228.38 versus $215. MSFT's unchanged v012 base scenario is **12.05%** after withholding/costs at $512.90, but its model 12% hurdle ceiling is only $513.64, leaving a **0.14% price buffer**. That is not a superior margin of safety. NVDA's unchanged base at $228.38 is **14.33%**, below its own 15% hurdle, and its inherited bear case is far worse than the two defensive candidates. The 14-name v012 model does not provide a coherent positive case for a switch to these names at current prices. [Fresh dated quote reconciliation](../MARKET_RECONCILIATION.csv).

Claude v011's RSG, LH, ADP and TJX merit further business study, but their September 30 prices ($209.87, $308.11, $258.06 and $132.31/$132.39) alone cannot make them funded substitutes. v012 already flagged an enterprise/equity cash-flow mismatch in RSG and a debt/reimbursement bridge in LH; ADP and TJX lack a like-for-like, fresh after-tax/cost downside-and-entry comparison to AMP/PAYX. If a replacement case wins after standardized underwriting, record the rejected candidate and the full opportunity-cost bridge. **Do not fill a 10% direct sleeve merely to use capacity.**

There is no validated stock-selection alpha in the inherited point-in-time work. Treat v011's 20-name all-stock portfolio as an archived proposal/shadow portfolio, not a default replacement. Its own GFC replay loses about 47%; the pasted audit's 70% equity “moderate” proposal loses about 39.5% in that diagnostic, beyond the owner's currently stated 15–20% tolerance. The replay applies today's stocks to old crises and is not an investable backtest. See the separate [portfolio challenger](../portfolio_challenger/PORTFOLIO_CHALLENGE.md) for the precise crisis-method limits. At **25% index/75% reserve**, v011's SPY GFC episode decline of 55.19% translates mechanically to about **−13.8%** with flat reserve, and a constructed simultaneous index −60%/reserve −2% shock produces **−16.5%**. The 15/85 comparator loses −10.7% in that same constructed shock. Thus 25/75 uses more of the stated risk budget while avoiding direct-name risk; it is **not a guaranteed 15–20% loss floor**. An equity wipeout alone would lose 25%, and reserve/currency loss could worsen it. If the index earns 2–3 percentage points more annually than the reserve, the incremental 10% index weight adds only about 0.2–0.3 percentage points to portfolio annual return before taxes/costs; this is an assumed spread, not a forecast.

## Conditions that would change this view

1. **AMP:** Fresh filed results establish at least the $44 economic EPS anchor through weaker-market conditions, continued positive wealth wrap flows, credible funding of actual repurchases after dividends and regulated-capital needs, and an independently defensible terminal multiple. A lower price around $440 can reopen underwriting *only if these conditions remain intact*. If actual EPS falls or outflows worsen, the price trigger is invalid.
2. **PAYX:** Two subsequent quarters demonstrate normalized owner cash comfortably above the dividend, sustained Management Solutions growth and recurring Paycor costs within the $5.85 anchor. Rebuild a terminal multiple from peer, own-history and cash-yield evidence. Around $87 warrants a fresh investment-committee review, not an order; a deterioration in cash or EPS invalidates the level.
3. **Either:** A revised primary-source operating forecast, an explicitly justified exit P/E and a purchase price that clear the 12% hurdle **with a real scenario buffer**, plus current event-calendar, account capacity, overlapping fund holdings, tax/FX and executable-price checks. A price alone does not change WAIT to BUY.
4. **Risk policy:** A later direct owner statement accepting a substantially larger temporary loss could justify a different equity cap after stress and liquidity analysis. It would not cure weak single-stock expected-return inputs. Until that answer is received, keep the direct 15–20% tolerance and v012 25% equity maximum as the governing upper bound.

## Validation and limits

The Python arithmetic independently reproduces v012's purchase-ceiling conventions to rounding and reconciles the September 30 close against two public market endpoints for AMP, PAYX, MSFT and NVDA. Those are dated reference quotes, not an executable current ask or proof of official exchange-close auction settlement. The primary documents above support reported operating facts. This challenge does **not** independently certify a 20-year multiple distribution, all 336 Claude dossiers, account taxes, future cash generation, a calibrated probability of outperformance, or an optimizer's expected returns. Recommendation to WAIT is an investment judgment motivated by scenario fragility and the owner's current risk constraint, and should be recorded as a material correction if adopted.

## Judgment for a full-equity stock mandate

I accept the user's explicit **stocks-only, fully invested** instruction as the construction constraint. It entails equity drawdowns materially beyond the earlier 15–20% tolerable decline; a stop or rebalance rule cannot assure that old tolerance. The old blanket 12% projected-return hurdle cannot be applied as if it selects twenty stocks here: several defensible high-quality businesses fail it under conservative unchanged-multiple assumptions. If this is a forced fully invested portfolio, disclose the shortfall and choose among imperfect candidates; do not invent expected returns or re-ratings to make every stock pass.

**Primary challenge to the proposed 20-stock slate:** `AMP 6%, HIG 5%, MA 5%, AXP 5%, USB 4%` is exactly **25% in financials**, the inherited sector cap. A simultaneous 60% loss in those five holdings alone costs **15 percentage points** of total portfolio value, before second-order financial sensitivity in ADP, PAYX or the rest. The four more balance-sheet-sensitive positions `AMP/HIG/AXP/USB` are 20% and would cost 12 points under the same shock. Name count does not diversify a common credit/equity-market regime. I would **cut HIG from 5% to 0–2%, AMP from 6% to 2–3%, and USB from 4% to 2–3%** unless the parent can present a documented, more favorable current underwriting. Reassign freed exposure to *nonfinancial* names only after the whole-portfolio sector and scenario checks; I have not audited every possible replacement to justify exact new weights.

**Interpretation of the table.** The relative ordering is a judgment about evidence quality, capital funding and valuation robustness under a forced fully invested allocation. It is **not** a ten-name optimizer output, a claim that the first four clear 12%, or a validated prediction that ADP will outperform PAYX. The only stock above its inherited 12% base hurdle among the first five at 30 September is AMP, and that depends on substantial re-rating. Risk-adjusted weight should reflect the same uncertainty. In particular, high annual yield does not turn WEC's growth capex into distributable cash, and a repurchase authorization does not turn AMP's future buyback cash into a second independent return source.

# WFC — Wells Fargo & Company (Diligence dossier, agent F49, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** (24–36 month horizon). A money-center bank that spent seven years (2018–2025) under a Federal Reserve-imposed asset cap is now growing its balance sheet again, with earnings, ROTCE and capital return all accelerating — and the current price still implies close to *zero* long-run earnings growth. No V1 systematic valuation row exists for WFC (bank valuation methodology differs; not run by V1) — this verdict rests on my own reverse DCF and primary-source fundamentals (§7).

## 2. Business in plain English
Wells Fargo is one of the four large US "money-center" banks — it takes deposits, makes consumer and commercial loans, and runs wealth-management, corporate/investment-banking and payments businesses across the whole US. It earns money on the spread between what it pays depositors and what it earns on loans/securities (net interest income), plus fees (noninterest income) from cards, wealth management, trading and investment banking. From Feb 2018 to June 2025 the Federal Reserve capped WFC's total assets at ~$1.95 trillion as a consequence of its 2016–18 sales-practices scandal; that cap is now lifted.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
**Entity scope: all figures are Wells Fargo & Company consolidated, from the 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-06-30 (filed 2026-07-28), unless marked "Bank" (Wells Fargo Bank, N.A., the subsidiary bank) or "Standardized"/"Advanced" (regulatory capital approach).**

- **Deposits (consolidated, instant, XBRL):** $1,340.7bn (2025-06-30) → $1,426.2bn (2025-12-31) → $1,454.9bn (2026-03-31) → **$1,501.4bn (2026-06-30)** — deposits up **+12.0%** in the twelve months since the asset cap was lifted (June 2025), directly corroborating the triage thesis with primary-source XBRL data, not just a news summary.
- **Capital (consolidated, Standardized Approach — the company's stated "binding framework"):** CET1 ratio **10.26%** at 2026-06-30, vs. 10.29% at 2026-03-31 and **11.13%** a year earlier (2025-06-30) — declining as risk-weighted assets grow with the balance sheet (RWAs $1,342.3bn vs $1,2xx bn a year prior), but still ~176bp above the regulatory minimum-plus-buffers of 8.50% (10-Q, Table 29/discussion, p. "Capital Management"). This is the expected and disclosed mechanism: WFC is now deploying capital into growth that the asset cap previously prevented.
- **Efficiency ratio** improved to 60% (Q2 2026) from 64% (Q2 2025) — operating leverage from revenue growth (+9%) outpacing expense growth (+2%).
- **Credit quality:** provision for credit losses $914M (Q2 2026) vs $1,005M (Q2 2025) — down, not up, despite loan growth; no deterioration signal in the primary data reviewed.
- **Capital return:** quarterly dividend raised to **$0.45/share** (Q1–Q2 2026, up from $0.40 a year earlier, +12.5%), and the Board approved a further increase to **$0.50/share for Q3 2026** on 2026-07-28 (10-Q, Capital section). $40bn buyback authorization (announced 2025-04-29, open-ended); **$7.1bn repurchased in H1 2026 (84 million shares)**; common shares outstanding fell from 3,220.4M (Jun 2025) to 3,028.5M (Jun 2026), **-6.0% in one year**.
- **Book value per share** $54.48 (tangible $46.13) at 2026-06-30, up from $51.13 (tangible $43.18) a year earlier (+6.6% / +6.8%).
- No net debt/EBITDA metric applies to a bank; the relevant leverage gauge is CET1 (above) and the Tier 1/Total capital ratios, both also comfortably above regulatory minimums (Tier 1 11.38% vs. 10.00% minimum; Total 13.72% vs. required buffer stack) per the same 10-Q table.

## 8. Bull case
1. Deposits +12% and RWAs growing in the twelve months since the Fed lifted the seven-year asset cap (June 2025) — primary-source XBRL-confirmed, not just a news claim.
2. ROTCE 17.7% (Q2 2026) and efficiency ratio improving to 60% show the bank converting balance-sheet growth into profit, not just growing for its own sake.
3. Aggressive, funded capital return: $7.1bn of buybacks in H1 2026 alone (-6% share count YoY) plus a dividend raised twice in fifteen months ($0.40→$0.45→$0.50), while CET1 still sits ~176bp above the regulatory minimum.

## 9. Bear case
1. CET1 has been declining for four consecutive quarters (11.13%→10.29%→10.26%) as capital is deployed into growth and buybacks — a sharper-than-expected credit cycle would compress the buffer faster than management may want.
2. Active regulatory/legal overhangs, not yet closed: a formal agreement with the OCC on AML/sanctions risk management (entered 2024-09-12, still open per the Q2 2026 10-Q Note 9) and government "fair access to banking" inquiries tied to a 2026 executive order — both are named, current, and could reintroduce growth restrictions similar in spirit to the 2018 asset cap if findings are adverse.
3. A securities-fraud class action related to hiring-practices disclosures received final court approval for an **$85 million settlement on 2026-05-21** (10-Q Note 9) — small in size but confirms the DOJ/SEC/court process, even though the DOJ and SEC investigations themselves closed without action.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. CET1 (Standardized Approach, consolidated) falls below **9.0%** (vs. 10.26% now and an 8.50% regulatory minimum+buffer) — would signal capital is being deployed too aggressively relative to the growth opportunity.
2. Provision for credit losses (consolidated, quarterly) exceeds **$1.5bn** for two consecutive quarters (vs. $914–1,135M in the last two quarters) — early credit-cycle turn signal.
3. Efficiency ratio (consolidated) rises back above **65%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. 60% now) — operating-leverage thesis breaking.
4. Diluted EPS growth YoY turns negative for two consecutive quarters (vs. +25% and +21% in the last two) — growth thesis broken.
5. A new formal enforcement action or growth restriction is imposed by the OCC/Federal Reserve (distinct from the AML agreement already disclosed) — would directly threaten the "unshackled" thesis.

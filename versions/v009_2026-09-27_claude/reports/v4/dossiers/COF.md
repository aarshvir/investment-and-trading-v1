# Capital One Financial Corporation (COF) — Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F74 (wave 4) | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close (px $200.20, mktcap $122.82bn per b1_live_scores.csv 2026-09-25)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH.** Thesis horizon 12–24 months. A genuinely improved franchise — Capital One closed its ~$35bn all-stock
acquisition of Discover on 18 May 2025 and now owns a closed-loop card network (Discover Global Network/Diners Club)
instead of renting Visa/Mastercard rails — but the stock's P/B has already re-rated to the 88th percentile of its
own 10-year history (b1_live_scores.csv) while trailing return on equity (9.2% TTM) still sits at or below the
9–10% cost of equity the market is pricing in. Not a rejection: credit trends are improving and capital return is
real. The condition that would move this to INCLUDE-SMALL: two more quarters of the current NCO/delinquency
improvement trend without labor-market-driven reversal, confirming ROE durably above ~11%.

## 2. Business in plain English
Capital One is a US bank holding company. Three segments: **Credit Card** (Domestic Card $259bn loans + Discover's
card book, now the largest chunk of earnings), **Consumer Banking** (auto loans ~$87bn + branch/online retail
deposits), **Commercial Banking** (CRE + C&I lending, ~$91bn loans, the smallest segment). The Discover deal added
a fourth, structurally different source of profit: network fees on the ~$190bn/quarter of volume now running over
the Discover/Diners rails, comparable to how American Express earns both issuer and network economics (source:
10-Q period 2026-06-30, filed 2026-07-28, "Business Segment Financial Performance").

## 3. Why the model likes it / is this durable
b1_live_scores.csv (as_of 2026-09-25): composite decile 10 (top decile), live_rank 38/500, driven by Value
(pct_bp/pct_ep high, P/B 1.09x vs peer median 1.56x per V1) and Quality families. **Partly durable, partly an
artefact.** Durable: the vertically-integrated network is a structural, one-time change to the earnings model, not
a cyclical peak. Artefact/noise: 2025 GAAP earnings were badly distorted by a one-time acquisition-accounting charge
(see §4) — a screen reading "cheap on trailing P/E" off 2025 numbers would be reading noise, not signal. The triage
red flag ("the data card's reported revenue-growth figure (11.11) appears to be a data anomaly") is confirmed as a
symptom of this same distortion, not a separate data error.

## 5. Guidance track record
Capital One does not issue formal quarterly EPS/revenue guidance (typical for large banks). The closest analogue —
Discover-deal cost/revenue synergy targets — were **not found stated anywhere in the Q2 2026 10-Q** (searched; no
"synerg*" hits), so no synergy-delivery track record can be verified from the filing; this is a **data gap**, not a
finding of a broken promise, and is flagged as a limitation.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (consolidated, GAAP; entity = Capital One Financial Corporation)
- **Capital (Basel III standardized approach, consolidated):** CET1 ratio **13.7%** at 2026-06-30 vs **14.3%** at
  2025-12-31 (10-Q, "Capital Management") — comfortably above the 4.5% regulatory minimum plus buffers; the decline
  reflects capital return, not stress.
- **Capital return:** Q2 2026 — $501m common dividends paid + **$2.7bn** share buybacks; H1 2026 — $1.0bn dividends +
  **$5.2bn** buybacks (10-Q Executive Summary).
- **Credit quality, total company (10-Q "Credit Risk Profile"):** total loans held for investment $448.5bn (+$98bn
  YoY, Discover addition). Net charge-off rate 3.23% in Q2 2026, down 1bp YoY. Allowance coverage ratio 5.02% of
  loans HFI, down 14bps since 2025-12-31 "primarily driven by favorable credit per[formance]."
- **Credit Card segment (the core driver):** total credit card loans $271.2bn (avg), NCO rate **4.71%**, down 49bps
  YoY and 72bps QoQ; 30+ day delinquency 3.37%, down 56bps YoY; allowance coverage 7.02%, down 16bps. Segment net
  income $2.411bn in Q2 2026 vs a **$(4.917)bn loss** in Q2 2025 (same Day-2 CECL mechanic as above).
- **Consumer Banking (auto):** NCO rate **1.48%**, *up* 18bps YoY (1.30%→1.48%) — a real, if small, deterioration
  consistent with the triage flag on "near-prime consumer-credit exposure in a slowing labor market."
- **Commercial Banking:** NCO rate **0.53%**, up 20bps YoY on a small base ($91bn loans); segment net income down
  15% YoY ($239m vs $280m) — modest but real weakening, worth tracking, not yet a red flag on its own.
- **Total assets:** $682.1bn (2026-06-30) vs $572.4bn a year earlier — Discover-driven growth.
- **FCF-equivalent:** not a clean concept for a card issuer/bank; net income and capital-return capacity (above) are
  used as the distributable-earnings proxy.

## 7. Valuation snapshot & reverse DCF
V1 valuation (v1_valuation_table.csv, as of 2026-09-25): P/B **1.09x** vs peer median 1.56x (COF trades at a
discount in absolute terms) but at the **87.6th percentile of its own 10-year P/B history** (i.e., rich vs its own
past even though cheap vs peers today — a bank that historically traded at an even bigger discount). V1's
excess-return model backs out an implied required ROE of **10.78%** against a cost of equity of **10.12%**, versus
**9.24% delivered actual (TTM) ROE** — this is why V1 tags the name **"excessive"** (verdict_score −0.52).
**My own reverse DCF (excess-return / justified-P/B framework):** using the same COE (~10.1%), the market is pricing
in a sustainable forward ROE of roughly 10.5–11%. Recent quarterly runs are inconsistent but trending the right way:
Q1 2026 annualized ROE ≈ 7.7%, Q2 2026 annualized ROE ≈ 10.6% (Q2 NI $3.02bn / avg. equity ~$113bn), with NCO and
delinquency rates improving each of the last two quarters. **I agree directionally with V1 but weight the P/B-vs-
own-history percentile more heavily**: even if ROE reaches ~11% (achievable if Discover network economics and
credit normalization both continue), the stock has already re-rated most of the way there, capping upside. Implied
growth is judged **in line** with my base case, not below it — the valuation reconciliation in §1 explains why this
is WATCH rather than INCLUDE-SMALL despite improving fundamentals.
**Scenario returns (3yr annualized, my own build):**
- Bear (labor-market-driven credit cycle turn; card/auto NCO rates rise back toward 6%/2%; ROE compresses to ~7%;
  P/B mean-reverts toward 0.85x): **−8%/yr**
- Base (credit normalization continues at the current pace; ROE settles ~10.5–11%; P/B roughly flat near current
  multiple given it is already at a rich own-history percentile): **+5%/yr**
- Bull (Discover network revenue synergies materialize, cost synergies land, ROE reaches 12–13%, buybacks continue
  at pace, P/B re-rates toward peer median 1.3–1.4x): **+15%/yr**

## 8. Bull case
1. Vertically integrated card network (Discover Global Network + Diners Club) is a structural earnings-model
   upgrade that Amex-style closed-loop peers have historically monetized well; purchase volume +26% YoY and Global
   Payment Network volume +156% YoY (Q2 2026, 10-Q) show real scale already flowing through.
2. Credit quality is improving, not deteriorating, in the core Credit Card segment (NCO rate down 49bps YoY,
   delinquencies down 56bps YoY) even as the loan book grew nearly 50% with Discover.
3. Aggressive, sustained capital return ($2.7bn buybacks in Q2 2026 alone) at a P/B below peer median, funded from a
   CET1 ratio (13.7%) still well above regulatory minimums.

## 9. Bear case
1. Trailing actual ROE (9.24%) still sits below the ~10.1% cost of equity the market requires, and the stock's P/B
   is already at the 88th percentile of its own 10-year range — most of the "normalization" good news may already
   be priced in.
2. Auto-loan and small-commercial-book NCO rates are both *rising* (+18bps and +20bps YoY respectively), a leading
   indicator consistent with the triage's flagged "near-prime consumer exposure in a slowing labor market" —
   Domestic Card is improving, but not every book is.
3. $1.625bn of combined disclosed litigation reserves (below) sit against a still-integrating balance sheet; a
   Discover-integration misstep (systems, compliance, or the CFPB order below) is a real, underwritten-for risk
   that has not yet been tested through a full credit cycle under the combined entity.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable triggers)
1. Credit Card segment net charge-off rate rises above **5.5%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. 4.71% today).
2. CET1 ratio (Basel III standardized, consolidated) falls below **12.0%** in any quarter without a stated,
   credible capital-plan explanation (vs. 13.7% today).
3. Consumer Banking (auto) NCO rate exceeds **2.0%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. 1.48% today).
4. Any new, material CFPB or DOJ enforcement action against the combined entity (distinct from the two inherited,
   already-reserved matters in §12).
5. Quarterly buyback pace falls below $1bn for two consecutive quarters with no stated reason (a signal management
   itself sees less value or needs the capital), reversing the current $2.7bn/quarter run-rate.

## 12. Red-flag scan
- **Litigation — Discover Card Product Misclassification (10-Q Note 14, inherited from Discover):** on 2023-07-19
  Discover disclosed it had misclassified certain credit cards into its highest merchant/acquirer pricing tier.
  Discover and DFS Services entered a Federal Reserve consent order (2025-04-18, civil money penalty paid pre-close)
  and separately **reserved $1.2 billion** for a merchant class-action settlement; a related shareholder securities
  class action is also pending. This liability was known and priced into the acquisition; it is disclosed, reserved
  and ongoing, not a new surprise.
- **Litigation — CFPB Order on inherited private student-loan servicing (10-Q Note 14):** the CFPB order remains in
  effect **until December 2030** for issues predating the sale/transfer of that servicing — a long-tailed but
  bounded, already-known compliance obligation.
- **Litigation — Savings Account Litigation (Capital One's own, E.D. Va., filed 2023-07-10):** 360 Performance
  Savings customers allege breach of contract for not being offered a new, higher-rate product. An initial
  settlement was **rejected by the court in November 2025** after 17 state attorneys general filed an amicus brief
  opposing it; a new settlement (**$425 million** fund) was reached in December 2025 and given preliminary approval
  2026-06-09 — **not yet final.**
- **No material change to risk factors** since the FY2025 10-K per Part II Item 1A of the Q2 2026 10-Q. No auditor
  change, restatement or going-concern language found. Insider Form 4 pattern and short-seller reports not checked
  in the time available (disclosed limitation).
- **Data conflict vs triage/b1:** triage flagged an apparent data anomaly in a reported "11.11" revenue-growth
  figure; this dossier attributes it to the Q2 2025 GAAP loss distorting YoY comparisons, not a separate data error.

# ALB — Albemarle Corporation

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — thesis horizon 24–36 months. Named reservation: ALB is a single-commodity
(lithium) price bet wrapped in a chemicals company; its own disclosed FY2026 sensitivity table shows adjusted EBITDA
ranging **4.5x** between a repeat of the 2025 price trough (~$0.95bn) and a return to the 2021–2025 average price
(~$4.3bn). The balance sheet is now genuinely strong (net debt/adjusted EBITDA ≈0.5x post-divestitures) and the
recovery already under way is real and primary-source-verified, not merely a favorable comp — but position size
should reflect that this is a commodity-price call, not a diversified-quality call.

## 2. Business in plain English

Albemarle mines and processes lithium (from Chile brine and Australian hard-rock spodumene, largely through the
Talison joint venture) into lithium carbonate/hydroxide sold mostly under long-term contracts to battery and EV
makers (Energy Storage segment, ~75% of FY26 sales), plus a smaller, steadier Specialties segment (bromine — flame
retardants, oilfield fluids — and lithium specialties for pharma/electronics). It makes money on the spread between
its (largely fixed, mining-cost-based) production cost and the market price of lithium, so profits are extremely
levered to a single commodity price that has swung from ~$70+/kg (2022 peak) to ~$10/kg (2025 trough) and back to
~$20/kg (2026). Its moat is scale, low-cost brine/hard-rock resource ownership and long-term customer contracts
(~40% of Energy Storage salts volume), not pricing power over the commodity itself.

**Momentum (fam_M≈0.81)** and **Earnings momentum/SUE (fam_S≈0.61)** are the strongest drivers — both real: the

lithium settles over the next 1–3 years — a price the company itself declines to forecast (its own guidance is a
three-scenario sensitivity table, not a point estimate, §5). The quant score cannot distinguish "durable" recovery
from "cyclical bounce"; only the commodity-price view can.

## 5. Guidance track record: a scenario table, not point guidance (state so explicitly)

Albemarle does **not** issue traditional next-quarter/next-year point guidance; every quarterly release instead
publishes a **full-year sensitivity table keyed to three observed lithium-price scenarios** — this is disclosed
explicitly and is, if anything, a more honest practice than false-precision point guidance:

| Release | FY2026E scenario (avg. lithium $/kg LCE) | Net sales | Adjusted EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 2026 (2026-05-06) | ~$10 / ~$20 / ~$30 | $4.1–4.3bn / $5.7–6.0bn / $7.5–7.8bn | $0.9–1.0bn / $2.4–2.6bn / $4.2–4.4bn |
| **Q2 2026 (2026-08-05)** | ~$10 / ~$20 / ~$30 (**unchanged**) | $4.1–4.3bn / $5.7–6.0bn / $7.5–7.8bn | $0.9–1.0bn / $2.4–2.6bn / $4.2–4.4bn |

Total-corporate scenario framework was **maintained, not raised**, Q1→Q2 2026 — but within it, **Specialties segment
guidance was raised** (net sales to $1.4–1.6bn, adjusted EBITDA to $275–325m, "due to stronger-than-expected pricing
and volume performance") and **FY26 capex guidance was cut** to ~$500m (from a higher prior figure) "due to ongoing
capital efficiency improvements" — a real, if modest, positive revision, correctly distinguished here from "guidance
raised" (avoiding the v3-audit error of asserting a raise without checking the prior range).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

**Balance sheet is the single best fact in this dossier:** as of 30 Jun 2026, total debt **$1.9bn**, cash **$1.6bn**,
liquidity ~$3.2bn (incl. $1.5bn undrawn revolver), **net debt/adjusted EBITDA ≈0.5x** (company's own credit-agreement
definition) — a dramatic improvement funded by the Ketjen/Eurecat divestitures and the earnings recovery. Q2 2026
operating cash flow $710m, free cash flow $638m (83% OCF conversion — company flags this was boosted by Talison JV
dividend timing and non-recurring working-capital benefits, i.e. **not fully repeatable at this conversion rate**).
Preferred stock: 7.25% Series A Mandatory Convertible Preferred (NYSE: ALB PR A) remains outstanding — a real, fixed
dividend obligation and a source of common-share dilution on conversion (V1's diluted share count of 136.2m vs the
raw 118.0m shares outstanding reflects this if-converted treatment). GAAP-vs-adjusted gap is significant in the
recovery quarters (Q2 2026 adjusted EPS $3.75 vs GAAP $3.52 — an 6% adjustment, modest and disclosed) but was much
wider in the loss quarters (large non-cash impairment charges drove GAAP losses far below adjusted losses in
2024–2025) — appropriately flagged, not glossed over.

## 7. Valuation snapshot — V1 says EXCESSIVE; I partially disagree with the input, not the caution

**V1 (`v1_valuation_table.csv`/`v1_valuation.json`):** NTM P/E 9.65x, own-history percentile **7th** (i.e. ALB has
traded *above* today's multiple in ~93% of the last 152 months — nominally cheap), peer median 17.7x, but V1's
reverse-DCF implies **33.4% revenue growth needed for 10 years** to justify the price at a 10.53% WACC → **verdict:
excessive**, base 3-yr annualised return **−1.2%**, bear −40.6%, bull +77.8%.

**Where I think the specific input is wrong:** V1's reverse DCF uses a **TTM EBIT margin of 4.27%** (`ebit_margin_used`
in `v1_valuation.json`) as its forward base. That TTM window spans Q3 2025 through Q2 2026 and is still dragging in
the Q3 2025 operating loss (−$217.0m) — it has not caught up to the current run rate. **Primary-source correction:**
Q2 2026 alone had a GAAP operating margin of 452.9/1,743.3 = **26.0%**; Q1 2026 was 233.5/1,428.7 = **16.3%**. Holding
a 4.27% margin fixed and asking for 33% revenue growth to reach the same target EV mechanically produces an
extreme, near-impossible growth requirement — it is an artefact of the stale margin input, not evidence that the
market is pricing in something irrational.

**My own reconciliation, built from the company's own FY26 sensitivity table (§5) rather than a margin-fade DCF:**
applying peer-appropriate EV/EBITDA multiples (8x bear / 7x base / 9x bull) to the company's own $0.95bn / $2.5bn /
$4.3bn EBITDA scenarios, net of ~$0.3bn net debt, on 136.2m diluted shares: bear ≈$54/share, base ≈$126/share, bull
≈$282/share — against the $109.73 close. **This reads closer to FAIR than EXCESSIVE**, and the implied growth in the
current price looks roughly **in line** with the company's own already-realized ~$20/kg run rate, not a bet on a
return to the $30/kg 2021–2025 average. `valuation_view_vs_v1.consistent = false`; both analyses agree on the
*direction and magnitude of the risk* (a return to $10/kg trough pricing would still be a severe outcome) even
though they disagree on the *point estimate*.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case

**Bull:** (1) lithium demand (EV + grid storage) structurally grows faster than new supply can be brought online,
sustaining or improving on the $19.53/kg realized in Q2 2026; (2) balance sheet is now clean enough (0.5x net
debt/EBITDA) to fund growth capex or return capital without re-leveraging; (3) portfolio simplification (Ketjen/
Eurecat exits) focuses management and capital entirely on the highest-return lithium/bromine franchises.

**Bear:** (1) lithium is a genuinely volatile, oversupplied-prone commodity (Chinese lepidolite/African supply can
swing on marginal-cost economics) — a return to the ~$10/kg 2025 average would cut EBITDA ~65% from the Q2 2026 run
rate; (2) ~40% of Energy Storage salts volume is on long-term contracts, meaning realized pricing can lag or lead
spot in either direction, adding a layer of estimation risk beyond spot price alone; (3) the mandatory convertible
preferred is a real, non-trivial claim on cash flow and a dilution overhang not fully reflected in headline EPS.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. **Realized Energy Storage price falls back below $15/kg LCE for two consecutive quarters** (vs $19.53/kg in Q2
   2026) — the clearest sign the recovery is reversing.
2. **Net debt/adjusted EBITDA rises above 1.5x** (vs ≈0.5x at 30 Jun 2026) — would signal renewed balance-sheet stress.
3. **Two consecutive quarters of negative adjusted EBITDA** in the Energy Storage segment.
4. **FY2026 total-corporate scenario table is cut below the "~$10/kg" case** (i.e., management signals a worse floor
   than the 2025 trough) at any quarterly release.
5. **Any further material impairment (Item 2.06 8-K) outside the already-disclosed Ketjen/Eurecat divestitures.**

- **Material impairment, Item 2.06 8-K (filed 2025-10-27):** tied to the Ketjen catalysts-business sale agreement —
  explains the outsized Q4 2025 GAAP net loss (−$414.2m) relative to the operating trend; **disclosed and completed**
  (sale closed 2026-03-02 for ~$547m), not an open/unresolved item.
- **Item 2.01 8-K (filed 2026-03-06):** confirms Ketjen sale completion and the concurrent redemption of the 4.650%
  Senior Notes due 2027 — a deleveraging use of proceeds, not a red flag.
- No going-concern language, no restatement, no auditor change identified in the reviewed filings.
- **Single-commodity concentration** (lithium) is itself the dominant risk factor for this name — see §7/§9.

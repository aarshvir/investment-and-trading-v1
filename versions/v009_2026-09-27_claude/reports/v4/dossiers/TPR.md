## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE (full weight).** Thesis horizon 12–24 months. Coach is executing at a level rarely seen in mature accessories brands (four straight quarters of 20%+ growth with margin expansion), guidance was raised at every one of the last four releases, and the balance sheet has been fully repaired since the failed Capri deal. The risks are real but bounded: Kate Spade remains a genuine drag, FY2027 guidance itself concedes a sharp deceleration to mid-single-digit growth, and the stock has already re-rated ~4.6x from its 2023 low — this is a "keep riding a proven winner, size for the deceleration already guided" call, not a deep-value entry.

## 2. Business in plain English
Tapestry is a "house of brands" in premium/accessible-luxury handbags and accessories: **Coach** (86% of FY26 revenue, the growth engine), **Kate Spade** (~13%, declining), and (until divested 4-Aug-2025) **Stuart Weitzman** footwear. It sells through its own stores/e-commerce (direct-to-consumer) and wholesale/department-store distribution, with Greater China and North America the two largest and fastest-growing geographies. Revenue is booked as principal (1P) — the company owns the inventory it sells even through department-store concessions — so standard margin and revenue metrics are directly comparable across periods without the marketplace-style distortions the sector playbook warns about.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Prelim rank: **Q 0.936, V 0.683, M 0.628, composite 0.749.** Independently verified: FY26 GAAP operating margin 23.9% (vs 5.9% in FY25 — see §6 for why FY25 was depressed), FY26 non-GAAP operating margin 23.4% (+340bps YoY on a clean basis), FY26 diluted EPS GAAP $7.27 / non-GAAP $7.05. **The Quality read is durable at the Coach-brand level** — mid-teens AUR (average unit retail) growth alongside mid-teens unit growth is genuine pricing power plus volume, not just price-led inflation, and it is broad-based (every FY26 quarter posted double-digit Coach growth, in every major region). It is **not durable at the consolidated level** in the sense that ~all of the growth and margin expansion is a single-brand (Coach) story riding on top of a still-shrinking Kate Spade (FY26 revenue −10%, following an **$854.8m** Kate Spade brand-intangible/goodwill impairment taken in FY2025). The Momentum score (0.628) is well earned — TPR has raised guidance in each of the last four quarters — but momentum factors are backward-looking by construction and the FY2027 initial guide explicitly signals the pace won't continue.

SBC $104.5m (~1.3% of revenue), unremarkable. **GAAP-vs-adjusted gap is unusually structured** (see §4 note) — driven by net *favorable* one-offs (IEEPA tariff refunds) rather than the more common pattern of GAAP being dragged down by charges; non-GAAP ($7.05) is the more conservative, recurring-earnings figure and is the one to use for a multiple. **Balance sheet fully normalized post-Capri:** long-term debt is $2,396.6m (Q4 FY26), down from $7,071.5–7,422.0m in the quarters when ~$6.3bn of escrowed acquisition notes (issued Feb-2024 to fund the never-completed Capri deal) sat on the balance sheet; cash fell correspondingly from a ~$6.9–7.0bn peak to $974.7m as those notes were redeemed after the deal's termination. **Capri deal aftermath, quantified:** Tapestry paid Capri a **$45.1m** expense-reimbursement fee under the Nov-13-2024 Termination Agreement (sourced from secondary legal/news coverage — Skadden, Clifford Chance, UPI — the primary termination 8-K returned persistent SEC EDGAR 503 errors this session and could not be independently re-verified; flagged Tier-4). ## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Coach's mid-teens AUR growth *and* mid-teens unit growth together, sustained for four straight quarters across every region, is a rare, high-quality signal that pricing power is real, not just inflation pass-through. (2) Balance sheet is fully repaired (net debt trivial relative to ~$1.8bn FCF) and capital returns are large and growing (dividend +16%, buyback >100% of FCF planned for FY27). (3) Guidance discipline has been excellent — four consecutive raises followed by a beat, with tariffs fully quantified and apparently anniversarized for FY27.
**Bear:** (1) Kate Spade is not a "hold at breakeven" problem — it lost money in FY26 (non-GAAP Q4 operating loss $28.9m, FY $27.2m) after an $854.8m impairment the year before; if Coach ever slows, there is no second engine. (2) FY2027 guidance itself concedes the growth rate falls from >20% to mid-single-digits — the multiple re-rating this stock has enjoyed assumed continuation of a pace management says is ending. (3) The stock's own re-rating (4.6x off the 2023 low) plus 8% short interest suggests a crowded "already worked" trade with an asymmetric downside if Q1 FY27 (due ~Nov 5, 2026) merely meets rather than beats the newly modest guide.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Coach quarterly growth falls below 10% for two consecutive quarters (from the mid-20s/30s currently).
2. Kate Spade revenue decline steepens past −15% YoY, or the brand posts a further impairment.
3. Non-GAAP operating margin gives back more than 200bps YoY in any quarter (FY26 exit rate: 19.3% adj. Q4).
4. FY2027 guidance is cut at any point during the year (would break the four-quarter raise streak).
5. Refiling or revival of the Capri-related securities litigation, or any new SEC inquiry tied to the terminated merger's disclosures.

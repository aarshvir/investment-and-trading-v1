# Mastercard Incorporated (MA) — Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F54 | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close ($567.65) | **Depth:** Standard

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** (24–36 month horizon). A duopoly card network with structural double-digit revenue growth,
46%+ operating margins and very low leverage, priced (25.6x NTM P/E, per `d4_live_snapshot.parquet`) for
roughly 7%/yr long-run FCF-per-share growth in a reverse DCF — well below both consensus (+15.5% FY1 EPS
growth) and the company's own recent run-rate (mid-teens revenue growth, high-teens EPS growth). Litigation
and a pending stablecoin acquisition are real but sized well inside the balance sheet.

## 2. Business in plain English
Mastercard runs the payment "rails" that connect banks, merchants and consumers — it authorizes, clears and
settles card transactions and takes a small fee on the dollar volume and transaction count that flows across
its network (Payment network segment), plus a fast-growing Value-Added Services and Solutions (VAS) segment
(cyber/fraud, data & insights, loyalty, open banking). It does not lend money or carry credit risk — issuing
banks do that — so it is a toll-road business on global consumer and B2B spending, competing mainly with Visa
(global duopoly) and, in specific corridors, local schemes and real-time payment rails.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Triage (Q06) flagged 25.6x NTM P/E against +27% forward EPS growth (a stale/rounded figure) and called it
"fair, not excessive... in a duopoly." Confirmed and refined with primary data: `b1_live_scores.csv`
(2026-09-25 snapshot) shows ROE 289.7% (pct_roe 0.983) — an artefact of a thin, buyback-shrunk equity base,
not a red flag; the more meaningful gross-profit/assets score (pct_gp_a 0.994) and 42.7% operating cash
flow/assets rank are consistent with a genuinely high-quality, asset-light network. `d4_live_snapshot.parquet`
shows FY1 consensus EPS growth +15.5%, revenue growth +12.6%, NTM P/E 25.6x, FCF yield 3.4% — all consistent
with the SEC filings below. This is a durable structural growth story (secular cash-to-card and cross-border
e-commerce shift), not a one-off.

TTM FCF/share from `d4` FCF $16.96bn ÷ ~876M shares; cost of equity 7.9% from CAPM with beta 0.735, rf 4.2%,
ERP 5%; 10-year explicit growth then a 22x terminal FCF multiple, roughly the market's own long-run average
quality multiple, i.e. a *conservative*, mean-reverting exit assumption below MA's current 29x trailing
P/FCF) implies the market is pricing in **≈6.8%/yr** FCF-per-share growth for 10 years. That is well below
both sell-side consensus (FY1 EPS growth +15.5%, `d4`) and MA's realized 2024–26 run-rate (revenue +14–18%
YoY, EPS +13–22% YoY, per §4). **This is priced below the base case — a genuine margin of safety, not a
"growth priced for perfection" story.** Sensitivity check: using the Gordon-growth (perpetuity) form instead
of an exit multiple, the implied growth is more WACC-sensitive (ranges ~4–8% for WACC 7.5–8.5%) but never
above consensus growth under any reasonable assumption set tried.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) VAS (cyber/fraud, data & insights) is growing faster than the payment network (+20% YoY 2Q26
vs +10% for payment network) and carries structurally higher incremental margin as it scales; (2) cross-
border volume growth (+12% ccy-neutral, 2Q26) keeps outrunning domestic volume, and cross-border take-rates
are several multiples of domestic; (3) BVNK gives MA an early, owned position in stablecoin settlement rails
if that channel disintermediates card rails over the next decade — optionality, not yet priced.
**Bear:** (1) the April-2026 U.S. merchant class action seeking to void the scope of the 2023 Damages Class
Settlement release could reopen interchange-fee claims dating to January 2019 at much larger scale than the
$296M currently reserved (see §11); (2) rebates/incentives to customers grew faster than gross payment-
network revenue in 2Q26 (+22% vs +10%), a mix shift that could compress net take-rate if it continues;
(3) regulatory interchange caps (EU, and periodic U.S. state/federal proposals) remain a standing structural
risk to the core payment-network economics that no dossier section can fully price.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Net revenue growth falls below 8% YoY (reported) for two consecutive quarters.
2. Adjusted operating margin compresses more than 300 bps YoY for two consecutive quarters.
3. The BVNK acquisition fails to close by 2026-12-31, or closes with a materially adverse regulatory
   condition disclosed in an 8-K.
4. A U.S. court ruling narrows or voids the Damages Class Settlement release (the April-2026 merchant suit),
   with an accrued or disclosed potential loss exceeding $1bn.
5. Consolidated net debt/TTM EBITDA exceeds 1.5x (from ≈0.6x currently) without a corresponding buyback
   pause disclosed by management.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **Litigation (entity: consolidated, U.S. Merchant class action, filed April 2026):** a putative class
  action on behalf of U.S. merchants seeks damages for interchange fees on Mastercard and Visa transactions
  since January 2019, and separately moved for summary judgment asserting the 2023 Damages Class Settlement
  release does not bar these claims — i.e. this is a fight over the *scope* of an already-settled case, not a
  new liability theory (10-Q Note on Legal Proceedings, filed 2026-07-30).
- **Litigation (entity: consolidated, EU/Portugal, ongoing):** a Portuguese consumer class action seeking
  ≈€0.4bn (≈$0.5bn as of 2026-06-30) leveraging the 2019 European Commission resolution on interregional
  interchange fees; Mastercard has filed a defense disputing the claims (same 10-Q note).
- **Accrued litigation reserve** (consolidated balance sheet) fell from $800M (2025-12-31) to $296M
  (2026-06-30) — a large reduction suggesting a settlement/payment occurred in 1H26; the specific matter
  resolved was not identified in the text reviewed (data conflict — flagged, not resolved, in this dossier).
- **Long-term debt** (consolidated) rose $3.9bn in six months (2025-12-31 to 2026-06-30) — consistent with
  new note issuance, likely partly to fund the BVNK deal and buybacks; specific new issuances were not named
  in the 10-Q text extracted (open item).
- No auditor changes, material weakness, going-concern language, or restatement language found in the
  10-Q reviewed.

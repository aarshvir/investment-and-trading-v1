# NTAP — NetApp, Inc.

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — genuine business acceleration, but at $201.15 (25 Sep 2026 close) the stock already prices in a decade of growth well above anything NetApp has delivered historically. Horizon: 12–24 months (re-underwrite once FY2027 durability is confirmed or denied by 2–3 more quarters).

## 2. Business in plain English
NetApp sells data-storage hardware and software (mostly all-flash arrays under the ONTAP/ASA brands) plus a smaller public-cloud storage-as-a-service business, to enterprises and hyperscalers. It makes money selling systems and support/software attach, increasingly subscription-like via Keystone/cloud. Competitive position: a top-3 branded enterprise storage vendor (with Dell, HPE, Pure Storage, Hitachi) in a market that is technically mature/commoditizing but is currently being re-rated by AI-training/inference data-pipeline demand.

## 3. Why the model likes it / is it durable?
Quant factors like the earnings-momentum (SUE) and improving quality trend. This is a real, not artefactual, signal this quarter: record all-flash array revenue ($1.3B, +47% YoY) and record Public Cloud revenue ($206M, +28% YoY) in Q1 FY2027, plus the DataPelago acquisition for AI data-pipeline infrastructure. The open question is durability — NetApp's own 5-year and 10-year revenue CAGR are 5.2% and 2.9% (V1/D3), so a 30% YoY quarter is a large outlier versus the company's history, not a new steady state confirmed by multiple years of data.

## 5. Guidance track record
- **Initial FY2027 guide** (given with Q4 FY26 results, 28 May 2026): revenue $7.325B–$7.575B; GAAP EPS $6.51–$6.81; non-GAAP EPS $8.70–$9.00. Q1 FY27 guide at the time: revenue $1.750B–$1.900B.
- **Q1 FY27 actual** (2 Sep 2026): revenue $2.025B — above the *top* of the prior guide range; GAAP EPS $1.88 and non-GAAP EPS $2.58, both above the prior range.
- **New FY2027 guide** (raised, 2 Sep 2026): revenue $7.975B–$8.225B (+~$650M / +~9% at the midpoint vs initial); GAAP EPS $7.35–$7.65; non-GAAP EPS $9.73–$10.03 (+~12% at the midpoint). New Q2 FY27 guide: revenue $2.025B–$2.175B.
This is one of the largest single-quarter guidance raises in NetApp's recent history — a real, not sandbagged, upward revision. Sources: NetApp 8-K Ex-99.1 filings, 28 May 2026 and 2 Sep 2026 (SEC EDGAR).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF conversion:** Q1 FY27 OCF $503M − capex $102M = FCF $401M vs GAAP NI $375M → 107% conversion. FY2026 (company-reported) FCF was $1,869M on NI of ~$1.28B-scale — strong, consistent conversion; the Q4 FY26 quarterly FCF I derive from XBRL ($900M) matches NetApp's own disclosed "$900 million" free cash flow figure for that quarter, a good cross-check.
- **SBC:** ~5.4% of TTM revenue (V1: SBC $397M / TTM revenue $7,391M).
- **Balance sheet (30 Sep quarter, i.e. 31 Jul 2026):** cash $1,573M + short-term investments $2,003M = $3,576M liquid, vs long-term debt $1,938M + current debt $550M = $2,488M — **net cash position of ~$1,088M** (matches V1's net_debt of −$1,088M). This is a fortress balance sheet; stockholders' equity is thin ($1.35–1.50B) purely because of a decade of buybacks reducing paid-in capital, not distress.
- **Capital return:** Q1 FY27 — $200M buyback + $102M dividends; next dividend $0.52/share, 28 Oct 2026.
- **Share count:** trending down via buybacks (consistent with V1's assumed −3.7% to −7.3% share-count CAGR in its scenarios).

## 7. Valuation snapshot — reconciled with V1
V1 (`v1_valuation_table.csv` / `.json`, as of 25 Sep 2026 close): **NTM P/E 19.23x**, in line with peer median 19.59x (Dell, Pure Storage, HPE, HPQ, Apple) and at the **39th percentile of NTAP's own 173-month history** — i.e., NOT rich on a simple multiple basis. But V1's reverse DCF (FCFF, WACC 9.40%, 10-year fade to 3% terminal growth, TTM revenue base already includes the Q1 FY27 blowout quarter) says the price implies **15.28% revenue growth compounded for 10 years**, versus NetApp's own delivered 5-year CAGR of 5.17%, 10-year CAGR of 2.91%, and Street consensus FY1 growth of only 5.80%. V1's base case (revenue growth fading from ~2.5% initial, margin ~11.5%) gives a **3-year annualised return of −17.2%**; bear −38.3%; bull +12.5%. V1 verdict: **"excessive."**
**I agree with V1's verdict**, with one qualification: the reason the stock trades at only a peer-median multiple despite this quarter's 30% growth print is precisely because the market (correctly, in my view) is not underwriting 30% growth for a decade — but it is still pricing in high-single/low-double-digit durable growth, more than the 5–6% NetApp has shown it can sustainably deliver in a competitive, commoditizing storage market. The bull case (AI-storage becomes a multi-year structural upgrade cycle, not one to two quarters of pent-up refresh demand) is plausible but unproven — one quarter is not a trend.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) All-flash + AI data-pipeline demand (DataPelago, StorageGRID 12.1) is a genuine multi-year hyperscaler/enterprise refresh cycle, not a one-off; (2) Public Cloud (+28% YoY) scales into a real recurring-revenue engine; (3) fortress net-cash balance sheet funds continued buybacks regardless of the cycle.
**Bear:** (1) Storage is structurally competitive (Dell, HPE, Pure Storage, hyperscaler-native storage) — pricing power is not durable; (2) NetApp's own 10-year CAGR of 2.9% is the more representative base rate than one blowout quarter; (3) even after a huge raise, FY2027 guided growth (~15–19% at the new range vs FY26's $6.925B) still falls well short of the 10-year 15.3% the current price requires *every year for a decade*.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. All-flash array revenue growth decelerates below 20% YoY for two consecutive quarters.
2. Consolidated revenue growth falls back below 10% YoY for two consecutive quarters.
3. GAAP gross margin falls below 68% for two consecutive quarters (pricing pressure).
4. Any FY2027 guidance cut from the raised (2 Sep 2026) range.
5. Public Cloud segment revenue growth falls below 15% YoY for two consecutive quarters.

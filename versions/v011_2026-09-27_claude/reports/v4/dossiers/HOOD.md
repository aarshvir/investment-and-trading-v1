# HOOD — Robinhood Markets, Inc. (Diligence agent F95, wave 6)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — 12–36 month thesis horizon. Robinhood's core brokerage franchise is genuinely improving (net income up ~46% YoY in Q2 2026, options/crypto/prediction-market diversification working), but the stock (45.6x NTM P/E) already prices in roughly two more decades of AI-fintech-grade growth, and two specific, dated regulatory developments — a March 2026 U.S. Senate bill to ban prediction-market event contracts and an Illinois transaction tax effective July 2026 — sit directly against the fastest-growing part of the revenue mix. This is a good business at a price with limited room for a regulatory setback.

## 2. Business in plain English
Robinhood Markets is a retail-focused financial-services holding company (parent "RHM") whose regulated subsidiaries operate a commission-free brokerage (equities, options, futures), a crypto exchange, a "prediction markets" event-contracts platform (via joint venture Rothera, a CFTC-licensed designated contract market/derivatives clearing organization/swap execution facility), margin lending, a "Gold" subscription and a nascent international wealth-management push. It earns money mainly from payment-for-order-flow-style transaction rebates, net interest income on customer cash/margin balances, and subscription fees. Competitive position: the largest U.S. "app-first" retail brokerage by younger-demographic engagement, now competing directly with Interactive Brokers, Coinbase and traditional wealth managers as it expands into new products.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
b1_live_scores composite is modest (0.013, live_rank 490 — Financials sector is not where the quant model ranks HOOD highly), but earnings-momentum and growth factors are strong (revenue +30–50% YoY per triage), and the triage data (Q05) explicitly flagged this as a name worth full diligence despite a rich 45.6x forward P/E. The revenue growth is real and filed (Q2 2026 revenue $1,308m vs $989m Q2 2025, +32.3%), but the **mix is shifting**: crypto trading revenue fell 38% YoY in Q2 2026 as crypto markets cooled (triage red flag, corroborated by the 10-Q MD&A: "cryptocurrency revenues decreased primarily due to lower cryptocurrency rebate rates... a 16% and 24% decrease in the number of users placing crypto trades"), while event-contract (prediction-market) revenue is what's accelerating. Durability therefore depends heavily on a product line that is under live legislative and tax threat (Section 6/9 below) — this is a genuine, not artefactual, risk to the growth durability.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Entity scope — important distinction:** the program's automated market-data feed (Yahoo, via d4_live_snapshot) shows `totalDebt` ≈ **$22.9bn** and `totalCash` ≈ **$23.9bn** for HOOD. This is **not** comparable to a normal company's funded debt: it reflects the consolidated broker-dealer's balance sheet, which is dominated by customer-related payables (securities-lending/repurchase obligations $3.78bn at Jun 30 2026, up from... payable growth as the brokerage/margin-lending book scales; total liabilities rose from $28.99bn to $47.0bn in six months, per the 10-Q balance sheet) — not corporate borrowing. **Recorded as a data_conflict below.**
- **Actual funded debt (parent, "RHM"):** on June 25 2026 Robinhood issued **$2.2bn aggregate principal 0.00% Convertible Senior Notes due 2029** (including a $200m over-allotment exercised in full), via an indenture between RHM and U.S. Bank Trust Company as trustee, with capped-call transactions to limit dilution. Carrying/fair value $2,208m at Jun 30 2026 (10-Q, Note 8/11). Cash and cash equivalents (consolidated) were $5,362m at Jun 30 2026 (up from $4,162m a year earlier). **This is the only meaningful corporate-level debt; net debt at the parent level is effectively negative (cash exceeds the convertible).**
- **Broker-dealer capital adequacy (subsidiary-level, from the Jun 30 2026 10-Q net-capital table):** Robinhood Securities (RHS) net capital $3,947m vs $504m required (excess $3,443m); Robinhood Financial (RHF) net capital $196m vs $0.25m required; Robinhood Derivatives (RHD, the FCM subsidiary, CFTC-regulated) net capital $435m vs $9m required; TradePMR net capital $13m vs $0.25m required. **All four regulated entities were in compliance with regulatory capital requirements as of June 30, 2026** — well in excess, not marginal.
- **Cash flow volatility (important caveat for the reverse DCF):** operating cash flow for a broker-dealer swings enormously with customer cash/margin/securities-lending balances, not with the underlying economics of the business — H1 2026 OCF was $2,758m, H1 2025 OCF was $4,151m, and full-year 2025 OCF was only $1,638m (implying a sharply negative H2 2025 OCF of roughly −$2.5bn). **OCF is not a reliable FCF proxy for this name**; I use net income for the reverse DCF instead (Section 7).
- **Share count:** 790m Class A + 109m Class B + a smaller Class C tranche issued and outstanding as of June 30, 2026 (10-Q cover page); ~912m diluted shares is a reasonable approximation consistent with the market-cap/price ratio.
- **No dividend.**

A 3-year scenario check: a base-case exit near the current NTM multiple lands close to the Street's own $130 mean target (~+3%/yr from here); the Street's own $57 low target implies roughly −23%/yr, which is what a prediction-markets ban plus continued crypto softness would plausibly produce.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3 points):** (1) net income has grown roughly 4x since 2024 (Q1 2024 $157m → Q2 2026 $561m) on genuine product diversification (options, international, Gold, prediction markets), not just markets-driven trading volume; (2) all four regulated subsidiaries carry regulatory capital multiples above 6x their minimum requirement (RHS: $3,947m held vs $504m required), leaving ample room to keep scaling margin lending and event-contract clearing without a capital raise; (3) the 0%-coupon convertible funds growth investment without cash-interest drag, and net cash at the parent level remains comfortably positive after the raise.

**Bear (3 points):** (1) a bill to ban prediction-market event contracts (the "Prediction Markets Are Gambling Act," introduced in the U.S. Senate March 23, 2026, per the 10-Q's own risk-factor disclosure) targets precisely the fastest-growing revenue line — passage would be a direct, quantifiable hit; (2) Illinois's new prediction-market tax (1.75%/3.5% of contract value, effective July 1, 2026, cited in the same 10-Q) is a live example of state-level revenue erosion that could spread to other states; (3) crypto-trading revenue fell 38% YoY in Q2 2026 on both lower rebate rates and a 16–24% decline in the number of users placing crypto trades — a second consecutive quarter of this trend would materially change the growth algebra the current 45.6x forward multiple assumes.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. **Federal or state legislation materially restricting or banning prediction-market event contracts is enacted** (tracking the March 2026 Senate bill and the Illinois tax precedent) — a binary, dated, easily observable trigger.
2. **Crypto-trading revenue declines YoY for a second consecutive quarter** (already −38% YoY in Q2 2026) — would confirm a structural, not one-quarter, decline in that segment.
3. **Any regulated subsidiary's excess net capital falls below 2x its required minimum** (vs RHS ~7.8x, RHF ~784x, RHD ~48x, TradePMR ~52x as of Jun 30 2026) — would signal capital strain from rapid balance-sheet growth (margin lending, securities lending).
4. **Total Platform Assets or net deposits growth decelerates below 15% YoY for two consecutive quarters** (company's own KPI, defined in the 10-Q) — a leading indicator of customer-engagement softening ahead of revenue.
5. **The $2.2bn convertible notes' conversion feature triggers material dilution disclosure, or a second convertible/equity raise occurs within 12 months** — would suggest the current cash position is not as comfortable as the Jun 30 2026 balance sheet implies.

## 11. Red-flag scan

Robinhood Markets* (Washington Equal Pay and Opportunity Act, job-posting wage-scale disclosure claims); *Dey v. Robinhood Markets* (breach of fiduciary duty, gross negligence, negligent misrepresentation claims). All are described by the company as ordinary-course or unresolved-outcome matters; no admission of liability or reserve disclosed as material in the sections reviewed.

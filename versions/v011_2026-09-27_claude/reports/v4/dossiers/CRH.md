# CRH — CRH plc

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE** — thesis horizon 24–36 months. The largest US-focused building-materials/aggregates group trades at a
statistically cheap multiple (~8.6x current EV/adjusted EBITDA, ~13–14x P/E) against a genuinely improving margin
trend (12 consecutive years of margin expansion, per management), a clean litigation record, and moderate leverage —
against a real, disclosed catalyst risk (the pending $8.5bn all-cash Arcosa acquisition, financed with committed
bridge debt) that raises leverage but is explicitly sized to preserve investment-grade credit.

## 2. Business in plain English

CRH is the largest vertically integrated building-materials company focused on the US market: it owns the quarries,
cement/aggregates plants and asphalt/ready-mix operations (Americas Materials Solutions), value-added building
products (Americas Building Solutions) and an International Solutions segment (Europe/other), selling to public
infrastructure (roads, bridges), non-residential and residential construction customers. It makes money by
vertically integrating from raw-material extraction (aggregates, cement) through to finished products and paving/
construction services, capturing margin at each step, and by consistently acquiring smaller regional players
("bolt-ons") to densify its footprint. Its moat is irreplaceable, permitted quarry/reserve locations near population
centers (aggregates are heavy/low-value-per-ton, so transport cost creates local pricing power) plus scale in
procurement and logistics.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

**Leverage is rising and is the main balance-sheet watch-item.** Total debt (segment note, FY 10-K): **$17,653M**
(FY2025) vs $13,968M (FY2024) vs $11,642M (FY2023) — up 26% and 20% in the last two years respectively, funding a
$4.1bn/38-acquisition FY2025 M&A program plus $1.7bn of growth capex. As of 30 Jun 2026 (10-Q XBRL): long-term debt
$15,410M + current portion/short-term borrowings $2,516M ≈ **$17.93bn total debt**, cash **$3.025bn**, net debt
**≈$14.9bn**. Against FY26-guided adjusted EBITDA of $8.1–8.5bn, that is **net debt/EBITDA ≈1.8x today** — moderate,
investment-grade-consistent leverage, but the trend and the pending Arcosa deal (below) both point higher. FCF
quality is strong: FY2025 adjusted FCF $5.0bn against net income $3.8bn (~132% conversion, aided by depreciation
add-back typical of a capital-intensive business) — no adjusted-vs-GAAP gap concerns identified; the FY2025 GAAP-
vs-adjusted EPS difference was small ($5.51 GAAP vs $5.57 "pre-impairment," i.e. a modest, disclosed impairment
drag). Share count ~665.3m, quarterly dividend $0.39/share (+5% YoY), active $0.3bn/quarter buyback program
alongside the acquisition spend — capital return continues even while funding M&A, a sign of balance-sheet
confidence, though also a reason leverage is rising on both fronts simultaneously.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case

**Bull:** (1) the pending **$8.5bn all-cash acquisition of Arcosa** (announced 22 Jun 2026, 11.5x 2026E EBITDA, ~35mt
of 2025 aggregates shipments plus a top-3 critical-infrastructure-products platform) reinforces CRH's #1 US
aggregates position and is management-guided to be **accretive to earnings, margin and cash flow within 12 months**
of completion, with $175m of run-rate synergies targeted by year three; (2) US infrastructure/data-center/reshoring
capex provides multi-year structural demand for aggregates and related products; (3) 12 consecutive years of margin
expansion plus continued bolt-on M&A discipline is a proven, repeatable capital-allocation model ("CRH Winning Way").

**Bear:** (1) leverage is rising on two fronts simultaneously (organic M&A pace + the debt-financed Arcosa deal,
pro forma FY2026E net debt/adjusted EBITDA guided to **2.4x** by the company itself) — a US non-residential/
infrastructure demand slowdown (e.g., federal IIJA funding lapse) would hit EBITDA and leverage at the same time;
(2) Arcosa closing is **not guaranteed** — subject to Arcosa stockholder approval and regulatory (antitrust)
clearance, expected to close only in **Q1 2027**, leaving a multi-quarter integration/execution risk window; (3) the
stock's 12-month momentum (−20.5%) shows the market is currently unwilling to pay up for this story despite the
fundamentals — a "value trap" risk if the re-rating catalyst (index inclusion, Arcosa accretion) does not show up in
the numbers on schedule.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. **Net debt/adjusted EBITDA rises above 3.0x** (vs ≈1.8x today, 2.4x pro forma guided for the Arcosa close) —
   would jeopardize the stated investment-grade commitment.
2. **Arcosa deal fails to close or closes later than Q1 2027 guidance** (stockholder vote or regulatory block) —
   a specific, dated, binary catalyst-failure trigger.
3. **FY2026 guidance (Net income $3.9–4.1bn / Adj. EBITDA $8.1–8.5bn) is cut at any remaining 2026 release** — the
   first guidance cut since the framework was initiated in Feb 2026 would be a material trend break.
4. **Adjusted EBITDA margin contracts more than 100bps YoY in any two consecutive quarters** (vs the +100bps FY2025
   expansion and the 12-year expansion streak management cites).
5. **Any quarter of negative organic (ex-M&A) revenue growth in Americas Materials Solutions**, the core aggregates
   franchise, for two consecutive quarters.

## 11. Red-flag scan

- **Item 3 Legal Proceedings (FY2025 10-K):** boilerplate — "The Company is from time to time a party to various
  legal proceedings that arise in the ordinary course of business. We do not believe any pending legal proceeding...
  will have a material effect" — **no material litigation identified**, a notably clean profile relative to typical
  large-cap peers.
- **Item 4 Mine Safety Disclosures:** routine MSHA-pattern disclosure requirement for a quarry/mine operator (Item
  1.04 8-Ks filed periodically, e.g. 2026-03-04, 2026-05-01) — standard regulatory-compliance reporting, not evidence
  of a safety problem beyond what any large aggregates operator discloses.
- No going-concern language, no restatement, no material-weakness or auditor-change items identified.
- **Rising leverage trend (§6/§8) is the closest thing to a red flag** in this filing set — real, disclosed,
  quantified, and explicitly bounded by management's own 2.4x pro forma target.

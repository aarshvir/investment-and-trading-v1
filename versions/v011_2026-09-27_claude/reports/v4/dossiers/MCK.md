# McKesson Corporation (MCK) — Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F103 | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close ($866.64, NYSE) | **Wave:** 6

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE.** Thesis horizon: 12–36 months.
One-sentence reason: the largest US drug distributor, now compounding faster via its oncology/specialty platform, trades at a reverse-DCF-implied growth rate well *below* both its own guided growth and its recent delivery, even after pricing in a major pending business separation and a real (already-reserved) opioid liability.

## 2. Business in plain English
McKesson buys pharmaceuticals in bulk from manufacturers and distributes them to pharmacies, hospitals and physician practices across the US — a low-margin, high-volume logistics business (North American Pharmaceutical, ~82% of revenue). Layered on top is a faster-growing, higher-margin **Oncology & Multispecialty** segment (specialty drug distribution plus the US Oncology Network of community cancer clinics) and a smaller Prescription Technology Solutions (data/analytics) business. It is in the process of separating its lowest-growth segment, Medical-Surgical Solutions (branded "Wellverse"), into a standalone company.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet — **entity scope stated explicitly**
- **Consolidated** operating cash flow, FY2026 (year to 2026-03-31, 10-K): $6.155B; but Q1 FY2027 (Apr–Jun 2026) alone was **negative $220M** (seasonal working-capital swing typical for this business, consistent with Q1 FY2026 also being negative $918M) — not a new problem, but a structural feature that makes single-quarter FCF unreliable as a valuation input (see §7 caveat).
- **Consolidated balance sheet, as of 2026-06-30 (10-Q filed 2026-08-05):** cash & equivalents $5.164B (up sharply from $3.975B at FY2026-end, 2026-03-31); long-term debt + capital leases (consolidated) $8.432B (up from $5.259B at FY2026-end) — **this jump is explained by a new $2.25B secured Term Loan B raised 2026-06-09 specifically to support the Medical-Surgical Solutions separation**, not by organic releveraging. **Stockholders' equity (consolidated) is negative: −$4.240B** as of 2026-06-30 (was −$2.172B at FY2026-end) — this is a known, longstanding feature of McKesson's capital structure (decades of buybacks against a low-margin, asset-light distribution balance sheet) and is not itself a solvency red flag given the cash-generative operating model, but it does make equity-based ratios (ROE, book value) meaningless here; leverage should be assessed on debt/EBITDA instead. EBITDA (TTM, d4 cross-check) ≈ $7.071B; gross debt $9.729B (LongTermDebt tag, 2026-06-30) → gross debt/EBITDA ≈ 1.4x, cash-adjusted ≈ 0.65x — **manageable**, not a red flag on its own.
- **Opioid litigation liability (consolidated, from the FY2026 10-K, period 2026-03-31):** accrued liability **$5.692B total** ($601M current + $5.091B long-term), already on the balance sheet (this is why equity is so negative) — this is a known, sized, multi-year cash-payment schedule, not an open-ended tail risk, and materially different in character from JNJ's unresolved talc exposure (§9 of the JNJ dossier).
- **Pending deal — material, in progress:** McKesson is separating its Medical-Surgical Solutions segment (~$13B implied valuation) into a standalone company ("Wellverse"), having sold Apollo Funds a 13% minority stake for $1.25B (closed 2026-06-01) and raised $2.25B in new debt (2026-06-09) to support the separation; a further IPO/spin is planned but not yet executed as of this writing. This is the direct cause of most of the FY2027 GAAP/adjusted EPS gap and the debt/cash swings noted above — **flagging this clearly is the main correction this dossier makes to the underlying quant data, which does not price in transaction complexity.**
- Share count: 116.6M shares outstanding, down from prior periods — McKesson has been an aggressive net repurchaser (consistent with the negative-equity capital structure).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Oncology & Multispecialty segment growing 33% YoY, higher-margin, structurally shifting the mix away from thin-margin pure distribution.
2. FY2027 adjusted EPS guided +13–15%, raised twice this year, with a tangible 15% dividend hike backing management's confidence.
3. Wellverse separation should crystallize value for a slower-growing, lower-multiple segment and let the remaining pharma-distribution + oncology business re-rate on its own higher growth profile.

**Bear:**
1. GAAP EPS **fell** 17.6% YoY this quarter — the "20% adjusted growth" headline hides real cash cost from the separation and NCI remeasurements; if transaction costs run longer/higher than guided, adjusted-EPS credibility erodes.
2. Negative consolidated equity and a newly-added $2.25B Term Loan B mean the balance sheet is more levered through the separation window than the pre-separation numbers suggest; execution risk on the Wellverse IPO/spin (timing, market conditions, retained stake value) is real and unhedged in the current price.
3. $5.7B of opioid liability remains on the balance sheet with multi-year cash outflows still due; a new opioid-related claim wave (e.g., from PBM or hospital classes not yet settled) would add to this.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Oncology & Multispecialty segment revenue growth falls below **15%** for two consecutive quarters (loss of the core re-rating thesis).
2. Consolidated gross debt/EBITDA rises above **2.5x** (from ~1.4x now) — would signal the separation is costing more, or taking longer, than guided.
3. FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance is **cut** (not just held) at either of the next two quarterly releases.
4. The Wellverse separation/IPO is **abandoned, delayed beyond FY2028, or priced at a material discount** to the implied $13B valuation from the Apollo transaction.
5. Cumulative opioid-related cash payments in a single year exceed **$1.5B** (materially above the historical run-rate implied by the $601M current-liability figure), signalling settlement terms are worse than currently accrued.

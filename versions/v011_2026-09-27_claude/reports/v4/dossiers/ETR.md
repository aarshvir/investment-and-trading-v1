## 1. Verdict

## 2. Business in plain English
Entergy is a regulated electric (and some gas) utility holding company serving Louisiana, Mississippi, Texas and Arkansas — the Gulf Coast/lower Mississippi corridor — through several state-regulated operating subsidiaries plus a nuclear generation fleet. It earns a regulated return on the capital ("rate base") it invests in generation, transmission and distribution, approved by each state's public service commission (PSC) and, for wholesale/transmission matters, FERC. Its current growth story is a large pipeline of hyperscale data-center and industrial customers wanting to interconnect to its grid, which — if signed and rate-based — drives above-average capex and EPS growth for a utility.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
b1 flags ETR on growth and momentum: management affirmed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of **$4.25–$4.45** with a **>8%** long-term EPS CAGR outlook through 2030, and disclosed a **7–12 GW** hyperscale data-center interconnection pipeline plus 3–5 GW of traditional industrial interest (Q2 2026 earnings release and call, Source 1, Source 2). This is durable *if* the pipeline converts to signed, PSC-approved contracts under Entergy's "Fair Share Plus" policy, which requires data-center customers to cover their own service costs and contribute roughly **$7bn** in customer bill benefits (i.e., the incremental load is structured not to raise cost-of-service for existing ratepayers) (Source 2) — a mechanism designed to reduce regulatory pushback risk, but it is still a pipeline, not fully signed and in rate base, so the growth is a forecast, not yet a delivered number.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) 7–12 GW hyperscale pipeline plus 3–5 GW industrial interest, structured under Fair Share Plus so incremental load funds its own costs — a rate-base growth engine most regulated utilities do not have; (2) management affirmed >8% EPS CAGR guidance through 2030, well above the sector's typical 5–7%; (3) Gulf Coast industrial demand (LNG, petrochemicals, data centers) is a multi-year secular trend, not a one-off.
**Bear:** (1) the pipeline is largely unsigned/uncontracted — actual conversion rate to firm, rate-based load is the single biggest swing factor and was not itself quantified in the sources reviewed; (2) consolidated leverage (Debt/Equity ~1.96x) is above the industry median, so a $57bn capex plan implies continued heavy financing needs, and rate pressure/political backlash against large data-center-driven bill increases is a real regulatory risk despite Fair Share Plus; (3) two NRC "white" findings in 2026 at Grand Gulf and River Bend are a data point on operational execution at the nuclear fleet that underpins reliability of the growth story.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. 2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cut below the current $4.25–$4.45 range at any subsequent quarterly release.
2. Signed/contracted hyperscale data-center load pipeline falls below 5 GW (from the current 7–12 GW range) as disclosed in a future release.
3. Any state PSC formally rejects or materially amends the Fair Share Plus cost-allocation framework for a signed data-center deal.
4. Consolidated Debt/Equity exceeds 2.3x (roughly 20% above the current level) on a subsequent 10-Q.
5. NRC issues a "yellow" or "red" significance finding (escalation from the current "white" findings) at any Entergy-operated nuclear unit.

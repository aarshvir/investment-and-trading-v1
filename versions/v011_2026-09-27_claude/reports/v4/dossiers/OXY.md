# OXY — Occidental Petroleum Corporation

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Reservation: single-commodity (oil/gas price) exposure plus a large, cash-consuming, unproven direct-air-capture (DAC) business line; size accordingly. Thesis horizon: 12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Occidental is a large US oil & gas producer (dominant Permian Basin position after the 2023 CrownRock acquisition), which until 2 Jan 2026 also owned OxyChem, a commodity-chemicals maker. It sells crude oil, natural gas and NGLs into global commodity markets, and now also sells engineered carbon-removal credits through its 1PointFive direct-air-capture (DAC) subsidiary. It earns money from the spread between its low-cost Permian production and prevailing oil prices; Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway is its largest shareholder (~34% including warrants), a signal of quality but also a governance/overhang factor.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1 factor scores (composite decile 7/10, quintile 4/5, live_rank 153/~3,500): strong quality/value family scores (fam_Q 7 factors, fcfp percentile 0.68, ep percentile 0.83) driven by post-CrownRock scale and margin improvement, not a one-off. The 9.6% "normalized" FCF yield the Q04 triage cited is largely durable low-cost-Permian economics, but part of the very high trailing GAAP EPS ($3.13 Q1 2026, $2.75 Q2 2026) is **not** durable — it is inflated by a one-time $3.1bn after-tax gain on the OxyChem sale (closed 2 Jan 2026) and by quarterly mark-to-market swings on the Berkshire warrants, both GAAP items that are not operating earnings.

## 5. Guidance track record
Occidental does not publish a single consolidated multi-quarter numeric revenue/EPS guidance range in the way software companies do; it guides production volumes, capex and OxyChem-proceeds use of cash by press release/8-K. On the 2026 OxyChem sale (announced Oct 2025, closed 2 Jan 2026 for $9.7bn cash), management committed **$6.5bn of proceeds to debt reduction**, targeting total principal debt below $15bn (post-CrownRock target); the balance sheet confirms this was executed — `LongTermDebtNoncurrent` fell from $20.6bn (31 Dec 2025) to $13.5bn (30 Jun 2026), and cash rose from $2.0bn to $4.2bn over the same period. This is a guidance commitment **delivered**, which is a genuine positive; verify the exact FY26/FY27 production and capex guidance figures against the next 8-K before increasing size. (Sources: OXY 8-K exhibit 99.1 press releases; oxy.com/news/news-releases/occidental-completes-sale-of-oxychem/.)

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **GAAP vs adjusted gap:** Large. Reported net income (ProfitLoss) is distorted quarter to quarter by (a) the ~$3.1bn net-of-tax OxyChem sale gain booked in Q1 2026, and (b) mark-to-market revaluation of the Berkshire warrants (exercise price $59.62/share, most recently amended 3 Mar 2025 to temporarily cut the publicly traded warrants' strike to $21.30) — a real, recurring source of non-cash EPS volatility that is unrelated to operating performance. Any headline EPS "beat" must be decomposed against these items before being treated as an operating signal.
- **Balance sheet (consolidated, per 10-Q Jun-30-2026):** Cash $4.15bn; long-term debt (noncurrent) $13.54bn (down from $20.6bn at 2025FY-end); stockholders' equity attributable to Occidental $41.75bn (total equity including noncontrolling interests $42.38bn, `us-gaap:StockholdersEquityIncludingPortionAttributableToNoncontrollingInterest`); total assets $80.36bn. Leverage has genuinely improved post-OxyChem-sale; net debt/EBITDA is roughly 0.7x on TTM EBITDA of $13.7bn (Yahoo/d4 snapshot) — consistent with the Q04 triage's "improved to 0.8x" note.
- **Cash conversion:** OCF/NI is not a clean metric here given the one-off gain; on an operating-cash-flow basis alone, H1 2026 OCF ($5.55bn) covers H1 2026 capex ($3.14bn) with ~$2.4bn of FCF, in line with historical norms once the Q1 timing dip is excluded.
- **Dividend/buybacks:** Dividend yield 1.93%, payout ratio ~29.5% (Yahoo/d4) — modest and covered.
- **DAC (1PointFive):** capital-intensive, revenue-immaterial today, backed by a US DOE award of up to $500m and carbon-removal offtake deals (Bain & Co., Microsoft historically) but still pre-commercial-scale cash flow; treat as an option, not as base-case value.

**Reverse DCF (own, qualitative):** at a 9% cost of capital for an E&P (commodity + reserve-depletion risk premium) and a 6.7% current FCF yield, the price implies roughly **2–2.5% perpetual FCF growth** — i.e., the market is pricing in flat-to-modestly-growing production/margins at broadly today's oil price, with no credit for DAC optionality or further buybacks. OXY's own targeted production growth (mid-single digits from the Permian, subject to capital discipline) plausibly clears this low bar in a base case of $60–70/bbl WTI. **Implied growth is at or below my base case → supports INCLUDE, tempered to INCLUDE-SMALL by commodity-price and DAC-capital risk.**

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Debt reduction from OxyChem proceeds frees cash for buybacks/dividend growth once the ~$15bn debt target is fully hit; (2) best-in-class low-cost Permian position (post-CrownRock) delivers margin resilience even at $60 WTI; (3) Berkshire's continued accumulation (34% stake) is a credible quality signal and caps downside sentiment.
**Bear:** (1) OXY is a leveraged bet on oil price — a sustained drop to $50 WTI would pressure the reverse-DCF-implied cheapness away; (2) 1PointFive/DAC could consume capital for years before (if ever) becoming self-funding, diluting capital discipline; (3) Berkshire's warrants (83.5m shares at ~$59.62 strike) and preferred stock represent a structural share-count overhang if exercised, and GAAP EPS volatility from warrant mark-to-market makes headline "beats/misses" hard to interpret.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. WTI averages below $55/bbl for two consecutive quarters (would break the reverse-DCF base case).
2. Consolidated long-term debt (noncurrent, per 10-Q) rises back above $18bn (reversal of the OxyChem-proceeds paydown).
3. Quarterly free cash flow (OCF − capex) negative for two consecutive quarters (Q1 2026 was already negative once).
4. 1PointFive/DAC capex exceeds $1.5bn in any fiscal year without matching signed offtake revenue disclosed.
5. Berkshire Hathaway stake falls below 20% (loss of the anchor-quality signal) or files to sell down materially.

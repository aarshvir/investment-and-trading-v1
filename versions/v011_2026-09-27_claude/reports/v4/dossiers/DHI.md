## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Reason: the market is pricing DHI for roughly flat-to-low-single-digit long-run FCF growth, close to what a cyclically depressed but structurally durable #1 US homebuilder can plausibly deliver — but fiscal-2026 guidance has been cut twice in a row and margins are still compressing, so conviction is capped. Horizon: 24–36 months (through a housing-cycle trough and recovery).

## 2. Business in plain English
D.R. Horton is the largest homebuilder in the US by volume, building and selling single-family homes (and increasingly rental homes and multi-family) across ~120 markets in 36 states, plus a captive mortgage-origination arm (DHI Mortgage) and a majority-owned land developer (Forestar). It makes money by buying/developing lots, building homes at scale with a "just-in-time," asset-light-relative-to-peers model, and selling to first-time and move-up buyers; competitive position rests on national scale, broad price-point coverage and a large controlled lot pipeline that lets it flex volume with demand.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) #1 national scale and the most flexible lot pipeline let DHI gain share as smaller builders retrench in a soft market. (2) Balance sheet (15.4% homebuilding debt-to-capital, $2.08bn cash) funds ~7.5% combined buyback+dividend yield through the downturn without external financing. (3) Structural US underbuilding since 2008 supports a multi-year demand floor once mortgage rates normalize.
**Bear:** (1) Guidance has now been cut twice in two consecutive quarters (revenue midpoint −3.7% in one quarter); incentives are "elevated" and expected to stay so into Q4 FY26 — margin compression is ongoing, not one quarter's noise. (2) Home sales gross margin of 20.7% (Q3 FY26) is well below the 2022–23 peak (~26–27%); further normalization toward the mid-teens historical trough is plausible if affordability doesn't improve. (3) Housing is rate-sensitive and cyclical; a "flat-to-low-single-digit" implied growth price still assumes no further deep leg down, which a renewed rate spike or recession could break.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Full-year FY2026 consolidated revenue guidance cut a third consecutive time (i.e., below the current $32.5–33.0bn range at the Q4 FY26 release, due 2026-10-29).
2. Home sales gross margin falls below 18.0% for two consecutive quarters (from 20.7% in Q3 FY26).
3. Homebuilding-segment debt-to-capital exceeds 25% (from 15.4% at 6/30/26), on the homebuilding-only entity basis, not consolidated.
4. Consolidated operating cash flow negative for two consecutive quarters (proxy for a disorderly inventory liquidation).
5. Net new orders (not disclosed numerically in the releases reviewed) or homes closed fall below the low end of the Q3 FY26 guide (32.5bn revenue / current pace) for two consecutive quarters.

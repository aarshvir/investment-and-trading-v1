# TransDigm Group Incorporated (TDG) — Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F57 (wave 3) | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close (px $1,116.20, mktcap ≈$62.43bn, per d4_live_snapshot.parquet 2026-09-25)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH.** Not held at the current price. Condition to revisit: consolidated net leverage declines meaningfully
(toward <5.0x net debt/EBITDA As Defined) and/or organic (ex-acquisition) growth demonstrates it can sustain
high-single-digits on its own for several quarters. This overturns the triage's ADVANCE call: TDG is an exceptional,
sole-source aftermarket-parts monopoly with real pricing power, but the reverse DCF — done properly, accounting for
the debt-service drag that is *already visible in the reported numbers* — implies more per-share cash-flow growth
for a decade than a defensible base case can deliver, at an already very high (~5.5–6.0x) and still-rising leverage
level.

## 2. Business in plain English
TransDigm designs and manufactures highly engineered, proprietary aircraft components (cockpit security, actuators,
ignition systems, specialty pumps and more) that are often the sole FAA-approved source for a given aircraft
platform. It sells to commercial OEMs, the commercial aftermarket (repair/replacement parts — its highest-margin,
most durable revenue) and defense customers, and its core strategy is to acquire and then re-price such sole-source
niches under a highly leveraged capital structure (source: TDG FY2025 10-K, Item 1 Business, filed 2025-11-12).

## 3. Why the model likes it / is this durable
Q08_triage.json: quality 5/5, growth 4/5, price_vs_growth 3/5. "22.5% revenue growth and unmatched pricing power
justify a premium multiple even after the recent pullback; pricing-practice scrutiny is a recurring but so-far
non-disqualifying watch item." This dossier confirms the 22–23% headline growth (FQ3 FY2026 net sales +23% YoY,
company's own release, 2026-08-04) but finds it is **materially M&A-driven, not organic**: organic sales growth was
only **13%** of the 23% in FQ3 FY2026 (company's own disclosure), with the remainder coming from the Simmonds, Jet
Parts Engineering (JPE) and Victor Sierra (VSA) acquisitions closed in FY2026. The moat (sole-source aftermarket
pricing power) is real and durable; the *growth rate*, and the leverage used to fund it, are the parts this dossier
finds less durable than the triage implied.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (**consolidated TD Group Incorporated (the public parent) and subsidiaries**
— entity scope stated explicitly per the wave-3 mandate; TDG has no bank/insurer/REIT segment-scope ambiguity, but
does have a large **redeemable noncontrolling interest** that creates a real, disclosed gap between `NetIncomeLoss`
and EPS-attributable income — flagged below)
- **FCF:** TTM (through FQ3 FY2026, differenced from SEC XBRL YTD cash-flow tags) — OCF $2.198bn, capex $0.271bn →
  **FCF ≈$1.927bn**. This is the dossier's own primary-source figure; the **d4 vendor snapshot shows `freeCashflow`
  of only $1.457bn TTM for TDG — a data conflict**, flagged in `data_conflicts` (direction of conflict means the
  vendor figure, if used, would make TDG look even more expensive on an FCF-yield basis than this dossier finds).
- **Leverage (consolidated balance sheet, 10-Q period 2026-06-27, filed 2026-08-04, Table 5/balance-sheet data):**
  current portion of long-term debt $139M + short-term trade-receivable-securitization borrowings $725M + long-term
  debt $32,621M = **total debt $33,485M**; cash $2,773M → **net debt ≈$30,712M**. Against TTM "EBITDA As Defined"
  (company's own covenant-basis metric, ≈$5.2–5.3bn on a trailing basis given the FY2026 guidance midpoint of
  $5,520M for the full year and 9-month actual of $3,981M) → **net debt/EBITDA As Defined ≈5.5–5.9x**; against a
  stricter GAAP-adjacent EBITDA (op. income + D&A, TTM ≈$5.1bn per vendor cross-check) → **≈6.0x**. Either way, this
  is genuinely high leverage — TDG's own capital-structure model, not a data error, and it just went **up**: the
  company raised **$1.5bn of incremental debt on 2026-04-17** ($0.5bn of 6.125% senior subordinated notes due 2034
  plus $1.0bn of Tranche N term loans due 2033) to help fund the JPE/VSA acquisitions.
- **TD Group stockholders' deficit:** −$9,809M at 2026-06-27 (vs. −$9,686M at 2025-09-30) — a **negative consolidated
  book equity**, a long-standing, disclosed feature of TDG's leveraged-recap/buyback history, not a new solvency
  concern in itself, but the context in which the leverage figures above should be read.
- **EPS-vs-NetIncomeLoss gap:** TDG carries a small **redeemable noncontrolling interest** ($81M at 2025-09-30, per
  the balance sheet) whose redemption-value changes are deducted from consolidated net income to arrive at income
  available to common stockholders for EPS purposes; this is why per-share figures should not be reconstructed by
  simply dividing `NetIncomeLoss` by the current share count — this dossier uses the company's own reported EPS
  figures directly (§4/§5) rather than a derived per-share number, to avoid exactly the kind of GAAP/adjusted or
  entity-scope mixing the wave-3 mandate warns against.
- **Capital returns:** $1.8bn of share buybacks in the first 39 weeks of FY2026 (809,101 shares repurchased in FQ3
  alone at an average $1,208.20/share); no regular quarterly dividend (TDG's capital-return model is buybacks plus
  occasional special dividends, not a recurring dividend).
- **Goodwill/intangibles:** $12.166bn goodwill + $4.724bn other intangibles = $16.89bn of $26.754bn total assets
  (63%) at 2026-06-27, up from $10.612bn/$3.454bn (61%) at 2025-09-30 — rising with the acquisition pace, as expected
  for a serial-acquirer capital structure.

## 7. Valuation snapshot & reverse DCF (own build; no V1 row exists for TDG — not in the original 69-name V1
universe). **Given TDG's negative book equity and very high leverage, this dossier reverse-DCFs the equity value
directly (levered free cash flow to equity, discounted at a cost of equity) rather than an EV/unlevered-FCFF
approach**, since the OCF-minus-capex figure in §6 is already stated after interest paid.
Current TTM GAAP P/E ≈33.9x (cross-checks exactly against the vendor's `trailingPE`); NTM P/E ≈23.1x.
Reverse DCF (2-stage: 10-yr explicit constant growth + Gordon terminal; terminal growth 3.0%; cost of equity tested
at 9.5–10.5% given the elevated leverage): solving for the FCF-to-equity CAGR that reconciles today's ~$62.4bn
market cap to the DCF value, using the TTM FCF-to-equity of $1.927bn from §6:
- At **ke = 9.5%: implied growth ≈12.5%/yr**
- At **ke = 10.0%: implied growth ≈13.6%/yr**
- At **ke = 10.5%: implied growth ≈14.75%/yr**
My own base case for a 10-yr FCF-to-equity CAGR — blending the company's own guided EBITDA As Defined growth
(~15–20% YoY currently, heavily acquisition-assisted) with the demonstrated debt-service drag (FQ1 FY2026 net income
fell even as operating income rose, §4) and the practical limit to how long a ~6x-levered acquisition pace can
continue — is **≈9–12%/yr**, i.e. **below the entire implied range (12.5–14.75%) across every cost-of-equity tested**.
**implied_vs_base = above.** If anything this understates the gap: the $1.927bn TTM FCF figure blends three
fiscal quarters of *lower* interest expense (before the 2026-04-17 debt raise) with only ~10 weeks of the new,
higher run-rate — a fully-loaded forward FCF base would likely be lower, not higher, which would push the true
implied growth requirement even further above the base case. This is a direct overturn of the triage's "quality
premium is earned" framing, based on primary-source debt-service evidence the triage (working from `triage_cards.csv`
and general DoD-IG background) did not have.

## 8. Bull case
1. Genuine sole-source aftermarket pricing power: FQ3 FY2026 commercial-aftermarket growth of 17% YoY, EBITDA As
   Defined margin of 52.8% — best-in-class economics, unmatched in industrials.
2. Proven, repeatable M&A playbook (Simmonds, JPE, VSA closed in FY2026; Prince & Izant agreed 2026-07-27) with a
   disciplined "value-driven operating strategy" that has compounded EBITDA for two decades.
3. $1.8bn of buybacks YTD FY2026 (39 weeks) plus a large ($5.0bn, authorized November 2025) remaining repurchase
   capacity show continued aggressive capital return even while leverage is elevated.

## 9. Bear case
1. Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated, company's own "As Defined" basis) is ~5.5–5.9x and rising, funded partly by a
   6.125% senior subordinated note — expensive debt whose service cost is already visibly eating into GAAP net
   income growth (FQ1 FY2026 NI −9.7% YoY despite op. income +7%).
2. Reported "22–23%" headline growth is materially M&A-driven (organic only 13% of the 23% total in the latest
   quarter) — a growth rate that depends on continuing to find and finance accretive sole-source targets, which by
   definition become scarcer and more expensive as TDG (and imitators) bid them up.
3. The recurring DoD Inspector General scrutiny of TransDigm's aftermarket parts pricing (2019–2022 findings that the
   company's business model is "specifically designed to raise prices on defense contracts" after acquiring
   sole-source suppliers) is a persistent reputational/political tail risk even though no new enforcement action was
   found in the reviewed FY2025 10-K or the FQ3 FY2026 10-Q (both state "no material changes" to legal proceedings
   and risk factors vs. the FY2025 10-K).

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Net debt/EBITDA As Defined (consolidated, per the company's own quarterly reconciliation table) exceeds **6.5x**.
2. GAAP net income growth is negative YoY for two consecutive fiscal quarters (already occurred once, FQ1 FY2026;
   a repeat would confirm a trend rather than a one-off financing-timing effect).
3. Organic sales growth (company's own disclosed figure, ex-acquisitions) falls below **7%** for two consecutive
   fiscal quarters (vs. 7.4%/11.0%/13% in the last three quarters).
4. FY guidance is **cut** (not raised or maintained) at any subsequent quarterly release, versus the current
   $10,470–10,550M sales / $35.38–36.21 GAAP EPS range.
5. A new DoD Inspector General report or a congressional/DoD enforcement action (refund demand, mandatory
   cost-data-disclosure order) is issued against TransDigm specifically (vs. the historical 2019–2022 pattern with
   no new action found as of this dossier).

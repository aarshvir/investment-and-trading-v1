# UDR, Inc. (NYSE: UDR) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F14 | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close (price $33.84) | **Classification:** Multi-Family Residential REIT

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** — thesis horizon 12–36 months. UDR is a statistically cheap, well-capitalized apartment REIT buying back its own stock, but this is a valuation-repair/capital-allocation thesis, not a near-term growth story: FFO-as-Adjusted per share has been range-bound ($0.61–$0.65) for eight straight quarters, the company's own FY2026 guidance implies roughly flat per-share cash flow versus FY2025, same-store NOI is outright declining in the Southeast and Southwest, and leverage (5.6x net debt/EBITDAre) is on the high side of the multifamily peer group. Reservation: hold at reduced weight until same-store NOI reaccelerates broadly (not just in the West/Northeast) or leverage comes down.

## 2. Business in plain English

UDR owns and operates ~60,000 apartment homes (as of Q2 2026) concentrated in coastal gateway markets (Boston–DC corridor, California, Seattle) and Sunbelt growth markets (Florida, Texas, Southeast). It makes money from rent, minus operating costs, property taxes and depreciation; it also runs a "Debt and Preferred Equity" program that lends to other apartment owners/JVs and recycles capital via property sales into new development and buybacks. Competitive position: a top-10 U.S. apartment REIT by scale, S&P 500 member, geographically diversified (no single region is more than a third of same-store NOI), competing with AvalonBay, Equity Residential, Camden, Mid-America and Essex Property Trust for both tenants and capital.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **FCF conversion / cash-flow quality:** 1H26 operating cash flow was **$390.9M, down 3.8% YoY** ($406.5M in 1H25) — even as GAAP net income more than doubled (1H26 net income $276.6M vs $114.4M 1H25). The gap is explained by a **$193.1M gain on sale of real estate in 1H26 vs $47.9M in 1H25** — a non-cash, non-recurring item that inflates GAAP net income and EPS (Q2'26 diluted EPS $0.21 vs $0.11, +91% YoY) without a matching cash or FFO improvement (FFO per share actually fell -1.6% YoY in Q2).
- **Capex:** 1H26 "capital expenditures and other major improvements — real estate assets" were **$104.2M**, plus $53.0M of development spend (~$314M combined annualized, ~18% of TTM revenue). This is materially higher than the $7.8M TTM figure in the firm's own D3/V1 dataset (see §7 — a data conflict).
- **SBC:** $16.4M amortized in 1H26 (~1.9% of revenue) — immaterial.
- **Net debt/EBITDAre (adjusted, company-reported):** **5.6x** at Q2'26, up from 5.5x a year ago (5.6x also at Q1'26, 5.5x at Q4'25, 5.5x at Q3'25) — a mild but persistent creep, and on the higher side of the multifamily REIT peer set (EQR/AVB/CPT/MAA/ESS have typically run mid-4x to low-5x in recent years; not independently re-verified this cycle within the time box).
- **Debt maturities:** total debt $5.81B, weighted-average rate 3.4%, weighted-average years-to-maturity 3.9. Only $808M (14%) matures through 2026 (includes the revolving $480M commercial-paper balance, which rolls); no maturity wall before 2029 ($966M). $1.3B revolver fully undrawn; ~$885M total liquidity. In compliance with all covenants.
- **Share count / buybacks:** repurchased 8.2M shares in 1H26 (avg $36.43, $300.3M) — note this average price is **above** the current $33.84 quote, so recent buybacks were not at today's cheaper level (still net share-count accretive: 328.3M→321.3M shares outstanding since YE2025, -2.1%). Board expanded authorization to ~30M shares; 25.5M remain. ATM program: 14.0M shares available, unused YTD (no dilution from that channel).
- **M&A:** none pending. Routine portfolio recycling (sold a 1977-vintage Nashville asset for $41.5M in Q2; $252.5M more under contract).
- **Dividend:** switched from quarterly to **monthly** dividends starting July 2026 ($0.145/month, $1.74/yr annualized, +1.2% YoY) — the first residential REIT to do so; ~69% of FY26 guided FFOA, a sustainable payout.

## 7. Valuation snapshot — reconciliation with V1

**V1's verdict: "attractive," score 0.874, base 3-yr annualised return +21.9%/yr** (bear -7.4%, bull +57.4%). V1's method: P/FFO(proxy) 13.3x vs the stock's own 177-month history's **10.2nd percentile** (i.e., statistically cheap vs its own past); reverse-DCF implied 10-year growth of **-5.2%** (vs consensus FY1 +2.7%, delivered 5y CAGR +6.97%); WACC 6.54%.

**I partly agree, partly disagree — and V1's own inputs contain a real, quantified error.** V1's reverse-DCF used TTM capex of just $7.76M for UDR (0.45% of revenue). The Q2'26 10-Q cash-flow statement shows **$104.2M of "capital expenditures and other major improvements" plus $53.0M of development spend in 1H26 alone (~$314M annualized, ~18% of revenue)** — roughly 20–40x the figure V1 used. Understated capex inflates V1's computed FCFF, which mechanically makes the reverse-DCF's implied growth look **more negative** than it should be (i.e., V1 overstates how "underpriced for growth" UDR is). Correcting this would likely bring implied growth up from -5.2% toward something closer to 0%, still comfortably below the 5–7% growth UDR has actually delivered — so the stock still screens as reasonably priced, just not as dramatically as V1's score (0.874, near the top of the scale) implies. Separately, V1's peer_median (44.6x) is **NTM GAAP P/E of peers (CPT, ESS, MAA, and an unrecognised "VMRK" ticker), not true P/FFO** — flagged by V1 itself as unreliable, and I did not have time to independently rebuild real peer P/FFO multiples. Note also that two of UDR's closest peers, **AvalonBay and Equity Residential, announced an all-stock merger in 2026** (~$52B combined equity value, ~$69B enterprise value) and have suspended their own FFO guidance pending closing — reshaping the multifamily peer set and adding a sector-consolidation/re-rating theme that V1's static peer snapshot cannot capture.

**valuation_view_vs_v1: dossier view = cheap; consistent = false.** I agree UDR is statistically cheap vs its own history, but I do not endorse V1's degree of conviction: the capex-input error means the "achievable minus implied growth" gap driving most of V1's 0.874 score is overstated, and V1 does not see that FY2026 guided FFOA is flat YoY or that leverage has crept up — both of which argue for a smaller position than V1's score alone would suggest. Net: cheap-but-slow-growing, hence INCLUDE-SMALL, not full INCLUDE.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:** (1) Trades at the bottom decile of its own 15-year P/FFO range with an active, board-expanded buyback shrinking the share count ~4%/yr annualized; (2) same-store fundamentals are healthy in West/Northeast/Mid-Atlantic (+3–4% NOI) as new supply moderates nationally into 2027; (3) monthly dividend and 217 consecutive quarterly-equivalent payouts signal capital-return discipline, covered at ~69% of FFOA.

**Bear:** (1) FFOA per share has not grown in two years and FY2026 guidance implies another flat year; (2) Southeast (-2.0%) and Southwest (-2.2%) same-store NOI are still contracting on new supply (23% of the same-store book) — the cited "waning supply" thesis is not yet visible in the numbers there; (3) leverage (5.6x net debt/EBITDAre) has crept up, not down, over the last year, reducing balance-sheet flexibility just as buybacks and development ($167M remaining commitment on 2 wholly-owned projects, plus $200M remaining JV commitments) compete for capital.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria

The specific, observable, time-bound conditions that would invalidate this thesis:

1. Same-store NOI growth negative for two consecutive quarters (FY26 guide is barely positive at +0.625% midpoint).
2. Consolidated Net Debt-to-EBITDAre (adjusted) rises above 6.0x (from 5.6x now).
3. FFO-as-Adjusted per-share guidance is cut (not just plain FFO) at either of the next two quarterly updates.
4. Southeast or Southwest same-store NOI decline exceeds -4% YoY in any quarter (supply absorption failing, not just slowing).
5. Share buyback program paused or reduced to a token level for two consecutive quarters (loss of the primary capital-return signal underpinning the thesis).

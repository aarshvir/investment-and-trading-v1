# FTNT — Fortinet, Inc. (Diligence dossier, F85)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — durable moat and accelerating growth, but the 25 Sep 2026 close ($~139, NTM P/E ~47x per V1) prices in more
growth than my base case delivers. Horizon: 12–24 months (revisit on a pullback or on evidence the SASE mix shift
sustains >20% growth into 2027).

## 2. Business in plain English
Fortinet sells network-security appliances (FortiGate firewalls) and an expanding software/services layer (SASE,
SD-WAN, SOC/XDR) bundled under the FortiOS operating system, sold mostly through channel partners to enterprises,
service providers and government. It earns hardware/product revenue up front and high-margin recurring service
revenue (support, subscriptions, cloud security) over the life of each box, giving it a large, sticky installed base
that must periodically refresh hardware — the current growth driver.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Quant flags: quality and growth factor scores both 4/5 (per triage). Growth is currently driven by (a) a multi-year
firewall refresh cycle as the FG-6000/7000 generation ages out and (b) a genuine new-product cycle — Unified SASE and
"SASE firewall" convergence — not merely price increases. Q2 2026: total revenue +26% YoY, product revenue +52% YoY,
billings +33% YoY (Fortinet Q2 2026 earnings release/call, 29 Jul 2026). This is a real installed-base upgrade cycle,
not a one-off; but a refresh cycle by nature decelerates once the base is upgraded — durability beyond ~2027 is the
open question, not an artefact today.

**I partially disagree with V1's bear case magnitude but agree with the core call.** V1's implied 10-year growth
(42%) is far above even the company's own newly-raised FY2026 guidance (~19% revenue growth at the midpoint) and
above the 26% YoY the company is currently printing at the very top of an upgrade cycle. A 10-year run-rate of 42%
implied by the price requires either (a) growth *reaccelerating* well past today's already-cyclical high, or (b)
material multiple expansion from here — neither is my base case. My own base case (below) puts fair-implied growth
closer to 16–20% (in line with consensus FY1 and slightly below the current print, reflecting refresh-cycle
deceleration into 2027–28), which is **below** the price-implied 42% — i.e., the stock is priced for more than I
believe it will deliver. This is directionally consistent with V1's "excessive" verdict; **dossier_view = expensive**,
**consistent = true**.

**Reverse DCF (own, sanity check on V1):** starting FY2026E FCF ≈ $3.2–3.4B (implied by non-GAAP op margin ~38% and
low capex on ~$8.1B revenue), 25 Sep 2026 close and ~$67–68B market cap ⇒ a 10-year FCF CAGR in the high-30s%/low-40s%
(with a terminal multiple consistent with today's) is required to justify the price on a simple DCF — corroborating
V1's implied-growth figure independently rather than just taking it on faith.

## 8. Bull case
1. Firewall refresh + SASE convergence is a multi-year, not one-year, cycle — FortiGate installed base is enormous
   and refresh has historically run 5–7 years per generation; billings growth (+33% YoY, Q2 2026) is a leading
   indicator that the cycle has legs into 2027.
2. Net-cash balance sheet ($2.9B cash vs ~$0.5B LT debt) plus $966M of quarterly FCF (per company release) funds
   continued buybacks without leverage risk, supporting EPS growth even if revenue growth normalises.
3. Highest-margin/lowest-capex profile among large-cap cybersecurity peers (33.7% GAAP operating margin and rising),
   giving room to defend margin even if pricing competition from Palo Alto/Check Point/Zscaler intensifies.

## 9. Bear case / kill criteria
1. **Refresh-cycle payback:** organic product-revenue growth decelerates to <20% YoY for two consecutive quarters
   (vs. +52% in Q2 2026) — the clearest sign the upgrade wave has crested.
2. **Multiple compression without a growth catalyst:** NTM P/E falls back toward its own 10-year median (well below
   the current 47x, per V1's percentile framing) on flat-to-down guidance — the valuation has no margin of safety if
   growth normalises to the 11–15% consensus range.
3. **Guidance cut or miss** on the next release (vs. the just-raised $8.02–8.18B FY2026 revenue range) — a hard,
   binary trigger.
4. **Competitive share loss:** SASE billings growth (currently +35% YoY, FortiSASE >100%) decelerates sharply as
   Palo Alto/Zscaler/Cloudflare take share in unified SASE — watch the next 2–3 quarterly billings splits.
5. **Consolidated net debt/EBITDA > 1.0x** (currently net-cash on a consolidated basis) — would signal a change in
   capital allocation discipline (e.g. a large debt-funded acquisition) that the market has not priced.

# Verisk Analytics, Inc. (Nasdaq: VRSK) — Diligence Dossier
Agent: F115 | Depth: Standard | Data cutoff: 25 Sep 2026 close (price $169.21, mkt cap ≈$22.2bn on 130.9m diluted shares) | Most recent period incorporated: Q2 2026 (10-Q filed 2026-07-29, accession 0001437749-26-024749, period ended 2026-06-30); checked for events to 2026-09-25.

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — the underlying data/analytics moat is real and the price implies less growth than the base case, but a debt-funded buyback has pushed consolidated stockholders' equity into a **deficit**, management has now promised a growth "return to Investor Day targets" in two consecutive quarters without delivering it, and there is an active driver-data-sharing MDL. This combination — cheap-looking multiple + deteriorating capital structure + unproven growth reacceleration — is exactly the "great business, priced for more than it can currently deliver on a risk-adjusted basis" pattern the mandate asks us to flag as WATCH rather than INCLUDE. Horizon to revisit: 2–3 quarters (next 2 earnings releases), not 12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Verisk sells proprietary data, analytics, and software mainly to the global property & casualty insurance industry: "Underwriting" (forms/rules/loss-cost content that insurers are often contractually or regulatorily required to use) and "Claims" (anti-fraud analytics, property/restoration solutions). It makes money via high-margin subscriptions with strong renewal economics because its content (loss costs, rating rules, catastrophe models) is embedded in customers' regulatory filings and workflows — a genuine switching-cost moat, not merely a network effect.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and durability — with an explicit conflict flagged
Quant flags (triage_cards.csv): quality 4/5, growth 2/5, price_vs_growth 3/5, no red flags listed; "Growth has slowed to mid-single digits since divesting non-core units, but the moat (28% margin) is intact and 20x NTM P/E is a fair price for that durability." **Data conflict / durability check:** management's own language in three consecutive quarterly releases (Q4'25, Q1'26, Q2'26) has stated growth "should return to levels consistent with our Investor Day targets" — a promise repeated, not yet fulfilled: Q2 2026 organic-constant-currency (OCC) revenue growth was 5.8%, similar to Q1'26's implied pace, not a demonstrated step-up. ["I am pleased to share that Verisk delivered second-quarter financial results that reflect the sequential acceleration in growth we previously articulated... our growth should return to levels consistent with our Investor Day targets" — Elizabeth Mann, CFO, Exhibit 99.1, Q2 2026 earnings release, 8-K filed 2026-07-29, accession 0001437749-26-024748] Net income actually **fell** 9.8% YoY in Q2'26 (to $228.6m) "mainly driven by increases in our effective tax rate, net interest expense, and legal fees incurred in connection with ongoing litigation" — i.e., the growth story is intact at the EBITDA line but is being eaten by financing costs and litigation costs below the line, which is precisely a capital-structure/legal risk, not a business-quality one.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet — the central red flag
Consolidated GAAP entity (Verisk Analytics, Inc. and subsidiaries; Verisk does not report a segment-level balance sheet), per the Q2'26 10-Q balance sheet (30 Jun 2026 vs 31 Dec 2025), accession 0001437749-26-024749:
- Cash fell from **$2,178.2m to $551.4m** in six months (−75%).
- Consolidated long-term debt rose from **$3,228.3m to $4,215.8m** (XBRL tag `LongTermDebt`, CIK0001442145, cross-checked at $4,217.2m for the same period -- a $1.4m/0.03% rounding difference against the press-release balance sheet figure used here, immaterial); total debt (long-term $4,215.8m + short-term/current portion $257.7m = **$4,473.5m**, arithmetic sum, not itself a single filed XBRL fact) fell from $4,737.2m (long-term $3,228.3m + current $1,508.9m) as the current portion was refinanced into a $999m senior-notes issuance plus a $500m term loan and $750m revolver draw/repay during H1'26 (cash-flow statement).
- **Total Verisk stockholders' equity (consolidated) went from +$309.0m (Dec 2025) to −$1,189.0m (Jun 2026) — a stockholders' deficit.** This was driven by $1,571.9m of H1'26 share repurchases (plus $255.1m not yet settled) funded substantially by the new debt issuance, not by retained cash flow alone.
- Entity scope: this is the **consolidated** GAAP balance sheet; Verisk does not disclose a segment-level balance sheet split the way SNA does, so no segment-vs-consolidated confusion applies here — but the *quality* of the equity figure (a deficit driven by buybacks, not losses — retained earnings actually rose from $7,810.5m to $8,142.7m over the same period) should be read as a capital-structure choice, not operating distress.
- FCF: Q2'26 free cash flow (company non-GAAP: OCF − capex) was $297.9m, +57.9% YoY, aided by "the timing of certain vendor and tax payments" [company's own explanation] — i.e., part of the improvement is working-capital timing, not run-rate. H1'26 FCF $624.3m vs H1'25 $579.7m, +7.7% — the more representative six-month figure.
- Net debt (consolidated, 30 Jun 2026, derived — not itself a single filed XBRL fact): total debt (long-term $4,215.8m per XBRL `LongTermDebt` + short-term/current portion $257.7m, summing to $4,473.5m) minus cash and cash equivalents $551.4m as of 30 Jun 2026 (per the consolidated balance sheet in the Q2'26 earnings release and 10-Q text itself; the XBRL companyfacts snapshot pulled for this dossier only carries a `CashAndCashEquivalentsAtCarryingValue` fact through 31 Mar 2026 ($524.5m) and has no 30 Jun 2026 value under that tag — treated as a filing-to-XBRL lag/mistagging in the machine-readable copy, not an error in the $551.4m figure, which is quoted directly from the filed balance sheet; distinct in any case from the $3,228.3m long-term-debt balance reported six months earlier at 31 Dec 2025) = a derived net debt of **$3,922.1m**. Against the Company's own guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA midpoint (quoted above, "$1,790" to "$1,830" million, i.e. $1,810m) → consolidated net debt/adj. EBITDA ≈ **2.2x** on a run-rate guidance basis (vs. the triage snapshot's 2.7x, likely computed on trailing FY2025 EBITDA before the H1'26 debt increase — i.e., the true multiple is probably *higher* than either figure once the full-year effect of the new debt and the ASR financing cost is reflected for a full 12 months).
- M&A: no acquisitions closed in Q2'26 (cash flow statement shows $0 vs $20.3m Q2'25); the AccuLynx acquisition was **terminated** in December 2025 after "the U.S. Federal Trade Commission... had not completed its review of the transaction by December 26, 2025, the termination date" [Q4'25 release, "Portfolio Actions"] — a real regulatory/antitrust setback, not a voluntary walk-away, worth noting for any future M&A-driven growth assumption.
- Share count: diluted weighted-average shares fell from 140.3m (Q2'25) to 130.9m (Q2'26), a 6.7% YoY reduction — the primary driver of adjusted-EPS growth outpacing revenue/EBITDA growth.

## 7. Valuation & reverse DCF
NTM P/E 20.1x, FCF yield ~4.8% (triage snapshot, consolidated basis). Reverse DCF (10-yr explicit + Gordon terminal, WACC 8.0%, terminal growth 3.0%, EV ≈$26.07bn [mkt cap $22.15bn + net debt $3.92bn], FY2025 FCF base $1.192bn): **implied 10-year FCF growth ≈ 3.8% p.a.** Base case (mid-single-digit OCC revenue growth per management's own reiterated guidance quoted verbatim in Section 5, similar EBITDA growth, plus buyback-driven per-share accretion) is ~7–8%. **Implied vs base: BELOW** on a pure DCF-arithmetic basis. **However**, this reverse DCF uses today's EV, which already embeds the post-buyback debt load; if the buyback pace continues at the H1'26 rate ($1.57bn/half-year) funded further by debt, net debt/EBITDA will keep rising and WACC (cost of capital) should rise with it, which would raise the implied growth bar back toward or above base case. Net: the valuation looks attractive only if the current capital-structure trajectory does not continue — which is precisely the point of the WATCH verdict rather than INCLUDE.

## 8. Bull / bear
**Bull:** (1) genuine regulatory/contractual embedding of Verisk's underwriting content in customer workflows gives real pricing power (OCC underwriting revenue +5.6% in Q2'26 "primarily due to an annual increase in prices"); (2) claims/anti-fraud analytics growing faster (+6.1% OCC) as insurers invest in loss-cost control; (3) buyback-driven EPS accretion is real cash returned to shareholders, not merely financial engineering, if the debt is serviced comfortably (interest coverage still solid at ~7x EBITDA/interest based on guided figures).
**Bear:** (1) consolidated stockholders' deficit and consolidated net debt/EBITDA trending toward or above 2.5–3x removes balance-sheet flexibility exactly when management wants to keep buying back stock; (2) growth-reacceleration has now been promised in three straight releases without a clear step-up in reported OCC growth (4.7% Q1'26 → 5.8% Q2'26 is directionally positive but still below what would be needed to justify "acceleration" framing over a full year); (3) the Telematics/driver-data class-action MDL is unresolved, actively adding to legal costs each quarter, and carries tail risk given the size of the certified/putative classes (see below).

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Full-year 2026 revenue or adjusted EBITDA guidance is **cut** below the unchanged range quoted in Section 5 above ("$3,190" to "$3,240" [million] revenue / "$1,790" to "$1,830" [million] Adjusted EBITDA) at the Q3 or Q4 2026 release (guidance has been flat for 2 quarters — any cut would be a first).
2. Consolidated net debt/adjusted EBITDA exceeds 3.0x at any quarter-end (was ≈2.2x on run-rate guidance basis as of Jun'26, up from ≈1.7x implied at Dec'25 on the initial FY26 EBITDA guide).
3. A material adverse ruling, class certification, or settlement in **In re: Consumer Vehicle Driving Data Tracking Litigation** (MDL No. 1:24-md-03115-TWT, N.D. Georgia) — the GM/OnStar/LexisNexis/Verisk telematics-data-sharing class actions — resulting in a disclosed reserve or payment exceeding, illustratively, $100m.
4. Underlying/OCC subscription revenue growth fails to exceed 6% for two consecutive quarters despite two prior quarters of management guidance promising a "return to Investor Day targets."
5. Any credit-rating agency downgrade of Verisk's consolidated debt, or Verisk's consolidated stockholders' equity remaining in deficit for more than four consecutive quarters without a clear path back to positive.

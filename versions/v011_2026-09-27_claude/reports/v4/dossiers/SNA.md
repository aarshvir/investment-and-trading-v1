# Snap-on Incorporated (NYSE: SNA) — Diligence Dossier
Agent: F115 | Depth: Standard | Data cutoff: 25 Sep 2026 close (price $369.20, mkt cap ≈$19.1bn on 52.5m diluted shares) | Most recent period incorporated: Q2 2026 (10-Q filed 2026-07-23, accession 0000091440-26-000144, period ended 2026-07-04); checked for events to 2026-09-25.

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** — high-ROE tool/franchise compounder, net-cash consolidated balance sheet, no formal numeric guidance to break (a governance point, not a red flag here) and a price that implies far less growth than the business has been delivering. Thesis horizon: 24–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Snap-on makes and sells hand tools, diagnostics equipment, and repair/shop-management software to professional mechanics and technicians (automotive, aerospace, military, natural resources, manufacturing). It sells mainly through a network of ~4,700 franchised mobile "tool vans" plus direct/distributor channels, and runs an in-house Financial Services arm that finances both franchisees' van purchases and technicians' tool purchases — a captive-finance model that locks customers into the ecosystem and is itself a profit center. Three operating segments: Commercial & Industrial (C&I), Snap-on Tools Group (the van channel), and Repair Systems & Information Group (diagnostics/OEM). Moat: the franchise-van relationship plus proprietary financing creates high switching costs and repeat purchase behavior.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and durability
Quant flags (triage_cards.csv): quality 4/5, growth 2/5, price_vs_growth 3/5, no red flags listed; "Slow growth (4.2%) but a very high-quality compounder at a reasonable 17.6x NTM P/E with net cash — a fair-priced, high-ROE franchise." This is durable and not an artefact: Q2 2026 gross margin expanded to 51.4% from 50.5% a year ago on only 4.7% sales growth (3.0% organic), and the highest-growth segment (C&I, +11.0% organic) also expanded operating margin +330bp to 16.8% — margin expansion is coming with volume, not from cost-cutting alone. ## 5. Guidance track record
Snap-on does **not issue formal numeric revenue/EPS guidance**. Its releases give only a qualitative "Outlook" plus two hard figures (capex, tax rate):

Snap-on currently anticipates that its full-year 2026 effective income tax rate will approximate 22%."*
- This has been consistent language across at least the last several releases (qualitative "runways for growth" framing repeated). **No numeric revenue/EPS guidance exists to be raised, cut or maintained** — flagged explicitly per the wave-3 mandatory rule rather than inferring a range that was never given.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet — entity scope is critical here
Snap-on reports a **consolidated** balance sheet plus a non-GAAP "supplemental consolidating" split between **Operations** (tools/diagnostics, with Financial Services carried at equity) and **Financial Services** (the captive finance arm). This split matters for any leverage kill-criterion:
- **Consolidated** (July 4, 2026, 10-Q balance sheet): cash $1,644.7m; notes payable/current LTD $317.0m; long-term debt $887.0m → **total consolidated debt $1,204.0m, net cash position of ≈$440.7m** (net debt/EBITDA ≈ **−0.2x**, matching the triage snapshot).
- **Operations segment alone** (non-GAAP supplemental table): **zero** third-party debt — $17.2m of "notes payable and current maturities" at the Operations level is intersegment, and the segment carries $789.5m of *intersegment long-term notes receivable* (i.e. Operations lends TO Financial Services).
- **Financial Services segment alone**: carries essentially all of the group's debt — $299.8m current + $1,676.5m long-term-and-intersegment debt as of 4 Jul 2026, funding its $597.6m + $1,274.0m (short+long) finance-receivables book. This is the franchisee/technician financing book, not corporate leverage.
Quoting the filing: *"The financing needs of Financial Services are met through intersegment borrowings and cash generated from Operations; Financial Services is charged interest expense on intersegment borrowings at market rates... Transactions between the Operations and Financial Services businesses are eliminated to arrive at the Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements."* [SNA Q2'26 Exhibit 99, "Non-GAAP Supplemental Consolidating Data"] — any kill-criterion referencing "SNA leverage" must specify **consolidated** (net cash) vs. **Financial Services segment** (the levered financing book) to avoid the entity-scope error flagged in prior dossiers (GM automotive vs. consolidated cash; Raymond James bank vs. consolidated capital).
- FCF: H1 2026 consolidated OCF $640.2m − capex $44.3m = FCF **$595.9m**; FY2025 OCF $1,081.7m (down from $1,217.5m FY2024, a **−11.2%** YoY decline — a genuine deceleration in cash conversion despite stable earnings, driven per the cash-flow statement by working-capital swings, not disclosed as a one-off in this pass — flag as an item to revisit, not glossed over).
- Buybacks/dividends: H1 2026 treasury purchases $191.3m + dividends $253.2m = $444.5m returned, funded from consolidated OCF of $640.2m (69% payout of OCF).
- Two acquisitions closed in Q2 2026 (Hi-Force Hydraulic Tools; Diesel Laptops) for a combined $154.0m cash outflow in the quarter — modest, self-funded, no incremental disclosed debt.
- Originations (Financial Services) fell 4.1% YoY in Q2'26 ($281.0m vs. prior year) — an early-warning indicator for franchisee/technician demand, worth tracking (see kill criteria).

## 7. Valuation & reverse DCF
NTM P/E 17.6x, FCF yield ~5.8% (triage snapshot; our own H1'26-annualized calc of consolidated FCF ≈$1.19bn/mkt cap $19.1bn ≈6.2%, broadly consistent). Reverse DCF (10-yr explicit + Gordon terminal, WACC 8.0%, terminal growth 2.5%, EV ≈$18.94bn [mkt cap $19.38bn − net cash $0.44bn], FY2025 FCF base ≈$0.982bn): **implied 10-year FCF growth ≈ 2.9% p.a.** Base case, given 3.0–11.0% organic growth by segment, consistent margin expansion, and a buyback that has been shrinking the share count for years, is 5–7%. **Implied vs base: BELOW**, and by a wide margin — the market is pricing SNA as a low-growth industrial, while segment-level evidence (C&I +11% organic, gross margin +90bp) suggests it is currently compounding faster than that.

## 8. Bull / bear
**Bull:** (1) C&I segment reaccelerating sharply (+11.0% organic, +330bp margin) as Snap-on pushes into "critical industries" beyond auto repair; (2) net-cash consolidated balance sheet plus a captive-finance moat gives capital-allocation optionality (bolt-on M&A, buybacks) without leverage risk; (3) no numeric guidance to miss removes a specific catalyst-risk (negative guidance surprise) that other names in this batch carry.
**Bear:** (1) FY2025 consolidated OCF fell 11.2% YoY despite stable net income — a cash-conversion wobble not yet explained by a one-off in the materials reviewed; (2) Financial Services originations down 4.1% YoY (Q2'26) could presage softening technician demand/credit tightening; (3) Repair Systems & Information Group and Snap-on Tools Group both lost margin YoY in Q2'26 (OEM-dealership softness, cost pressure) — if C&I's strength fades, blended growth reverts toward the "slow" 3–4% the quant model already flags.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Two consecutive quarters of negative **organic** sales growth (was +3.0% organic, Q2'26).
2. Consolidated OCF declines >10% YoY for a second consecutive fiscal year (FY2025 was already −11.2% YoY — one strike already).
3. Financial Services originations fall >10% YoY for two consecutive quarters (was −4.1% YoY, Q2'26 — one early flag).
4. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA (i.e., group-wide, not segment) turns positive beyond 0.5x, eroding the net-cash cushion.
5. C&I segment organic growth decelerates below 5% for two consecutive quarters (was +11.0% in Q2'26 — the segment currently carrying blended growth).

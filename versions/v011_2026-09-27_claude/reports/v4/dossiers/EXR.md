## 2. Business in plain English
Extra Space Storage is the second-largest self-storage operator in the US, owning, operating and managing self-storage facilities where individuals and small businesses rent space by the month. It makes money from rental income on ~1,870 same-store properties plus fee income from managing storage facilities it does not own (2,373 managed stores for third parties/joint ventures as of Q2 2026) and from a tenant-reinsurance/insurance product sold alongside storage units. Competitive position rests on scale (call-center and revenue-management technology, density in major metros) and a large third-party-management platform that generates high-margin fee income with little capital at risk.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) Full-year same-store NOI growth guidance swung from negative to positive at the midpoint in a single quarter (Q2 2026 release) — the clearest, most recent primary-source evidence the multi-year new-supply overhang is clearing. (2) The third-party management platform (2,373 managed stores, +67 in Q2 2026 alone) grows fee income with minimal incremental capital, a structurally higher-margin growth lever than owning stores outright. (3) Core FFO guidance has now been raised, not just reaffirmed, for the second time in 2026.
**Bear:** (1) Same-store occupancy of 94.2% is down 20bps YoY even as revenue/NOI improved — the recovery is being driven by rate, not volume, which is more fragile if competitors discount to fill space. (2) Estimated consolidated net debt/EBITDA of ~5.2–5.4x (analyst estimate) plus a disclosed floating-rate SOFR assumption of 3.73% means further Fed easing helps, but a rate spike would hurt, and the company itself quantifies this sensitivity in its guidance assumptions. (3) The XBRL taxonomy gap (no single "total debt" tag since 2021) is itself a minor disclosure-quality flag — total leverage must be hand-assembled from the balance sheet each quarter rather than read off one machine-readable line.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Full-year 2026 Core FFO/share guidance cut below the current $8.25–$8.40 range at the Q3 2026 release (next print, estimated late October 2026).
2. Same-store NOI growth guidance reversed back to negative territory (from the current 0.50%–2.50% full-year range).
3. Same-store occupancy falls below 93.0% for two consecutive quarters (from 94.2% at 6/30/26).
4. Estimated consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above 6.5x (analyst estimate methodology as in §6).
5. Third-party-managed store count declines quarter over quarter (a reversal of the fee-income growth lever central to the bull case; currently +67 gross / +48 net stores added in Q2 2026).

# REG — Regency Centers Corporation (F104, wave 6)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — a high-quality, grocery-anchored shopping-center REIT with low tenant-bankruptcy exposure,
raised same-property NOI and Core FFO guidance mid-year, and a reverse DCF that implies below-base-case growth.
Reservation: pro rata net debt/EBITDAre (~5.0x) is a REIT-appropriate but non-trivial leverage level, and the
FFO-based reverse DCF here is a lower-confidence proxy than a NAREIT-FFO-native model would be. Thesis horizon:
12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Regency Centers owns and operates ~470 grocery-anchored shopping centers across the US, leasing space to
supermarkets (anchors) and smaller "shop" tenants (restaurants, services, specialty retail) around them. It makes
money from long-term percentage/base rent leases, redevelopment of its existing centers, and selective ground-up
development, benefiting from the resilience of grocery-anchored traffic versus enclosed malls or big-box-only
centers, and from limited new supply of well-located shopping centers.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (consolidated + pro rata as labelled; REG CIK 0000910606)
- **FFO-based earnings, not GAAP net income, is the correct earnings metric for a REIT** (GAAP net income is
  depressed by real-estate depreciation that does not reflect economic reality); this dossier uses Nareit FFO and
  Core Operating Earnings per share (company-defined, non-GAAP but standard for the sector) as the primary
  results metric in §4, with GAAP net income shown for completeness. Consolidated GAAP net income has grown
  steadily (~$90–129mm/quarter over the last 8 quarters, no volatility flag).
- **Leverage**: pro rata (i.e., REG's proportionate share of its unconsolidated joint ventures' debt, plus
  wholly-owned debt — **not** purely consolidated) **net debt to TTM operating EBITDAre = 5.0x at 30 Jun 2026**
  (down from 5.2x at 31 Mar 2026), per the company's own Q2 2026 supplemental (Ex. 99.1 to the 8-K). This is the
  entity-scope-correct metric REG itself guides to; a purely consolidated-balance-sheet net debt/EBITDA would
  differ (not separately re-derived this pass — the pro rata figure is the one management and analysts use for
  shopping-center REITs given the JV structure, and is preferred here).
- **Tenant credit risk**: tenants currently in bankruptcy and still occupying space represent only **0.4% of
  pro rata annual base rent** as of 31 Mar 2026 (down from 0.69% at 31 Dec 2025), with no single tenant exceeding
  0.5% of pro rata ABR (REG 10-Q risk-factor disclosure, cross-checked via search). This is a materially better
  bad-debt exposure than the sector's recent Party City/Big Lots/Joann wave would suggest, and directly supports
  the "low leverage, resuming same-store growth" framing in the triage.
- **Leasing spreads**: blended cash rent spreads were +12.1% (Q1 2026) and +10.4% (Q2 2026) on new/renewal
  leasing — still solidly positive (i.e., re-leasing at higher rents than expiring leases) though decelerating
  quarter over quarter, a trend worth watching but not yet a red flag.
- **Occupancy**: same-property percent leased 96.6% (31 Mar 2026, flat YoY) → 96.9% (30 Jun 2026, up 40bps YoY) —
  improving, near full occupancy for the format.
- **Development pipeline**: in-process development/redevelopment projects had an estimated $635mm (Q1 2026) →
  $680mm (Q2 2026) of remaining net costs — a growing, self-funded pipeline that is the main source of FFO growth
  beyond same-property NOI, not a red flag at this scale relative to a ~$13bn asset base.
- **Dividend yield** ~4.1% (d4 snapshot), consistent with a mature, income-oriented REIT; payout is covered by
  Core Operating Earnings ($1.16/quarter vs. an implied ~$0.75/quarter dividend at the trailing dividend rate
  of $3.02/year, i.e., comfortably covered — not independently re-derived to the cent this pass).

## 7. Valuation — reverse DCF and reconciliation with V1
**No V1 row exists for REG** in `v4/outputs/v1_valuation_table.csv` (checked directly) → **v1_verdict = null**.

Reverse DCF, using **TTM Nareit FFO as the cash-generation proxy** (FFO/share ~$1.16–1.21/quarter run-rate ×
~187mn shares ≈ **$0.90bn TTM**, a more appropriate REIT cash-flow base than the raw operating-cash-flow-minus-
all-capex "FCF" figure, which understates a REIT's distributable earnings by treating growth capex the same as
maintenance capex): two-stage model, N=10 years at growth g, terminal growth 2.5%, discount rate 7.2% (a REIT-
appropriate WACC estimate given ~5.0x leverage and a BBB+/A- typical rating for this quality tier — not
independently verified against REG's actual rating this pass), solving for g against enterprise value ($18.77bn,
d4 snapshot): **implied growth ≈ 1.9%/yr**.

**Base case** (evidence-based): full-year 2026 Core Operating Earnings guidance of $4.62–$4.66/share on a
starting base of roughly $4.44–4.45/share (2025 actual, back-solved from the four quarterly Core Operating
Earnings figures shown, ~$1.09+$1.10+~$1.11+~$1.13 estimate) implies **~4% per-share growth in 2026**; combined
with the raised same-property NOI guide (+3.7% to +4.1%) and a self-funded ~$680mm development pipeline, a
reasonable 10-year base case for FFO/share growth is **~4–5%/yr** (NOI growth plus modest external growth, net of
share-count dilution from equity-funded development). **The reverse-DCF implied growth (1.9%) is meaningfully
below this base case → supports INCLUDE.** The half-weight (INCLUDE-SMALL rather than full INCLUDE) reflects (a)
the lower confidence inherent in a proxy FFO-based reverse DCF versus a purpose-built REIT valuation model (own-
history P/FFO percentile and full peer set were not separately re-derived this pass — disclosed limitation), and
(b) 5.0x pro rata leverage, while improving, is not low in absolute terms for a rising-rate environment.

**Scenario basis** (annualised 3-yr total returns): starting point trailing EPS/FFO run-rate ~$4.65 Core Operating
Earnings (2026 guide midpoint), dividend yield ~4.1% (dividendRate $3.02), exit multiple assumption on a P/FFO
basis of 14–17x (current P/FFO ≈ 73.23/4.65 ≈ 15.7x):
- **Bear**: same-property NOI growth decelerates toward the low end of guidance in 2027–28, leasing spreads
  compress further, P/FFO derates to 14x → ≈ **−3%/yr**.
- **Base**: FY2026 guidance is met, ~4–5% FFO/share growth continues into 2027, P/FFO holds ~15.5x → ≈ **+7%/yr**.
- **Bull**: leasing spreads and occupancy continue improving, development pipeline delivers ahead of schedule,
  P/FFO re-rates to 17x → ≈ **+14%/yr**.

## 8. Bull case
1. Guidance was raised twice in 2026 (Q1 maintained, Q2 raised on both Core Operating Earnings and same-property
   NOI growth) — real, delivered operating momentum, not a hoped-for turnaround.
2. Tenant bankruptcy exposure is only 0.4% of pro rata ABR, well below the sector's recent experience with
   Party City/Big Lots/Joann closures — grocery-anchored positioning is doing its job.
3. Leverage (5.0x pro rata net debt/EBITDAre) is trending down (from 5.2x in Q1 2026), giving balance-sheet
   capacity for the ~$680mm development pipeline without needing to stretch further.

## 9. Bear case
1. Leasing spreads are decelerating (cash spreads +12.1% in Q1 2026 → +10.4% in Q2 2026) — still positive, but
   the trend, if it continues, would eventually compress the re-leasing tailwind that drives same-property NOI
   growth.
2. 5.0x pro rata net debt/EBITDAre, while improving, still means REG is meaningfully exposed to refinancing rate
   risk if long-term rates rise from here (debt maturity ladder not independently re-derived this pass — disclosed
   limitation).
3. The reverse DCF here is a proxy (TTM FFO run-rate, not a full NAREIT-FFO-native multi-year model), so the
   "1.9% implied growth" conclusion carries real methodology uncertainty; a REIT-specialist model (as V2R did for
   other REIT holdings per STATE.md) might read the valuation differently.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Same-property NOI growth falls below the low end of the raised guidance range (+3.7%) for two consecutive
   quarters.
2. Pro rata net debt/EBITDAre rises above 5.5x (vs. 5.0x now).
3. Blended leasing spreads (cash basis) turn negative or fall below +5% for two consecutive quarters (vs. +10.4%
   in Q2 2026).
4. Tenant-bankruptcy exposure (pro rata ABR from bankrupt-but-occupying tenants) rises above 1.5% (vs. 0.4% now).
5. FY2026 Core Operating Earnings guidance ($4.62–$4.66/share) is cut at the Q3 2026 (Oct 28) release.

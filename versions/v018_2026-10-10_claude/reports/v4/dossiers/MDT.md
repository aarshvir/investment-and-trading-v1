## 2. Business in plain English
Medtronic designs, manufactures and sells medical devices and therapies across four segments: Cardiovascular (pacemakers, defibrillators, cardiac ablation catheters, structural heart), Neuroscience (spine, cranial, neuromodulation, pelvic health), Medical Surgical (surgical stapling, energy devices, endoscopy, the Hugo robotic-assisted surgery system) and, until its planned separation, Diabetes (insulin pumps and continuous glucose monitors, under the MiniMed brand). It sells mostly to hospitals and health systems worldwide and competes on clinical outcomes, physician relationships and regulatory approvals. It is incorporated in Ireland (redomiciled from the US in 2015) with operating headquarters in Minneapolis.

## 9. Bear case
1. **Applied Medical antitrust verdict:** on 2026-02-05, a federal jury (C.D. Cal.) awarded Applied Medical Resources Corporation **$382 million in damages**, which the court is required to **automatically treble** by law (i.e., a potential ~$1.15bn exposure before injunctive relief and attorneys' fees, which Applied is separately seeking); Medtronic has not recorded a liability, believes the verdict is "inconsistent with the law and evidence," and plans to appeal — but the exposure is real, live, and unresolved as of the Q1 FY27 10-Q (filed 2026-09-03).
2. **Hernia mesh MDL:** ~10,500 individual plaintiffs as of 2026-08-05; the first bellwether trial (D. Mass.) returned an **$88 million** jury verdict against Medtronic on 2026-08-04 — also unaccrued and under challenge, but the first of potentially many bellwether outcomes across thousands of claims.
3. Both litigation items sit alongside routine, ongoing anti-corruption dialogue with the SEC/DOJ regarding certain international-market practices (no liability recorded; disclosed as a standing item, not a new development) — a reminder that Medtronic operates a large, globally regulated device business with multiple simultaneous legal exposures.

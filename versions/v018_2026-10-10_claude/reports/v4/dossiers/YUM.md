# YUM — Yum! Brands, Inc.

**Agent:** F116 (wave 7 standard diligence). **As of:** 2026-09-25 close (YUM $138.63, mkt cap ~$37.8bn — stockanalysis.com, live retrieval 2026-09-27).

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — the franchise economics (KFC/Taco Bell unit and margin growth, 60%+ digital mix) are genuinely strong and the Pizza Hut divestiture is a credible focusing catalyst, but (a) the balance sheet carries negative shareholders' equity and rising near-term debt, (b) a live, unresolved Taco Bell food-safety event (July 2026 Cyclospora outbreak) sits in the current quarter with no reported sales impact yet disclosed, and (c) the current price already implies growth close to the company's own long-run algorithm — this is not a bargain on the numbers in front of us, even though the business quality is real. Revisit after Q3 2026 results show whether the outbreak dented Taco Bell same-store sales and after the Pizza Hut sale closes (expected Q3 2026). **[Corrected 2026-10-09: stale: the Pizza Hut sale had closed before the 25 Sep as-of date (China 7 Aug 2026, ex-China 1 Sep 2026); see the Correction section]** Horizon: 12–24 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Yum! Brands is the worldwide franchisor of KFC, Taco Bell and (until its pending sale closes) **[Corrected 2026-10-09: stale: sale completed 7 Aug and 1 Sep 2026]** Pizza Hut. It makes money almost entirely from franchise royalties and fees on system-wide sales generated by independently owned restaurants, plus a smaller company-owned restaurant base — a capital-light model that generates high margins and heavy free cash flow relative to its own invested capital, which the company has historically returned to shareholders via debt-funded buybacks (hence negative book equity; see §6). Its competitive position rests on brand scale (34,747 KFC units, 9,046 Taco Bell units globally per the Q2 2026 release), franchisee economics, and increasingly a proprietary technology platform ("Byte by Yum!") for digital ordering.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Triage (Q02) called out YUM as having "the strongest revenue growth (+12.2%) and margins (25.4%) in the restaurant group" but flagged a data-quality red flag: "forward EPS growth of -12% vs. trailing, inconsistent with reported margin/revenue strength." Having gone to the primary filings, the -12% forward figure in the triage data card is not corroborated by anything in the four quarterly earnings releases reviewed — GAAP and adjusted EPS are both growing (see §4), and the company's long-term algorithm target ("at least 8% Core Operating Profit Growth," repeated verbatim in every release since 2022) has not been cut. The triage's revenue-growth/margin read is durable and structural — royalty-based system-sales growth plus a self-funding digital platform. The specific -12% EPS figure looks like a data artefact (possibly comparing GAAP EPS across periods with very different special-item mix, e.g. the Q2'26 quarter's large Pizza-Hut-related special item) rather than a real deceleration signal — logged as a **data conflict** in the summary JSON.

**Verdict: guidance maintained, not raised or cut**, across all four releases in the window. This is a long-run algorithm, not quarterly numeric guidance, so "raised/cut vs. prior range" does not strictly apply — flagged rather than forced into that framework.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:**
1. KFC and Taco Bell divisions (ex-Pizza Hut) grew system sales +7% ex-F/X, units +6% and Core Operating Profit +8% in Q2 2026 — the core, retained business is accelerating, not decelerating, and digital system sales excluding Pizza Hut "approached $9 billion, with digital mix exceeding 60%" (Q2'26 release).
2. The Pizza Hut sale ($2.7bn aggregate, ~$2.3bn net proceeds) removes the group's slowest-growing, most China-exposed division (Pizza Hut division system sales were down 2% ex-F/X in Q2'26) and funds an incremental $4bn buyback authorization — a credible portfolio-focusing move, not a distress sale (both buyers are strategic: a PE firm and Yum China, the existing master franchisee).
3. Reverse DCF implies ~4.2% perpetual growth priced in, below both the stated 8%+ algorithm and the 12.4% TTM delivered adjusted-EPS growth.

**Bear:**
1. Consolidated shareholders' deficit of $(7.1)bn and consolidated total debt of $12.3bn (no single XBRL tag reports "total debt"; this is short-term borrowings $2,813m + long-term debt $9,462m, both from the same 2026-06-30 balance sheet, accession 0001041061-26-000160) against only $674m of consolidated cash is a genuinely leveraged capital structure; the reclassification of ~$2.4bn from long-term to current debt in the Q2'26 10-Q is a fact to track into the Q3 2026 filing — if it signals refinancing at higher rates rather than a clean repay-from-proceeds plan, interest expense could rise.
2. A "multistate outbreak of Cyclospora" in July 2026 forced "Taco Bell U.S. removed certain lettuce from its nationwide supply chain that was later recalled by its vendor" (10-Q for 2026-06-30, accession 0001041061-26-000160, Risk Factors update) — this happened *after* the Q2 2026 quarter closed, so no same-store-sales impact from it is visible in any figure in this dossier; it is a live, unquantified risk into Q3 2026 reporting.
3. GAAP EPS of $3.08 in Q2 2026 vs adjusted $1.62 shows how easily a one-time item (likely Pizza Hut-related) can make headline growth look far stronger than run-rate earnings power — a reminder to underwrite off adjusted, not GAAP, numbers, and a sign that reported "GAAP EPS +131%" in the release headline is not organic.

# MDLZ — Mondelez International, Inc. (F104, wave 6)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — a genuinely durable global snacking franchise, but the 25 Sep 2026 close ($60.25) prices in more
earnings recovery than the evidence currently supports; leverage is elevated for the sector and the cocoa-cost
recovery management itself dates to 2027. Thesis horizon: 12–24 months (re-underwrite once cocoa normalizes and
FY2026 guidance is confirmed or missed).

## 2. Business in plain English
Mondelez sells packaged snacks — Oreo, Cadbury, Milka, Ritz, belVita, Toblerone — in ~150 countries, split roughly
between developed markets (North America, Europe) and emerging markets (Latin America, AMEA), the latter being a
larger share of the mix than most staples peers. It makes money on brand-led pricing power and scale distribution,
not on being the low-cost producer; its single biggest input-cost swing factor is cocoa, which spiked to record
prices in 2024–25 and is only now beginning to ease.

## 7. Valuation — reverse DCF and reconciliation with V1
**No V1 row exists for MDLZ** in `v4/outputs/v1_valuation_table.csv` (checked directly; MDLZ is outside the V1
universe) → **v1_verdict = null**, no reconciliation possible; this dossier is the sole systematic check.

Reverse DCF (two-stage: N=10 years at growth g, terminal growth 2.5% thereafter, discounted at 7.5% — a
BBB-rated staples WACC estimate, consistent with the S&P BBB rating in §6): solving for the g that equates the
company's enterprise value ($98.07bn, d4 snapshot 25 Sep 2026 close) to the discounted FCF stream:
- Using the **depressed trailing FCF** ($2.25bn) as the base, implied growth ≈ **12.0%/yr** for 10 years — clearly
  unachievable and an artefact of using a cyclically low starting FCF.
- Using the **company's own normalized FY2026 FCF guide** ($3.0bn) as the base (the more honest starting point,
  since cocoa cost phasing is explicitly temporary per management), implied growth ≈ **8.4%/yr** **[Corrected 2026-10-08: levered FCF was set against enterprise value; on an equity basis net of SBC at 7.65% the price implies about 6.8%, still above base; see Correction DV27]**; at the higher end
  of the historical guide ($3.5bn), implied growth ≈ **6.4%/yr**.

**Base case** (evidence-based): organic revenue growth 3–4% (in line with the long-run category and the FY2026
guide's floor), plus 2–3 years of above-trend adjusted-EPS growth (say 6–9%) as cocoa normalizes in 2027–28
per management's own framing, settling to mid-single-digit (5–6%) FCF/EPS growth longer run once margin recovery
is complete. That puts a reasonable 10-year annualized base case at **~5–6%**, below the 6.4–8.4% the market is
paying for on normalized FCF. **Implied growth is at or above the base case → "above" or at best "in_line" at the
most generous normalized-FCF assumption, not "below."** Per the Loop-2 discipline (a great business priced for
more than it can deliver is WATCH, not INCLUDE), and given the leverage flag, this dossier assigns **WATCH**.

**Scenario basis** (annualised 3-yr total returns): starting point trailing EPS $2.73, applying the above growth
paths to EPS with dividends reinvested (current yield ~3.3%, dividendRate $2.08) and an exit multiple assumption
of 16–20x (current NTM P/E 18.4x per d4, own-history and peer context not separately re-derived this pass —
disclosed limitation):
- **Bear**: cocoa costs stay elevated into 2027, organic growth slows to ~1–2%, multiple compresses to 15x →
  ≈ **−4%/yr**.
- **Base**: guidance is met (organic ≥2% in 2026, mid-single-digit adjusted EPS growth resuming 2027), multiple
  holds near 18x → ≈ **+6%/yr**.
- **Bull**: cocoa relief arrives faster than guided, adjusted EPS growth reaccelerates to high-single-digits,
  multiple re-rates to 20x → ≈ **+15%/yr**.

## 8. Bull case
1. Category leadership in biscuits and chocolate with pricing power that has held share through the cocoa spike.
2. Cocoa costs are a well-flagged, temporary headwind; management explicitly guides to margin recovery starting
   2027, and the FY2026 organic-growth guide was just raised, not cut.
3. Emerging-market weighting (~40%+ of revenue) gives structurally higher long-run volume growth than US/EU
   staples peers, a genuine differentiator versus, e.g., Hershey or General Mills.

## 9. Bear case
1. Net debt/EBITDA ≈3.8x (consolidated) is elevated for the sector and constrains buybacks; a cocoa-driven margin
   miss would pressure the BBB rating (S&P's own downgrade sensitivity is not separately quantified here — flagged
   as a limitation).
2. FY2026 FCF guidance was cut to ~$3bn from ~$3.5bn delivered in FY2025 even as revenue guidance was raised —
   cash conversion, not just GAAP earnings, is under near-term pressure.
3. Continued Russia operations (3.8% of revenue) carry reputational/consumer-boycott risk that is not priced into
   any conventional multiple and could accelerate if EU/US sanctions regimes tighten.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Organic net revenue growth below 2% for two consecutive quarters (below the low end of the raised FY2026 guide).
2. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above 4.0x (vs. ~3.8x now) or S&P/Moody's place the rating on negative
   outlook.
3. FY2026 free cash flow comes in below $2.8bn (below the ~$3bn guide), signalling the cocoa cash drag is not
   temporary.
4. Adjusted EPS guidance is cut (from "flat to 5% cc") at either the Q3 2026 (Oct 27) or Q4 2026 release.
5. Russia revenue share or reputational pressure escalates into a formal boycott with measurable US/EU volume
   impact (qualitative trigger, monitor via 10-Q Russia disclosure).

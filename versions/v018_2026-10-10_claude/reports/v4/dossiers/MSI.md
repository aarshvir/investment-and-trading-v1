# MSI — Motorola Solutions, Inc. (Agent F80, wave 5, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — a near-monopoly, mission-critical public-safety technology franchise with record backlog and two
consecutive guidance raises, but at ~$456.88 (2026-09-25 close) and ~24x NTM P/E the multiple leaves little room for
error; reservation is entry price/sizing, not the business quality. Horizon 24–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Motorola Solutions sells land-mobile radio (P25 digital radio) systems, 911 emergency-dispatch software, and video
security/access-control products (via its Avigilon acquisition) mainly to US and international government public-safety
agencies (police, fire, EMS) and large enterprises. It earns money through hardware sales plus a growing recurring
software/services revenue stream tied to long-duration government contracts. Its moat is regulatory/interoperability
lock-in: public-safety radio networks are single-vendor, multi-decade infrastructure that is extremely costly and risky
to rip out, giving MSI durable pricing power and near-monopoly incumbency in US P25 radio.

## 8. Bull case
1. Record backlog of $15.6B, +11% YoY, growing faster than revenue — the clearest forward-demand signal in this dossier [6].
2. Two consecutive quarters of guidance raises (Q1→Q2 2026) on both revenue and non-GAAP EPS, not just maintenance [6].
3. Regulatory/interoperability lock-in on P25 public-safety radio is a genuine multi-decade moat, insulating pricing.

## 9. Bear case
1. GAAP diluted EPS growth (+9.5% YoY Q2) is materially below the non-GAAP (+24%) growth the company promotes and the
   market prices off — the gap (intangible amortization, SBC add-backs) understates the true cost of MSI's acquisitive
   growth strategy if it is not normalized correctly.
2. At ~24–26x forward non-GAAP earnings, valuation already assumes the backlog converts smoothly; any slippage in
   government budget cycles or contract timing would compress the multiple with no valuation cushion.
3. Thin, buyback-driven equity base ($2.672B) mechanically inflates ROE (0.799 in b1) — the "high ROE" quality signal is
   partly a capital-structure artefact, not purely operating excellence, and should be discounted accordingly.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Total backlog growth turns negative YoY (from +11%/$15.6B at Q2 2026) for two consecutive quarters.
2. Non-GAAP EPS guidance cut below the current $17.62–$17.72 FY2026 range at any remaining 2026 release.
3. GAAP-to-non-GAAP EPS gap widens beyond the current ~2.5x ratio (9.5% GAAP vs 24% non-GAAP growth, Q2 2026) for two
   consecutive quarters, signalling deteriorating underlying earnings quality.
4. Consolidated long-term debt (`LongTermDebtNoncurrent`, XBRL) rises more than 20% above the 2026-07-04 level of
   $8.417B without a matching backlog/revenue increase.
5. Revenue growth falls below 8% YoY for two consecutive quarters (from 13.3% at Q2 2026), below the low end of what the
   raised FY26 guide implies.

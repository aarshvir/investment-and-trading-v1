## 2. Business in plain English
Xcel Energy is a holding company for four fully rate-regulated electric/gas utilities — PSCo (Colorado), NSP-Minnesota, NSP-Wisconsin and SPS (Texas/New Mexico Panhandle) — serving ~3.8m electric and ~2.2m gas customers across eight states. It earns a regulator-set return on its invested capital (rate base); growth comes from building transmission, distribution, generation and grid infrastructure that regulators allow into rates, not from selling more units. Competitive position is that of a monopoly franchise utility with no direct competitor for its wires and pipes.

## 3. Why the model likes it / is it durable
Q15 triage (`v4/outputs/Q15_triage.json`) scored quality 4, growth 4, price_vs_growth 4, citing "24% forward EPS growth at 15.8x NTM P/E." b1 factor data (`v4/data/b1_live_scores.csv`, as_of 2026-09-25) shows XEL in decile 6 (mid-pack) with `pct_sue`=0.37 (moderate positive earnings-surprise momentum) and `pct_ep`=0.30/`pct_bp`=0.55 (unremarkable value scores, as expected for a fairly-valued regulated utility). The growth driver — rate-base expansion funded by a filed, board-approved five-year capital plan and reinforced by signed large-load (data-center) contracts — is durable and structural (secular return of load growth to a utility that had none for two decades), not an accounting artefact. The "24% forward EPS growth" figure in the triage note is not confirmed by the company's own guidance (see §5); management guides 6–8% long-term EPS CAGR off a $3.80 (FY2025 ongoing) base, which is the correct, durable read.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) $60bn 2026–2030 capital plan is filed and substantially pre-approved across four state jurisdictions, giving unusually high visibility to the 6–8% EPS algorithm; (2) genuine, signed large-load demand (NSP-Minnesota/Google data-center agreement, 1,900 MW, pending MPUC approval, expected ~$1.1bn customer benefit — Q2'26 release) adds upside optionality on top of the base capital plan; (3) guidance discipline is excellent — four straight periods without a cut, and FY25 landed inside the pre-set range.

**Bear:** (1) Smokehouse Creek Fire Complex (Texas Panhandle, Feb-2024, SPS-caused per Texas A&M Forest Service) has $503m of total estimated losses as of 2026-06-30, only ~$80m of the $525m annual insurance layer remaining, a pending Texas Attorney General lawsuit (filed Dec-2025) seeking civil penalties, and management "unable to reasonably estimate... the upper end of the range" (10-Q, Note, filed 2026-07-30) — a real, open-ended tail that the Q15 triage note never mentioned (it only flagged the smaller, now-settled Marshall Fire); (2) consolidated net debt/EBITDA (~6.2x) is already at the high end of the healthy band for the plan's early innings — a rate-case disappointment or a materially larger Smokehouse Creek judgment would pressure the balance sheet further; (3) EV/EBITDA (13.9x) is near the top of the sector's historical range, so multiple compression risk exists if growth merely meets (rather than beats) guidance.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (what would break the thesis / thesis-invalidation triggers)

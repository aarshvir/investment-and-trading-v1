**Agent:** F74 (wave 4) | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close (px $20.31, mktcap $14.00bn per b1_live_scores.csv 2026-09-25)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 24–36 months. Named reservation: the Lab (life-science) segment —
34% of same-store NOI — is in a sector-wide oversupply downturn (-3.2% same-store NOI in Q2 2026) with no confirmed
bottom, and Healthpeak's economic exposure to its fastest-growing segment (senior housing) is being diluted by the
2026 partial IPO of that business as a separate public company (Janus Living, Inc., NYSE: JAN). The offsetting case
for including at half weight: management raised full-year FFO guidance on a beat, insiders bought back stock at
$16.81/share (23% below the current price), leverage is conservative (4.7x net debt/EBITDAre), and there is no
material litigation. GAAP EPS/P-E for this name is meaningless (see §3/§7) — everything below is built on FFO/AFFO.

## 2. Business in plain English
Healthpeak is a diversified healthcare real estate investment trust: it owns outpatient medical office buildings
(56% of same-store NOI, leased mostly to hospital systems and physician groups), life-science lab buildings (34%,
leased to biotech/pharma tenants), and — until a 2026 restructuring — senior housing communities. In March 2026 it
spun its senior-housing portfolio into a newly IPO'd, separately-traded REIT, **Janus Living, Inc. (NYSE: JAN)**,
in which Healthpeak retains a **73.6%** economic stake (down from 81.6% at the March IPO) that is still consolidated
into Healthpeak's financial statements (source: Q2 2026 earnings release, 8-K Ex.99.1, filed 2026-08-04; and
Janus Living Form 424B4, sec.gov, March 2026).

## 3. Why the model likes it / is this durable
b1_live_scores.csv (as_of 2026-09-25): composite decile 4, quintile 2, live_rank 338/500 — not a top-tier quant
score; triage (Q13) advanced it mainly on price-vs-growth (4/5) after correcting for the GAAP distortion below.
**GAAP P/E of ~160x (flagged by triage as misleading) is a real accounting artefact, not a data error**: REIT
straight-line depreciation on appreciating buildings depresses GAAP net income structurally (stock-analysis skill,
`references/sectors/realestate-reit.md`, "REIT depreciation" distortion). The correct earnings measure is Nareit
FFO / FFO as Adjusted, used throughout this dossier. Durability of the "why it's cheap" story is **mixed**: the
outpatient-medical base is durable (hospital-system tenants, long leases); the lab weakness reflects a genuine,
sector-wide supply glut (triage-flagged, confirmed below) that could persist multiple years; the senior-housing
growth is real and accelerating but Healthpeak's own claim on it is shrinking as Janus Living issues more equity.

## 8. Bull case
1. Beat-and-raise FY2026 guidance on both FFO as Adjusted and same-store cash NOI in the two most recent releases,
   with senior-housing same-store NOI accelerating to +19.2% (Q2 2026).
2. Management bought back stock with real capital at $16.81/share in April 2026, already ~21% below the current
   price — a rare, verifiable "put your money where your mouth is" signal from a REIT management team.
3. Conservative balance sheet (4.7x net debt/EBITDAre) and a covered ~70%-payout dividend give real flexibility to
   keep buying back stock or absorb further lab-segment weakness without a distribution cut.

## 9. Bear case
1. Life-science/lab same-store NOI is *negative* (−3.2% Q2 2026) and represents over a third of the same-store
   pool; the sector-wide oversupply behind it (triage-flagged) has no confirmed end date in the primary sources
   reviewed.
2. Healthpeak's economic stake in its own fastest-growing segment (senior housing, now Janus Living) fell from
   81.6% to 73.6% in a single quarter post-IPO — a structural, ongoing dilution of the best part of the story that
   could continue.
3. Total revenue grew 9–11% YoY in the two most recent quarters, but diluted FFO as Adjusted per share was flat to
   slightly down over the same period — the growth is not yet converting into per-share value, an earnings-quality
   question this dossier could not fully resolve in the time available.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable triggers)
1. Lab (life-science) same-store NOI growth stays negative for **4 consecutive quarters** (currently 1 quarter,
   Q2 2026 at −3.2%).
2. Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDAre rises above **6.0x** (vs. 4.7x today).
3. Full-year FFO as Adjusted guidance is **cut** at any subsequent quarterly release (two consecutive raises so far).
4. Healthpeak's economic ownership of Janus Living falls below **60%** (loss of majority-economics claim on the
   best-performing segment; currently 73.6%).
5. The monthly common dividend is cut from the current $0.10167/share rate.

## Correction (verification DV13, 2026-10-06)
**Verification scope:** Q2 2026 results and guidance (8-K Ex-99.1 acc. 0001628280-26-052608, 10-Q acc. -053373, XBRL), debt and entity scope, Janus Living ownership, buyback and dividend, reverse DCF and verdict logic. Verdict INCLUDE-SMALL is **unchanged**; `implied_vs_base` changes from "below" to "in_line" (F74_summary.json, DOC entry, updated).

**FAIL 1 - Consolidated debt understated (section 6).** Wrong text: "Consolidated debt: senior unsecured notes $6.786bn + mortgage debt $0.104bn = $6.89bn gross". Correct (consolidated balance sheet, 2026-06-30): bank line of credit and commercial paper $1,495,994k + term loans $1,646,282k + senior unsecured notes $6,785,697k + mortgage debt $104,213k = **$10,032.2m**, i.e. $3.14bn (46%) more than stated. The company-reported Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDAre of 4.7x already reflects the full debt, so the leverage kill criterion (6.0x) is unaffected. Also: the $558m Janus Living cash / "no outstanding debt" figure is as of 3 Aug 2026 after about $1.0bn of acquisitions (not at 30 Jun), and Janus has an undrawn $500m revolver and $100m delayed-draw term loan. After quarter end DOC used the Brookfield joint-venture proceeds to repay $650m of 3.25% notes and about $375m of commercial paper. Verdict unchanged.

**FAIL 2 - Reverse DCF discount rate (section 7).** The dossier solves g = cost of equity - yield with a cost of equity of 8.0%-8.5%, only 2.8-3.3 points over the 5.17% 10-year Treasury (25 Sep 2026) and with no stated ERP; that gives "2.0-2.5%". On the dividend basis the dossier uses (appropriate for a REIT; D0 $1.22, price $20.31, g = (rP - D0)/(P + D0)): at a cost of equity of 9.0% (beta 0.85 x 4.5% ERP + 5.17%) implied growth is 2.9%; at 9.67% (beta 1.0) it is 3.5%. The dossier's own base case is 2-3%/yr, so implied is now **in line** (at the top of the base range, or 0.5 point above it), not "below". The probability-weighted scenario returns (base +8-9% = 6% yield + 2-3% growth) sit at or just under the 9.0-9.7% hurdle, consistent with in_line. The INCLUDE-SMALL verdict still stands on this evidence (precedent: DHI, INCLUDE-SMALL at in_line), but the "modestly cheap" wording is no longer supported; the half-weight is justified by the lab-segment and Janus-dilution reservations alone. Scenarios are unchanged.

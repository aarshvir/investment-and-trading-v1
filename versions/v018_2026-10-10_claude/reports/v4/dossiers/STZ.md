## 1. Verdict

Thesis horizon: 12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Constellation imports and sells Mexican beer brands (Modelo Especial — the #1 beer brand by US dollar sales, Corona Extra, Pacifico, Victoria) under exclusive US brewing/distribution rights, plus a smaller, actively-being-reshaped wine and spirits portfolio (Robert Mondavi, Kim Crawford, The Prisoner, Mi CAMPO Tequila) after divesting a large chunk of lower-end wine brands in 2025. Revenue comes from wholesale shipments to US beer/wine distributors; the beer business is the profit engine (~37-38% operating margin guided) while wine and spirits is a much smaller, lower-margin, turnaround-stage segment.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Modelo Especial is the #1 dollar-share beer brand in the US and the beer segment continues to be the #1 dollar-share gainer in Circana-tracked channels even through a soft demand period — durable brand strength, not just favorable comps.
2. Depletions troughed and improved sharply (from FY26's -4%/-2% guided decline to Q1 FY2028's -0.3% actual), consistent with the demand shock being cyclical/macro (tariff and Hispanic-consumer-spending related) rather than structural share loss, as the triage itself hypothesized.
3. Valuation (~9.8x NTM comparable P/E, ~8%+ FCF yield) already prices in a flat-to-declining outlook, capping downside if the beer recovery continues, while wine-and-spirits turnaround (organic net sales +8% in the latest quarter) is a free option not yet reflected in the multiple.

**Bear:**
1. An active federal securities class action (Meza v. Constellation Brands, W.D.N.Y., Case No. 6:25-cv-6107) alleges the company made materially false or misleading statements about the beer business's prospects; a motion to dismiss was fully briefed as of December 12, 2025 with oral argument held July 22, 2026 (source: Form 10-Q, accession 0000016918-26-000029) — **no ruling was found in the filings reviewed for this dossier**, so this is an unresolved overhang, not a settled matter. Related derivative suits (Silva, Mason, Wasserman) are stayed pending the Meza outcome.
2. The 10-Q's own forward-looking-statement risk factors explicitly list "our President and Chief Executive Officer transition" as a named risk — leadership change during an already-fragile demand recovery adds execution uncertainty.
3. Beer demand is exposed to immigration-enforcement/Hispanic-consumer-spending dynamics that are macro/political, not within management's control, and the current -0.3% depletions print, while much improved, is still negative — one more adverse quarter would reopen the original triage concern rather than resolve it.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Beer depletions turn negative by more than **-2%** YoY for two consecutive quarters (reopening the FY26-style demand shock).
2. An adverse ruling or settlement in the Meza securities class action that results in a disclosed material liability or admission.
3. Consolidated net debt/comparable EBITDA rises above **3.5x** (target/current ~3.0x).
4. FY2027 comparable EPS guidance ($11.20-$11.90) is cut in any subsequent quarterly release.
5. The CEO transition disclosed as a risk factor results in a strategy change materially deviating from the current beer-first capital-allocation priorities.

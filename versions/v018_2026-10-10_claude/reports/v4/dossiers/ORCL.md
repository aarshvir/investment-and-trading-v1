# Oracle Corporation (ORCL) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Verdict: WATCH** — condition to upgrade: free cash flow turns positive (or credibly on a funded, disclosed path to positive within 2 fiscal years) without a further credit downgrade.
One-sentence reason: an enormous, real ($664B) contracted backlog is being built with debt-funded capex so large that free cash flow has gone deeply negative and S&P has cut Oracle to one notch above junk — the backlog is genuine, but the balance-sheet and single-customer-concentration risk are severe enough that this is "not now," not "buy with a reservation."

## 1. Business in plain English
Oracle's legacy business is on-premises database licenses and support (mature, high-margin, slow-growing). The growth story is Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) — renting out GPU/CPU compute capacity — sold in very large, multi-year contracts, most prominently a reported ~$300B, multi-year OCI capacity deal with OpenAI and involvement in the Stargate data-center buildout (alongside SoftBank, OpenAI and MGX). Oracle is functioning increasingly like a capital-intensive AI-infrastructure landlord, not just a software vendor, and is financing the buildout with a rapidly rising debt load.

## 4. Guidance track record / backlog trajectory (the real growth signal here)
Oracle does not guide quarterly revenue with the same precision as NVDA; the trackable, filed metric is **Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO)**: **[Corrected 2026-10-08: Oracle does give numeric guidance: Q2 FY27 revenue +30-34%, non-GAAP EPS $1.85-1.93; FY27 revenue at least $90bn, non-GAAP EPS $8.10 (Q1 FY27 release)]**
- FY2025 year-end (31-May-2025): RPO $138B.
- Q1 FY2026 (Aug-2025): RPO $455B (per press coverage). [1]
- FY2026 year-end (31-May-2026): RPO $638B.
- Q1 FY2027 (31-Aug-2026, latest reported): RPO **$664B**, after booking **>$30B of additional AI cloud contracts** in the quarter. Of the $664B, ~13% is expected to convert to revenue in the next 12 months, 37% in months 13–36, 34% in months 37–60, the remainder after. [2][3]
This is an extraordinary, real, filed acceleration (RPO up ~4.8x in five quarters) — the bookings momentum is not in question. What is in question is whether Oracle can fund the capex to deliver on it without further credit deterioration (§6).

## 5. Moat and competition
- OCI is a distant #4 cloud-infrastructure provider behind AWS/Azure/GCP; its "moat" here is not market share but **contracted, prepaid-leaning capacity deals** — per management commentary, "most of the increase in RPO...relat[es] to large scale AI contracts where Oracle does not expect to have to raise any incremental funds," because equipment is either funded upfront via customer prepayments or supplied directly by the customer. [3] If true in full, this materially de-risks the capex-funding story; this analyst could not independently verify what fraction of the *already-spent* $55.7B FY2026 capex (§6) was prepayment-funded versus balance-sheet-funded, which is the crux of the bear case.
- **Stargate/OpenAI structure:** Stargate LLC plans $500B of AI infrastructure investment over four years ($100B deployed immediately); SoftBank and OpenAI each committed $19B (40% ownership each), Oracle and MGX $7B each, remainder from limited partners and debt. Oracle's exposure is smaller than headlines suggest at the JV level, but Oracle's own OCI capex (separate from the JV contribution) is what has driven its FCF negative. [4][5]
- **Customer concentration — the central risk:** OpenAI is reported to be Oracle's single largest new-bookings driver. OpenAI itself carries ~$665B of purchase commitments that do not appear as debt on its own books, alongside reports questioning whether OpenAI can fund >$1 trillion of promised compute spend — i.e., Oracle's growth engine is contractually enormous but its single largest counterparty's own ability to pay is externally debated. [4][6] This is the same circularity risk flagged in NVDA.md/AVGO.md, concentrated in one counterparty here rather than spread across six.

## 7. Valuation — reverse DCF (no V1 systematic valuation row for ORCL; standard constant-FCF-growth reverse DCF is not meaningful here because trailing FCF is negative)
Given negative TTM FCF, this analyst used a **revenue-growth-plus-margin-recovery** reverse DCF instead: assume FCF margin improves linearly from today's −40% (TTM FCF/TTM revenue) to a terminal margin over 5 years, then holds flat for years 6–10; solve for the revenue CAGR that justifies EV (market cap $414,554M + net debt ≈$93–130B, i.e. EV ≈ $508–545B, using total debt $129.5B less cash $36.4B as the central net-debt estimate, WACC 8.5%, terminal growth 3.5%).
| Terminal FCF margin assumed | Implied 10y revenue CAGR |
|---|---|
| 15% (pessimistic — IaaS-style thin margins persist) | 16.9% |
| 20% (moderate recovery) | 13.2% |
| 25% (optimistic — approaches mature SaaS/hyperscaler-infra economics) | 10.4% |
| 30% (best case) | 8.2% |
**This range is the central finding of this valuation: the answer to "what growth is priced in" depends almost entirely on an unverifiable margin-recovery assumption**, not on the revenue growth rate itself (which is well-evidenced by the RPO backlog). Net-debt assumption (tested $100–160B) moves the answer by only ~1pt either way.
- **Bear case:** revenue CAGR ≈5–8%, margin recovery stalls near 10–15% (heavy compute-hosting economics persist, OpenAI concentration risk materializes, a further downgrade to junk raises the cost of capital and forces more dilution).
- **Base case:** revenue CAGR ≈12–15% (near-term RPO conversion supports continued strong growth, moderating as bookings growth normalizes), margin recovers to ~20% by year 6–7.
- **Bull case:** revenue CAGR ≈20%+, margin recovers to 28–30% (OCI economics approach hyperscaler-infra norms, RPO fully monetized without customer stress).
**Implied vs. base: IN LINE**, but this label is fragile: at the moderate (20%) margin assumption the implied 13.2% sits inside the 12–15% base range; at a more conservative 15% margin the implied 16.9% would be **above** any reasonable base case. ## 8. Bull case
1. RPO of $664B, up ~4.8x in five quarters, is a real, filed, contracted backlog — not a projection.
2. Management states most incremental AI-contract capex is prepayment- or customer-supplied-equipment funded, which if true substantially de-risks the funding gap.
3. Operating margin is nominally improving (28%→35%) even during the heaviest capex years.

## 9. Bear case
1. TTM FCF ≈ −$28.7B and independent estimates see the annual gap widening toward −$42B if spending continues at pace — this is happening now, not a projection.
2. S&P has already cut Oracle to BBB−, one notch above junk, with an adjusted leverage path toward mid-4x — a second downgrade is a real, proximate risk, not a tail scenario.
3. The single largest driver of bookings growth (OpenAI) itself carries externally-debated ability to fund >$1 trillion of promised compute spend — Oracle's growth engine is only as strong as one counterparty's balance sheet.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria — thesis-invalidation triggers (what would change the verdict / break the thesis)
1. S&P or Moody's downgrades Oracle to junk (BB+ or below).
2. FCF remains negative beyond FY2028 (more than two further fiscal years of negative FCF).
3. RPO growth decelerates below 15% YoY for two consecutive quarters (bookings momentum breaks).
4. OpenAI (or any single customer) renegotiates or cancels more than 20% of its contracted RPO.
5. Adjusted net debt/EBITDA rises above 5x.

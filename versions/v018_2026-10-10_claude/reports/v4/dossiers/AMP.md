## 2. Business in plain English

Ameriprise is a diversified financial-services holding company built around three businesses: **Advice & Wealth Management** (its largest segment — roughly 10,000+ affiliated financial advisors managing client assets for a fee, plus banking/lending products), **Asset Management** (Columbia Threadneedle Investments, running mutual funds/ETFs/institutional mandates), and a smaller, run-off-oriented **Retirement & Protection Solutions** legacy annuity/insurance book. It earns most of its money on fees tied to assets under management/administration (AUM/AUA) rather than on spread or underwriting risk — a capital-light model that requires relatively little balance-sheet capital per dollar of revenue, which is why its return on equity is structurally very high (see §6).

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:**
1. AUM/AUA growing 13–19% across every segment (Q2 2026 YoY), a genuine structural tailwind (flows + markets), not a one-quarter pop.
2. Capital-light model returns ~90%+ of operating earnings to shareholders every quarter — one of the most shareholder-friendly capital-return policies in the S&P 500 financial sector, directly compounding per-share value via buybacks.
3. Single-digit NTM P/E (9.8x) for a business generating 62% ROE is a genuinely unusual combination even after accounting for the thin-equity-base mechanical effect.

**Bear:**
1. ROE sits at roughly the 80th percentile of AMP's own 10-year history — some mean reversion (already partly priced by V1's base case) is a real risk, not a remote one, if markets fall or flows reverse.
2. GAAP earnings are genuinely volatile quarter to quarter from insurance/annuity mark-to-market effects — a real legacy-block risk that a purely "Adjusted operating EPS" read can understate.
3. Continuous heavy buybacks (>$2.9bn/yr) that have driven much of the ROE/EPS story could decelerate if capital generation weakens, removing a key compounding lever.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria

1. TTM ROE falls below **40%** (vs. ~62% now) for two consecutive quarters — would indicate faster-than-expected mean reversion.
2. Total AUM+AUA growth (YoY) falls below **5%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. 13–19% segment growth now).
3. Capital returned to shareholders falls below **60% of operating earnings** for two consecutive quarters (vs. ~91% in Q2 2026) — would signal a capital-return policy change.
4. Any named, material litigation or regulatory enforcement matter (e.g. a cash-sweep-program-style industry action) is disclosed in a future 10-Q's Legal Proceedings item.
5. Adjusted operating EPS YoY growth turns negative for any quarter (it has been +16% to +22% in each of the last four).

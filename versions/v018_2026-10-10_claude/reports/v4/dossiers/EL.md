## 1. Verdict

**WATCH** — overturning the triage's implicit "advance" optimism on valuation discipline grounds, not on business quality. One-sentence reason: the FY2026 turnaround is real and primary-source-confirmed (organic sales accelerating quarter over quarter, FCF nearly doubled, adjusted EPS +24–34% guided for FY2027), but only one fiscal year into it, and the reverse DCF shows the 2026-09-25 price already embeds more sustained FCF growth than a fair base case supports — a great business, one year into proving itself, priced for more than one year of proof can justify. Condition to revisit: two more quarters of organic growth ≥3% and adjusted operating margin tracking toward the FY2027 12.7–13.5% target with no incremental litigation charge.

## 2. Business in plain English

Estée Lauder makes and sells prestige beauty products — skin care (La Mer, Estée Lauder, Clinique), makeup (MAC, Estée Lauder), fragrance (Jo Malone, Le Labo) and hair care (Aveda) — through department stores, specialty retailers, travel retail (airport/duty-free) and its own e-commerce, mostly outside the mass-market channel. It makes money on gross margins near 73–76%, monetized through a mix of North America/EMEA "The Americas"/"Europe, Middle East & Africa" and Asia/Pacific regions, with mainland China and travel retail (notably Hainan) historically an outsized swing factor. Competitive position rests on brand equity and department-store/travel-retail distribution relationships built over decades; new CEO Stéphane de La Faverie (since 2025) is executing a company-wide restructuring ("One ELC") targeting cost savings and operating-margin recovery after several difficult years.

## 3. Why the model likes it — and whether that's durable

Quant context (b1_live_scores.csv): decile 5 / quintile 3 (mid-pack composite), but momentum 61st percentile and SUE (earnings surprise) 80th percentile — the model is picking up the real, recent earnings beats. Quality-family reads are mixed: ROE 87th percentile (flattered by a genuinely small equity base post-impairments — see §6 — not by superior returns on a normal balance sheet) and asset-growth 40th percentile. **Durable**: three straight quarters of accelerating organic sales growth (Q1 FY26 +3%, Q2 +4%, Q4 +5% **[Corrected 2026-10-08: omits Q3 FY26 at +2%; path was +3, +4, +2, +5, not steady acceleration]** — see §4/§5) and FCF nearly doubling YoY are real, filed, primary-source facts, not narrative. **Not yet proven durable**: the 49% one-year volatility (triage's own flag) and the fact that FY2026's GAAP operating result was still a loss in the final quarter (Q4 FY26, driven by continued restructuring cash charges — see §4) show the turnaround is not yet fully clean even on management's own numbers.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **GAAP vs. Adjusted gap is large and requires care**: FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS $0.50 vs. Adjusted EPS $2.51 — a $2.01/share gap, reconciled in the filing as $1.83/share of restructuring/other charges plus $0.18/share of securities class-action settlement, net of a small FX item. FY2027 guidance keeps a smaller but still real gap (GAAP $2.52–$2.85 vs. Adjusted $3.10–$3.35), because restructuring charges continue into FY2027 ("vast majority of the full run-rate benefits still expected to be realized during fiscal 2027"). **This dossier treats GAAP as primary and Adjusted as a labeled reconciliation, per the sector playbook — never the reverse.**
- **FCF conversion improved sharply**: FY2026 operating cash flow $1,773m (FY2025: $1,272m) less capex $457m (FY2025: $602m) = **FCF $1,316m** (FY2025: $670m) — FCF nearly doubled YoY, a genuinely strong, primary-source-confirmed cash improvement, not just an accounting narrative.
- **Balance sheet**: cash $3,498m (FY2025: $2,921m); long-term debt $6,803m (FY2025: $7,314m) plus ~$503m current portion; net debt ≈ **$3,808m**. TTM EBITDA ≈ operating income $780m + D&A $796m = $1,576m → **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 2.4x**, improved from the prior year and moderate for the sector. Stockholders' equity fell to ~$3.9–4.0bn (from a much larger base pre-impairment) — the FY2025 goodwill/intangible impairments (§4) permanently reduced the equity base, which is why ROE now screens unusually high; this is a genuine artefact of past write-downs, not current-period outperformance, and should discount the quant Quality-family ROE score accordingly.
- **Dividend**: $1.40/share declared for FY2026 ($0.35/quarter), paid throughout the restructuring — not cut, a modest positive signal on management's confidence in the cash outlook.
- **Litigation/impairment history is real and recent, not merely historical**: $159m Talcum-litigation settlement charges (FY2025), $911m Americas goodwill impairment + $375m Asia/Pacific intangible impairment (FY2025), and an $84m loss-contingency (net of insurance) for a **pending securities class-action settlement recorded in FY2026** — see §11.

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse DCF

NTM P/E ~27.3x, trailing GAAP P/E ~189x (distorted by the depressed $0.50 GAAP EPS base — the trailing multiple is not a meaningful cheap/expensive signal this year and should be disregarded in favor of NTM). No V1 systematic valuation row exists for EL in `v1_valuation_table.csv` — **v1_verdict is null**; this section is this dossier's own reverse DCF. Method: two-stage DCF (10-year explicit FCF growth + 3% terminal growth) on FY2026 as-reported FCF ($1,316m — no restructuring normalization applied, a deliberately conservative choice given the litigation/impairment history in §6). Cost of capital: CAPM cost of equity = 5.17% + 1.274 (sourced beta, FMP profile) × 4.14% ERP = 10.4%; after-tax cost of debt ≈ 3.7%; weights from EV (10.0% debt / 90.0% equity, EV ≈ $38.0bn = $34.2bn market cap + $3.8bn net debt) → **WACC ≈ 9.8%**.

**Implied 10-year FCF growth at the 2026-09-25 close ($94.47): ≈ 11.7%** **[Corrected 2026-10-08: recomputed on equity basis with SBC deducted ($1,006m FCF): about 10.0% at 10.4% cost of equity; still above the 7-8% base]** (sensitivity: 8.6% at WACC 8.5%, to 13.2% at WACC 10.5% — highly sensitive because FY2026 FCF is still coming off a depressed, one-year-old base).

**My base case: ~7–8%/yr** — supported by the FY2027 guide (organic sales +3–5%, margin expanding toward 12.7–13.5% from ~10.7–11.0% in FY2026 **[Corrected 2026-10-08: FY2026 adjusted operating margin was 11.2%]**, i.e. meaningful but not unlimited margin-recovery runway versus EL's pre-crisis ~18–20% operating margins) — but **clearly below the ~11.7% implied at the central WACC estimate, and below the implied growth even at the low end of the WACC sensitivity range (8.6%)**. This is the core of the WATCH call: the market is pricing a faster, more complete margin/growth recovery than one year of (genuinely good) evidence can yet support.

Bear/base/bull (3-year annualized total return, dividend-inclusive):
- **Bear** (g = −5%: China/travel-retail relapse, incremental litigation charge, restructuring benefits disappoint): **−42%/yr**
- **Base** (g = 7.5%): **−2%/yr** (a directly disciplined result of the price already embedding faster growth than the base case)
- **Bull** (g = 14%: turnaround fully succeeds, margins return to high-teens ahead of schedule): **+24%/yr**

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:**
1. Four consecutive quarters of beat-and-raise, with organic sales growth accelerating each quarter (Q1 +3% to Q4 +5%) — the turnaround has real, improving primary-source momentum, not just a low base.
2. FCF nearly doubled YoY ($670m to $1,316m) while still absorbing $813m of restructuring cash costs — underlying cash generation is stronger than the headline GAAP loss quarters suggest.
3. MAC back to #1 in color cosmetics and travel retail (Hainan) growth resumed double digits per the triage's cited sources — brand-level share gains, if durable, support the margin-recovery thesis independent of macro.

**Bear:**
1. Reverse DCF: at a fair, sourced-beta WACC, the price already embeds sustained double-digit FCF growth for a decade — a lot to ask of a company one year into a restructuring, with restructuring charges still running into FY2027.
2. Litigation and impairment history is recent and real: $911m + $375m of impairments and $159m of Talcum-litigation charges in FY2025, plus a fresh $84m securities-class-action settlement recorded in FY2026 — this is not a company with a clean recent record, and 49% one-year volatility (triage) reflects genuine, not merely sentiment-driven, uncertainty.
3. China/travel-retail — historically EL's biggest swing factor — must prove durable beyond the quarters reported so far; the FY2027 guide itself flags "a lower base of travel retail shipments in the first half of fiscal 2026" as one of the reasons FY2027 organic growth should look strong in H1, meaning some of the guided growth is a base-effect, not pure demand acceleration.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria

1. Organic net sales growth falls back below 3% for **two consecutive quarters** (it has ranged 3–5% for the last four quarters; **[Corrected 2026-10-08: wrong: last four quarters ranged 2-5% (Q3 FY26 +2%)]** a reversal would break the accelerating trend).
2. Adjusted operating margin fails to reach at least **12.0%** for FY2027 (vs. the guided 12.7–13.5%).
3. Any **new** goodwill/intangible impairment or litigation settlement charge exceeding $100m is recorded in FY2027.
4. Net debt/EBITDA rises above **3.0x** (vs. ~2.4x now).
5. GAAP operating income is negative for **two consecutive quarters** in FY2027 (it was negative in Q4 FY2026 alone due to restructuring cash costs — a repeat pattern would suggest the restructuring is not on the disclosed timeline).

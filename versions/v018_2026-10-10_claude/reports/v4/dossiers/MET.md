## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight), horizon 12-36 months. Overturns the quick triage ("No": "FCF yield -33.7%, growth looks like comp rebound"). The -33.7% FCF yield is a Yahoo artefact (d4 shows FCF -$21.0bn and total debt $50.3bn; the 10-Q shows consolidated debt of ~$20.1bn and MetLife's own free-cash-flow metric is a holding-company dividend-capacity ratio, 81% average for 2024-25). Growth is not only a rebound: adjusted EPS rose 8% in FY2025 and 20% in Q2'26 on volume plus underwriting. Named reservation: adjusted earnings exclude large recurring derivative and investment losses (TTM GAAP EPS $5.22 vs adjusted ~$9.71, 54%) and the recent step-up leans on favorable Group Benefits underwriting and private-equity variable investment income. Reverse DCF implies roughly 0-1.4% long-run growth in distributable earnings against my 6% base.

## 2. Business in plain English
One of the largest US life insurers. It sells employee benefits (group life, disability, dental) to US employers, retirement and annuity-type products including pension risk transfers (RIS), life and health insurance in Japan/Asia and Latin America, plus EMEA and an institutional asset manager (MetLife Investment Management, enlarged by the PineBridge purchase). Earnings come from premiums, fees and investment spread on a ~$480bn investment portfolio. Its edge is scale, brand and group-benefits underwriting data.

## 8. Bull / bear
Bull: (1) broad volume growth in every segment and PRT/longevity pipeline; (2) adjusted ROE at the top of 15-17% with buybacks ~4-5%/yr and a fresh $3.0bn authorization; (3) Q3'26 VII >= $600m gives an earnings tailwind. Bear: (1) adjusted EPS excludes ~$3bn/yr of derivative/investment losses, GAAP ROE 11.5% vs adjusted 17.0%; (2) Group Benefits underwriting (25% earnings jump) and private-equity VII are volatile; Q2'26 VII missed by ~40%; (3) rate and credit sensitivity: $18.7bn unrealized losses, $39.6bn commercial mortgages, yen and equity-market exposure in Asia.

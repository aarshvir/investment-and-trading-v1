## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (horizon 12-36 months). Cheap (12.2x FY26 non-GAAP EPS; about 5.7% normalized free cash flow yield; a $3B buyback authorization that equals 28% of the market cap) and the price does not need heroic growth (implied about 5.6%/yr of free cash flow growth vs my 6% base). But FY26 guidance has been cut twice since March, CooperVision (CVI, about two thirds of revenue) grew 0% in the latest quarter with a US channel-inventory reduction that "will continue to impact Q4", and my base 3-year return (+6.8%/yr) is below a 10% required return. What would change it: a December 2026 print showing CVI organic growth back to 0-2% and FY27 guidance at or above about $4.70 EPS with free cash flow at or above $650M; or a price near $45 (10x).

Triage (Q07) said "cheap but needs evidence of reacceleration"; it cited revenue growth of +0.6% and sourced "general knowledge". I confirm the conclusion with primary evidence and correct one point: FY26 nine-month revenue growth is +4.8% reported (3,171.8 vs 3,027.3), not a flatline; the stall is in the latest quarter and is partly channel destocking. There is no V1 row for COO, so v1_verdict is null.

## 2. Business in plain English
Cooper makes contact lenses (CooperVision: daily MyDay, toric/multifocal, MiSight for myopia control in children) sold to eye-care professionals and retailers worldwide, and medical devices and services for women's health and fertility (CooperSurgical: IVF lab products, clinics' consumables, cord-blood storage, office and surgical devices, the Paragard IUD). Revenue is repeat-purchase consumables. CVI competes in an oligopoly with Alcon, J&J Vision and Bausch + Lomb; CSI is a niche leader in fertility consumables.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and whether that is durable
b1 composite 0.851 (decile 9, live rank 74): value (V 0.84) and stability (S 0.97) drive it; Q 0.50, momentum only 0.26 (12-1 month return +2.2%). The value reading (earnings yield 5.4%, FCF yield 6.4% TTM) is real but partly flattered: TTM free cash flow of $678M includes a $111M working-capital inflow (9 months vs a $260M outflow last year) and does not yet include the roughly $263M net cash paid to settle the fertility-media recall claims in August 2026. The earnings yield is on non-GAAP EPS, which excludes amortization of acquired intangibles ($0.73 a share for nine months). Treat as cheap, but a cheapness that the market has priced for a reason.

## 8. Bull and bear

# Prudential Financial (PRU) - Fundamental Diligence Dossier (F127, standard depth)

**Verdict: WATCH**, 12-36 month horizon. Reason: a cheap (about 8x) life insurer and asset manager with a 4.7% dividend yield, but its Japanese life unit is under a voluntary sales suspension through 5 Nov 2026 with an open regulatory inspection, the cost estimate already moved once (90 days to 270 days), and an extension costs $50-60M pre-tax a month. Condition that changes it to INCLUDE-SMALL: Japan FSA inspection closes without group-wide sanctions and Prudential of Japan resumes sales on or before the Q4 2026 results (February 2027). The price implies roughly 1.5% a year distribution growth against my 4-5% base, so valuation is not the obstacle; event risk is.

## 1. Business in plain English
Prudential sells retirement products (pension risk transfer, annuities), group and individual life insurance in the US, life insurance in Japan and Brazil, and runs PGIM, a $1.49 trillion asset manager (PGIM AUM, 30 Jun 2026). It earns fees, investment spread and underwriting margin, and returns about $3B a year to shareholders. Moat: scale, PGIM brand and Japan distribution via Life Planners, which is the very channel now under regulatory scrutiny.

## 2. Why the model likes it / durability

## 4. Guidance track record
PRU gives no numeric annual EPS guidance in the four releases I read; it gives quantified estimates only for the Japan suspension and a cost-savings target.
- Japan impact, from the Q2 2026 10-Q (0001137774-26-000176, MD&A): "We estimate that the suspension of new sales as extended will result in a reduction of pre-tax adjusted operating income in the range of $525 to $575 million for 2026, inclusive of the six month impact above, and in the range of $400 to $450 million for 2027". Also: "The suspension of sales resulted in an estimated reduction of $235 million in International Businesses' pre-tax adjusted operating income through the first six months of 2026." And: "Should the suspension of new sales activities extend beyond November 2026, we estimate that International Businesses' pre-tax adjusted operating income would be reduced by $50 to $60 million per each additional month."
- The 21 Apr 2026 8-K (0001193125-26-166839) extended the original 90-day suspension by "an additional 180 days" and described the estimated financial impacts on an investor call; I did not find the earlier 90-day-period dollar estimate in the documents retrieved, so I cannot state whether the range was raised from a prior range (status: raised in scope by duration, prior range not verified).
- Cost savings: 10-Q: "an enterprise-wide cost savings initiative expected to reduce annual operating expenses by approximately $750 million by year-end 2028."
- The 2026 annual capital guidance and the PGIM margin target are referenced ("on track to achieve its margin expansion target", Q1 2026 release) without a numeric figure in the text I read; not quoted.

## 7. Bull and bear
Bull: (1) $750M annual cost savings by 2028 is about $1.7 of after-tax EPS on 345M shares if retained; (2) Japan drag shrinks from $525-575M pre-tax in 2026 to $400-450M in 2027 and sales restart; (3) PGIM AUM grows 4% with third-party net inflows ($4.6B in Q2 2026) and retirement sales momentum ($6.8B in Q2 2026).
Bear: (1) an extension beyond Nov 2026 costs $50-60M pre-tax per month, and the company is "proactively reviewing the sales practices" of Gibraltar Life, so contagion is possible; (2) retirement earnings were flat (392M vs 397M) with weaker PRT underwriting; (3) balance-sheet credit and real-estate marks, with AOCI at -$4.1B, could impair book value.

## 8. Kill criteria
1. Prudential of Japan new-sales suspension extended beyond 5 Nov 2026 by more than 90 days (cost $50-60M pre-tax a month).
2. Japan FSA imposes a business improvement or suspension order on Gibraltar Life or a group Japanese entity, or a stated ESR for any Japanese life subsidiary falls below the company's target.
3. Quarterly adjusted operating EPS below $3.30 (the Q4 2025 level) for two consecutive quarters excluding identified assumption updates.
4. Parent company highly liquid assets below $3.0B (4.2B at 30 Jun 2026) or share repurchases cut below the $250M a quarter pace.
5. PICA RBC ratio below 375% at any statutory year-end (415% at 31 Dec 2025).

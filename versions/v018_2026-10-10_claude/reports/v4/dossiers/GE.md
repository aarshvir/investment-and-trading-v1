# GE Aerospace (GE) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Verdict: WATCH** — thesis horizon 12–36 months if valuation resets or the growth algorithm proves out for longer than 3 years.
One-sentence reason: the LEAP/CFM engine duopoly and its ~70%-of-revenue aftermarket annuity are as good a moat as this analyst has diligenced this wave, guidance has been raised or held at every checkpoint for three straight quarters, but at ~35–42x earnings the stock already prices in about 17%/yr FCF growth for a decade versus a ~13%/yr evidence-based base case, so it is a "great business, full price," not a name to add at the current price.

## 1. Business in plain English
GE Aerospace designs, manufactures and services jet engines for commercial aircraft (Commercial Engines & Services, "CES" — LEAP via the 50/50 CFM International joint venture with Safran, plus GEnx, GE90) and military/defense propulsion (Defense & Propulsion Technologies, "DPT" — T700, F110, Avio Aero). It makes most of its money the way razor-blade businesses do: engines are often sold near breakeven, and the real profit comes from decades of high-margin spare parts, overhaul and repair ("shop visits") on an installed base of ~50,000 commercial and ~30,000 military engines. GE Aerospace is the standalone industrial successor to the old General Electric conglomerate, following the 2023 spin-off of GE HealthCare and the April-2024 spin-off of GE Vernova; the consolidated entity still carries a legacy run-off insurance block and a Polish mortgage portfolio (Bank BPH) from the old GE Capital, which are not part of the aerospace operating business (see §6, §12 for entity-scope detail).

## 5. Moat and competition
- **Duopoly + annuity model:** CFM International (GE/Safran 50-50 JV) LEAP engines and legacy CFM56 compete narrowbody-for-narrowbody essentially only against Pratt & Whitney's PW1000G geared turbofan (which has had its own well-publicized durability/inspection issues industry-wide); GE also holds GEnx/GE90 widebody positions. ~70% of GE Aerospace's revenue is services/aftermarket on an installed base that is still young relative to its service life, i.e. shop-visit revenue should keep growing for years even without a single new engine sale.
- **LEAP durability program:** completed certification of the LEAP-1B durability kit (upgraded HPT blade) in Q2 2026, expected to ~2x time-on-wing, full fleet cutover from "beginning of 2027" — a real, dated engineering fix to a known durability issue, not yet fully executed.
- **Commercial momentum:** Q2 2026 orders $16.5bn (+17%); total engine deliveries +31% in H1 2026 (LEAP deliveries +41%); named wins include Copa Airlines (up to 120 LEAP-1B), Turkish Aerospace (F404), Leonardo Helicopters (CT7), American Airlines (300+ LEAP-1A), United/Delta (GEnx).
- **Defense & Propulsion Technologies:** smaller, lower-margin (13.8% Q2 2026 operating margin vs. CES 27.3%) but growing (orders +12% Q2 2026), with early-stage next-gen programs (XA102, GE426 for the USAF's Autonomous Collaborative Platform).

## 8. Bull case
1. A genuine engine duopoly with a young, growing installed base (~50,000 commercial + ~30,000 military engines) and a >$210bn services backlog — one of the highest-quality moats diligenced this wave, independent of near-term price.
2. Guidance has been raised or held at every checkpoint for three consecutive quarters, backed by verifiable order growth (+17% to +87% YoY across the last several quarters) and delivery growth (+31% H1 2026, LEAP +41%).
3. The LEAP-1B durability kit (certified Q2 2026, ~2x time-on-wing) directly addresses the industry's best-known engine-durability risk and should reduce shop-visit frequency risk once fully cut over from 2027.

## 9. Bear case
1. The price already implies ~17%/yr sustained FCF growth for a decade at a premium 35–42x multiple — a high bar even for an exceptional aftermarket annuity business, leaving little room for a normalization of growth toward mid-cycle industrial rates.
2. Consolidated financials still carry legacy run-off Insurance ($36.2bn liabilities) and Bank BPH Poland mortgage exposures explicitly flagged by the company as capital/liquidity risks in its own forward-looking-statement disclosure — a real, if secondary, source of tail risk not present in a "pure-play" engine maker.
3. The durability-kit fix is not yet fully executed (full fleet cutover begins 2027); any slippage, cost overrun or a new durability/safety finding (an industry-wide risk given the PW1000G precedent) would directly hit the aftermarket thesis the valuation depends on.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria — thesis-invalidation triggers (what would change this verdict)
1. FY adjusted EPS guidance is cut in an upcoming release (breaking the initiate→maintain→raise pattern of the last three checkpoints).
2. Total engine deliveries growth decelerates below +15% YoY for two consecutive quarters (LEAP ramp stalling).
3. Non-GAAP operating profit margin contracts below 20% for two consecutive quarters.
4. Total borrowings rise back above $20bn for two consecutive quarters (releveraging, reversing the current delever trend).
5. A confirmed new LEAP/CFM durability or safety issue requiring a fleet-wide airworthiness directive beyond the already-announced HPT-blade durability kit.

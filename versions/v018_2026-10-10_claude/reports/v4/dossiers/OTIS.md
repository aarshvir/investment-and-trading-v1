# OTIS — Otis Worldwide Corporation

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon: 12–24 months.
Reason: the Service annuity (≈67% of sales, ~23% segment operating margin even after this year's compression) is a genuine, well-evidenced moat, and the 25-Sep-2026 close prices in less 10-year growth than a reasonable base case — but two consecutive FY2026 adjusted-EPS guidance cuts and a CEO retirement announced 15-Sep-2026 make the next 2–3 quarters genuinely uncertain, so this is a starter position, not a full-weight holding.

## 2. Business in plain English
Otis is the world's largest elevator and escalator company by units serviced, spun off from Raytheon Technologies (formerly United Technologies) in April 2020. It designs, makes and installs new elevators/escalators (New Equipment segment) and — the far bigger profit driver — maintains, repairs and modernizes an installed base of roughly 2.4 million units worldwide under multi-year contracts (Service segment). Money is made mainly on the Service annuity: once a building buys an Otis elevator, Otis typically keeps the maintenance contract for decades, and that recurring revenue is much stickier and higher-margin than selling new units.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and durability
Otis passes on Value/quality-style scores (`b1_live_scores.csv`: EP 6.0%, FCF/P 6.8%, EBIT/EV 7.0%, live rank 282/503, momentum -20.5% trailing 12-1). The triage read — "market is over-discounting a China new-equipment problem that barely touches the recurring-revenue core" — is half right: the Service book is real and durable (see §4), but the primary-source Q2 2026 segment data (below) show the New Equipment weakness has already bled into Service-segment margin too, which is a newer and less "contained" development than the triage anticipated. That is an artefact worth discounting, not purely a durable-moat story right now.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF conversion:** Q2 2026 operating cash flow $267M / adjusted FCF $290M; H1 2026 OCF $680M / adjusted FCF $562M; FY2026 guide is adjusted FCF of $1.50–1.55bn (8-K exhibit 99, 2026-07-22). FY2025 actual adjusted FCF was $1.6bn on GAAP EPS of $3.50 / adjusted EPS $4.05 — conversion is reasonable for an asset-light Service model.
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** FY2025 GAAP EPS fell 14% to $3.50 while adjusted EPS rose 6% to $4.05 — a large and growing gap, driven mainly by restructuring/transformation ("UpLift") charges per the 10-Q (Note, accession 0001781335-26-000113): "In 2023, we announced UpLift to transform our operating model... organizational changes which result in restructuring[-type] costs." Q2 2026 GAAP EPS $1.12 vs adjusted EPS $1.01 — here GAAP is *above* adjusted, the reverse of FY2025, so the gap direction is not stable quarter to quarter; treat any single quarter's GAAP/adjusted comparison with caution.
- **Balance sheet (consolidated, 10-Q, 30-Jun-2026, accession 0001781335-26-000113):** cash $813M; long-term debt $8,226M (incl. current portion) + short-term borrowings $210M ≈ **total debt $8.44bn**; net debt ≈ $7.6bn. **Total shareholders' equity (deficit) is −$5,748M** (vs −$5,392M at 31-Dec-2025) — a legacy of spin-off accounting (large negative retained-earnings/APIC base) compounded by ongoing share buybacks (treasury stock −$5,005M at 30-Jun-2026 vs −$4,198M at 31-Dec-2025); this makes ROE not a meaningful metric for Otis and is disclosed here so it is not silently misread as distress. Net debt / FY2026 guided adjusted operating profit (~$2.4bn) ≈ 3.2x — consistent with the quant card's `net_debt_to_ebitda` of 3.0x.
- **Share count / capital return:** quarterly dividend raised from $0.42 to $0.44/share (10-Q Statement of Changes in Equity); ~$400M of buybacks in Q2 2026 alone ($800M H1 2026) against a $2.0bn program authorized 16-Jan-2025, of which ~$500M remained as of 30-Jun-2026 (10-Q, Item 2).

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) Service is 67–70% of sales and the segment carries a 23%+ margin even after this year's compression — an annuity book with high switching costs. (2) Modernization orders +9% cc and backlog +24–26% cc in Q2 2026 give revenue visibility for 2027+ regardless of New Equipment. (3) Stock is down ~20% trailing 12 months, a re-rating catalyst (a strong CEO successor, or New Equipment stabilization) could close much of that gap.
**Bear:** (1) Two consecutive FY2026 guidance cuts (Apr, Jul 2026) show the "contained" New Equipment problem is not contained — Service segment margin also compressed 170bps YoY in Q2 2026. (2) CEO Judy Marks (in the role since the 2020 spin) is retiring, with a successor search that could run to July 2027 — real execution-continuity risk layered on top of an already-cut guide. (3) Negative book equity and continued buybacks funded partly by debt leave less balance-sheet flexibility if New Equipment weakness deepens.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is cut a third time below the current $4.01–$4.05 range.
2. Organic New Equipment sales stay negative for 4 or more consecutive quarters.
3. Net debt / adjusted operating profit rises above 3.5x (from ~3.0x currently) **[Corrected 2026-10-08: net debt $7.6bn / guided adjusted operating profit $2.4bn is 3.2x]**.
4. No permanent CEO successor is named by July 2027 (the outside date in the transition agreement).
5. Modernization order growth decelerates below 10% (constant currency) for two consecutive quarters (from +9% cc / backlog +24–26% cc in Q2 2026).

# NKE — NIKE, Inc. (Diligence dossier, agent F69, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (24–36 month horizon), with a specific reservation: the headline FQ4 FY2026 "beat" (49.2% gross margin, $0.72 diluted EPS) was **inflated by a one-time, non-recurring $986M IEEPA tariff-refund benefit** (≈900bps of the 890bps margin gain, $0.52 of the $0.72 EPS); excluding it, gross margin was 40.2%, essentially flat YoY, and free cash flow fell 33% for the year. The world's largest sportswear brand, priced at >50% off its highs, is not obviously cheap once the one-off is stripped out, and FQ1 FY2027 results — due **2026-10-01, five days after this dossier** — are the near-term test of whether the promised margin inflection is real.

## 2. Business in plain English
Nike designs, markets and sells athletic footwear, apparel and equipment globally under the Nike and Converse brands, through wholesale partners and its own "Nike Direct" stores/digital channels. It earns high gross margins on brand strength, product innovation and marketing scale; its moat is brand equity, athlete/league endorsements and global distribution reach. Under CEO Elliott Hill (since Oct 2024), the company is executing a "Sport Offense" turnaround: rebuilding wholesale relationships, cutting stale inventory, and re-emphasizing performance/running product after several years of DTC overreach and stale lifestyle-product reliance.

## 8. Bull case
1. Balance sheet is a genuine strength through the downturn: net debt ≈ $0.4bn against $14.9bn of equity, giving Nike years of runway to fund the turnaround without financial distress, unlike many turnaround stories.
2. Management has given a specific, dated inflection point (FQ2 FY2027, i.e., the quarter ending November 2026) for underlying gross-margin expansion to begin, with FQ1 FY2027 flagged as the last quarter of "material" tariff headwind — a falsifiable near-term test (see kill criteria).
3. Wholesale revenue is growing (+6% reported FY2026) even as NIKE Direct declines (-6%), suggesting the marketplace-cleanup/wholesale-repair strategy is gaining traction with partners, which is the crux of the "Sport Offense" plan.

## 9. Bear case
1. The FQ4 FY2026 "beat" is not real operating improvement: ex the $986M one-off IEEPA benefit, gross margin was 40.2%, essentially flat YoY (down ~10bps) — the market may be paying for a margin inflection that has not yet shown up organically.
2. Greater China is now a structural, not cyclical, problem: full-year segment EBIT down 20% for a second consecutive year, with management itself guiding continued "marketplace cleanup" and reduced sell-in through FY2027 — no near-term stabilization is promised.
3. Free cash flow nearly halved from FY2024 levels and fell a further third in FY2026; buybacks were suspended (down 95% YoY) to preserve cash — capital return, a historical pillar of the Nike bull case, is currently absent.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable — what would break this thesis / invalidation triggers)
1. Underlying gross margin (**excluding** any tariff-refund benefit) fails to expand YoY in FQ2 FY2027 (quarter ending ~2026-11-30, reporting ~Dec 2026) as management explicitly guided.
2. Greater China segment revenue or EBIT (as reported each quarter) declines by more than 15% YoY for two more consecutive quarters (vs. -20% for FY2026 overall).
3. Full-year free cash flow (OCF − capex) for FY2027 comes in below FY2026's ~$2.18bn (i.e., the multi-year FCF decline continues into year three).
4. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 1.0x (from ~net-debt-neutral today) without a specific, disclosed strategic rationale (e.g., large buyback re-acceleration funded by new debt).
5. The pending consumer class action (Portland, OR, filed 2026-05-08) or the securities-related investigation results in a material adverse ruling, settlement, or confirmed SEC enforcement action (currently pre-litigation/investigation stage only).

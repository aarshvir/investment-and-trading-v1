## 2. Business in plain English
Colgate-Palmolive sells toothpaste, toothbrushes, personal-care and home-care products (Colgate, Palmolive, Softsoap, Hill's Pet Nutrition) in over 200 countries. It is the global #1 in toothpaste with roughly 40% volume share and has the deepest emerging-market distribution network of any Western consumer-staples company (Q03 triage). It makes money on brand-driven pricing power and scale manufacturing/distribution efficiency; Hill's pet food is now a meaningful, faster-growing profit contributor alongside the legacy oral/personal-care core.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Consolidated** stockholders' equity per XBRL `StockholdersEquity`: $363m (Mar-25) → $702m (Jun-25) → $856m (Sep-25) → **$54m (Dec-25)** → $145m (Mar-26) → $236m (Jun-26). This is genuinely near zero/thin — Colgate has bought back stock in excess of retained earnings for decades, so consolidated book equity is structurally minimal (Yahoo `bookValue` per share $0.296, `priceToBook` 290x). **This is a known, long-standing feature of CL's capital structure, not a new distress signal**, but it means there is essentially no equity cushion and leverage ratios must be read off debt/EBITDA rather than debt/equity.
- **Consolidated** long-term debt (XBRL `LongTermDebt`, 10-K FY2025 table): $7.839bn (2025-12-31) vs $7.005bn (2024-12-31) — debt is rising. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: total debt per the balance sheet was $7,857m at 30 Jun 2026 vs $7,988m at 31 Dec 2025 and $8,758m at 30 Jun 2025 (net debt $6,404m vs $7,346m a year earlier): debt is falling, not rising]** Net debt/EBITDA ≈ (7.86bn debt − 1.45bn cash) / 4.98bn TTM EBITDA (Yahoo) ≈ **1.3x** — moderate, investment-grade-consistent leverage.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Input-cost and tariff pressure genuinely eases through 2027, restoring the 21%+ operating margin last seen in 2024; (2) emerging-market volume reaccelerates on Colgate's unmatched EM distribution; (3) Hill's Pet Nutrition keeps outgrowing the core and becomes a larger share of mix, lifting the growth algorithm.
**Bear:** (1) Tariffs/input costs stay elevated and opex inflation (seen in the last two quarters) persists, capping EPS growth near zero for several more quarters; (2) EM currency weakness (a recurring CL risk) offsets constant-currency volume gains; (3) rising debt ($7.0bn→$7.8bn YoY) combined with near-zero equity **[Corrected 2026-10-06: total debt fell about $0.9bn YoY to $7,857m]** limits flexibility for further buybacks if a margin/FX shock hits simultaneously.

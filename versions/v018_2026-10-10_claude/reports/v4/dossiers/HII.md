# HII — Huntington Ingalls Industries (F149, wave 8, STANDARD depth)

**1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL (half weight).** Sole-source US Navy shipbuilder priced at about 13x NTM EPS with a $57.3bn backlog; the 25 Sep 2026 price implies roughly 5% 10-year cash-flow growth, below my 7% base. Named reservation: free cash flow is back-half loaded (H1'26 FCF was about -$611m against the guide "FY26 free cash flow between $500 and $600 million", cash is $12m) and EPS leans on non-cash pension income and contract-estimate adjustments. Horizon 24-36 months. Downgrade to WATCH if the FY26 FCF guide is cut at the Q3 release.

**2. Business in plain English.** HII builds and maintains nuclear aircraft carriers, submarines, destroyers and amphibious ships for the US Navy at two yards (Newport News, Ingalls) and sells unmanned systems, nuclear and technical services through Mission Technologies (about $3bn revenue, roughly 5% margin). Customer is essentially the US Government; contracts are largely cost-plus and fixed-price with slow, lumpy cash timing. Moat: only builder of US nuclear carriers, one of two for nuclear submarines; constraint is labour and supply-chain throughput, not demand.

**3. Why the model likes it (b1/d4).** Value family 66th pct: earnings yield 85th pct, book/price 92nd pct, sue 58th pct; quality family is weak (13th pct): OCF/assets 4th pct, accruals 4th pct (bad), ROE 26th pct; FCF yield 3rd pct (b1 fcfp -0.8%). Composite decile 3. Durable part: demand and cheap multiple. Artefact risk: the low P/E excludes that FCF has been poor (FY24 FCF $40m) and EPS includes a $213m non-operating retirement benefit (guidance table, about $4.4 per share after 17% tax on 39.4m shares). Triage (Q08): quality 3, growth 2, price-vs-growth 4, not advanced; I partly overturn on price/backlog but keep the quality reservation.

**8. Bull / bear.** Bull: (1) backlog $57.3bn (30 Jun 2026) is about 4.3x FY26 revenue with awards $6.7bn in Q2'26 (Ingalls FF(X) frigate lead-yard support; carrier and submarine work); (2) throughput improving (about 14% in 2025, target 15% in 2026) and new contracts priced for the post-COVID cost environment; (3) cheap on earnings and on backlog coverage. Bear: (1) FY26 FCF guide needs a $1.1-1.2bn H2 after an H1 of -$611m; (2) carriers are still generating unfavourable estimate changes (-$48m on USS George Washington RCOH in Q2'26) and a 2024 welding-quality investigation at Newport News remains open, outcome "cannot at this time predict"; (3) EPS includes a $213m pension credit and thin 5-6% margins leave little room for a fixed-price overrun.

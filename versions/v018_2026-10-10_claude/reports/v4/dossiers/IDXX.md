## 2. Business in plain English
IDEXX sells the instruments, reagent kits ("CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue") and cloud software that veterinary clinics use to run blood, urine and fecal-antigen tests in-house, plus reference-lab testing and water/livestock testing. Revenue is ~85% recurring (consumables + software), sold through a large placed-instrument base (IDEXX VetLab, Catalyst, SediVue). Customers are companion-animal veterinary clinics globally; IDEXX and Zoetis effectively duopolize point-of-care vet diagnostics in the U.S. Moat: high switching costs (instrument lock-in to proprietary reagents), scale in reference labs, and a large sales/service force clinics rely on.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) new-instrument cycle (inVue Dx cellular analyzer, fecal antigen tests) reaccelerates placements and recurring-revenue attach; (2) international (ex-U.S.) growth has been running double-digit and is a smaller, less penetrated base; (3) two consecutive guidance raises show execution momentum continuing into H2 2026.
**Bear:** (1) U.S. companion-animal clinical visits have been flat-to-down for several years — management calls this a "persistent headwind" every quarter, and IDEXX cannot grow revenue meaningfully faster than visit volumes plus price/mix indefinitely; (2) at 32–36x earnings, any deceleration below high-single-digit organic growth would likely compress the multiple materially (peer/duopoly partner Zoetis trades meaningfully cheaper); (3) the company paid an ~$80M judgment in April 2025 to conclude a legacy (2004–2017) patent-royalty dispute — resolved, but a reminder that litigation tail risk exists even for a "clean" compounder.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. CAG Diagnostics organic recurring-revenue growth < 5% for two consecutive quarters (vs. ~10-11% organic in Q1–Q2 2026).
2. A guidance **cut** (vs. the immediately preceding range) on revenue or comparable EPS.
3. Net debt / TTM operating income > 1.0x (currently ≈0.2x) — would signal a debt-funded acquisition or buyback change in capital policy.
4. GAAP-vs-comparable EPS gap widens beyond the current ~10–15% spread without a disclosed one-time driver.
5. U.S. clinical-visit growth (management's own disclosed metric) turns negative by more than 2% for two consecutive quarters.

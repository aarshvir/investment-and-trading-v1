## 2. Business in plain English
American Express issues its own charge/credit cards, operates the payment network those cards run on, and acquires
merchant transactions — a "closed loop" model (unlike Visa/Mastercard, it is issuer, network and acquirer at once).
It earns discount revenue from merchants (a fee on every transaction), net interest income on revolving card
balances, and card/membership fees, concentrated on affluent consumers and businesses who spend more per card and
default less (source: company profile, 10-Q period 2026-06-30, filed 2026-07-24, "Business Introduction").

## 3. Why the model likes it / is this durable

issuer and network economics) verified below against six consecutive quarters of accelerating, non-one-off spend and
revenue growth, not a single good quarter.

## 8. Bull case
1. Six-plus consecutive quarters of accelerating FX-adjusted Card Member spend growth (culminating in +9% Q2 2026,
   "the highest rate we've seen in three years," per the CEO in the Q2 2026 release) with best-in-class, non-eroding
   credit metrics — growth is not being bought with looser underwriting.
2. Heavy, sustained buybacks (~3%/yr share-count reduction) funded from a highly profitable core business, on top of
   organic EPS growth.
3. Reverse DCF shows meaningful valuation cushion versus the demonstrated growth rate even under conservative
   discount-rate assumptions.

## 9. Bear case
1. Merchant anti-steering/non-discrimination provisions — central to Amex's above-average merchant discount
   economics — are under renewed antitrust challenge (see red-flag scan); an adverse ruling on the OptBlue-focused
   cases would pressure the core economics differently than the 2018 *Ohio v. Amex* precedent addressed.
2. CET1 has drifted down for five straight quarters (10.6%→10.4%); continued balance-sheet growth without a
   commensurate profitability increase would eventually constrain buyback capacity.
3. Premium-card growth (Platinum refresh) is a large share of the recent acceleration; a normalization of that
   product-cycle tailwind would slow the growth rate that today's price is not even fully paying for, but could
   still disappoint a market that has re-rated the stock on the recent acceleration.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable triggers that would invalidate the thesis)
1. Net write-off rate (principal, interest & fees; consolidated, per the quarterly statistical supplement) rises
   above 3.0% for two consecutive quarters (vs. 2.2–2.3% today).
2. CET1/RWA (Basel III, consolidated) falls below 10.0% in any quarter without a stated capital-plan explanation.
3. FY guidance (revenue growth or EPS range) is cut at any subsequent quarterly release (none has occurred in the
   four releases reviewed).
4. Card Member spend growth (FX-adjusted) decelerates below 5% for two consecutive quarters.
5. An adverse final judgment (not just an adverse motion) in the Pizza Hazel or 5-Star General Store anti-steering
   antitrust litigation.

## 12. Red-flag scan
- **Litigation (10-Q, period 2026-06-30, Note 7 "Contingencies"):** *Pizza Hazel, Inc. et al. v. American Express Co.*
  (D. Mass., filed 2024-09-30) and *5-Star General Store (Bento LLC) et al. v. American Express Co.* (D.R.I., filed
  2024-03-21) — merchant class actions (OptBlue program) alleging Amex's anti-steering/non-discrimination merchant-

  (formerly Oliver) v. American Express Co.* (E.D.N.Y., filed 2019-01-29) — an indirect-purchaser antitrust class
  action by non-Amex cardholders. Company states none of these, in its current assessment, would have a material
  adverse effect, but calls the outcome uncertain (10-Q, same note).

### 3. Valuation re-test (price $308.89)
Equity-based reverse DCF: year-0 EPS $17.60 (FY26 guide midpoint), book value per share $48.41, constant 36% ROE for 10 years (reported ROE 36.4%), retention = growth in book value per share, terminal growth 3.0% with terminal ROE 25%, ten annual cash flows. A bank holding company, so P/TBV against ROTCE rather than FCF.
| Cost of equity | Implied 10-yr EPS CAGR | Value per share at 9% base | vs price |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9.4% (ERP 4.14%) | 6.2% | $372 | +20% |
| 9.9% (ERP 4.5%, central) | 7.3% | $344 | +11% |
| 10.4% (ERP 5.0%) | 8.6% | $317 | +3% |
Terminal sensitivity at 9.9%: terminal growth 2.5% / 3.5% gives 7.8% / 6.8%; terminal ROE 20% / 30% gives 7.7% / 7.1%. IRR at the 9% base is about 10.6% versus 9.9% required, a margin of only 0.7 pt. Implied growth is 1.7 pts below the base at the central rate and 0.4 pt below at ERP 5.0%: **in_line**, not "well below".
P/B and P/TBV cross-check: price / common book = 6.38x. Tangible book is about $40 a share (goodwill $4.87bn at 31 Dec 2025 plus $136m Swisscard goodwill and at least $0.36bn intangibles, all approximate; the intangibles line is not itemised in the filings read), so P/TBV is about 7.6x and ROTCE about 40%. Justified P/B = (ROE - g) / (k - g): at 36% ROE and 9.9% cost of equity a perpetual 5% growth gives 6.3x, which is the market's 6.4x; at 30% ROE it is 5.1x. The price already assumes ROE stays at 36% with 5% perpetual growth.

## 2. Business in plain English
Goldman Sachs is a global investment bank and asset manager: it advises on M&A and raises capital for
companies (Investment Banking), trades stocks/bonds/currencies/commodities and finances clients' positions
(Global Banking & Markets), and manages money for institutions and wealthy individuals (Asset & Wealth
Management, $4.04tn of assets under supervision). It has been steadily unwinding its lower-return consumer
lending push (Marcus, the Apple Card partnership) to refocus capital on these higher-return, less
capital-intensive franchises.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Triage (Q05, sourced to `data/triage_cards.csv`) flagged "42.5% revenue growth from a strong trading/banking
cycle, 16.9% ROE, at just 12.9x forward." Verified against the 2Q26 earnings release (Exhibit 99.2, filed
2026-07-14): 2Q26 net revenues $20,338M (+39% YoY, record), annualised ROE 23.5% (2Q26) — already
*above* the 16.9% triage used, because ROE has accelerated every quarter for a year per the four quarterly
earnings releases cited in §12: 14.2% (3Q25) → 16.0% (4Q25) → 19.8% (1Q26) → 23.5% (2Q26). Part of this is
durable (management states AWM/alternatives mix-shift
and the Apple Card exit lifted returns ~500bps since its last Investor Day) and part is cyclical (record
Equities intermediation/financing, record Debt underwriting, #1 in announced/completed M&A — all classic
late-cycle capital-markets strength that mean-reverts). The model's "growth" score is real but its
persistence at the current rate is not something the filings let me confirm.

## 5. Guidance track record
GS does not issue formal EPS/revenue guidance (typical for a trading-driven bank); it does guide effective
tax rate and expense discipline qualitatively each quarter. YTD 2026 effective tax rate 18.5%, up from 13.2%
in 1Q26 (fewer share-based-award tax benefits) — a modest, disclosed headwind, not a red flag. No formal
"beat/miss vs. prior guidance" table is applicable; the closest analogue is the accelerating results above,
which the firm itself attributes to a "flywheel of activity" it "expects to continue" (management's own
forward view, not a fact).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) IB fee backlog rose QoQ into 3Q26 even as debt-underwriting backlog fell — advisory strength
looks self-sustaining; (2) AWM alternatives platform hit a record $59bn quarterly fundraising, a durable,
less-cyclical fee stream; (3) capital return (dividend +25% YoY, $4bn/quarter buybacks) directly supports
per-share value even if trading normalizes.
**Bear:** (1) GBM (trading+banking) generated 76% of 2Q26 net revenues — a downturn in volumes/spreads
reverses the ROE story quickly, as 2Q25's dip (ROE well below the 1Q25/3Q25 quarters) already showed;
(2) CET1 buffer has compressed two straight quarters; **[Corrected 2026-10-08: CET1 rose in 2Q26 (12.5% to 12.9%); only the SLR fell two quarters, DV19]** (3) the Malaysia arbitration (~$1bn demand) and
ongoing securities class action are unresolved tail risks on a firm with $123bn of equity.

## 9. Kill criteria
1. Standardized CET1 ratio below 11.5% for two consecutive quarters.
2. GBM (Global Banking & Markets) net revenues down >15% YoY for two consecutive quarters.
3. Annualised ROE below 12% for two consecutive quarters.
4. An adverse 1MDB/Malaysia arbitration ruling requiring a cash payment >$500M.
5. Quarterly common dividend cut or suspended (current $5.00/share).

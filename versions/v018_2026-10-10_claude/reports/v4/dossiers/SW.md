# Smurfit Westrock plc (NYSE: SW / LSE: SWR) — Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F78 | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close | **Depth:** Standard

## 1. Verdict

**WATCH**, thesis horizon 24–36 months. Reason: a genuinely global, scale-leading containerboard/packaging franchise with an attractive ~3.9% dividend yield and a reasonable EV/EBITDA multiple, but FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance was **cut** this year (not raised), organic net sales growth is roughly flat (~1%), and the reverse-DCF-implied 10-year FCF growth (~7.2%/yr) sits *above*, not below, this analyst's own conservative base case — i.e., the price is asking for more than the current trajectory is delivering. This is a "priced for a synergy story that has to keep working, against a cost backdrop that is currently working against it" situation, not a broken business — hence WATCH rather than REJECT.

## 2. Business in plain English

Smurfit Westrock is the world's largest containerboard/corrugated-packaging company by scale, formed 8-Jul-2024 by the merger of Ireland's Smurfit Kappa and the U.S.'s WestRock. It grows trees/recycles fibre, makes paper (containerboard, kraftliner, coated recycled board) in its own mills, and converts that paper into corrugated boxes and consumer packaging for food, beverage, e-commerce and industrial customers across North America, Europe/APAC and Latin America. It earns money on the spread between fibre/energy input costs and the price it can charge per ton of paper and per box, so it is a scale-and-mill-utilisation business, not a differentiated-product one; its "moat" is being the low-cost, highest-scale integrated producer in most of the regions it competes in.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?

b1 factor data (25-Sep-2026): decile 4, composite ~0.38 — mid-pack, not a standout. Quality mixed (gp/assets pct 46%, ROE pct 35% — modest, depressed by merger integration and purchase-accounting amortisation), Value moderate (ep pct 63%, fcfp pct 48%, ebit/ev pct 77% — cheap on an EBIT/EV basis specifically, which fits a capital-intensive, high-D&A business), weak Momentum (pct 44%) and weak SUE (pct 52%). **Read:** the case here is a classic post-merger "cheap on EBIT/EV, cheap-ish on other Value measures, unexciting on everything else" profile — consistent with the market waiting to see whether merger synergies and pricing actions offset the freight/input-cost inflation management has flagged in each of the last two quarters. This is a real, evidence-based valuation gap, not a factor artefact, but it is a *value* case, not a growth or quality case, and needs the synergy delivery to actually show up in the numbers (see §5–6).

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:**
1. The company itself states it beat its original $400m merger-synergy target in year one; further cost-out (headcount down >3,000 in 2025, ~600,000 tons of high-cost capacity closed) is a self-help lever independent of the macro pricing cycle.
2. EMEA/APAC and Latin America regions are "outperforming," per management, with containerboard price increases being implemented and recovered with a normal lag — a genuine multi-region diversification most single-region packaging peers lack.
3. ~3.9% dividend yield, raised (not cut) through the recent guidance cut, provides a return floor while the synergy/integration story plays out.

**Bear:**
1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance was cut this year ($5.0–5.3bn → $4.9–5.1bn) on freight/input-cost inflation that management has now flagged for two consecutive quarters — the "recovery through pricing" is a forecast, not yet delivered.
2. Organic net sales growth is roughly flat (~1% YoY on clean same-entity quarters) two years after the merger closed — the growth thesis rests almost entirely on synergy capture and eventual pricing, not volume.
3. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA of ~2.5x limits flexibility if input costs stay elevated longer than management expects, and the reverse-DCF-implied FCF growth (7.2%/yr for a decade) is above this analyst's own base-case estimate.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable signposts that would break this thesis)

1. FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance is cut **again** below the current $4.9–5.1bn range (it has already been cut once, from $5.0–5.3bn, in Jul-2026).
2. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA (company-guide-midpoint basis) rises above **3.5x**.
3. Same-entity organic net sales growth is negative for **two consecutive quarters** (vs. the current ~+1–4% range).
4. The quarterly dividend ($0.4523/share) is cut or suspended.
5. The Brazil CARF tax dispute (~$156m) or an adverse appellate ruling produces a cash outflow of more than **$100m** in a single reporting period.

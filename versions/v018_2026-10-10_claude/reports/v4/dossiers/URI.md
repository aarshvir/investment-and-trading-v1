# United Rentals, Inc. (URI) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F93, standard depth)

**1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL (half weight), 12–36 month horizon.** The largest US equipment-rental company, a real
beneficiary of mega-project/data-center/infrastructure capex, with accelerating growth and raised guidance twice in
2026 — but it is a capex-intensive, cyclical industrial already priced at a full multiple, funding buybacks partly
with rising net debt. Reservation: construction/capex-cycle sensitivity and the debt-funded capital-return pace.

**2. Business in plain English.** United Rentals rents construction and industrial equipment (aerial work platforms,
earthmoving equipment, generators, specialty tools) to professional contractors, industrial customers, and — an
increasingly important growth vector — data-center and mega-infrastructure builders, through the largest branch
network in North America. It earns rental income on its fleet, plus equipment/parts sales. Its moat is scale (fleet
availability, national-account relationships, used-equipment residual-value management), not IP or switching costs.

**3. Why the model likes it / durability.** b1_live_scores.csv: composite decile 7/10, quintile 4/5 (upper-mid),
roe percentile ~0.87, gp_a and momentum both strong. This is largely a genuine quality+growth combination, not an
artefact: revenue growth has been accelerating quarter over quarter into 2026 (see table below) alongside real
guidance raises, consistent with the AI-data-center and reshoring capex tailwind cited in the Q10 triage note. The
main durability risk is cyclicality — equipment rental is one of the more capex-cycle-sensitive industrial models,
and current growth partly reflects an unusually strong non-residential construction cycle that will not run forever.

**8/9. Bull case.** (i) Scale/fleet-availability moat plus a real AI-data-center and reshoring capex tailwind — Q2
2026 revenue growth (+11.8% YoY) is accelerating versus Q1 2026, not decelerating. (ii) Two consecutive FY2026
guidance raises (Q1 and Q2) on both revenue and adjusted EBITDA. (iii) Consistent buybacks and dividends returning
capital while operating margin is expanding (23.6%→23.9% 1H YoY).

**Bear case.** (i) Non-residential/mega-project construction is cyclical; a capex slowdown (rate-driven or
AI-capex-driven) would hit rental utilization and pricing quickly, and URI's fleet is a depreciating asset that must
keep being replaced regardless of demand. (ii) Consolidated net debt rose in 1H 2026 to fund buybacks that already exceed free
cash flow — leverage capacity, not surplus cash, is funding shareholder returns. (iii) At ~25x trailing EPS / ~10x
EV/EBITDA, the stock is not statistically cheap; multiple compression on any growth disappointment is a real risk.

**9b. Kill criteria (measurable).**
1. Quarterly revenue growth (YoY, consolidated) decelerates below 5% for two consecutive quarters.
2. Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated, using guided or trailing adjusted EBITDA) rises above 2.5x.
3. Free cash flow (OCF − gross capex) turns negative for two consecutive quarters without a matching capex pull-back.
4. Buybacks are suspended or cut for two consecutive quarters (signals management sees the leverage/FCF gap as unsustainable).
5. FY guidance is cut (versus the prior stated range) at any quarterly release.

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 12–24 months. Real cost-out ($2.1bn run-rate achieved) and a genuine valuation trough (14.8x NTM P/E, 13th percentile of 13-year own history) are offset by a fragile earnings quality picture: ~2.5pt of the most recent quarter's celebrated margin gain and ~$0.17 of adjusted EPS come from a one-off IEEPA tariff refund, and GAAP EPS is additionally inflated by a divestiture gain — both non-repeating. Size as a smaller position pending confirmation that organic volumes (not one-offs) are actually turning.

## 2. Business
Stanley Black & Decker sells power tools, hand tools and outdoor equipment (DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN, STANLEY, BLACK+DECKER) mainly through big-box/home-improvement retail and professional/industrial distribution — this is the **Tools & Outdoor** segment (~85%+ of revenue and profit). A much smaller **Industrial** segment (engineered fastening for autos/aerospace, infrastructure) makes up the rest. GICS labels SWK "Industrial Machinery," but the profit driver is branded, repeat-purchase durables sold through retail/pro channels — **routed to `fmcg-consumer.md` ("Tools & Accessories")** per the stock-analysis sector router, not `infra-capitalgoods.md`; the small Industrial segment is a secondary capital-goods-style overlay. Competitive position: #1/#2 branded power-tools franchise (DEWALT) in North America, competing with Techtronic Industries (Milwaukee/Ryobi) and Bosch.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Tariff refund confirms the prior v2 note.** Q2 FY26: "Gross margin of 33.0%, up 600 basis points… both included a benefit of roughly 250 basis points from net tariff refunds" and "EPS of $2.33; adjusted EPS of $1.57; both included a benefit of approximately $0.17 from net tariff refunds" (IEEPA tariff-refund gain, net of related incentive-comp/tax costs). **Confirmed: ~2.5pt of margin gain is a one-off**, present in both GAAP and non-GAAP figures.
- **GAAP > Adjusted in Q2 FY26** ($2.33 vs $1.57) — unusual direction, driven by a $273.7m gain on the CAM (Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing) divestiture sitting inside GAAP operating income but excluded from adjusted. The "90% GAAP EPS growth" in the FY26 guide midpoint is largely a divestiture-accounting artefact, not operating improvement.
- **Cost programme is real and largely complete:** Global Cost Reduction Program, launched mid-2022, reached ~$2.1bn of cumulative pre-tax run-rate savings by Q4 FY25 (its original target), generating $120m incremental in Q4 FY25 alone; management is now "institutionaliz[ing]" continuous improvement rather than running a discrete program — i.e., the easy, one-time cost-out phase is ending.
- **Leverage:** Net debt $4,165.5m (D3, Q2 FY26 10-Q) vs GAAP TTM EBIT $2,176m + D&A $368m ≈ $2,544m → ~1.6x net debt/adjusted-EBITDA-proxy, but this TTM EBIT includes the CAM gain and tariff-refund quarter, so the **clean run-rate ratio is likely higher** (company narrative: "meaningful leverage reduction," debt cut $1.7bn in Q2 FY26 using CAM proceeds plus ~$240m in FY25). Treat 1.6x as a floor, not the true figure.
- **Dividend coverage is tight:** FY25 paid ~$500m in dividends against FCF of $688m (73% of FCF) and against GAAP EPS $2.65 (payout ratio 81% per D4 snapshot, a 63-year increase streak — "Dividend King" status is a strong incentive to protect it, but leaves little room if one-offs don't repeat).
- Recurring restructuring/impairment charges across multiple recent quarters (Q3 FY25 $169m impairment, Q4 FY25 $20.4m impairment + $37.0m restructuring, Q2 FY26 $5.3m impairment + $15.1m restructuring) — a multi-year transformation that keeps generating "non-recurring" charges is itself a soft red flag.

## 8. Bull case
1. Cost programme largely done ($2.1bn run-rate achieved) plus CAM proceeds funding debt paydown — deleveraging without further heavy lifting.
2. DEWALT/tools brand share gains in North America retail and C&I channels; adjusted gross margin has expanded for six straight quarters even before the tariff refund.
3. Valuation trough: 14.8x NTM P/E at the 13th percentile of 13 years of history, ~10% FCF yield — meaningful margin of safety if organic growth merely stabilises.

## 9. Bear case
1. ~2.5pt of margin and $0.17 of adjusted EPS in the latest quarter is a non-repeating tariff refund; strip it out and the "raise" is far smaller.
2. Organic volumes remain weak (Q4 FY25 volume -7%; Q1 FY26 Tools & Outdoor organic -2%) — the growth story is price/tariff-refund/divestiture-gain driven, not volume-driven.
3. FY25 guidance was cut mid-year on one bad quarter (impairment), showing the "on track" narrative is fragile; dividend payout (~80% of GAAP EPS) leaves little room if one-offs don't recur.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Tools & Outdoor organic volume negative for two more consecutive quarters.
2. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA fails to decline from FY25 level despite CAM proceeds.
3. Adjusted gross margin **excluding** the tariff-refund benefit contracts YoY.
4. Dividend increase paused (streak at risk) or payout > 90% of adjusted EPS.
5. Another guidance cut.

## 2. Business in plain English
Exelon is a holding company for four regulated electric (and in three cases, gas) distribution/transmission utilities:
ComEd (northern Illinois), PECO (southeastern Pennsylvania), BGE (central Maryland) and PHI (the District of Columbia,
Delaware, Maryland and New Jersey, operating as Pepco, Delmarva Power and Atlantic City Electric). It earns a regulated
return on the capital it invests in wires, substations and pipes ("rate base"), set by state and federal regulators, not by
selling commodities — Exelon spun off its power-generation business (nuclear/renewables) as Constellation Energy in 2022 and
has been a pure "wires" company since. Revenue is largely insulated from weather and usage swings by "decoupling" mechanisms
at most of its utilities.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Revenue decoupling at ComEd, BGE and PHI's Maryland/DC operations means distribution earnings are largely insulated from
   weather and usage — a structurally lower-volatility earnings stream than most regulated peers, evidenced by the tight
   GAAP/adjusted EPS gap in §6.
2. Rate base growth of 7.9% over the $41.7bn four-year plan is regulator-approved-in-principle capital spend (transmission
   and distribution investment to serve growing demand, including data-center-adjacent load in the Mid-Atlantic footprint
   per the triage note), which converts fairly mechanically into earnings growth under cost-of-service regulation.
3. Cheapest large-cap utility P/E in its peer set (13.5x NTM per the triage screen) with a ~21% gap to the analyst mean
   target price — a valuation cushion that is not present in many bond-proxy utility peers currently.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Consolidated debt has grown from $41.3bn to $49.4bn in two years to fund the capital plan, with further equity dilution
   (~$3.4bn planned through 2029) — a company that must continually access capital markets on favorable terms; a sustained
   rise in long-term rates would raise financing costs and could pressure the EPS growth algorithm.
2. The BGE Maryland rate case (filed July 2026, $156M ask, 10.40% ROE ask, decision due Q1 2027) is a real near-term
   regulatory event; a materially reduced award (a common regulatory outcome) would be a direct, quantifiable hit to the
   utility's earnings trajectory and a specific data point to re-check at the Q1 2027 decision.
3. "Hold" consensus rating (17 analysts) despite the target-price gap suggests the sell-side is not enthusiastic about
   near-term upside despite acknowledging valuation support — worth understanding why (not resolved in this pass; possible
   read-through is rate risk or the equity-dilution overhang).

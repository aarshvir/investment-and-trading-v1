# Welltower Inc. (WELL) — Diligence Dossier (F32, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH**, not INCLUDE. Thesis horizon 12–24 months (re-underwrite when either the price or the growth catches up to the other). One-sentence reason: this is a genuinely excellent, demographically-favored senior-housing REIT with real, accelerating growth — but the reverse DCF shows the price already requires that growth to continue near its current cyclical-recovery pace for a decade, funded substantially by heavy share issuance, which a conservative base case does not support; a great business priced for more than it can plausibly deliver is a WATCH, not an INCLUDE.

**Thesis (≤25 words):** Owns senior-housing real estate operated by top platforms, riding an accelerating 80+ demographic wave — priced for that wave to keep accelerating for a decade.

## 2. Business in plain English
Welltower owns healthcare and senior-living real estate — predominantly private-pay senior housing (independent/assisted living/memory care), plus outpatient medical and long-term post-acute facilities — and contracts third-party operators (e.g., regional and national senior-living platforms) to run the properties day to day. Most of its growth today comes from its Seniors Housing Operating ("SHOP") portfolio, where Welltower participates directly in the property-level NOI (not just a fixed lease payment), so it benefits fully from occupancy and rate gains as the post-COVID senior-housing recovery continues, on top of a demographic tailwind: the 80-plus US population is now entering its fastest growth phase in decades.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
Net debt/adjusted EBITDA: 2.36x (Q3'25) → 3.03x (YE'25) → 2.99x (Q2'26) — leverage has drifted modestly higher over the past year as the company funded a large, ongoing acquisition pipeline ($15.5bn of gross investments closed/under contract YTD through Q2'26, including a C$4.1bn Canadian acquisition, Amica Senior Lifestyles, closed just after Q1'26) but has stayed within a ~3x band, helped by ~$9.5bn of available liquidity. **The dominant per-share dilution driver is equity issuance, not stock-based compensation dilution in the ordinary sense:** diluted shares outstanding grew from 668.1m (Q2'25) to 737.96m (Q2'26), **+10.4% in one year** — funding growth substantially through new share issuance (a normal REIT capital-raising method, but a direct drag on how much of the *aggregate* FFO growth shown in §4 actually reaches *per-share* FFO, which is the metric guidance and this valuation both use). **Separately, a real governance red flag:** WELL's FY2025 stock-based compensation expense was **$1.557bn** (10-K, filed 2026-02-12), driven almost entirely ($1.409bn) by a new "Ten Year Executive Continuity and Alignment Program" under which the five named executive officers agreed to take only $110,000/year base salary through 2035 in exchange for a single, large, front-loaded long-term equity award (OP units). Activist short-seller Land & Buildings Investment Management published an April 2026 white paper, "Compensation Plan Hubris," describing this as "one of the most egregiously management-friendly compensation structures in public REIT history," citing easily-reachable performance hurdles. This SBC is a non-cash add-back excluded from normalized FFO (so it does not distort the FFO growth numbers in §4–5), but it is real economic dilution to long-term per-share value and a governance concern independent of the current operating momentum.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) the 80+ US population is entering its fastest growth phase in decades — a multi-decade demand tailwind for senior housing that is unusually visible and hard to derail; (2) SHOP occupancy, while recovering fast (+330 bps YoY in Q2'26), is still below pre-COVID peak levels, leaving genuine further room to run before the "recovery" component of growth is exhausted; (3) four consecutive guidance raises and a 15% dividend hike show real, credible execution, not just a favorable narrative.
**Bull risk turned bear:** (1) the very same occupancy-recovery math that drives today's 20%+ same-store NOI growth is mechanically self-limiting — once SHOP occupancy normalizes toward (not perpetually above) prior peaks, same-store growth must decelerate toward a structural mid-single-digit rate, and the 10-year reverse DCF already prices near-term growth persisting for a decade; (2) diluted share count grew 10.4% in one year — a large share of "aggregate" FFO growth is funded by issuing new equity, which means per-share compounding (the only thing that matters to a holder) is running meaningfully below the headline growth rate once the acquisition pipeline normalizes; (3) the Ten Year executive compensation program ($1.4bn front-loaded 2025 charge, criticized publicly by an activist short-seller as management-friendly with easily-reachable hurdles) is a governance red flag that, while not affecting near-term FFO, signals potential future capital-allocation and incentive-alignment risk over the multi-year horizon this thesis requires.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Same-store total-portfolio NOI growth decelerates below 8% YoY for two consecutive quarters (from 15.5% in Q2'26) without a corresponding guidance cut — signals the recovery has ended.
2. Normalized FFO/share guidance cut (reduction to the low end of the then-current full-year range) at a subsequent release.
3. Diluted share count grows more than 8% YoY for two more consecutive years, while per-share FFO growth falls below aggregate FFO growth by a widening gap (worsening dilution drag).
4. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA rises above 3.5x (from 2.99x) without a stated de-leveraging plan.
5. Price/normalized-FFO multiple stays above 30x while same-store NOI growth guidance for the following year is below 10% (i.e., the market keeps paying a growth multiple after the growth itself has normalized).

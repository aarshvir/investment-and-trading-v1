## 2. Business in plain English
Edwards Lifesciences designs, manufactures and sells medical devices that treat structural heart disease, primarily transcatheter heart valves implanted via catheter rather than open-heart surgery. Its main product lines are **TAVR** (transcatheter aortic valve replacement, its largest and most mature franchise) and **TMTT** (transcatheter mitral and tricuspid therapies, its newer, fastest-growing franchise). Customers are hospitals and structural-heart surgical/interventional teams; Edwards competes on clinical outcomes data, device durability and physician training/relationships built over ~two decades as the TAVR pioneer.

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse DCF

Using a 10-year explicit-growth-then-terminal DCF (WACC 8.0%, terminal growth 3.0%, both simplifying assumptions typical for a large-cap medtech, not independently derived from a CAPM build in this window), solving for the growth rate that reproduces the $86.29 price gives an **implied 10-year FCF growth rate of roughly 12.5%/yr** **[Corrected 2026-10-08: WACC 8.0% untied to 5.17% UST; on TTM FCF after SBC and Ke 9.67% implied ~15.5%/yr; conclusion 'above' unchanged]**.
**Base case:** Edwards's own FY2026 guidance is 10–11% constant-currency sales growth with high-teens adjusted-EPS growth this year (partly a tax-rate/comp effect, see §5); a reasonable multi-year base case — blending TAVR's guided 8–9% with TMTT's much faster growth off a smaller base, plus continued margin leverage and buybacks — is roughly **9–10%/yr** sustained FCF/EPS growth, not 12.5%/yr. **`valuation_view_vs_v1.implied_vs_base = "above"`.** Under the wave-7 rule, this means EW does **not** qualify for INCLUDE/INCLUDE-SMALL even though the underlying business quality is genuinely good — consistent with the "great business priced for more than it can deliver is WATCH, not INCLUDE" instruction, and consistent with the Q07 triage's own price_vs_growth score of 3 (not 4 or 5).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. TMTT sales growing 44.8% y/y and TMTT guidance raised (per the §5 quote: "Increasing TMTT sales guidance: $760 to $780 million from $740 to $780 million") — the newer franchise is scaling faster than TAVR did at a comparable stage, per the company's own guidance track record.
2. Net-cash balance sheet (consolidated: $2.9bn cash vs. ~$0.6bn long-term debt) plus an active, accelerating buyback ($1.5bn remaining authorization) gives management optionality without balance-sheet risk.
3. FCF conversion improved from ~67% to ~91% of net income year over year (H1 2025 vs. H1 2026), a genuine earnings-quality improvement, not an accounting artefact identified in this window.

**Bear:**
1. Medtronic's Evolut platform and Abbott's Navitor (seeking label expansion) are named, active competitive threats in the TAVR segment, and Boston Scientific's planned re-entry with Acurate Neo2 adds a fourth serious competitor to a market where the top five players already hold >78% share — the "8% to 9%" TAVR growth target quoted in §5 could compress if any of these gain share.
2. ROE (~9% per the b1 factor data) is modest for a ~29x NTM P/E; the stock is priced more like a durable-moat compounder than its current returns-on-capital profile fully supports.
3. Reverse DCF implies ~12.5%/yr FCF growth priced in vs. a ~9–10%/yr evidence-based base case — the stock is not cheap on the numbers even though the growth story is real.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. TAVR constant-currency sales growth falls below **7%/yr** for two consecutive quarters (below even the low end of the pre-raise range in the §5 quote "8% to 9% from 7% to 9%").
2. TMTT sales growth decelerates below **25%/yr** y/y for two consecutive quarters (roughly half its current ~45% pace).
3. Adjusted EPS guidance is **cut** from the current "$2.95 to $3.05" range quoted in §5 (any downward revision).
4. FCF/NI conversion falls back below **70%** on a trailing-two-quarter basis (vs. ~91% now).
5. A major TAVR or TMTT clinical trial (e.g., relating to Boston Scientific's Acurate Neo2 re-entry or an Abbott Navitor label expansion) results in a materially adverse label change or safety signal for an Edwards device.

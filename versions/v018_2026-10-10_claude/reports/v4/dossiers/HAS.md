## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE.** Thesis horizon 18–36 months. Hasbro has become a Magic: The Gathering / Wizards of the Coast (WotC) company with a legacy toy business attached, not the reverse. WotC is growing double-digit-to-high-20s%, guidance has been raised or held in three of the last four releases (including through an active cyberattack), and the stock trades below the Street's own 12-month low target. Sized full-weight, but with explicit awareness that Consumer Products (toys) is loss-making at the operating line and carries real, tariff-driven impairment history.

## 2. Business
Hasbro is a "games, IP and toy" company reporting three segments: **Wizards of the Coast & Digital Gaming** (Magic: The Gathering tabletop + MTG Arena/MTGO digital, Dungeons & Dragons, digital licensing) — now the profit engine; **Consumer Products** (Nerf, Transformers, Play-Doh, Monopoly, Peppa Pig — physical toys sold through mass retail, largely China-sourced); and **Entertainment** (film/TV licensing and location-based, shrunk after the 2023 eOne Film/TV divestiture). **Routing note:** GICS/Yahoo file Hasbro under "Leisure" → `fmcg-consumer.md`, appropriate for Consumer Products, but the *dominant profit segment* (WotC) is a hybrid of branded tabletop IP and a growing subscription/digital card-game business (Arena) that behaves more like `it-saas.md`/`telecom-media.md` (bookings, digital mix) — **explicitly flagged as a multi-segment company where the profit-driving segment does not cleanly fit one playbook**; Consumer Products and Entertainment are read through `fmcg-consumer.md` and `telecom-media.md` respectively as secondary lenses.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **The FY25 GAAP loss is real and tariff-linked, not accounting noise:** a $1,021.9m non-cash goodwill impairment in Consumer Products in Q2 FY25 was recorded "following completion of an interim quantitative assessment of goodwill triggered by the implementation of tariffs" — direct primary-source confirmation that tariffs did lasting, balance-sheet-level damage to the toy business, not just a margin headwind.
- **Consumer Products is structurally weak even in a good quarter:** Q2 FY26 segment operating loss of $15m (adjusted -$8m), "reflects incremental tariff expense, entertainment-related mix shifts, and normal seasonality." Q3 FY25 CP adjusted operating profit was down -32% YoY "primarily driven by tariff expense."
- **A second impairment recurred in Q2 FY26:** $56m against the "refocused Digital Games portfolio for 2028 and beyond" (video-game unit) — inside the otherwise-strong WotC segment, showing the digital-gaming diversification bet has had its own stumbles.
- **Unresolved cybersecurity incident:** unauthorized network access identified late March 2026 caused an estimated $25m revenue impact and $11m direct cost in Q2 FY26 alone; "the Company expects to incur additional costs related to the incident in future periods," and no insurance recoveries had been recognized as of Q2 FY26 — an open-ended, unquantified tail risk (disclosed Item 8.01, 8-K filed 2026-04-01).
- **Capital return:** Q2 FY26 dividend $0.70/share quarterly ($99m paid in the quarter); new $1.0bn buyback authorization (Feb 2026) replacing the 2018 program; debt paydown of $55m in Q2 FY26. Dividend yield 3.23%, payout ratio ~50% of adjusted EPS (per D4 snapshot) — comfortable on an adjusted basis, though the FY25 GAAP loss means the dividend was not covered by GAAP earnings that year.

## 8. Bull case
1. WotC/Magic is a structurally higher-margin, faster-growing business than the legacy toy company the market may still be pricing: first-ever $500m Magic quarter (Q2 FY26), +27% segment revenue, high-40s/50%+ segment operating margins, "line of sight to continued growth in 2027" (CEO).
2. Guidance raised or held in three of the last four releases, including through an active, costly cyberattack — genuine execution credibility.
3. Trades below the Street's own 12-month low target and near the cheapest NTM multiple in company history.

## 9. Bear case
1. Consumer Products (traditional toys) is shrinking, operating-loss-making in Q2 FY26, structurally exposed to China-sourcing tariffs, and already took a $1.02bn goodwill write-down in 2025 — a large share of revenue sits in secular/tariff-driven decline.
2. The network-intrusion incident is open-ended: total cost and insurance-recovery timing are both unknown, and management explicitly warns of "additional costs… in future periods."
3. Profit growth is concentrated in a single IP (Magic: The Gathering); a misstep in release cadence, secondary-market/collector dynamics, or competitive TCGs would hit consolidated results disproportionately, and the Digital Games impairment shows the diversification hedge has its own execution risk.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
1. WotC/Digital Gaming revenue growth decelerates below ~10% YoY for two consecutive quarters.
2. Consumer Products operating margin fails to turn positive in the seasonally strong Q4.
3. Cumulative unauthorized-access costs or any disclosed regulatory/privacy penalty exceeds ~$50m.
4. FY guidance is cut (breaking the current positive streak).
5. Any goodwill/intangible impairment inside WotC itself (as opposed to CP or Digital Games) — would signal the core thesis is broken.

## 2. Business in plain English
KKR is a global alternative-asset manager (private equity, credit, infrastructure, real assets, capital markets) that earns management and performance fees on ~$796bn of assets under management (AUM), plus balance-sheet investment gains. Since 2021 it has also owned 100% of Global Atlantic, a fixed-annuity/life-insurance platform whose "spread" income (investment yield minus policyholder crediting rate) is now folded into KKR's "Insurance Operating Earnings." It makes money from (a) recurring management fees on locked-up capital, (b) performance fees ("carry") when funds exit investments profitably, and (c) insurance float invested into KKR's own credit strategies. Competitive position: top-3 global alternative manager alongside Blackstone and Apollo, differentiated by the insurance-balance-sheet flywheel that funds origination.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Q04 triage flagged 13.6x NTM P/E and strong forward growth, but the **worst statistical-model rank in the batch (495/500)** and negative 12-month momentum (-15%), largely attributable to Global Atlantic's private-credit/annuity exposure and sector-wide insurance-affiliate concerns (flagged alongside Apollo in Mar 2026 press). My read: the low-multiple, high-growth combination is durable at the segment level — Total Operating Earnings (management fees + insurance + strategic holdings; management's preferred "more durable and recurring" metric) rose 19% LTM to $5.5bn (2Q'26 LTM) from $4.7bn (2Q'25 LTM), and 84% of segment earnings now come from these more-recurring sources per the 2Q'26 earnings release. The negative momentum/model rank looks like a market discount for insurance-book opacity, not a break in the fee-earning franchise — but it is not an "artefact" either; it is a real, currently-unresolved risk (Section 6).

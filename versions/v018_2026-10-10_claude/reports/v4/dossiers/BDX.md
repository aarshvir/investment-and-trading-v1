# BDX — Becton, Dickinson and Company — Diligence Dossier (Agent F73, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). One-sentence reason: a genuinely durable, diversified med-tech franchise now trading
as standalone "New BD" post spin-off, with guidance raised in both quarters since the separation and cash generation
ahead of plan — tempered by a live, unresolved FDA quality-system problem (repeat warning letters, potential
ChloraPrep recall) and thin quality-of-earnings visibility in only two clean post-separation quarters.
Thesis horizon: 24–36 months (let the standalone story re-rate once BD Excellence margin gains and the FDA remediation
are proven, not assumed).

## 2. Business in plain English
BD makes the disposable and durable hardware hospitals and clinics use every day — needles and syringes, IV catheters
and infusion pumps, medication-dispensing cabinets (Pyxis), patient monitors, and devices used in surgery, urology and
interventional care, plus injection systems supplied to pharma/biotech customers (BioPharma Systems) for drugs like
GLP-1s. It sells mostly to hospitals, ambulatory care and pharma manufacturers on razor/razor-blade economics (placed
equipment, recurring consumables). Competitive position: #1 or #2 share in most of its categories (vascular access,
infusion, specimen management, pharmaceutical delivery systems), which is also why its FDA quality-system record
matters — its products touch nearly every hospital bed in the U.S.

## 8. Bull case and bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. BD Excellence cost program continues to expand adjusted operating margin (10.5%→13.3%→15.6%+ quarterly progression **[Corrected 2026-10-06: continuing-ops GAAP operating margin was 10.4% (Q1), 2.0% (Q2, after the 450M impairment), 13.3% (Q3); 15.6% is the Q3 FY25 GAAP margin, not an FY26 or adjusted figure, so there is no 10.5%-13.3%-15.6% progression]**
   in FY26 even through a loss-making impairment quarter suggests underlying margin trajectory is intact).
2. Standalone "New BD" re-rates toward peer med-tech multiples (16–20x) as trading history lengthens and index
   inclusion/ownership normalises post-spin, without needing any upgrade to the growth outlook.
3. BioPharma Systems (prefillable syringes/injection pens for GLP-1s and biologics) is a genuine structural
   volume-growth tailwind tied to obesity-drug demand, largely outside BD's control but a real multi-year tailwind.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. The FDA quality-system problems are not resolved, not one-off, and touch multiple facilities/product lines (§11) —
   a further Warning Letter, an actual ChloraPrep recall, or a consent-decree expansion would directly cut into the
   Medical Essentials/Connected Care franchise the model likes.
2. Only two clean post-spin quarters exist; "New BD" stand-alone cost allocation, TSA income roll-off (as corporate
   overhead currently borne by Waters via TSA unwinds), and dis-synergies are not yet stress-tested by a full fiscal
   year.
3. Hernia-mesh and other product-liability litigation (~7,000 pending claims even after the FY24 settlement) is a
   recurring cash drag ($52–63M pre-tax legal charges in a single quarter) that could re-escalate.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. A new FDA Warning Letter, Form 483, or consent-decree expansion covering any BD manufacturing site within the next
   4 quarters.
2. Full-year FY26 adjusted diluted EPS guidance is **cut** (vs. the current $12.62–$12.72 range) at any future
   quarterly release.
3. Organic/reported revenue growth falls below the "low single-digit" floor of guidance for two consecutive quarters.
4. Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated) rises above 3.0x (from ~2.1x currently), signalling debt-funded buybacks outrunning **[Corrected 2026-10-06: kill criterion 4 starts from a wrong base: the corrected ratio is already about 3.0x adjusted / 4.0x GAAP, so the 3.0x trigger must be re-based]**
   deleveraging.
5. Any voluntary or FDA-mandated recall of a top-5-revenue product line (e.g., BD Alaris, Pyxis, ChloraPrep, or a
   BioPharma Systems platform).

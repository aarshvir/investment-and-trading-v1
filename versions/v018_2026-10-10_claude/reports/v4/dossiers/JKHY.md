## 2. Business in plain English
Jack Henry sells core processing software and payments/digital-banking technology to U.S. community banks and credit unions — the back-office system that runs a bank's deposits, loans and transactions. Its customers are small/mid-size financial institutions with high switching costs (a core conversion is a multi-year, high-risk project banks avoid unless forced), so revenue is overwhelmingly recurring (subscription/outsourcing + processing fees) with very high retention. It grows via new-client wins ("competitive core wins"), cross-selling payments/digital modules into its existing base, and price increases, rather than via a broad secular tailwind.

## 3. Why the model likes it / is it durable
Triage cited near-zero net debt, 24% ROE, and a fair (not cheap) 20x NTM P/E against what it read as +12% forward EPS growth. The quality/ROE/balance-sheet strength is durable — it's the switching-cost moat, confirmed by FY2026 results showing 58 competitive core wins (management's own characterization: the most in 20+ years) and a third consecutive year of >60bps non-GAAP operating-margin expansion (92bps in FY2026). **The growth figure is where I disagree with the quant snapshot** — see §5/§7.

## 8. Bull case
1. Record FY2026 competitive-win year (58 core wins, largest in 20+ years) is a leading indicator for FY2027/FY2028 revenue as new clients convert and ramp.
2. Near-zero net debt and 138% FCF/NI conversion give it optionality to accelerate buybacks or bolt-on M&A without balance-sheet strain.
3. 20x forward P/E is below JKHY's own recent multiple history per several data sources (21.5–25x cited elsewhere), i.e., not paying a premium versus its own past even with the growth-guide caveat above.

## 9. Bear case
1. FY2027 guidance itself shows margin-expansion deceleration (20–40bps guided vs. 92bps delivered in FY2026) and explicit first-half cost headwinds (medical costs, AI/cyber/data-center spend) — the growth story the triage relied on is softer than assumed.
2. Q4 FY2026 already showed a real single-quarter deceleration (operating income -12.2%, EPS -10.2% YoY) — a pattern to watch, not a one-off given the FY2027 guide corroborates continued pressure into 1H.
3. Buyback pace jumped ~13x YoY while cash fell to $20.6mn by Q3 FY2026 — a newly aggressive capital-return posture for a company with historically conservative capital allocation, worth confirming is sustainable rather than a one-time use of a low point in cash.

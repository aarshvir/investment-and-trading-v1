# ADBE — Adobe Inc. (Application Software, Information Technology)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — 12–36 month horizon. Adobe is priced by the market as if genAI will structurally erode Creative Cloud, but the reverse DCF shows the stock needs 10 years of *shrinking* free cash flow to justify today's price, while the company is actually growing revenue and FCF double-digits and buying back ~5–7% of its share count a year. Sized small, not full weight, because the core bear case (AI-native tools substituting for paid seats) is genuinely unresolved and the company just lost both its CEO and CFO within six months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Adobe sells subscription software: Creative Cloud (Photoshop, Premiere, Illustrator, and the Firefly generative-AI tools bundled into it) to designers, marketers, photographers and students; Document Cloud (Acrobat/PDF, e-signature) to every knowledge worker; and Experience Cloud (marketing/analytics platforms) to enterprises. Revenue is ~97% subscription, billed annually or monthly, so it is recurring and priced with high switching costs (file formats, muscle memory, team workflows). It makes money the way most SaaS companies do: high (~89%) gross margin, low incremental cost per seat, most of the cost base is R&D, sales/marketing and content-delivery infrastructure. Its competitive position is entrenchment in professional creative and document workflows against a wave of new AI-native point tools (Midjourney, Sora, Figma, Canva) that can each do a slice of what Adobe does, for less.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Adobe screens at the very top of the v4 quant model's composite (composite_qvm 0.885, decile 8/10, rank 118): quality (fam_Q 0.99 — gross-profit/assets 98th pct, ROE 84th pct) and value (fam_V 0.92 — FCF/price 93rd pct, earnings yield 92nd pct) are both exceptional, while momentum is in the bottom quintile (fam_M 0.166, 12-month momentum 17th pct) and the earnings-surprise/sentiment factor is also weak (fam_S 0.326, SUE 33rd pct). This is a classic contrarian value-versus-momentum split, **not an artefact**: quality and value are computed straight from filed financials that are, in fact, still growing (revenue +13% y/y, non-GAAP EPS +15% y/y in the most recent quarter); the weak momentum/sentiment factors are the market's real-time verdict on the AI-disruption narrative and the Q4 revenue guide landing a hair below consensus. Both readings are correct simultaneously: the business is executing, and the market is discounting it hard for a risk that has not yet shown up in the numbers.

## 8. Bull case
1. Firefly/AI-first ARR is real and compounding fast (+150% y/y, >$650M ARR, Firefly ARR +40% q/q) — Adobe is monetizing generative AI as an add-on to, not a replacement for, its core seats.
2. The stock is at the cheapest multiple in its history (0th percentile) while revenue growth has not decelerated for eight straight quarters (10–13% every quarter since FY24).
3. Balance sheet is nearly debt-free, buybacks are shrinking share count ~5–6%/year, and management has a $25B authorization still to deploy.

## 9. Bear case
1. Generative AI tools (OpenAI/Sora, Midjourney, and Figma/Canva at the workflow layer) are a genuine, still-developing threat to Creative Cloud seat growth; the Q4 guide (in line to slightly light) is the first data point the market has read as early evidence, even though Adobe itself denies deceleration.
2. Two C-suite departures within six months (CEO Narayen → Chakravarthy effective 1 Dec 2026; CFO Dan Durn's exit disclosed with Q2 FY26 results) **[Corrected 2026-10-09: a third senior exit, David Wadhwani (President, Creativity & Productivity), was announced 2 Sep 2026, effective 27 Sep 2026]** create real execution and continuity risk exactly when the AI strategy needs steady-handed defense.
3. A $150M DOJ/FTC "dark pattern" settlement (below) signals a company that let growth-at-all-costs subscription tactics get ahead of compliance — a governance yellow flag, not just a fine.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Digital Media (Creative + Document Cloud) segment ARR growth falls below **8% y/y for two consecutive quarters**. **[Corrected 2026-10-09: FY26 releases report total Adobe ARR, not Digital Media segment ARR; the trigger may need redefining to total ARR growth]**
2. Firefly/AI-first ending ARR quarter-over-quarter growth falls below **15% for two consecutive quarters** (the AI-monetization bridge stalling).
3. A **second** senior-leadership departure (CEO, CFO, or a business-unit president) is announced within 12 months of the Dec-2026 CEO handover.
4. Net debt/EBITDA rises above **1.0x** (i.e., debt-funded buybacks beyond organic FCF + the $25B authorization).
5. A quarterly revenue guide is **cut** (not just merely in-line) versus the prior quarter's stated range.

# CCL — Carnival Corporation Ltd. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F73, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). One-sentence reason: the world's largest cruise operator has delivered twelve
consecutive quarters of record net yields, delevered from a COVID-era balance-sheet crisis to investment-grade
recognition (Fitch, Dec 2025) and a Moody's upgrade (as of the Q2 2026 release), and reinstated its dividend — but it
still carries ~$23.4B of long-term debt, a mid-2026 guidance **trim** on constant-currency net yields (driven by a
forced Middle East/Arabian Gulf redeployment), and a next-earnings date (Sep 29, 2026) that lands three days after
this dossier's cutoff, so the freshest quarter is not yet in evidence; named reservation: **size for the leverage and
event risk, not just the yield-growth story**. Thesis horizon: 24–48 months (multi-year deleveraging and PROPEL
target period runs through 2029).

## 2. Business in plain English
Carnival is the world's largest cruise-ship operator by berths, running nine brands (Carnival Cruise Line, Princess,
Holland America, Seabourn, Costa, AIDA, Cunard, P&O Cruises UK/Australia) that sell all-inclusive multi-day ocean
vacations to consumers worldwide, plus onboard spending (dining, casino, excursions) and destination experiences
(private islands). It makes money by filling ships (occupancy), charging more per passenger per day (net yield), and
managing largely fixed costs (fuel, crew, ship financing) per available berth day. Competitive position: #1 global
scale (ahead of Royal Caribbean and Norwegian Cruise Line), which gives it purchasing/fuel-hedging and
newbuild-financing scale advantages, but the business remains capital-intensive, fuel-cost-sensitive and exposed to
geopolitical routing disruption.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
**Entity scope: Carnival Corporation Ltd., consolidated**, balance sheet as of May 31, 2026, per 10-Q filed
2026-06-26 (accession 0000815097-26-000096).
- **Cash flow / cash conversion:** the company does not report a single clean "FCF" line comparably across releases
  in the extracts reviewed, but disclosed record adjusted EBITDA ($1.3B Q1, $1.6B Q2) alongside continued heavy capex
  for newbuilds and private-destination investment (Celebration Key, RelaxAway-type projects referenced in the 10-Q)
  — d4/Yahoo aggregator TTM FCF of $1.9B on TTM operating cash flow of $6.8B implies capex of roughly $4.9B TTM, a
  large absolute capex load consistent with a fleet still taking newbuild deliveries.
- **GAAP vs. adjusted gap:** modest and shrinking — Q2 FY26 GAAP diluted EPS $0.39 vs. adjusted $0.41 (a $0.02 gap,
  ~5%), much narrower than a typical post-COVID-recapitalisation cruise-line adjusted/GAAP gap, indicating the
  business has largely worked through its one-time restructuring/impairment/debt-extinguishment noise from the
  2020–2023 crisis period.
- **Balance sheet:** cash $2,243M (May 31, 2026) vs. $1,928M (Nov 30, 2025); Long-Term Debt $23,418M, down from
  $24,037M at fiscal year-end — genuine deleveraging in-period. Customer deposits (a source of float, effectively
  interest-free financing from advance bookings) reached an **all-time high of $9.0 billion**, up over $450M YoY per
  the Q2 release — a positive, verifiable structural signal of booking strength (deposits are a real cash liability
  backed by real forward bookings, not a soft metric). Total shareholders' equity $12,968M (Carnival Corp Ltd.
  attributable) vs. $12,270M at FYE — equity is rebuilding. **Net debt/adjusted EBITDA = 3.1x** as of the Q2 2026
  release (company-disclosed), improved from ~3.7x a year earlier per the release's own "half a point" framing —
  this **updates and corrects** the triage's cited 3.3x, which was accurate as of its (Q1-era or earlier) source but
  is now stale; deleveraging has continued since. **Moody's upgraded Carnival's credit rating with a continued
  positive outlook** (stated in the Q2 2026 release), on top of Fitch's investment-grade recognition disclosed at the
  Q4 FY25 release (Dec 2025) — two independent rating-agency confirmations of the deleveraging trend, not just
  company messaging.
- **Capital return:** dividend reinstated (announced with the Q4 FY25 release, Dec 19, 2025) after a multi-year
  suspension; a new **$2.5 billion share buyback program** was announced at the Q1 FY26 release (Mar 27, 2026), with
  **over $450 million repurchased** by the Q2 FY26 release (Jun 23, 2026) — capital return resuming from a very low
  base, appropriately paced against continued deleveraging (buybacks are not crowding out debt paydown; both are
  happening).
- **Share count:** reducing via the new buyback program, still early days (~$450M of a $2.5B authorisation used).
- **Litigation-adjacent capital item:** a favourable **Helms-Burton Act** appellate reversal (Oct 22, 2024, 11th
  Circuit reversed a $110M+$4M District Court judgment against Carnival Corporation) removes a previously accrued
  contingent liability — a genuine positive resolution, not previously reflected in triage. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: on 2026-05-21 the Supreme Court vacated the 11th Circuit ruling and remanded (10-Q acc 0000815097-26-000096), so the $110M + $4M judgment risk is revived; company still expects no material impact]**
- **No pending M&A.** The only major corporate-structure event is the completed DLC unification/redomiciliation
  (§4), which is a governance/listing simplification, not an acquisition.

## 8. Bull case and bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Twelve consecutive quarters of record net yields, an all-time-high $9.0B customer-deposit book, and two
   independent rating-agency deleveraging confirmations (Fitch investment-grade, Moody's upgrade) are all genuine,
   verifiable, multi-source-confirmed signals of a durable demand and balance-sheet recovery — not a single company
   talking point.
2. PROPEL targets (>16% ROIC, >50% adjusted EPS growth 2025→2029, ~$14B/>40% of CFO returned to shareholders) give a
   concrete, management-committed capital-allocation framework against which to hold the company accountable each
   quarter.
3. At 8.8x NTM P/E and with net debt/EBITDA still de-levering from 3.1x, there is real re-rating room versus RCL if
   execution continues without a demand shock.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Absolute long-term debt of $23.4B is still large in dollar terms; a global travel-demand shock (recession, a new
   pandemic-style event, a major cruise-ship incident) would hit a still-leveraged balance sheet harder than it would
   hit RCL or NCLH's less-indebted (in NCLH's case, more indebted — but smaller absolute exposure) peers.
2. The Q2 2026 FY26 net-yield guidance **trim**, driven by forced Middle East/Arabian Gulf redeployment, shows the
   business remains exposed to geopolitical routing disruption in ways management cannot fully offset with cost
   discipline alone — and this risk (Red Sea/Gulf security) is not obviously resolved.
3. Gross-margin yield was down 3.9% in Q2 2026 on fuel-price inflation; if fuel prices move structurally higher
   (rather than the currently-assumed spot-price basis), the cost-discipline offsets that have supported adjusted-EPS
   growth so far could run out of room.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA (as disclosed by the company) rises back above **3.5x** (from 3.1x currently), reversing
   the deleveraging trend.
2. FY26 adjusted net income guidance is cut in **dollar terms** (not just a yield-metric trim) from the current
   ~$3.5–3.6B range at the Q3 (Sep 29, 2026) or any subsequent release. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the current FY26 guide is approximately $3.07B adjusted net income (Jun 23 release); the Q3 release of 2026-09-29 put it at approximately $3.08B, i.e. not a cut]**
3. Constant-currency net yield growth turns negative for any full quarter (from the current guided +1.75–2.25% for
   FY26), signalling a genuine demand deterioration rather than a routing/mix effect.
4. Customer deposits decline year-over-year (from the current all-time-high $9.0B), signalling a genuine forward-
   booking slowdown.
5. A new material Middle East/Red Sea-style routing disruption forces redeployment of a second major itinerary
   region (beyond the Arabian Gulf already disclosed) within the next four quarters.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **Litigation:** two purported COVID-era class actions (Australia, Italy federal courts) remain pending as of the
  Q2 FY26 10-Q, alleging negligence/failure-to-warn related to onboard illness exposure — long-running, not new, and
  the company continues to state it does not expect a material impact.
- **Regulatory/environmental — a genuine, unresolved red flag:** the U.S. Department of Justice and EPA notified
  Carnival on **March 14, 2022** of potential civil penalties and injunctive relief for alleged **Clean Water Act**
  violations by owned/operated vessels under the 2013 Vessel General Permit; as of the Q2 FY26 10-Q this remains
  **unresolved** ("we are working with these agencies to reach a resolution of this matter"). This sits on top of
  Carnival's well-documented 2016–2022 history of environmental-crimes probation with the same agencies (from an
  earlier, separate matter) — a **repeat-pattern** environmental-compliance risk that is real and ongoing, not fully
  captured by triage's fuel-cost-only framing, and worth flagging explicitly as this batch's clearest unresolved
  regulatory overhang.
- **Auditor/accounting:** no restatement, material-weakness or going-concern language found in the reviewed 10-Q. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the 10-Q also discloses an April 14, 2026 data security incident with six purported class actions (consolidated May 2026), not covered in this scan]**
- **Insider selling:** not reviewed in this pass (time-boxed) — flagged as a gap.
- **Corporate-structure event (not a red flag, but material):** the May 2026 DLC unification and Panama-to-Bermuda
  redomiciliation is a legitimate governance simplification (single share class, single listing entity going forward)
  — included here for completeness since it changes the legal entity name (now "Carnival Corporation Ltd.") that any
  downstream data pipeline should map correctly to CIK 0000815097 / ticker CCL.

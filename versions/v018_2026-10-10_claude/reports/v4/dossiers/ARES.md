**2. Business in plain English.** Ares manages money for pension funds, insurers and wealthy individuals, mainly private loans (credit, 66% of AUM), plus real estate and infrastructure, secondaries and private equity. It earns management fees on committed or invested capital, performance fees (carried interest and incentive fees) and other fees. At 30 Jun 2026 AUM was $671.3bn and fee-paying AUM $409.9bn (both +17% YoY); 84% of AUM and 94% of management fees came from perpetual or long-dated funds (Q2-26 earnings presentation, EX-99.2, acc. 0001628280-26-051191).

Durable part: recurring fee base. Fragile part: realized performance income (Q3-26 prelim about $10m) and fundraising dependent on private-credit sentiment.

## Correction (verification DV04, 2026-10-08)

**Verification DV04 (research only; primary sources: SEC EDGAR).** Quarterly GAAP EPS, after-tax realized income per share, FRE, AUM, corporate debt ($4,577.2m, cash $557.1m, 2.1x TTM FRE), consolidated-fund debt, accrued carry, equity and share counts, the dividend arithmetic, every quoted statement and both interim 8-Ks, and the Siegel derivative action all verify against the releases, 10-Q (acc 0001628280-26-054538) and 8-Ks. No fact error; the failure is the valuation method and therefore the verdict.

1. **Valuation-method error (Sec 7; summary implied_vs_base):** the reverse DCF discounts per-share after-tax realized income at a 10% cost of equity. (a) The rate is not built from the 10-year Treasury (5.17% on 2026-09-25) plus an equity risk premium: with Blume-adjusted 5-year weekly beta 1.42 (raw 1.63, v4 price panel) and a 4.14% ERP, Ke = 5.17% + 1.42 x 4.14% = 11.05%. (b) After-tax realized income adds back equity compensation (Ex-99.2 reconciliation: TTM $704.0m pre-tax = FY25 $506.4m + $234.2m acquisition-related, less H1-25 $259.2m + $163.8m, plus H1-26 $318.4m + $68.0m; $565.6m excluding acquisition-related). Stock-based compensation is a cost, not an add-back. At a 25% tax shield over 362.4m shares (= $467.6m / $1.29) it is $1.17 a share ($1.46 including acquisition-related), so NTM cash earnings are about $4.53, not $5.70.
2. **Recomputed (same model: ten years of growth, 3.0% terminal, price $122.01):** the dossier's own numbers reproduce (8.07% at Ke 10%, NTM $5.70). On $4.53 the price implies **13.4% a year at Ke 11.05%** (11.2% at Ke 10%; 15.2% at Ke 12%; 14.3% if all equity compensation is deducted). Correcting only the rate (no SBC deduction) gives 10.2%, i.e. already at the 10% base. At the dossier's own 10% base the corrected value is $95.7 (Ke 11.05%) or $112.0 (Ke 10%) against the $122.01 price. **implied_vs_base moves from "below" to "above".** The dividend ($5.40 a year) also exceeds SBC-burdened cash earnings (about $4.0 trailing).
3. **Verdict change: INCLUDE-SMALL -> WATCH.** The dossier's INCLUDE-SMALL rested on implied growth below the base, with the margin already described as thin; that no longer holds. Business evidence (fee base, fundraising, corporate leverage 2.1x) is verified and unchanged, so this is a price call, consistent with how comparable corrections were handled (APD, APH, AEE). Kill criteria stand. Scenario returns (-6.6% / +9.8% / +21.5% a year) reproduce arithmetically on their stated market-multiple basis and are not restated, but the base case uses 12% growth and an 18x exit multiple that are not tied to a discount rate.

# STX — Seagate Technology Holdings plc

## 1. Verdict

**WATCH** — thesis horizon 12–24 months. Seagate's nearline-HDD business is genuinely sold out, guidance has beaten
by wide margins for three straight quarters, and the balance sheet is being repaired fast — but the 25-Sep-2026 close
of $916.83 (mkt cap $208.4bn) already prices in a level of sustained growth this business has never delivered over a
full cycle, and the stock's own 5-year history (a >90% peak-to-trough round trip in 2021–2023) shows how quickly HDD
sentiment can reverse. This is a "great business, priced for more than it can deliver" case, not a "don't own it"
case: revisit after the next 1–2 prints if nearline pricing and hyperscaler capex commentary confirm durability.

## 2. Business in plain English

Seagate makes mass-capacity (nearline) hard disk drives — the spinning-magnetic-media storage that hyperscale cloud
providers (AWS, Azure, Google, Meta, etc.) buy in bulk to store the exabytes of data that AI training and inference
generate, plus a smaller consumer/enterprise storage tail. It is one of only two scaled global HDD makers left (with
Western Digital); Toshiba is a distant third. It makes money by selling drives at a price per terabyte that scales
with each new recording-technology generation (now HAMR/Mozaic platform), so revenue growth comes from a mix of unit
volume and $/TB pricing. Its moat is the extreme capital and IP barriers to building a competitive HDD business from
scratch (three players worldwide, unchanged in a decade) plus multi-year hyperscaler qualification cycles.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case

**Bull (3 points):** (1) AI training/inference data growth structurally raises the exabyte-per-dollar demand curve
for nearline storage in a way that is not just a cyclical restock — hyperscalers have publicly signalled multi-year
capacity plans; (2) HAMR/Mozaic technology transition gives Seagate a genuine areal-density/cost lead versus WDC for
several more product generations, supporting continued pricing power ($25–30/TB nearline pricing per the triage
screen); (3) balance sheet discipline (debt down $1.4bn in FY26) de-risks the equity story even if growth decelerates.

**Bear (3 points):** (1) HDD is a duopoly with a 20-year history of severe boom-bust cycles (STX went from ~$120 in
2021 to ~$35–55 in 2022–2023 on a unit-demand air-pocket) — a repeat on any hyperscaler capex pause would be brutal
given today's much higher starting multiple; (2) the reverse DCF implies growth well beyond any historical precedent
for this business, meaning the stock has very little margin of safety if guidance merely *meets* rather than beats;
(3) the unresolved BIS export-control settlement (below) carries real tail risk — failure to comply could lift the
suspended denial order and restrict Seagate's ability to export products at all.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. **Guidance beat-margin compresses to <3% (vs the ~4–14% beats of the last three quarters) for two consecutive
   quarters** — the first sign the upcycle is normalizing.
2. **Sequential revenue growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters** (vs the +7–24% sequential growth of the
   last four quarters).
3. **Net debt/EBITDA rises above 1.5x** (FY26 exit: debt $3.6bn, cash $1.7bn, EBITDA run-rate ~$4.5–5.0bn implies
   currently well under 0.5x — a reversal would signal capital-allocation or demand stress).
4. **Any BIS finding of non-compliance with the April 2023 Settlement Agreement**, which could lift the suspension of
   the denial order (see §11) — an existential, not incremental, risk if triggered.
5. **Nearline $/TB pricing (company-disclosed or via WDC's read-across) declines quarter-over-quarter** — the
   clearest, earliest signal that the "sold out through 2027" thesis is cracking.

## 11. Red-flag scan (primary source: FY2026 10-K, Item 3/Note 12; filed 2026-08-04)

- **BIS export-control settlement (material, ongoing):** on 18 Apr 2023 Seagate's subsidiaries settled with the U.S.
  Department of Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security over sales of HDDs to Huawei between Aug 2020–Sep 2021.
  Agreed to pay **$300 million in quarterly $15 million installments over five years from 31 Oct 2023** (i.e. through
  ~2028) plus three compliance audits (two completed). A denial order is suspended, waived only after 5 years of full
  compliance. As of 3 Jul 2026: **$120 million remains accrued** ($45m current + $75m non-current); $75m was paid in
  FY26. Non-compliance risk is explicitly disclosed as potentially triggering loss of the suspension.
- **Securities class action (In re Seagate Technology Holdings plc Securities Litigation, N.D. Cal.):** class period
  14 Sep 2020–19 Apr 2023 (bookended by the BIS matter), consolidated Sep 2023. **Settled in principle 3 Apr 2026 for
  $175 million total** (~$70m insurer-funded; Seagate recorded a **$105 million charge**). Preliminary court approval
  granted 7 Jul 2026; final approval hearing **17 Nov 2026**.
- **Patent litigation, both directions:** (i) *Lambeth Magnetic Structures v. Seagate* — district court found for
  Seagate (2022) but the Federal Circuit **vacated and remanded for a new trial on 17 Sep 2025**; (ii) *Godo Kaisha IP
  Bridge 1 v. Seagate* (D. Minn., 8 patents-in-suit, filed 2024) — ongoing, company says claims are without merit;
  (iii) Seagate is the *plaintiff* in an antitrust case against HDD suspension-assembly suppliers (NHK Spring et al.)
  — 9th Circuit revived Seagate's claims in Jan 2026 after a prior dismissal; NHK has petitioned the Supreme Court.
- **No material weakness, no going-concern language, no restatement** identified in the FY2026 10-K's Item 9A or
  auditor's report.

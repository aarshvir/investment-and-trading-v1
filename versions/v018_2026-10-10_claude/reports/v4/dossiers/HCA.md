## 2. Business in plain English
HCA is the largest for-profit hospital operator in the U.S. (~190 hospitals, ~2,500 sites of care, concentrated in Florida, Texas, Tennessee/Nashville metro). It makes money by owning dominant local hospital networks (#1 or #2 share in most of its markets), which gives it negotiating leverage with commercial payers and scale efficiencies on labor, supplies and capital equipment. Growth comes from same-facility volume (admissions, outpatient visits), pricing/payer mix, and bolt-on capacity expansion, funded by strong free cash flow that is recycled into buybacks and a modest dividend.

## 3. Why the model likes it / is it durable
Quality and price-vs-growth factor scores (per triage) reflect consistent double-digit ROIC, disciplined capital allocation and a multi-decade record of same-facility volume growth. This is durable — it's the local-market density moat, not a one-off. The risk the model may underweight is **policy risk** (ACA marketplace subsidy expiration, Medicaid supplemental-payment rule changes), which is a real, current, non-cyclical margin headwind (see §5), not noise.

## 8. Bull case
1. Density moat: #1/#2 share in most core markets gives pricing power versus payers that the model has sustained for 20+ years.
2. FCF conversion (113% of NI in FY2025) funds continued buybacks even if payer mix stays soft, protecting EPS growth.
3. 2027 consensus (+9.7–9.9% EPS) has not yet been cut despite the Q2 2026 guidance reduction — a beat against low-bar 2026 comps is plausible.

## 9. Bear case
1. ACA marketplace subsidy expiration (effective loss of enhanced premium tax credits after 2025 without Congressional extension) permanently shifts a slice of HCA's insured mix from commercial-exchange to self-pay/Medicaid, which is the exact mechanism management cited for the Q2 2026 cut — this is a multi-year, not one-quarter, risk.
2. Respiratory-season volume volatility (Q1 2026: admissions −42% YoY) shows HCA's "recession-resistant" narrative still has real quarter-to-quarter cyclicality.
3. 3x leverage plus continued >100%-of-NI buybacks leaves less room to absorb a second consecutive guidance cut without a re-rating.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. FY2026 diluted EPS prints below the new guided floor of $28.70 (i.e., another cut or a miss versus the July 2026 range).
2. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA (per 10-Q balance sheet and guided adjusted EBITDA) exceeds 3.5x.
3. Same-facility revenue growth < 3% for two consecutive quarters (ex. one-off flu/storm effects called out by management).
4. A second guidance cut at the Q3 2026 (2026-10-22-area) or Q4 2025-cycle release, evidencing the payer-mix issue is structural, not transitory.
5. Any new DOJ/HHS-OIG False Claims Act matter involving current (not historical/settled) billing practices.

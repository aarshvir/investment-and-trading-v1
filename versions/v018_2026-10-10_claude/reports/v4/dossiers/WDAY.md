# WDAY - Workday, Inc. (NASDAQ: WDAY) - standard-depth diligence (agent F140)

**1. Verdict: WATCH. Horizon 12-36 months.** The triage "solid but unremarkable" holds, with one correction: the business is better than "unremarkable" (13% growth, 31% non-GAAP margin, $2.8B of free cash flow) but the price is about fair for what it can deliver, and the lead indicators are slowing. On 25 Sep 2026 the price of $189.45 implies about 13.3% a year growth in free cash flow after stock compensation for ten years; my base case is 13.5%, so the market is pricing in roughly my base and nothing more. Three things stop me upgrading: total subscription backlog growth has fallen from 17.0% to 8.0% in four quarters, stock compensation is 16% of revenue, and the AI effect on per-seat HR and finance software is unresolved. Condition that would change it: a price near or below about $165 (base-case DCF at a 10% discount rate is roughly $165 a share), or two quarters with total subscription backlog growth stabilising at 10% or more.

**2. Business in plain English.** Workday sells cloud software that large organisations run their payroll, HR, finance and planning on, plus newer AI "agents" that do parts of that work. Customers sign multi-year subscriptions billed mostly annually in advance (unearned revenue $4,387M current at 31 Jul 2026, consolidated balance sheet), which is why cash arrives before profit. Subscription revenue was $2,471M of $2,649M total in FQ2 2027 (93%). Moat: switching costs (HR and payroll data) and deep integration into customers' finance and people processes. Main competitors named in the 10-Q risk factors: Oracle and SAP. The CEO is co-founder Aneel Bhusri (returned 6 Feb 2026 when Carl Eschenbach left, 8-K accession 0001327811-26-000007); Bhusri and co-founder David Duffield control about 68% of the voting power through Class B stock (same 8-K).

**3. Why the model likes it.** b1 composite decile 5 (rank 254). Family percentiles: quality 0.86, value 0.58, momentum 0.15, earnings surprise 0.43. So it is a quality-plus-value name held back by poor momentum (the stock has de-rated with the software sector; d4 peers trade at 8.5x to 14.7x forward earnings for ADBE, INTU, CRM and ADSK). Durable? The quality score rests on gross margin and cash flow, both real; but GAAP returns are flattered by a $1.52 a share tax benefit in FQ2 2027 (below) and the "value" signal is based on free cash flow before about $1.6B of annual stock compensation.

**8. Bull and bear.** Bull: (1) "AI driving more than 25% of our new ACV and more than 5,500 customers now using at least one of our organic agents" (CEO, FQ2 2027 release) could turn Workday into a per-outcome AI platform; (2) margin expansion and buybacks compound EPS (non-GAAP EPS $2.75 vs $2.21, +24%); (3) 12-month backlog still +14.2%. Bear: (1) total backlog growth +8.0% and guided subscription growth of 12% point to single-digit growth within two years; (2) AI agents reduce headcount at customers, compressing HR and finance seat counts; (3) stock compensation of $1.6B a year and repeated restructuring (Feb 2026 and Sep 2026) suggest margin gains are partly cost cuts rather than growth.

**9. Key risks and kill criteria.**
1. Total subscription revenue backlog growth below 5% year on year at any quarter-end (8.0% at 31 Jul 2026).
2. Full-year FY2027 subscription revenue guidance cut below $9.90B (current "$9.940 billion to $9.950 billion") or quarterly subscription growth guided below 10%.
3. Free cash flow after stock compensation below $900M on a trailing-four-quarter basis (about $1.2B at 31 Jul 2026).
4. Stock compensation above 17% of revenue for four consecutive quarters (17.4% in FQ2 2027, 16.2% TTM).
5. Consolidated cash and marketable securities below $2.0B while debt principal of $3.0B remains (cash $3.4B at 31 Jul 2026), which would signal buybacks outrunning cash generation.

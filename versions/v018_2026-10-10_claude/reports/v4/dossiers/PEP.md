# PEP — PepsiCo, Inc.

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon: 24–36 months.
Reason: a durable, cash-generative global snacks-and-beverage franchise with 54 consecutive years of dividend increases, priced at a discount to peer KO — but the GLP-1/health-conscious mix headwind on core snacks volumes is a real, unresolved structural question and underlying (core, non-GAAP) growth is materially slower than the eye-catching GAAP headlines this year.

## 2. Business in plain English
PepsiCo sells Frito-Lay salty snacks, Quaker foods, Pepsi/Mountain Dew/Gatorade and other beverages to consumers worldwide, through its own direct-store-delivery network in North America and a mix of owned and franchise bottling internationally. It makes money on brand-driven pricing power and massive route-to-market scale; roughly 40% of revenue is convenient foods (Frito-Lay/Quaker) and 60% beverages, split roughly evenly between North America and international. Competitive position is duopoly-like in US snacks (Frito-Lay) and strong #2 in global beverages behind Coca-Cola.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and durability
`b1_live_scores.csv`: ROE 51.5% (thin equity base from decades of buybacks, discussed in §6 — not a pure profitability signal), earnings yield 4.7%, FCF/P 5.3%, book/price 12.6%; live rank 323/503, 12-month momentum roughly flat (+2.9%). Triage (`Q03_triage.json`) flagged margin compression to 10.8% and a GLP-1/health-conscious headwind on salty-snack volumes as the live red flag, alongside an activist (Elliott Management) angle as the reason for the "advance." The GLP-1/volume concern is corroborated in the primary filings (see §4/§11); the Elliott activist-stake claim could **not** be confirmed against any SEC ownership filing in this pass (see §11) and should be treated as unverified, not as an established catalyst.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF / cash returns:** FY2026 guidance targets capital spending below 5% of net revenue, an FCF-conversion ratio (FCF / core net income) of at least 80%, and total cash returns to shareholders of ~$8.9bn (dividends $7.9bn + buybacks $1.0bn) (8-K exhibit 99, 09-Jul-2026). YTD operating cash flow was $2,365M through Q2 2026 vs $996M through Q2 2025 (10-Q cash-flow statement) — a large YoY improvement, though PepsiCo's cash flow is heavily H2-weighted (FY2025 full-year OCF was $12,087M).
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** see §4 — the $1,860M Q2 2025 impairment is the main driver of the large GAAP/adjusted divergence this year; core measures are the more reliable read on trend.
- **Balance sheet (consolidated, 10-Q, 13-Jun-2026, accession 0000077476-26-000035):** cash $10,251M; long-term debt (incl. capital leases) $42,612M + current portion $1,600M + short-term borrowings $10,602M ≈ **total debt $54.8bn**; net debt ≈ **$44.6bn**. Stockholders' equity $22,098M (thin relative to debt — a function of decades of buybacks/dividends against a large, mature cash-generative base, not distress). `net_debt_to_ebitda` per the quant card is a moderate 2.3x.
- **Litigation (see §11):** Baltimore, MD public-nuisance case against PepsiCo/Frito-Lay survives after most claims were dismissed with prejudice (Circuit Court for Baltimore City, 21-Jul-2025); oral argument on the surviving public-nuisance claim is scheduled for PepsiCo's fiscal quarter ending 05-Sep-2026.
- **Dividend:** 4% increase in the annualized dividend effective the June 2026 payment — the company's **54th consecutive annual increase** (8-K exhibit 99, 16-Apr-2026).
- **Buybacks:** $479M repurchased in Q2 2026 ($182M in Q1 2026) under a new $10bn program authorized 03-Feb-2026, running 01-Feb-2026 to 28-Feb-2030; $9.518bn of capacity remained as of 13-Jun-2026 (10-Q, Item 2). **[Corrected 2026-10-08: $479M is the 24-week year-to-date figure; the Q2 quarter alone was about $297M (XBRL 479 less 182) (DV33)]**

Illustrative reverse DCF (own model, not V1): FCF₀ = 4.5% × $175.7bn ≈ $7.9bn; discount rate 7.5% (large, low-beta staples name); 10-year explicit growth then 2.5% terminal growth. Solving for the FCF growth rate that reproduces the $175.7bn market cap gives **≈3.5%/yr for 10 years**.
- **My base case:** ~5–6%/yr blended 10-yr core-EPS/FCF growth, matching the current guidance range's midpoint and consistent with PepsiCo's own multi-decade record, assuming the GLP-1/mix headwind is offset (not solved) by pricing, innovation (protein/functional/zero-sugar) and cost actions. **Implied (~3.5%) is below my base case (~5–6%) → "below."** **[Corrected 2026-10-08: base of 5-6% growth no longer matches company guidance (FY2026 core EPS +2.5% to +3.5% after 8 Oct 2026); on TTM filing-based FCF after SBC the price implies about 1.2% to 3.4% a year at Ke 7.54% (5.17% + Blume beta 0.572 x 4.14%) on FCF bases of $8.96bn (filings) to $7.5bn (Yahoo less SBC), so implied_vs_base is in line and the verdict moves to WATCH (DV33)]**
- **Bear case (~2%/yr):** GLP-1/health-shift headwind intensifies, North America snacks volumes keep declining, no re-rating, multiple compresses toward 12x.
- **Bull case (~9%/yr):** Portfolio/cost actions (whether or not driven by an activist) succeed, volumes recover as innovation offsets the health-shift headwind, multiple re-rates toward 18x, narrowing the gap to KO.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) 54 consecutive years of dividend increases and an FCF-conversion target of ≥80% show a durable, disciplined capital-return machine even in a slow-growth year. (2) Trades at 14.5x NTM vs KO's materially higher multiple, with Street target upside of ~20% per the quant card. (3) Six of PepsiCo's largest markets are still beverage/snack categories with structural per-capita growth runway internationally (not verified quantitatively in this pass).
**Bear:** (1) Core EPS growth of only 4% (Q2) / 6% (YTD) — the headline "137% EPS growth" is a one-off impairment comparison, not real acceleration. (2) GLP-1/weight-loss-drug adoption and a broader shift to "better-for-you" eating is a structural, not cyclical, risk to salty-snack and full-sugar-soda volumes that two quarters of data cannot resolve either way. (3) Net debt of ~$44.6bn against $22.1bn of equity, funding both the dividend and a new $10bn buyback program, leaves less room to fund a larger portfolio pivot without added leverage.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Organic revenue growth falls below 2% for two consecutive quarters (from 2.4–2.6% in H1 2026).
2. FY2026 core EPS guidance (5–7% growth) is cut.
3. Core operating margin falls below 16% for two consecutive quarters (from 16.8% in Q2 2026).
4. Net debt / EBITDA rises above 3.0x (from ~2.3x currently).
5. The Baltimore public-nuisance case produces an adverse ruling with quantified damages.

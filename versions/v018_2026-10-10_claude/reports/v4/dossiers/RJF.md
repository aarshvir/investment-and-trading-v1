# RJF — Raymond James Financial, Inc. (F47, wave-2 diligence)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE**, thesis horizon 24–36 months. A well-run, conservatively capitalized diversified wealth-management/brokerage franchise, growing revenue and EPS at a double-digit clip, trading at a mid-teens trailing P/E — the reverse DCF (§7) implies the market is pricing in materially less growth than the firm's own recent run-rate and disclosed guidance. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: superseded: on P/TBV vs ROTCE the price implies a sustainable ROTCE of 21.7% against 22% delivered in fiscal 2026 and a 20% through-cycle base; implied vs base is in_line and the verdict is INCLUDE-SMALL; see Re-assessment (RA13) below]**

## 2. Business in plain English
Raymond James is a diversified financial-services holding company built around four segments: Private Client Group (retail brokerage and fee-based advisory — the largest profit driver, ~19,500 financial advisors), Capital Markets (equity/debt underwriting, M&A advisory, fixed income), Asset Management (proprietary and third-party managed accounts), and Raymond James Bank (a Fed-chartered bank subsidiary funding client cash sweep balances, primarily through securities-based lending and residential mortgages to the firm's own advisory clients). It makes money from advisory/brokerage fees on client assets, net interest income on client cash and bank loans, and investment-banking/underwriting fees — a business model built on advisor retention and a conservative "recruit-and-retain" culture rather than aggressive risk-taking.

## 3. Why the model liked it — durable or artefact?
Triage (Q05, quality 4/5, growth 4/5, price_vs_growth 4/5): "24% eps growth and 18.4% ROE at just 11.3x forward is attractively priced for a consistently well-run franchise." b1 quant: composite only **0.504** (decile 6, live_rank 247 — solidly mid-pack, not a top-decile quant screen hit); fam_Q (quality) 0.401, fam_V (value) 0.403, fam_M (momentum) 0.373, fam_S (earnings momentum) **0.862** — the strongest signal by far is earnings momentum (SUE), consistent with the string of EPS beats below. **Durability check:** RJF's growth is a mix of (a) structurally durable advisor-recruiting/asset-gathering compounding (assets under administration grow with markets + net new advisors + net new assets — a genuine, multi-decade moat) and (b) cyclical capital-markets and net-interest-income swings that are *not* durable at the current run-rate (Q3 FY25's dip to $436M NI/$2.12 EPS, the weakest of the last 9 quarters, coincided with softer investment-banking activity). The model's growth score is picking up both; the durable component alone still supports a positive but more moderate long-run growth rate than the recent 20%+ headline.

## 5. Guidance track record (quote the numbers; versus prior)
RJF guides to an **adjusted pre-tax margin target of "approximately 20%"** rather than an EPS or revenue number, and has **held (not raised or cut) that target across FY2026 to date**:
- Q1 FY26: adjusted pretax margin 20.0% (met target).
- Q2 FY26: adjusted pretax margin 19.7% (essentially met, slightly below).
- Q3 FY26: adjusted pretax margin 19.9% (met), "in line with its guidance of approximately 20%" per management commentary, despite disclosed headwinds from lower interest-related and investment-banking revenue that quarter.
- Forward (as of the Q3 FY26 release): Q4 FY26 Asset Management/administrative fees guided **up ~11%** from the Q3 level (driven by higher fee-based PCG assets); aggregate NII plus third-party bank fees guided **approximately flat** Q3→Q4; full-year non-compensation expense **on track at ~$2.3B** even after absorbing two recent (unnamed in the source pulled) acquisitions.
This is a genuinely consistent guidance track record — no raise-then-miss pattern, no "beat and raise" theatrics, numbers delivered within a few tenths of a point of the stated target for three straight quarters.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Cash conversion / FCF:** for a broker-dealer/bank holding company, operating cash flow and "FCF" are dominated by client cash and loan-book movements and are **not a meaningful quality signal** (per the sector playbook) — not used here as a primary metric. Earnings quality is instead read through the margin/beat consistency in §5 and the balance-sheet discipline below.
- **Balance sheet (2026-06-30, 10-Q):** total assets $94.24B, total liabilities $81.45B, stockholders' equity $12.70B (~13.5% equity/assets — high for a bank-affiliated broker-dealer). Cash $9.98B. Parent-level long-term debt is small (the `DebtLongtermAndShorttermCombinedAmount` XBRL tag shows only **$0.7–0.95B** across the last two quarters) — RJF funds its balance sheet mainly through client cash and Raymond James Bank deposits, not parent debt, which is a conservative funding structure by broker-dealer standards.
- **Raymond James Bank capital (per company disclosures, cross-checked via search, not independently re-derived from XBRL this pass):** total capital ratio ~22.5–24.3% and Tier 1 leverage ratio ~11.7–12.7% through FY2026 quarters — both far above regulatory minimums; management and third-party commentary describe credit quality of the bank's loan book (chiefly securities-based lending and residential mortgages to the firm's own advisory clients) as "improving" through 2026. Not independently verified against Call Report data in this pass — flagged as a gap given the standard-depth time-box.
- **Capital return:** dividend $2.16/share annualized (1.34% yield at 2026-09-25 close); the firm also runs an ongoing buyback programme (not separately quantified this pass).
- **M&A:** the Q3 FY26 release references "two recent acquisitions" affecting the non-compensation expense run-rate; neither was named in the sources pulled — flagged as an open item for a deeper pass.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull (3):**
1. Consistent execution against a stated ~20% adjusted pretax margin target for three consecutive quarters, with revenue up 16% YoY and pretax income up 33% YoY in the most recent quarter — a genuine, guided, delivered result, not a one-off beat.
2. Conservative funding structure: equity is ~13.5% of assets, parent debt is under $1B, and the bank subsidiary's capital ratios (total capital ~22–24%, Tier 1 leverage ~12%) run far above regulatory minimums — low balance-sheet-risk profile relative to a typical regional bank. **[Corrected 26 Sep 2026: parent debt is about $4.47bn (senior notes $3.52bn + other borrowings $0.95bn), and the 22–24% / ~12% ratios are RJF-consolidated; Raymond James Bank's own ratios are 16.4% total capital and 8.0% Tier 1 leverage. See the Correction section below.]**
3. Reverse DCF suggests the market is pricing in materially less growth (~4%/yr) than a reasonable base case (~7–9%/yr) for a firm still adding advisors and fee-based assets.

**Bear (3):**
1. A meaningful share of recent growth (net interest income, investment-banking fees) is cyclical and rate/market-dependent — Q3 FY25's dip to $436M net income (the weakest of the last nine quarters) shows how quickly a soft capital-markets quarter shows up in the P&L.
2. Industry-wide "cash sweep" litigation: RJF faces consolidated putative class actions (filed Aug/Dec 2024) alleging breach of fiduciary duty over cash-sweep interest rates paid to advisory clients; a March 2026 order dismissed some claims, a class-certification motion was filed May 2026, and the case remains pending as of this writing (two named plaintiffs after a July 2026 partial voluntary dismissal). Not unique to RJF (over a dozen firms face similar suits), but a real, unresolved legal and reputational overhang with an uncertain financial outcome.
3. Two unnamed recent acquisitions are already affecting the expense run-rate; without knowing what was bought or at what price, integration and goodwill risk cannot be ruled out (flagged gap, not a confirmed problem).

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria — thesis-invalidation triggers (measurable, would break the thesis; verbatim into summary JSON)

2. "Net income falls below $450M in any quarter for two consecutive quarters" (Q3 FY25's $436M was the low point of the last 9 quarters; a repeat/worsening would signal the cyclical component is structurally larger than assumed).
3. "Raymond James Bank's total capital ratio falls below 15%" (currently 22.5%+, a large buffer; a rapid decline would signal credit-quality or balance-sheet-growth stress not currently visible).
4. "The consolidated cash-sweep litigation results in a certified class and/or a disclosed reserve/settlement exceeding $250M."
5. "Parent-level debt rises above $5B without a clearly disclosed, accretive acquisition rationale" (current ~$0.7–0.95B; a large debt-funded deal would change the conservative-funding thesis).

## Correction (26 Sep 2026) — DA7 fact-check, both findings CONFIRMED against the primary filing
Source for both items: RJF 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-06-30, SEC EDGAR accession **0000720005-26-000070** (`rjf-20260630.htm`), retrieved and read in full this pass.

**(1) §6 "Raymond James Bank capital" and kill criterion 3 were RJF-consolidated ratios, mislabelled as the bank subsidiary's own.** The MD&A states verbatim: *"As of June 30, 2026, our tier 1 leverage ratio was 11.7% and total capital ratio was 22.5%, both well above regulatory capital requirements"* — this is RJF-consolidated, and is what §6 originally reported as "Raymond James Bank." The Notes to Condensed Consolidated Financial Statements (Regulatory Capital note, "Raymond James Bank" row) instead give the bank's own, standalone ratios:
| Raymond James Bank (standalone) | Required | Well-capitalized | Jun 30, 2026 | Sep 30, 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 leverage | 4.0% | 5.0% | **8.0%** ($3,612M) | **8.0%** ($3,434M) |
| Tier 1 capital | 8.5% | 8.0% | 15.1% ($3,612M) | 13.9% ($3,434M) |
| CET1 capital | 7.0% | 6.5% | 15.1% ($3,612M) | 13.9% ($3,434M) |
| Total capital | 10.5% | 10.0% | **16.4%** ($3,911M) | **15.2%** ($3,743M) |
**DA7's read is confirmed exactly.** §6's "total capital ~22.5–24.3%, Tier 1 leverage ~11.7–12.7%" is refuted as a description of the *bank*; it is the RJF holding-company figure. The bank-standalone total capital ratio is **16.4% at 2026-06-30, having been 15.2% at 2025-09-30** — only ~20bp above the 15% kill-criterion threshold nine months earlier, and currently ~140bp above it. This is materially closer to firing than §6/§9 originally implied, even though the trend over the last three quarters has been improving, not deteriorating.
**Kill criterion 3 is restated:** "Raymond James Bank's standalone total capital ratio (per the 10-Q Regulatory Capital note, not the RJF-consolidated ratio) falls below 15%" — currently 16.4% (2026-06-30 10-Q), up from 15.2% (2025-09-30); the ratio has been within ~20bp of this threshold within the last year and should be tracked on the bank-standalone figure going forward, not the consolidated one.

**(2) §6 "parent-level debt... only $0.7–0.95B" omitted senior notes payable and understated total parent-level debt by ~$3.5B.** The consolidated balance sheet (2026-06-30) shows, as separate liability line items: "Other borrowings $950M" (this is the figure my original XBRL pull captured, tag `DebtLongtermAndShorttermCombinedAmount`) **and, separately, "Senior notes payable $3,522M"** (2025-09-30: $950M→$700M other borrowings and $3,520M senior notes payable). **Total parent-level debt is therefore ≈$4.472B at 2026-06-30, not $0.7–0.95B.** DA7's figure of "~$4.47bn total" is confirmed exactly. The weighted-average rate on senior notes payable was disclosed at 4.91% in the interest-expense table. Against $12.7B of equity this is ~35% debt/equity at the parent level — a real, moderate leverage position, not the near-zero-debt profile originally described; still not high by diversified-financial-holdco standards, and covered by $2.5B of disclosed RJF corporate cash, but §6/§8 overstated how "conservative" the funding structure is.

**Net effect on verdict, implied-vs-base and scenarios:** the valuation and reverse-DCF work (§7) rested on earnings multiples and guidance-delivery consistency, not on the (incorrect) capital-structure claims, so **verdict remains INCLUDE, implied_vs_base remains "below," and scenario_returns_3y are unchanged.** The correction does, however, remove one leg of the original bull case (§8, bull point 2, "parent debt is under $1B") — that point should be read as retracted — and tightens the genuine distance-to-kill-criterion on bank capital adequacy, which is now flagged as a closer-to-firing metric than previously stated, not a comfortable buffer.

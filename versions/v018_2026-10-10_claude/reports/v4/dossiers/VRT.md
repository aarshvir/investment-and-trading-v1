# VRT — Vertiv Holdings Co

## 1. Verdict

**WATCH** — thesis horizon 12–36 months. Vertiv is a genuinely excellent business right now: the pure-play leader in power and liquid-cooling infrastructure for AI/hyperscaler data centers, growing net sales 24–30% YoY with margins expanding ~400+bps and free cash flow up 150–240% YoY, now sitting in a net-cash position with a record $15bn backlog. But the 25 September 2026 close (already down from a post-Q2-selloff level, after the stock fell as much as 26% on 29 July 2026 when reported net sales missed consensus by ~3%) still prices in continued strong growth on a reverse DCF (~14.3%/year for ten years) that is at or slightly above this dossier's own evidence-based base case (~11–12%/year, reflecting an expected deceleration from today's 30%+ organic growth as the current 2.9x book-to-bill normalizes). This is exactly the triage's own caution: "significant re-rating already happened; a hyperscaler capex pause is the key risk." A genuinely strong operating story, priced for more strength than a conservative base case can defend — WATCH, not INCLUDE, pending either a better entry price or firmer evidence that the current order/backlog surge is durable rather than a pull-forward.

## 2. Business in plain English

Vertiv makes the power and thermal-management ("cooling") equipment that keeps data centers running — uninterruptible power supplies, power distribution, switchgear, and (increasingly, as AI chips get hotter) liquid-cooling systems for the racks that house GPUs. It sells almost entirely to data-center owners and operators: hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta and others building AI infrastructure), colocation providers, and enterprises. It does not own or operate any data centers itself — it is a picks-and-shovels equipment and services supplier to the AI buildout, competing with Schneider Electric and Eaton. Its revenue is driven by (a) new data-center construction and retrofits tied directly to hyperscaler capital-expenditure cycles, and (b) a growing recurring services/parts business on its large installed base. ## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull (3):**
1. Backlog of $15bn (+109% YoY) and a Q2 2026 book-to-bill still-strong at a normalizing-but-elevated level, alongside FY2026 guidance raised at every release this year (including the quarter that missed on headline revenue), are real, filed evidence that demand for AI-data-center power/cooling infrastructure remains exceptionally strong.
2. Free cash flow conversion is now excellent and the company reached a **net-cash position** in Q2 2026 for the first time — a rapidly de-risking balance sheet funding continued capacity expansion (capex up >3x YoY) without external financing.
3. Adjusted operating margin expanded ~400–430bps YoY in both of the last two quarters, evidence of genuine operating leverage and pricing power (management noted favorable price-cost execution overcoming tariff concerns), not merely a volume story.

**Bear (3):**
1. The Q2 2026 revenue miss (reported vs. consensus, ~3% shortfall) triggered a stock decline of up to 26% in a single session and multiple plaintiff-law-firm "investor alert" solicitations (Schall, Hagens Berman, Pomerantz) — these solicitations are boilerplate and do not by themselves establish wrongdoing, but the magnitude of the single-day price reaction confirms the market now treats any revenue shortfall, however small or "timing"-related, as a serious threat to the growth thesis at this valuation.
2. A 2.9x book-to-bill ratio is, by the company's own multi-quarter history (1.4x as recently as Q3 2025), extreme and plausibly unsustainable; a normalization would mechanically slow the backlog-growth headline even without any change in end demand, and roughly half of planned US data-center builds have reportedly experienced delays or cancellations industry-wide, primarily from power-infrastructure constraints rather than demand destruction — a risk that could show up as further "timing" misses.
3. Trailing P/E of 57x and an implied-growth reverse DCF modestly above this dossier's own base case leaves limited margin of safety: even the base case here (not the bear case) implies a mid-teens 3-year annualized return only because EPS growth is assumed to stay very high (20%/year) for three more years — a materially more optimistic assumption than BR's or LDOS's base cases in this batch.

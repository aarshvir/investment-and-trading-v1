# AMAT — Applied Materials, Inc. (Semiconductor Materials & Equipment, Information Technology)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — 12–24 month horizon. This overturns the triage's ADVANCE call once a real reverse DCF is run. Applied Materials is executing extremely well (record revenue, guidance raised twice this fiscal year, net-cash balance sheet, buybacks slowed rather than chased into strength) and the stock has already fallen ~34–42% from its 30 June 2026 high. But even after that drawdown, the 10-year reverse DCF shows the market pricing in **~18–24%/year FCF growth for a decade** — far above any credible through-cycle base case for a capital-equipment company, and the independent factor data agrees: AMAT's value/FCF-yield percentile is in the bottom quintile of the whole universe (19th pct). A great business, priced for more than a capital-intensive, historically cyclical franchise can plausibly deliver. Condition to upgrade: either a further material de-rating (FCF yield rising toward ~3.5%+, i.e., meaningfully lower price for the same or higher FCF) or 3–4 more quarters of evidence that AI-driven WFE demand is a structural step-change rather than a supercycle that mean-reverts.

## 2. Business in plain English
Applied Materials makes the machines that make chips: deposition, etch, ion implantation, chemical-mechanical planarization and inspection/metrology tools sold to foundries, memory makers and IDMs (TSMC, Samsung, Micron, Intel and others) building fabs, plus a large recurring Applied Global Services (AGS) parts/service business tied to the installed base. It is the largest wafer-fab-equipment (WFE) vendor by revenue, with leading share in several deposition/etch categories and a growing advanced-packaging business tied to AI accelerators (2.5D/3D packaging, chip-on-wafer). It makes money by selling capital equipment (lumpy, tied to customers' capex cycles) plus steadier services/parts revenue on the installed base, and its economics are fundamentally a **fixed-cost manufacturing/engineering business layered on a physics roadmap** — margins swing hard with fab utilisation and the industry's capex cycle, not primarily with AMAT's own execution.

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse DCF

Vendor NTM P/E ≈ 26–27x (peers: Lam Research, KLA typically low-to-mid 20s–30s during upcycles; ASML higher given EUV monopoly) — **not obviously expensive on a simple forward-P/E basis**, which is exactly the trap the sector playbook warns about: *"the stock screens at 6–9x on peak earnings just as the correction begins... treat a mid-single-digit P/E on record earnings as a warning, not a valuation."* AMAT's 26–27x is not in that extreme zone, but its **FCF yield tells a different, much more cautious story**.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Quarterly sequential revenue **declines for two consecutive quarters** (the cycle turning down from this level).
2. China export-control revenue headwind **exceeds $1B annualized** (vs. the current $600M FY26 guide) without offsetting non-China demand.
3. Gross margin falls **below 46–47%** (vs. current ~50%) for two consecutive quarters — a utilisation/pricing deterioration signal.
4. A **second** BIS/export-control enforcement action, or a new DOJ/Commerce investigation, is disclosed.
5. Capex/revenue stays **above ~10% for four or more consecutive quarters** while OCF growth stalls — over-investing into a cycle top without revenue follow-through.

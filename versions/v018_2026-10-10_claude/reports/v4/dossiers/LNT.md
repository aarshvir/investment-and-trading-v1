One-sentence reason: a well-run Midwest regulated electric/gas utility with a genuine, contracted data-center demand tailwind and above-peer ROE, held to half weight because the growth case is funded by a large, dilution-risk-bearing capex plan and the dividend-implied valuation already prices in most of the near-term growth.

## 2. Business in plain English
Alliant Energy owns two regulated utility subsidiaries — Interstate Power and Light (Iowa) and Wisconsin Power and Light — that generate and sell electricity and natural gas to homes and businesses under state-regulated rates. It earns a return set by regulators on the capital it invests in the grid and power plants; growth comes from rate-base expansion (new generation, transmission, grid upgrades) approved by the Iowa Utilities Commission and Public Service Commission of Wisconsin. The current growth driver is large hyperscale data-center customers (Microsoft, Meta, Google have facilities on its Iowa grid) signing long-term electric-service agreements.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Genuine, contracted (not speculative) hyperscale data-center demand in Iowa (370 MW signed this quarter alone, 2–4 GW pipeline) — a real, multi-year rate-base growth driver most peer utilities lack at this scale.
2. Best-in-class ROE (11.1%) among the Midwest utility peer set, reflecting constructive regulatory relationships in both Iowa and Wisconsin.
3. Guidance tracking the **upper half** of the 2026 range and a 7%+ EPS CAGR guide for 2027–2029, both reaffirmed as of the most recent quarter.

**Bull case is priced generously already** given the dividend-yield-implied growth of only ~4.1% versus a 7%+ guide — most of the upside case requires believing management *and* believing regulators approve the associated rate increases without pushback.

**Bear:**
1. The $13.4B four-year capital plan requires heavy external financing; slower-than-planned equity issuance timing, higher-than-expected interest costs, or a credit-rating pressure event would compress the EPS-growth delivery below the 7%+ guide.
2. Data-center demand pipelines are frequently oversold industry-wide (multiple utilities citing overlapping hyperscaler pipeline MW); if even a portion of the 2–4 GW pipeline fails to convert to signed agreements, the growth re-rating reverses.
3. Regulatory/rate-case risk: Iowa/Wisconsin commissions could slow-walk the large capex-driven rate increases needed to fund the plan without harming affordability optics, especially with data-center customers seen as beneficiaries.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Adjusted/ongoing EPS growth falls below **5%** for a full fiscal year (vs. the 7%+ 2027–29 guide).
2. A signed data-center service agreement (any of the ~370MW+ pipeline) is **cancelled or materially downsized** before energization.
3. Debt/equity ratio rises above **1.8x** (from ~1.41x today **[Corrected 2026-10-08: 1.61x on total debt of $12,118M]**) without a corresponding equity raise, signalling capex is outrunning the planned financing mix.
4. A rate case in Iowa or Wisconsin is **rejected or approved at less than 70%** of the requested revenue increase.
5. Guidance is **cut** (not just reaffirmed) at either of the next two quarterly releases (next: 2026-11-05).

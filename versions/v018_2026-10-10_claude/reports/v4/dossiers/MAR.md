# MAR - Marriott International, Inc. (F138, wave 8, standard depth)

**1. Verdict: WATCH** (horizon 12-36 months). An excellent asset-light franchisor, but at $352.03 the price already assumes roughly 8.5% a year of free-cash-flow growth for ten years; my evidence-based base case is about 7%. Triage said "no" because the stock was up about 32% with little upside; the filings support the business quality but confirm the price is full.
Condition that would change it: a pull-back to roughly $300 (about 25x FY26E adjusted EPS), or evidence that fee growth is running at 10%+ (card-fee uplift plus RevPAR above 4%) on a sustained basis.

**2. Business.** Franchises and manages about 10,000 hotels (1.81m rooms, 148 countries) under Marriott, Sheraton, Westin, Ritz-Carlton, Courtyard and others. It owns almost no hotels; it earns royalty and management fees on hotel revenue, plus co-brand credit card fees from the Bonvoy loyalty programme (295m members). Pipeline of 4,186 properties and about 629,000 rooms gives visible unit growth.

**3. Why the model likes it.** b1 composite 0.526, rank 236 (decile 6). Driven by momentum (0.91, 12-1 month +32%) and quality on cash-flow/assets (0.70); valuation is the worst family (0.08). The model likes the trend, not the price.

**8. Bull / bear.**
Bull: (1) fee-based model with 4.5% unit growth, record pipeline 629,000 rooms and 44% under construction; (2) co-brand card contracts renewed with JPMorgan and Amex and already lifting franchise fees (+$73m in Q2); (3) four-quarter record of beating own guidance.
Bear: (1) 30x earnings for mid-single-digit RevPAR growth: my base return is 4.6% a year, below a 8.5% hurdle; (2) Middle East RevPAR down 43% and the 10-Q says the impact continues into Q3, plus a US World Cup boost that does not repeat; (3) returns are debt-assisted: buybacks exceed FCF, stockholders' deficit (4.5bn), net debt 2.9x adjusted EBITDA.

**9. Kill criteria.**
1. Worldwide comparable systemwide constant-dollar RevPAR growth below 1.0% for two consecutive quarters (company release, non-GAAP).

3. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance midpoint cut below $11.40 at any release.
4. Net rooms growth below 4.0% year on year for two consecutive quarters.
5. Consolidated operating cash flow less capex (FCF) below 90% of GAAP net income for a full year.

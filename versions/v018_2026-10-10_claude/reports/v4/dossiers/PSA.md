# Public Storage (NYSE: PSA) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F14 | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close (price $287.97) | **Classification:** Self-Storage REIT

## 1. Verdict

**WATCH** — thesis horizon 18–36 months. Condition to upgrade: two consecutive quarters of positive same-store NOI growth, AND at least one full reported (not guided) quarter of NSA-inclusive Core FFO per share confirming the deal is accretive as promised. Public Storage has an excellent balance sheet (2.9x net debt/EBITDA, A2/A rated) and just closed a transformative, industry-leading acquisition, but its core same-store business is genuinely **shrinking**, not merely decelerating, Core FFO per share has been flat-to-down for a year, and the systematic valuation model's inputs are stale on two separate counts (a capex-proxy error and a pre-merger share count) — there is no reliable, current valuation anchor to underwrite a full position today.

## 2. Business in plain English

Public Storage owns and operates the largest self-storage platform in the U.S. — 3,584 facilities pre-merger (June 2026), now **4,500+ facilities / 327M+ sq ft** after closing its acquisition of National Storage Affiliates (NSA) on July 22, 2026 — plus a 35% stake in Shurgard (Europe, 335 facilities) and a pending ~$1.2B acquisition of Public Storage Canada (68 facilities, expected to close Q3 2026) **[Corrected 2026-10-08: PS Canada closed on 1 Sep 2026 (8-K accession 0001193125-26-377690)]**. It makes money renting small storage units to households and small businesses on flexible month-to-month leases, monetized further through tenant-insurance/reinsurance fees and third-party property management. Competitive position: #1 by scale against Extra Space Storage (EXR) and CubeSmart (CUBE), both of which are also acquisitive; the sector just consolidated further via PSA/NSA.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **GAAP vs Core FFO gap:** Q2'26 GAAP diluted EPS **+44.9% YoY** ($2.55 vs $1.76) vs Core FFO/share **-2.6% YoY** ($4.17 vs $4.28). The GAAP gain is almost entirely a **foreign-currency swing on Euro-denominated notes** (+$17.2M gain in Q2'26 vs a -$146.1M loss in Q2'25 — a ~$163M swing), which Core FFO correctly excludes. **Operating income itself fell -6.7% YoY in Q2'26** ($466.7M vs $500.0M) — the core business got weaker, not stronger, even as headline GAAP EPS surged.
- **Cash-flow quality:** 1H26 operating cash flow **$1,563.2M, down 0.9% YoY** ($1,577.8M in 1H25) despite GAAP net income up 33.7% — same FX-driven divergence. FAD (PSA's AFFO-equivalent, deducting maintenance capex) was **$1,389.7M, -1.1% YoY** (1H26 vs 1H25) — a cleaner, still-declining cash-flow measure.
- **G&A:** Q2'26 G&A **+72.5% YoY** ($44.4M vs $25.7M), driven by NSA/PS Canada transaction and integration costs, a "corporate transformation" restructuring program ($6.9M of costs YTD, against a $954K opening reserve), and CEO-transition costs (Tom Boyle became CEO April 1, 2026; Ron Havner → Shankh Mitra as Chairman). Core FFO excludes these, so headline Core FFO understates the near-term cash G&A drag.
- **Capex:** 1H26 "capital expenditures to maintain real estate facilities" were **$86.5M (~3.5% of revenue annualized)**; including property-enhancement and energy-efficiency capex, ~$269M annualized (~5.5% of revenue) — see §7, a direct conflict with V1's capex assumption.
- **Leverage (own-reported):** **Net Debt to EBITDA 2.9x**; Net Debt-and-Preferred-Equity to EBITDA **4.2x** (up from 4.1x YoY); EBITDA-to-Fixed-Charges 6.6x (down from 6.9x). Credit ratings A2/S&P A, unchanged. This is a genuinely strong, investment-grade balance sheet — the clearest bull-case pillar. Preferred stack is large: $4.35B pre-merger, **plus new Series T (~$957M) and Series U (~$567M) issued to former NSA preferred holders**.
- **Debt maturities:** total debt $10.25B, weighted-average rate 3.30%, only 6.3% maturing in 2026 ($650M); no cliff before "thereafter" ($4.93B, mostly 2033+). $3.0B new unsecured revolver (undrawn) + $500M delayed-draw term loan + $1.0B commercial paper program, replacing a smaller $1.5B facility.
- **Recent/pending M&A and its financing — the central fact of this dossier:** PSA closed the **all-stock acquisition of National Storage Affiliates (NSA)** on July 22, 2026 (0.14 PSA shares per NSA share; ~$10.5B enterprise value at announcement). Public Storage issued **~11.2M new common shares, ~9.57M Series T preferred shares, ~5.67M Series U preferred shares, plus ~4.1M OP units and 0.66M Series T-1 preferred OP units**. A "Dropdown JV" holding 313 of the acquired properties (~$3.2–3.3B value) was formed with legacy NSA unitholders owning ~80% (PSA 20%); the JV raised **~$2.0B of secured mortgage debt (Goldman Sachs/Wells Fargo, matures Aug 2027) plus a $237M mezzanine loan from a PSA subsidiary**. Expected synergies: $110–130M run-rate over 3–4 years; expected full accretion $0.35–0.50/share per share **at stabilization** — not yet. Separately, PSA has agreed to acquire **PS Canada for ~US$1.2B** (~$889M in OP units + ~$310M cash; 68 properties, 5.3M sq ft), expected to close Q3 2026 — **a sitting PSA board member, Tamara Hughes Gustavson, and her family hold the (non-controlling, "less than 0.1%") equity interest in PS Canada being acquired**, a related-party dynamic disclosed in the 10-Q. YTD through Q2'26: **$5.9B of debt capital-markets activity and $6.0B of common/OP-unit issuance** — an unusually large financing year. Subsequent to Q2'26: $900M of senior notes issued July 20, 2026 (4.855% effective); C$400M (~US$290M) of notes issued Sept 16, 2026 (4.540%, likely partly funding the pending Canada deal). Three shareholder lawsuits were filed against NSA/PSA over the merger seeking injunctive relief and additional disclosures — company states the claims lack merit and supplemented its disclosures; this is routine "disclosure" litigation typical of large stock-for-stock deals, not evidence of fraud, but a real (low-severity) legal/cost overhang.
- **Share count trend:** essentially flat pre-merger (175.4M→175.6M shares, Dec'25→Jun'26; small net issuance via LTIP/ATM), **then +11.2M (~6.4%) in one step from the NSA close** — not yet reflected in trailing per-share metrics or in V1's inputs.

## 7. Valuation snapshot — reconciliation with V1

**V1's verdict: "fair," score 0.242, base 3-yr annualised return +18.2%/yr** (bear +7.6%, bull +27.8% — notably, even V1's own bear case is positive, unusual for this framework). V1's method: P/FFO(proxy) 15.8x vs the stock's own history's **9.6th percentile** (statistically cheap vs its own past); reverse-DCF implied 10-year growth of **+25.3%** (vs consensus FY1 +7.3%, delivered 5y CAGR +9.64%) — a very large "achievable minus implied" gap that is the main reason V1's score is only "fair" rather than "attractive" despite the cheap-vs-history read.

**I find V1's inputs unreliable on two separate, quantifiable counts, and the true picture is more uncertain than either "fair" or "attractive."**

*(a) Capex proxy error.* Because D3's capex_ttm field was null for PSA, V1 proxied capex with **full trailing D&A ($1.164B, 23.8% of revenue)**. The Q2'26 10-Q cash-flow statement shows actual maintenance-type capex (maintain + enhancements + energy) of **~$269M annualized (~5.5% of revenue) — roughly a quarter of what V1 assumed.** Overstated capex understates FCFF, which mechanically inflates the reverse-DCF's implied growth rate (25.3%). A corrected calculation would show materially lower implied growth — quite possibly close to or below the ~7–10% achievable/delivered range — which would flip the "achievable minus implied" score component from strongly negative toward neutral or positive, and push V1's composite score **up**, not down. In other words, correcting this input alone would likely make V1 say PSA looks *more* attractive on the reverse-DCF, not less.

*(b) Stale, pre-merger share count and financials.* V1's inputs (diluted_shares = 176,512,000; market_cap = $50.83B) are the **Q2 2026 weighted-average share count for the quarter ended June 30, 2026 — i.e., before** the July 22, 2026 NSA close. They do not include the ~11.2M new common shares (nor the new preferred, nor NSA's assets, earnings, or assumed/JV debt). At the reviewed $287.97 price, the corrected market cap (~175.5M pre-merger shares + ~11.2M NSA shares ≈ 186.7M shares, before PS Canada) is roughly **6% higher** than V1's figure, before even accounting for NSA's own FFO contribution or integration costs. **V1's entire valuation snapshot for PSA is therefore built on a company that, as of the 2026-09-25 cutoff, no longer exists in that form** — it was superseded two months earlier by a transformative, already-closed acquisition.

These two errors point in different directions (the capex fix would raise V1's score; the share-count/business staleness makes the whole computation moot until pro forma NSA financials are available), so I cannot simply "correct" V1's number — I can only flag that **no reliable, current systematic valuation exists for PSA right now**, on top of a same-store business that is independently showing declining revenue and NOI (§4, §6). Peer comparison: V1's peer_median (28.1x) is EXR's NTM GAAP P/E (peer_n=2, i.e. just EXR vs PSA), not a true P/FFO comparison — also flagged as unreliable by V1 itself; I did not have time to independently rebuild true peer P/FFO multiples for EXR/CUBE.

**valuation_view_vs_v1: dossier view = fair; consistent = false.** I do not simply defer to V1's "fair" label — I arrive at a more cautious place (WATCH, not INCLUDE-SMALL) via a different, evidence-based route: deteriorating same-store fundamentals and integration risk on an unproven, complex mega-merger, layered on top of a valuation model I know to be stale on two independent counts. This is a case where the correct action is to withhold conviction, not to average two wrong numbers into a number that sounds "fair."

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:** (1) Best-in-class, investment-grade balance sheet (2.9x net debt/EBITDA, A2/A) gives PSA capital-markets access no self-storage peer can match, evidenced by $5.9B of 2026 debt issuance at reasonable spreads; (2) the NSA deal creates the clear #1 global self-storage platform (4,500+ locations) with a credible, quantified synergy plan ($110-130M) and PS Next operating-margin uplift (PSA's 78% same-store margin vs NSA's historical 70%); (3) PS Canada extends the platform into a new, currently-unconsolidated market at a modest incremental cost.

**Bear:** (1) The core (same-store) business is **shrinking** in 2026 — revenue and NOI both negative YoY for two straight quarters, with full-year guidance still pointing to negative same-store NOI even after being raised; realized rent per square foot has now declined for two consecutive quarters; (2) Core FFO per share has fallen for three straight quarters (from $4.31 to $4.17) and FY2026 guidance implies essentially flat per-share cash flow versus FY2025 — not the +18%/yr V1 base case; (3) integration risk is real and unproven: a >$10B all-stock deal, a complex 80/20 "Dropdown JV" with $2.2B of new JV-level debt, routine-but-real shareholder litigation, and a simultaneous second acquisition (PS Canada) and CEO/Chairman transition all landing in the same 12 months.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria

The specific, observable, time-bound conditions that would invalidate this thesis:

1. Same-store NOI growth remains negative for full-year 2026 as **reported** (confirming the current -1.15%-midpoint guidance rather than a further raise into positive territory).
2. NSA merger fails to show positive Core FFO accretion in the first full reported quarter of combination (Q3 or Q4 2026 reported Core FFO per share, pro forma for the enlarged share count, below what a standalone-PSA trajectory would have produced).
3. Net Debt-and-Preferred-Equity to EBITDA rises above 5.0x (from 4.2x now).
4. Same-store realized annual rent per occupied square foot declines for four consecutive quarters (currently two: Q1'26 and Q2'26).

# FOX / FOXA — Fox Corporation (Diligence dossier, agent F66, standard depth; covers both share classes, one dossier, no separate FOXA vote)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (would revisit at 6–18 months, or immediately if the pending transaction below is resolved), overriding the triage's price-vs-growth read. Fox Corporation's standalone business (FOX News, FOX Sports, Tubi, FOX Entertainment, 29 local stations) is a genuinely strong, dual-class-controlled cash generator with net debt/EBITDA under 1x as of 30-Jun-2026. But on **14-Jun-2026** FOX signed a definitive merger agreement to **acquire Roku, Inc.** for approximately **$96.00 cash plus 0.9693 FOX Class A shares per Roku share** (a deal valued at roughly $23bn based on Roku's own 25-Sep-2026 market cap of $22.6bn), to be substantially funded by a **$12.0bn committed senior unsecured bridge facility plus a $1.0bn term loan**. This is the dominant fact for any investment decision today: it would roughly double-to-triple FOX's leverage, dilute existing shareholders by a large, currently-uncertain amount of new Class A stock, and make the standalone valuation math below largely moot until the deal's fate (close, or a $866M–$1.2bn break fee) is known. `v1_verdict` = null (no V1 row exists for FOX).

## 2. Business in plain English
Fox Corporation owns FOX News and FOX Business (cable news), FOX Sports (NFL, MLB, college football, NASCAR, FIFA World Cup rights), the FOX broadcast network and 29 local TV stations, Tubi (a free ad-supported streaming service), and FOX Entertainment's studio operations. It earns money three ways: affiliate/distribution fees (cable and virtual-MVPD operators pay to carry FOX channels), advertising (sold across cable, broadcast and Tubi), and licensing/content fees. Its moat is live, "appointment-based" news and sports programming that is hard to replace with on-demand content, plus multi-year carriage contracts. The company is controlled by the Murdoch family through super-voting Class B shares (held via LGC Holdco and related trusts); public investors mostly hold the non-voting-light Class A shares (FOXA) and lower-float Class B (FOX).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
**Entity scope: all figures are Fox Corporation consolidated, from the FY2026 10-K (fiscal year ended 30-Jun-2026, filed 6-Aug-2026), unless noted; this is materially different from, and should never be conflated with, the standalone financials of Roku, Inc., the pending acquisition target.**
- **Balance sheet (30-Jun-2026):** total assets $22.5bn; cash + restricted cash $4.2bn; long-term debt $6.6bn (current portion $0 at FYE, per the FY2026 10-K); stockholders' equity $11.6bn. **Standalone net debt ≈ $2.4bn against FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA of $3.9bn → net debt/EBITDA ≈ 0.6x** — very low leverage, which is precisely what allows the company to contemplate a $13bn+ debt-funded acquisition.
- **Capital return:** FY2026 returned ~$2.3bn to stockholders via dividends and buybacks, including a **$1.5bn accelerated share repurchase program**. The company also discloses a valuable tax asset from the step-up in basis following the 2019 Disney transaction, expected to provide an annual cash-tax benefit for years.
- **Equity investments:** FOX holds a stake in Flutter Entertainment plc (online sports betting/gaming) and an option to acquire 18.6% of FanDuel Group, a Flutter subsidiary — both mark-to-market items that add volatility to quarterly GAAP EPS (see §4) without affecting the core broadcast/cable/Tubi cash flows.
- **Pending Roku acquisition — financing and terms (Note 3, "Acquisitions, Disposals and Other Transactions," and Note 9, "Borrowings," FY2026 10-K):** definitive Merger Agreement signed **14-Jun-2026**; consideration $96.00 cash + 0.9693 FOX Class A shares per Roku Class A/B share (fixed exchange ratio, no collar); financing commitment letter for an **$12.0bn Bridge Facility ($11.0bn available as of 30-Jun-2026) and a $1.0bn Term Loan Facility**, both senior unsecured, entered concurrently with the Merger Agreement. Termination fees: **~$866M mutual** (either party, e.g., on a change of board recommendation); **FOX pays Roku up to an additional ~$1.2bn** if the deal fails specifically on antitrust/regulatory grounds; FOX also agreed to reimburse Roku up to $70M of deal costs if FOX cannot secure the required Class B stockholder approval of the share issuance. **Both companies' controlling holders have pre-committed support**: Roku founder/CEO Anthony Wood and affiliated trusts (majority of Roku's voting power) signed a voting agreement in favor; separately, LGC Holdco (the Murdoch-family FOX Class B holder, ~38.8% of FOX Class B voting power as of 30-Jun-2026) agreed to vote in favor of the FOX share issuance. **Not yet closed** as of the 25-Sep-2026 cutoff; still subject to HSR antitrust clearance and both companies' shareholder votes. **[Corrected 2026-10-10: both parties received a DOJ Second Request on 8 Sep 2026 (8-K 0001193125-26-385979); closing expected first half of calendar 2027]**
- **Rough dilution/leverage arithmetic (my own estimate, not company-disclosed):** using Roku's 25-Sep-2026 market cap ($22.65bn) and last quote (~$152.68) to back out ~148M Roku shares, the stock portion alone would issue roughly ~144M new FOX Class A shares (0.9693 × 148M) — a large increase versus FOX's current combined share count, and the cash portion (~$14.2bn) would draw heavily on the new bridge/term-loan facilities, pushing pro forma net debt from ~$2.4bn to a plausible **$14–16bn+** range before any Roku-side cash or synergies are netted in. This is a rough, undisclosed-by-the-company estimate flagged as such — the actual pro forma capital structure has not yet been filed.

## 8. Bull case
1. Standalone balance sheet is very strong (net debt/EBITDA ≈0.6x, $4.2bn cash), giving real optionality regardless of how the Roku deal resolves.
2. Tubi (FOX's AVOD platform) grew total view time 20% YoY in FY2026 and is one of the most-watched free ad-supported streaming services in the US (≈2.2% of all TV viewing per Nielsen); Television segment EBITDA rose 52% YoY, showing the non-linear parts of the business scaling profitably.
3. If the Roku acquisition closes and integrates well, FOX gains a leading US connected-TV operating-system/ad-tech platform (Roku) to pair with its own content and FOX One direct-to-consumer service — a plausible structural upgrade to the business, not just a levered bet.

## 9. Bear case
1. The Roku acquisition is a ~$23bn definitive commitment (cash + stock) funded mostly by a **new $12bn bridge facility and $1bn term loan** against a company whose own FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA is $3.9bn — a very large, leverage-increasing transaction relative to FOX's current size, with the actual pro forma capital structure not yet disclosed in any filing.
2. Even with both sides' controlling holders pre-committed to vote in favor, the deal still requires **HSR antitrust clearance**, and FOX has agreed to pay Roku up to an incremental ~$1.2bn if it fails specifically on antitrust grounds — a real, quantified regulatory risk.
3. Legacy litigation overhang: FOX's news businesses remain defendants in the Smartmatic defamation lawsuit (filed Feb-2021) and related derivative suits; the company states it "continues to believe" these claims are without merit but has already paid ~$800M to settle the separate Dominion matter in Apr-2023 — the FOX News-related legal-cost tail is real and recurring, not hypothetical.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. The Roku Merger Agreement is terminated under circumstances requiring FOX to pay Roku the ~$1.2bn antitrust-related termination fee (a direct signal of regulatory rejection).
2. FOX Class B stockholders fail to approve the Class A share issuance needed to close the transaction on its current terms.
3. Pro forma net debt/Adjusted EBITDA, once disclosed post-close, exceeds **4.0x** (vs. ≈0.6x standalone today).
4. Cable Network Programming segment distribution (affiliate-fee) revenue declines YoY for two consecutive quarters (cord-cutting outpacing rate increases; distribution revenue grew +5% in FY2026 despite subscriber declines, so a reversal would be a real trend break).
5. A jury verdict or settlement in the Smartmatic defamation litigation (or a related derivative suit) exceeding $500M.

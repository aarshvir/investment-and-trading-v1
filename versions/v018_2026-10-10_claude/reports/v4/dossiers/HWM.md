## 1. Verdict
**WATCH**, horizon 12–36 months. Operationally this is the strongest of the three: four straight beat-and-raise quarters, adjusted EBITDA margin up 340 bps to 32.1%, and a balance sheet at ~1.4x net debt/EBITDA after a $1.8bn acquisition (consolidated net debt ~$3.9bn, balance-sheet table in the Q2'26 Ex. 99.1). But $232.48 is 44x the FY26 adjusted EPS guide ($5.27) and a 2.0% free-cash-flow yield, and the reverse DCF needs ~17.5% 10-year FCF growth against my base of ~11% and even my bull of ~14%. A great business priced for more than it can deliver. The triage verdict (advance=false, "little margin of safety") is confirmed with numbers. Would move to INCLUDE-SMALL only near ~$150 (implied <= base), or if evidence lifts the base FCF CAGR to ~15% (for example multi-year gas-turbine capacity contracts plus engine-rate increases beyond current plans).

## 2. Business in plain English
Howmet makes the hard-to-make metal parts inside jet engines and airframes: investment-cast turbine airfoils, forged rings and discs, titanium structures, and aerospace fasteners (its 10-K says it "can produce more than 90% of all structural and rotating aero engine components"). FY25 revenue $8.25bn: aerospace ~70% (commercial and defense), commercial transportation (forged wheels) ~15%, gas turbines ~11%, other ~4%. RTX and GE Aerospace were each ~11% of FY25 third-party sales. Few qualified suppliers, long qualification cycles and spares aftermarket make it a moat business; its cost is cyclicality and dependence on a handful of engine makers.

## 8. Bull / bear
Bull: (1) "all our major markets in growth mode": commercial build rates against record backlogs, rising engine spares, defense healthy, and a gas-turbine market the CEO calls "extraordinary"; (2) engine-products margin 37.7% with ~485 net headcount absorbed, i.e. operating leverage still intact; (3) FCF ($1.9bn guided) funds buybacks, capex and deleveraging at once.
Bear: (1) 44x earnings and a 2.0% FCF yield leave no room for a single quarter below the raised bar; (2) customer and cycle concentration: RTX and GE ~11% each, aerospace ~70% of revenue, with the Q1'26 release flagging that engine spares "could" feel an effect from "the Iranian conflict"; (3) capex needs are rising into 2027 and H1'26 working capital already absorbed ~$360m.

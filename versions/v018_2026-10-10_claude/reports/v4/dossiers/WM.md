# WM — Waste Management, Inc. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F72, Wave 4)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — 12–36 month horizon. WM is a high-quality, route-density/landfill-permit moat business compounding price ahead of cost, but the reservation is valuation: at 12.9x EV/EBITDA and a P/FCF near 22x, the market is paying a premium multiple for a business now digesting a large, messy acquisition (Stericycle/Healthcare Solutions) whose GAAP-to-adjusted gap has widened materially. Reverse DCF implies the price is not aggressive on FCF growth (see §7), but the multiple leaves little room for integration slippage.

## 2. Business in plain English
WM collects, transfers and disposes of solid waste for residential, commercial and industrial customers across North America, and increasingly recycles materials and captures landfill gas for renewable energy. It makes money by charging for pickups and, most profitably, for disposal in landfills it owns — permits a competitor cannot replicate at any price. In November 2024 it acquired Stericycle (medical/regulated waste), now reported as the "WM Healthcare Solutions" segment, adding a second growth leg alongside legacy Collection & Disposal and the newer sustainability (recycling/renewable natural gas, "RNG") businesses.

Adding them lifts adjusted net debt to ~$26.4bn and adjusted net debt/EBITDA to ~3.2x. Q2'26 FCF (WM's own definition, after both "capital to support the business" and sustainability growth capex) was $1,104m vs $818m in Q2'25 (+34.5%).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Route density plus owned landfill disposal is a structural, non-replicable moat — no new large MSW landfill permits are being granted in most of WM's markets. (2) RNG and recycling investments are converting from capex to cash: three new RNG facilities completed in Q2'26 alone, adding to the sustainability EBITDA bridge management guides to $235–255m of growth for 2026. (3) Stericycle gives a second, less cyclical revenue stream (medical waste) with disclosed cost synergies still being realized (SG&A down on "synergies achieved from our acquisitions, particularly Stericycle" per the Q2'26 10-Q).

**Bear:** (1) The stock is not cheap on any absolute multiple (12.9x EV/EBITDA, 21.9x P/FCF) for a business now guiding down its own revenue outlook one quarter after reaffirming it. (2) The Stericycle integration is not clean: a related DOJ deferred-prosecution settlement (§11) and a persistently wide (20–25%) GAAP/adjusted gap suggest integration costs could run longer than modeled. (3) Sector-adjusted leverage (net debt + closure/remediation liabilities) is closer to 3.2x than the headline 2.8x, and WM's own volume trend is negative (Collection & Disposal volume -1.8% in Q2'26), meaning growth is entirely a price/mix story — vulnerable if commercial or industrial customers push back on 5%+ price increases in a softer economy.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (each, if it occurs, would invalidate the INCLUDE-SMALL thesis)
1. Core price minus WM's own cash-cost inflation turns negative for two consecutive quarters (the sector's central "is the moat still working" test; not yet observed — Q2'26 core price +5.7% still comfortably ahead of cost).
2. Sector-adjusted net debt/EBITDA (reported net debt + landfill/remediation liabilities, divided by adjusted EBITDA) exceeds **3.5x** for two consecutive quarters (currently ≈3.2x).
3. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance (currently $8.15–8.25bn) is cut in dollar terms (not just margin-adjusted) at the Q3'26 (Oct 27, 2026) or later release.
4. A material adverse development in the Stericycle DOJ deferred-prosecution matter (beyond the already-disclosed penalty) or a new SEC/DOJ inquiry tied to Healthcare Solutions.
5. Collection & Disposal volume decline exceeds 2% for two consecutive quarters excluding the disclosed wildfire-comparison effect (i.e., volume loss broadens beyond the flagged residential shedding).

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **DOJ/litigation (real, disclosed):** Stericycle (now wholly owned) received a 2020 DEA subpoena and DOJ/California DTSC search warrants tied to its now-divested "ESOL Retail Controlled Substances Business" (2015–2020 conduct, predating WM's ownership). **In May 2026, Stericycle entered a one-year deferred prosecution agreement with DOJ**, paid the agreed penalty and settlement amounts, and remains subject to ongoing compliance/reporting obligations during the DPA term (WM 10-Q for Q2 2026, Note on Litigation). WM states it does not expect a material adverse effect, but this is a live, ongoing compliance obligation, not a closed matter, and it sits inside the same segment driving the earnings-quality gap noted in §6.
- No auditor changes, no material-weakness or going-concern language found in the reviewed 10-Q/10-K.
- No PFAS-specific disclosure found in the Q2'26 10-Q text (a sector-wide watch item per the waste-environmental playbook; absence of disclosure is not the same as absence of exposure — worth a follow-up read of the FY2025 10-K's full risk-factor section, which was not re-read line-by-line in this pass).
- Financial assurance for closure/post-closure obligations is supported by letters of credit, surety bonds, insurance and a wholly-owned captive insurer per the 10-Q (Note 6) — a normal, disclosed structure, not itself a flag.

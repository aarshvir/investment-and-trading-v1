## Business in plain English
Martin Marietta mines and sells crushed stone, sand and gravel ("aggregates") plus cement and asphalt, mainly for
roads, highways and heavy construction across the US Sun Belt and Central regions. Local quarry permits are almost
impossible to replicate near growing metro areas, giving pricing power; volumes track infrastructure and
nonresidential construction spend.

## Why the model likes it / durability
b1 factor data (25 Sep 2026 snapshot): quality/value composite decile 1 (top decile) but momentum is **-17.4%** and
ROE is **8.9%** against a 36.7% operating margin — a capital-intensive asset base that converts high margins into a
mediocre ROE. The "growth" the quant model sees is largely value-family driven (cheap on FCF-to-price after a
drawdown), not an earnings-momentum signal. Not obviously durable without confirming the deal doesn't blow up the
capital structure.

## Bull case
1. Sun Belt infrastructure/IIJA-linked demand keeps aggregates pricing power intact; quarry-scarcity moat is real.
2. Lhoist adds a high-margin, reserve-rich lime/industrial-minerals platform with genuine "aggregates-like"
   economics and only modest ($85M) synergy target — upside if commercial synergies exceed that.
3. Four straight quarters of shipment growth (record Q1 2026 volumes, +12.4% YoY) shows underlying demand is intact.

## Bear case
1. $13.5B deal not yet closed; regulatory approval, financing cost (new $6.5B notes at higher-for-longer rates) and
   integration risk are real and unpriced in a smooth "accretive" narrative.
2. Q1 2026's headline net-income beat was a one-time divestiture gain, not operating strength — underlying
   continuing-ops earnings were down YoY in Q1 2026.
3. ROE (8.9%) already lags the 36.7% operating margin before the deal adds ~$13.5B of new capital employed —
   returns on incremental capital need to clear a high bar.

## Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Lhoist deal fails to close by end-1H 2027 or closes at materially worse terms/financing cost than disclosed.
2. Pro forma consolidated net debt/EBITDA stays above 3.0x for more than 4 quarters post-close (vs. management's <2.5x/24-month target).
3. Organic aggregates shipment growth turns negative for 2 consecutive quarters.
4. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance ($2.36–2.50B) is cut (not just reaffirmed) at Q3 or Q4 2026.
5. Continuing-operations ROE stays below 10% for two more fiscal years after Lhoist closes.

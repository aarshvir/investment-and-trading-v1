## 1. Verdict

**WATCH** — not now; would revisit on a pullback toward the mid-$80s or on two consecutive quarters of Product
Quality & Innovation (PQI) core growth back above ~4%. Veralto is a genuinely high-quality, Danaher-heritage
compounder (34% ROE, 17.3% net margin, low leverage, recurring consumables/service revenue), but the reverse
DCF shows the price already assumes roughly the growth rate the company can plausibly deliver given a soft PQI
segment — this is the "great business priced for more than it can comfortably deliver" case the mandate
explicitly asks to be honest about, not an INCLUDE. No V1 row exists for VLTO, so `v1_verdict` is null.

## 2. Business in plain English

Veralto (spun off from Danaher in September 2023) operates two segments: **Water Quality** (~57% of revenue —
instruments, chemistries and services for testing/treating municipal and industrial water, brands including
Hach and ChemTreat) and **Product Quality & Innovation** (~43% — marking/coding equipment (Videojet), and
packaging/color-management software and hardware (X-Rite, Pantone, and the newly acquired GlobalVision
packaging-compliance software)). Revenue is heavily recurring: consumables, service contracts and software
subscriptions tied to installed equipment, inherited from Danaher's playbook of pricing the instrument to sell
the annuity. The moat is regulatory-driven demand (water-quality testing is often legally mandated) plus
high switching costs once an instrument/workflow is validated into a regulated process.

**Sector routing note:** none of the stock-analysis skill's sector playbooks fit cleanly. `waste-environmental.md`
is explicitly about waste collection/disposal and landfill permits, which does not describe Veralto's analytical-
instrumentation and marking/software business at all; Veralto is closer to a diversified, high-margin
instrumentation/consumables business (the Danaher/Roper archetype). This dossier therefore uses the generic
core-factor/DuPont framework rather than forcing an ill-fitting sector playbook, and says so explicitly per the
skill's own instruction for when no playbook applies.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull (3):**
1. Water Quality core growth is genuinely accelerating (3.8% → 5.7% over two quarters) and is regulation-backed,
   recurring-revenue demand, not a cyclical bounce.
2. SBC at 1.3% of revenue and net debt/EBITDA of ~1.1x show real capital discipline — a Danaher-system business
   that compounds through bolt-on M&A (GlobalVision) and buybacks without diluting shareholders.
3. If PQI's Q1→Q2 2026 improvement (-1.0% → +2.0% core growth) continues into H2, the "soft segment" bear case
   weakens and the stock could re-rate off a currently fair, not cheap, multiple.

**Bear (3):**
1. The reverse DCF shows the price already assuming close to the growth rate a sober base case would forecast —
   there is little valuation cushion if PQI's recovery stalls or Water Quality decelerates.
2. Part of the FY2026 guidance raise is explicitly a one-off tariff refund ($0.05/share in Q2), not organic
   reacceleration; stripping it, the "raise" is smaller than headline.
3. A newly-issued $725M senior notes offering, even if framed as refinancing, modestly increases fixed
   obligations at a company whose PQI segment has been growing below the rate needed to comfortably grow into
   more leverage.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. PQI segment core sales growth falls back below 0% for two consecutive quarters (it was -1.0% in Q1 2026,
   +2.0% in Q2 2026 — the recovery is one data point, not yet a trend).
2. Water Quality core sales growth decelerates below 3% for two consecutive quarters (vs. 5.7% in Q2 2026).
3. FY2026 adjusted diluted EPS finishes below the guided $4.35–4.43 range, stripped of the disclosed $0.05
   tariff-refund one-off.
4. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.0x (currently ≈1.06x) without a clear, disclosed strategic rationale.
5. Total shareholder return over any rolling 3-year period trails the analyst base case above (+3.4%/yr) by
   more than 5 points with no segment-level explanation.

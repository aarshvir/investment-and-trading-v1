# ODFL — Old Dominion Freight Line, Inc.

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (12–36 month horizon). Best-in-class LTL network and balance sheet, but the 25 Sep 2026 close (**$173.30**, mkt cap ≈$35.9bn) prices in more growth than a mid-cycle freight carrier's own evidence supports once capex is normalized. This **overturns** the triage's "advance" call: the underlying business is sound, but price/growth is not.

## 2. Business in plain English
Old Dominion is the largest and most profitable asset-based less-than-truckload (LTL) carrier in the US, moving smaller freight shipments for industrial, retail and manufacturing customers through a dense network of ~260 service centers. It earns money on price per hundredweight (revenue/cwt) and network density; its moat is service consistency (near-zero claims ratio, on-time delivery) that lets it charge a premium versus peers, defended by a debt-funded, decades-long capex program that competitors can't easily replicate.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1 flags ODFL as Quality 5 / Growth 3 / Price-vs-Growth 3, i.e. a quality-factor pick, not a value one (composite decile 10, but `pct_ep`≈0.30 and `pct_fcfp`≈0.39 — cheap on neither earnings nor FCF yield). The quality signal (debt-free balance sheet, industry-best operating ratio) is durable and structural. The recent momentum (+43% 12-month, `mom_12_1`≈0.427) is an artefact of a freight-cycle inflection trade, not a re-rating of the moat — this is exactly the kind of one-off the addendum asks us to separate from durable quality.

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse DCF
NTM P/E (per `v1_valuation_table.csv`, ticker=ODFL row): **27.1x**, vs its own 5–10y history at the 58.8th percentile and a peer median of 24.1x — a modest premium to peers, not extreme, but rich against its own history for a freight cyclical mid-downturn. Consensus FY1 growth in that row is only 7.9%.

**V1's own reverse DCF** (same row) already computes an implied 10-year growth rate of **20.1%** at a 9.65% WACC, and flags this "excessive" with a **base-case 3-year annualized return of -5.9%** (bear -33.7%, bull +25.6%).

I ran an independent, simplified two-stage reverse DCF (10 years of FCF growth at rate *g*, 3% terminal growth, discounted at 9.65% WACC) off the **TTM FCF as reported** ($1.115bn, 3.10% FCF yield) and solved for the *g* that reproduces the $35.9bn market cap: **≈12–13%** — lower than V1's 20.1%, but only because TTM FCF is inflated by the capex pause described in Section 6. Re-running the same DCF off a **normalized** FCF (TTM OCF less a mid-cycle $550M capex run-rate, ≈$844M, FCF yield 2.35%) pushes the implied growth needed back up to roughly V1's ~20% range. **[Corrected 2026-10-08: replication gives about 16.5% on $844M FCF and about 14% on the company's $380M capex plan, not about 20%; V1's 20.1% comes from its fade model; conclusion (implied above base) unchanged]** **I agree with V1's "excessive" verdict**: the market is pricing this as if the current capex holiday and the freight-cycle inflection are both permanent, which is inconsistent — either capex normalizes (cutting FCF) or the inflection is real and capex should resume to fund the growth (also cutting FCF). Either path lowers today's apparent cheapness.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Freight cycle genuinely inflecting — Q2 FY26 was the first quarter of positive revenue growth in five quarters and August daily data extend it; (2) debt-free balance sheet and a 450bp sequential OR improvement **[Corrected 2026-10-08: 450bp is the year-on-year improvement (74.6% to 70.1%); sequentially it is 610bp from 76.2%]** give it room to keep taking price even if volumes stay soft; (3) LTL industry capacity has rationalized (peer bankruptcies/exits since 2023), so ODFL's density advantage compounds as freight returns.

**Bear:** (1) The 20%-ish embedded growth rate is a heavy ask for a cyclical trucker whose own 5-year delivered growth (`delivered_5y_or_actual_roe` in v1 row) is only ~4.1%; (2) capex has been unsustainably low for six quarters — normalization will compress FCF right as the market is paying for FCF strength; (3) one strong quarter (Q2 FY26) is not yet a trend; Q1 FY26 was still down YoY, and a re-softening freight cycle (tariff/trade disruption, industrial production rolling over) would leave the stock priced for a recovery that stalls.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Revenue/day growth (company-published monthly metric) turns negative again for **two consecutive months**.
2. Operating ratio deteriorates **>100bps YoY** for a full quarter (reversing the Q2 FY26 improvement).
3. Quarterly capex resumes above a **$180M/quarter** run-rate without a corresponding reacceleration in revenue growth (confirms the FCF-optical-cheapness thesis breaking).
4. Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated; currently net cash) turns **>0.3x** (loss of the debt-free position that anchors the quality score).
5. NTM P/E stays **>25x** while trailing-4-quarter revenue growth is **<5%** (valuation and fundamentals re-diverging).

# Aptiv PLC (APTV) — Diligence Dossier (F34, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH.** Thesis horizon 12–36 months. One-sentence reason: the post-Versigent-spin "New Aptiv" is statistically cheap and has a genuinely cleaner balance sheet and a growing software/non-auto business, but it cut every headline FY2026 guidance metric just one quarter after first issuing post-spin guidance, and the market has not yet seen two clean quarters that prove the cut was a one-off.

**Thesis (≤25 words):** Auto electrical/software supplier just cut its first post-spinoff full-year guide; cheap and better-capitalized, but not yet proven stable.

## 2. Business in plain English
Aptiv designs and supplies the electrical architecture, sensors, software and computing "brains" that connect a vehicle's systems (Engineered Components: connectors, wiring, electrification hardware; Intelligent Systems: ADAS, software-defined-vehicle platforms, user experience) to automakers globally. It earns money via long-term OEM platform contracts with high switching costs (once designed into a vehicle architecture, Aptiv content is difficult to re-source mid-cycle). On April 1, 2026, Aptiv completed the spin-off of its former Electrical Distribution Systems (wiring harness) business as an independent company, Versigent (NYSE: VGNT), leaving "New Aptiv" as a more software/technology-weighted supplier of safety, connectivity and electrification systems. Competitive position: a top-tier, diversified auto-technology supplier (alongside Bosch, Continental, Denso) with particular strength in vehicle electrical/electronic architecture and a growing software/AV stack.

The value case (fam_V pct 0.647) and quality case (fam_Q pct 0.418, helped by the deleveraged post-spin balance sheet) are real, but momentum/earnings-momentum this poor is a genuine, current fact, and it is corroborated below by an actual guidance cut in the most recent quarter — this is not merely a stale composite disagreeing with a rosier narrative.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
Free cash flow has been volatile through the separation: Q1'26 FCF was **−$362M** (vs +$76M in Q1'25) **[Corrected 2026-10-06: those Q1 figures are the consolidated as-originally-reported basis including EDS; on a continuing-operations basis Q1'26 FCF was about -$208M vs +$45M (YTD -$196M vs +$264M per the Q2 release)]** and Q2'26 FCF was only **$12M** (vs $219M a year earlier) even as adjusted EPS beat the prior-year figure — a real, current gap between adjusted-earnings optics and cash generation during the transition year, now guided to normalize to $625–725M for the full year. Balance sheet is genuinely improved post-spin: long-term debt fell from $9.25bn (Q1'26) to $5.33bn (Q2'26) after Aptiv received a **$1.9bn cash dividend from Versigent** at spin completion and used it (plus other cash) to redeem $1.847bn of senior notes in April 2026; cash fell in step (from $3.17bn to $761M) as that redemption occurred. Net debt ≈$4.6bn against guided FY26 adjusted EBITDA midpoint ~$2.34bn → **net debt/adjusted EBITDA ≈2.0x**, a comfortable, de-risked level versus the pre-spin consolidated entity. Share count has fallen from 223.8M (Q1'25) to 208.4M (Q2'26), a genuine ~7% reduction via buybacks over five quarters. Customer concentration: Aptiv's five largest customers were reported at roughly 42% of total net sales in the FY2025 10-K (secondary-source citation — the primary 10-K customer-concentration footnote was not independently re-fetched in this pass; flagged for verification), with GM, Stellantis, Ford and Volkswagen historically the largest individual names — a real, disclosed concentration risk in a soft-production auto environment.

## 7. Valuation snapshot & reverse DCF
Price (2026-09-25 close) $44.79; market cap ≈$9.48bn; net debt ≈$4.6bn; EV ≈$14.1bn. FY2026 P/E on the just-cut guided adjusted EPS midpoint ($5.70) ≈**7.86x**; FCF yield on EV (guided FCF midpoint $675M) ≈**4.8%** (on market cap alone, ≈7.1%) — well below the 17% Yahoo/d4 trailing figure, which is stale and still includes the divested EDS business (a data conflict disclosed here rather than carried forward). No dividend currently paid. **No V1 row exists for APTV** (checked `v1_valuation_table.csv` — no row) → v1_verdict = null; valuation view is independently derived.

**Reverse DCF:** EV = FCF₁×(1+g)/(WACC−g). Using FCF₁ = $675M (FY26 guide midpoint) and WACC ≈9% (cost of equity via CAPM: rf 4.2% + β1.33×ERP 5% = 10.85%; post-tax cost of debt ≈4.3%; debt weight ≈33% of EV), solving for g against EV $14.1bn gives **implied perpetual FCF growth ≈4.0%/yr**. My evidence-based base case is **~4–5%/yr** (structural content-per-vehicle growth from software/ADAS/electrification, richening non-auto mix which grew +12% in Q2'26, offset by genuine auto-production cyclicality) — so the reverse DCF reads **roughly in line**, not obviously cheap, at the perpetual-growth level. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: method restated in Correction section DV04 (rf 5.17%, SBC deducted, levered/EV basis fixed): implied growth 3.6-5.4%, still in line]** The more important near-term fact is that **guided FY2026 revenue growth is only ~2%**, well below even this "in line" perpetual assumption — meaning the price already requires a recovery from today's just-cut guidance that has not yet shown up in the numbers, which is the central reason for a WATCH rather than an INCLUDE verdict. Bear case (further cuts, FCF flat ~$625M, P/E stays at trough ~7–8x, no re-rating): **3-yr annualized return ≈ −3%**. Base case (cycle stabilizes over 12–18 months, EPS compounds ~8–9%/yr aided by buybacks, P/E re-rates modestly to 9x): **≈ +13–14%/yr**. Bull case (non-auto/software mix accelerates margin expansion, re-rating to "quality auto-tech" multiples of 11–12x): **≈ +27–29%/yr**.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Non-automotive/software revenue grew +12% in Q2'26 even as automotive fell 1%, evidence "New Aptiv" is increasingly a technology-content business rather than a pure cyclical parts supplier; (2) the post-spin balance sheet is genuinely conservative (net debt/EBITDA ≈2.0x) after using $1.9bn of Versigent proceeds to redeem debt, freeing cash for buybacks that have already cut the float ~7% in five quarters; (3) at ~7.9x guided FY26 adjusted EPS, the market prices in essentially no recovery even as vehicle electrical/software content keeps rising structurally across the industry.
**Bear:** (1) FY2026 guidance was cut on revenue, EBITDA, EPS and FCF simultaneously just one quarter after "New Aptiv" first guided — for reasons (China production schedules, delayed launches, software-timing) that are exactly the execution risk a newly separated company can least afford to demonstrate early, and the quant data's 1st-percentile momentum corroborates the market has not yet decided the cut is a one-off; (2) customer concentration is real (~42% top-5) in a soft, uneven global auto-production environment (China down, EMEA down 7% in Q1'26); (3) free cash flow has been genuinely volatile and at times negative during the separation year (Q1'26 −$362M, Q2'26 only $12M) even as adjusted EPS beat — the gap between adjusted-earnings optics and cash delivery is a current, not hypothetical, fact.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
Any of the following would break the thesis; each is a specific, observable, time-bound signpost to monitor at each earnings release, not a vague worry:
1. FY2026 guidance is cut again at the Q3'26 or Q4'26 release (any further reduction to the low end of the current $12.6–12.8bn revenue / $5.60–5.80 adjusted-EPS range).
2. Non-automotive (software/AV/enterprise) segment revenue growth decelerates below 8% YoY for two consecutive quarters (from +12% in Q2'26).
3. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA rises above 3.0x (from ~2.0x post-spin) without a stated value-accretive reason.
4. Free cash flow for two consecutive quarters comes in below the low end of the then-current full-year FCF guidance range (currently $625–725M).
5. Top-5 customer concentration rises above 45% of net sales (from ~42%), or GM/Stellantis/Ford cuts North American production schedules by more than 5% for a full year.

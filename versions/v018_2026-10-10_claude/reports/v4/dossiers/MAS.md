# MAS — Masco Corporation — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — 12–24 month horizon. Reasonable, business-appropriate balance sheet and a valuation sitting at the cheap end of both its own history and the Street's own 12-month target range, but two straight years of declining operating cash flow and a Q2 2026 guidance raise that is substantially a one-off tariff-refund story, not a volume recovery. Condition to upgrade to INCLUDE-SMALL: two consecutive quarters of organic (ex-tariff, ex-FX) revenue growth, or TTM operating cash flow inflecting positive year-over-year.

## 2. Business in plain English
Masco makes and sells branded home-improvement products — faucets and fixtures (Delta, Hansgrohe), paints and coatings (Behr), and cabinetry/other decorative building products — sold mainly through home-improvement retailers (Home Depot, Lowe's) and wholesale plumbing distributors to contractors and remodelers. It makes money on unit volume and price/mix across two reporting segments, Plumbing Products (~67% of Q2 2026 revenue) and Decorative Architectural (~33%). Its competitive position is strong (leading share in faucets and paint in North America, high brand recognition), but demand is tied to discretionary U.S. home-repair-and-remodel spending, which is cyclical and currently soft.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
B1 ranks MAS #52 (composite percentile ~89.6th) driven almost entirely by **Value** (earnings yield, FCF yield, EBIT/EV and book/price all in the 79th–87th percentile) and **Quality** (gross-profit/assets 90.6th percentile, OCF/assets 72.0th percentile), with weak **Momentum** (38.2nd percentile — the stock has lagged over the trailing 12-1 month window) and middling **Earnings-momentum/SUE** (70.1st percentile). This is **partly an artefact**: the "cheap and high quality" read is flattered by a shrinking denominator — MAS has been shrinking its own balance sheet and share count via buybacks (share count down from 233M in mid-2022 to 201.7M at Q1 2026, ‑13.4%) while GAAP net income has fallen in each of the last two fiscal years (FY2023 $908M → FY2024 $822M → FY2025 $810M, source: DEF 14A summary compensation table figures, filed 2026-04-21). Gross-profit/assets and OCF/assets ratios can stay statistically "high quality" even as the underlying earnings power softens, if assets shrink in step. The valuation is fair given the trend, not a mispricing being handed to us for free.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Repair-and-remodel is needs-based, not discretionary new-construction demand; management cites >$20bn of pent-up demand, and Q2 2026 adjusted margin expanded to 24.2% (+410bps YoY) even as revenue fell, showing real cost/price discipline. **[Corrected 2026-10-08: the ~$95M tariff refund (about 4.8 points of margin) accounts for all of it; ex-refund the adjusted margin was about 19.4% vs 20.1%; see Correction DV27]**
2. Buybacks at a below-average multiple (13.4% share-count reduction since mid-2022) compound per-share value even in a flat-volume world.
3. Valuation is already at the low end of Street targets and cheap vs. its own history; further de-rating (V1's −29%/yr bear case) looks like it would require a genuinely severe demand shock, not merely "more of the same."

**Bear:**
1. FY2025 revenue −3%, operating profit −8%, OCF down for a third straight year (cumulative −27.7% since 2023); Q2 2026's guidance raise is substantially a one-off ($95M tariff refund), and 2026 sales guidance is still only "low single digits" — no recovery is embedded even in management's own numbers.
2. Negative equity (−$242M) and a flat $2,945M of long-term debt for years mean the balance sheet has already been leaned on; if OCF keeps falling, buybacks would need to be trimmed or debt-funded.
3. Direct tariff exposure is real and outside management's control: ~$450M of China-sourced goods, with a scenario management itself has cited implying up to ~$625M of annualised incremental exposure in a worst case, plus ~$50M more from steel/aluminum/reciprocal tariffs — the Q2 refund is one-time, not a standing offset.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Organic net sales growth negative year-over-year for **three consecutive quarters** (already 2 of the last 3: Q4'25 −2%, Q2'26 −2.9%).
2. TTM operating cash flow declines for a **fourth consecutive fiscal year** (would mean FY2026 OCF below ~$1,000M).
3. Net debt/EBITDA rises above **2.5x** (currently ≈1.79x).
4. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is **cut** from the current $4.40–$4.60.
5. Incremental China-tariff exposure escalates materially above the ~$625–675M scenario management has already flagged.

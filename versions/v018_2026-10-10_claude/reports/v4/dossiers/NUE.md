# NUE (Nucor Corporation) — Diligence Dossier

Agent: F45 · Standard depth · Prepared 2026-09-26 · Price/market data as of 2026-09-25 close ($247.25; mkt cap ≈$56.5B)

## 1. Verdict

**WATCH** — 12–24 month horizon. Nucor is the best-run, best-capitalized company in North American steel
(A-/A-/A3 credit ratings, 213 consecutive quarterly dividends, disciplined buybacks), and current results are
genuinely excellent — but current EPS is running at a tariff/reshoring-cycle peak, and the price already assumes
that peak persists. Would upgrade on either (a) a pullback in price/multiple, or (b) 2+ more quarters confirming
steel-mill average selling prices and shipment volumes hold near current levels *without* a tariff-policy
reversal, which would support treating the current run-rate as a new, durable plateau rather than a cyclical
high. **Agrees directionally with V1's "excessive" verdict**, with one important correction to V1's own math
(Section 7).

## 2. Business in plain English

Nucor is the largest US steel producer (electric-arc-furnace/mini-mill model, more flexible and lower-fixed-cost
than integrated blast-furnace steelmaking) and North America's largest scrap-metal recycler. It makes and sells
steel bars, beams, sheet, plate, tubing, joists, deck, fasteners and other fabricated steel products, plus raw
materials (scrap, DRI/HBI, ferro-alloys) both internally and to third parties. Revenue is a function of
(volume shipped) × (price per ton), both currently elevated by Section 232 tariffs on imported steel, US
reshoring/infrastructure and data-center construction demand.

**Reconciliation (why I still agree with V1's direction):** the FCFF reverse DCF above takes TTM EBIT margin
(~11.8%) as the sustainable base — but that margin is itself a tariff/pricing-cycle high for a commodity
steelmaker whose margins have historically ranged far lower (and far higher) across cycles. A "6%/yr growth off
an already-cyclically-elevated earnings base forever" is a materially more demanding assumption than the
headline number suggests once you require margin durability, not just growth. V1's EPS-scenario approach, which
explicitly models margin mean-reversion, is the more appropriate lens for a cyclical commodity producer, and its
conclusion (current price is priced for more than a prudent normalized-margin base case delivers) is the one I
adopt. **valuation_view_vs_v1.consistent = true** (same directional conclusion), with the reconciliation being
about *why*, not *whether*, NUE looks expensive; **implied growth is above my base case.**

**3-year scenario returns (annualised, price+dividends):** bear −50.3% · base −10.5% · bull +65.3% — taken

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:** (1) Section 232 tariffs plus US reshoring and data-center/grid construction are driving genuinely
record shipment volumes (2 consecutive quarterly steel-mill shipment records), not just price. (2) Best-in-class
balance sheet and capital discipline (A-rated, 213 consecutive dividends, disciplined buybacks) mean Nucor can
self-fund its capacity expansion through a downturn without distress. (3) 2027 contract steel pricing is already
reportedly being written at $1,100–1,175/ton vs ~$800/ton in 2026 (secondary source, Q13 triage; not yet
independently verified against a primary Nucor disclosure). **[Corrected 2026-10-08: Reported steel-mills average selling price was $1,145/ton in Q2 2026 ($1,110 in H1 2026, $989 in H1 2025) and consolidated $1,367/ton (10-Q 0001193125-26-345891), so the claim of about $800/ton in 2026 contradicts the filings and kill criterion 1 sits 30% below the current level]**

**Bear:** (1) Tariff-dependent pricing is a *policy* moat, not a competitive one — a trade-policy reversal (a
real, named risk in the current environment) removes the pricing support quickly, and steel-mill EPS has already
shown it can swing >7x in five quarters. (2) FCF yield (~2.6% on EV) is low for a name trading as if it is
"cheap on P/E," because a large share of cash generation is being reinvested into capacity expansion that only
pays off if the current demand cycle is durable. (3) The current EPS run-rate is unprecedented in Nucor's own
recent history — extrapolating it is exactly the kind of trailing-momentum trap the triage note warned about.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. Steel-mill average selling price falls below **$800/ton** for 2 consecutive quarters (loses the current
   tariff-driven pricing premium referenced in 2027 contract discussions).
2. Quarterly diluted EPS falls back below **$2.00** for 2 consecutive quarters (below the 2025 trough-adjacent
   run-rate, evidence the cycle has turned).
3. Section 232 steel tariffs are repealed or substantially weakened (single trigger event — directly threatens
   the pricing premium underpinning current earnings).
4. Net debt/TTM EBITDA rises above **1.5x** (currently comfortably under 1x) via debt-funded capex overrun or
   M&A.
5. The 213-consecutive-quarter dividend streak is broken or a previously-declared increase is skipped (a
   de facto management signal of FCF stress from a historically very reliable capital-return payer).

## 2. Business in plain English
Wabtec makes and services locomotives, braking systems, signaling, and digital/analytics products for freight and transit rail worldwide, in a global duopoly with (predominantly) Progress Rail (Caterpillar) in North American freight locomotives. Over 60% of revenue is recurring services/aftermarket (parts, overhaul, digital subscriptions on the installed locomotive base), which smooths the cyclicality of new-equipment orders. It earns money on equipment sales plus long-dated service contracts tied to locomotive modernization and rail electrification/efficiency initiatives.

## 8. Bull case
1. Backlog (+42% YoY) provides multi-year revenue visibility rarely available in an industrial name; even if bookings slow from here, backlog conversion alone supports double-digit revenue growth for several more years.
2. Recurring services (>60% of revenue) dampen the historical boom-bust cyclicality of locomotive capex, supporting a structurally higher multiple than the pre-2015 Wabtec/GE Transportation-era business had.
3. Rail electrification/decarbonization and freight-efficiency mandates in North America and internationally are secular tailwinds for retrofitting the existing locomotive fleet, a high-margin services opportunity.

## 9. Bear case
1. Freight-rail capex is still fundamentally a cyclical, rail-carrier-capex-driven business; a freight-volume downturn (recession, trade disruption) would hit new-equipment orders hard even if services hold up, and the stock is priced with limited room for that scenario.
2. The Progress Rail/Caterpillar litigation settled in February 2026 without admission of liability — the underlying antitrust/breach-of-contract dispute (filed Q3 2023, U.S. District Court, District of Delaware) is closed, but it is a reminder that WAB operates in a two-player market that periodically draws competitive-conduct scrutiny; a renewed dispute or a genuine DOJ antitrust look at the duopoly structure (not currently pending, per this search) would be a re-rating risk.
3. Debt has grown 64% in five quarters while buybacks have slowed — if backlog conversion disappoints even modestly, the balance sheet has less flexibility than it did 18 months ago, and at 26.8x P/E there is little multiple cushion for an earnings miss.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. 12-month backlog (the shorter-duration, higher-signal book) growth turns negative YoY (vs. +11.3% now) for two consecutive quarters.
2. Adjusted EPS guidance is **cut** (not just held) at any quarterly release — the market has priced in continued raises.
3. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above **3.5x** (vs. ~2.6x now).
4. Freight-services revenue growth (the recurring, higher-margin segment) decelerates below **5%** YoY for two consecutive quarters, signalling the "less cyclical" thesis is not holding.
5. Any reopened antitrust/competitive-conduct litigation or regulatory inquiry involving the Progress Rail duopoly.

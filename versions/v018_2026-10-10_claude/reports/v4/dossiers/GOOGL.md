# GOOGL — Alphabet Inc. (Class A)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH.** Horizon 12–36 months. Google Cloud's growth is the single most impressive number in this whole batch (82% YoY), and the "cheap" trailing P/E (17.3x) is real but partly a GAAP artefact of one-off investment gains. On a 10-year reverse DCF, the $343.92 close requires a constant ~16.6% revenue CAGR — well above an evidence-based base case of ~11.3% — and the company just raised $70bn of new equity and debt to fund capex while an unresolved DOJ appeal (seeking Chrome divestiture) hangs over the core Search business.

## 2. Business in plain English
Alphabet mainly sells advertising attached to Google Search, YouTube and its ad network (still the majority of revenue and nearly all of its profit), plus a fast-growing Google Cloud business (infrastructure, data/AI platform services, and Workspace) sold to enterprises, and "Other Bets" (Waymo etc.), which lose money. It makes money by auctioning ad placements against user intent/attention and, increasingly, by renting AI compute and selling Gemini-based enterprise software.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durability check
b1 composite **0.820** — the highest of the four (decile 9, quintile 5, live_rank 89, closest to the quant top-30 of this group). Driven by very strong **Momentum** (fam_M 0.915) and **Earnings momentum** (fam_S 0.969) — both are trailing, backward-looking factors reflecting the stock's huge run and beat streak (8/8 EPS beats, avg surprise +50%), not necessarily forward durability. **Quality** (fam_Q 0.448) is middling and **Value** (fam_V 0.181) is the *weakest* of any family score shown here — i.e., on the model's own percentile-ranked earnings-yield/FCF-yield/EBIT-EV/book-price basis, Alphabet screens as **expensive**, not cheap, despite the low headline trailing P/E. This is the key "artefact" flag: **trailing EPS ($19.93) is inflated by non-operating equity/investment gains** (Alphabet reported a $99bn paper gain in Q2 2026 alone from its investment portfolio), which mechanically depresses the trailing P/E without reflecting recurring earnings power. The momentum/earnings-momentum drivers are real but not necessarily durable at this magnitude for a decade.

## 5. Guidance track record
FY2026 capex guidance was raised twice: from $180–190bn to **$195–205bn** at the Q2 2026 call, following an earlier raise from original guidance — each raise driven by AI infrastructure demand exceeding the prior plan, not by a miss. This is guidance being raised on *capex*, not necessarily on cloud revenue growth, which is a distinction worth holding onto: the company is confident enough in demand to keep re-raising spend, which is bullish for revenue but a genuine burden on near-term free cash flow (see §6).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- H1 2026 capex: **$80.6bn**, more than double H1 2025's $39.6bn. Depreciation is rising fast (R&D and "Other Cost of Revenues" both cited depreciation as a primary driver of their increases) [Alphabet Q1 2026 10-Q/press release].
- Operating cash flow adjusted for SBC climbed 35.2% YoY to a record **$148bn TTM** — the underlying cash-generative core (mostly Search/YouTube) remains very strong even as Cloud consumes capital.
- **Financing shift, not just self-funded growth:** in June 2026 Alphabet raised **$49.6bn of new equity** (Class A/C common plus mandatory convertible preferred) and **$20.3bn of net proceeds from senior unsecured notes** — its first meaningful external capital raise in years, and share count *rose* this quarter rather than falling, ending what one tracker called "the buyback era" for now [Beancount.io, MarketScreener, 2026-07]. This directly weakens the buyback-driven EPS tailwind embedded in consensus models.
- d4 snapshot: total debt $120.8bn, total cash $242.5bn (still net-cash), EBITDA $173.2bn, FCF (Yahoo definition) $22.7bn TTM — comfortably solvent, but FCF is being compressed by capex even before the July equity/debt raise is reflected. **[Corrected 2026-10-08: company-reported TTM FCF is $53.3bn (OCF $185.7bn less capex $132.4bn) with Q2 2026 FCF of $(5.9)bn, DV19]**

## 7. Valuation — reverse DCF (no V1 row exists for this ticker; valuation performed directly here)
**10-year reverse DCF:** using a 12% discount rate, 36% terminal operating margin (vs. today's 34% and rising), 18% tax, ~12.23bn diluted shares (implied from market cap/price) and a 21x terminal P/E (in line with Alphabet's own recent 5-year average forward multiple), the $343.92 close requires a **constant 10-year revenue CAGR of ~16.6%** to be justified. **[Corrected 2026-10-08: 12% is a 6.8-point premium over the 5.17% Treasury; at 9.67% the required CAGR is 14.4%, not 16.6%, DV19]**

**My base case:** Cloud decelerating from 82% today to the 20s% by year 3 (the segment is compounding off an increasingly large base — $514bn backlog already implies multi-year visibility, but 80%+ growth cannot persist for a decade against a $100bn+ annualized cloud business) blended with mature, single-digit-to-low-teens Search/YouTube growth. Explicit path (20%/17%/14%/12%/11%/10%/9%/8%/7%/6% company-wide) gives a 10-year compound-equivalent of **~11.3%** and a base-case fair value of **~$220** — **~56% below** the current $343.92, the largest gap of the four names reviewed. **[Corrected 2026-10-08: $220 is 36% below $343.92 (the price is 56% above $220); at a 9.67% discount rate base value is about $262, price 31% above, DV19]** **implied_vs_base: above.**

## 8. Bull case
1. Google Cloud's operating-margin inflection (20%→35% cloud margin YoY) is arguably the single best evidence in this whole batch that "AI infrastructure is becoming profitable," not just growing — a scale argument, not a hope.
2. $514bn backlog with >50% converting inside 24 months gives real multi-year revenue visibility, independent of new bookings.
3. Gemini app has 950m MAU and Alphabet can monetize AI at multiple points in the stack (infra, Vertex, Gemini Enterprise, Workspace) — more monetization surface area than a single-product competitor.

## Bear case
1. Trailing "cheap" valuation is partly a GAAP artefact of one-off equity/investment gains; on the model's own value-factor percentile (fam_V 0.18) Alphabet screens as expensive, not cheap.
2. The June 2026 equity+debt raise ($70bn combined) signals internally that even Alphabet's enormous free cash flow could not fully fund the capex plan without external capital — a genuine, dated data point on capex burden, not speculation.
3. **DOJ/state AGs cross-appealed** the December 2025 search-remedies final judgment, specifically pressing for **Chrome divestiture and termination of the Apple default-search agreement** — remedies the district court declined to order but that remain live on appeal, likely extending into 2027. This is unresolved binary risk to Search's distribution economics, not priced to zero.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (thesis-invalidation triggers — signposts to watch)
1. Google Cloud revenue growth decelerates below **40% YoY** for two consecutive quarters (a signal the 82% print was a peak, not a new trend).
2. Cloud operating margin fails to hold above **30%** for two consecutive quarters (would undermine the profitability-inflection thesis).
3. D.C. Circuit sets a briefing schedule that puts **Chrome divestiture or Apple-agreement termination** back in play as a live remedy (currently not yet scheduled as of this cutoff).
4. FY2026 capex guidance is raised a **third time** beyond $205bn without a corresponding upward revision to Cloud revenue guidance (would indicate capex is running ahead of demand, not behind it).
5. Full-year buybacks fall to zero / net share count rises for **two consecutive quarters** (confirms the "end of buyback era" is structural, not one quarter).

# TKO Group Holdings (TKO) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F93, standard depth)

**1. Verdict: WATCH, 24–36 month horizon.** Irreplaceable live combat-sports/entertainment IP (WWE + UFC) with
long-duration, contracted media revenue and real double-digit consolidated growth — but the Up-C structure means the
public Class A share only captures roughly a third of consolidated earnings, and on that shrunken per-share base the
implied growth priced into the stock (mid-30s P/E) is **above** this dossier's base case (Section 7): a great business
priced for more than its per-share economics have yet delivered. Condition that would move this to INCLUDE-SMALL:
attributable-to-TKO diluted EPS growth re-accelerating to a sustained 40%+ YoY for two consecutive quarters, or the
multiple compressing toward the mid-20s without a growth deceleration.

**2. Business in plain English.** TKO owns and operates WWE (scripted wrestling entertainment) and UFC (live mixed
martial arts), monetized through media-rights fees (the largest, most contracted revenue stream — e.g. WWE's Netflix
deal), live-event ticketing/sponsorship, and consumer products/licensing. It is the dominant player in both its
categories with no comparable-scale competitor, giving it real pricing power at each media-rights renewal.

**3. Why the model likes it / durability.** b1_live_scores.csv: composite decile 6/10, quintile 3/5, driven by
momentum and value/factor mix rather than a pure quality signal; sue (earnings-surprise) percentile only 0.34,
meaning recent EPS surprises have been middling on a per-share (i.e., NCI-diluted) basis even as consolidated revenue
and operating income grew strongly — an important nuance the triage's quality/growth score of 4/4 did not fully
capture. Durable driver: locked-in, multi-year media contracts (WWE–Netflix: ~$550m/yr, 10-year term through 2035 per
Wrestlenomics/POST Wrestling reporting, cross-checked, not a primary SEC filing) reduce revenue cyclicality; UFC's
scale/IP moat is structural, not an artefact.

**8/9. Bull case.** (i) WWE's Netflix deal (~$550m/yr, 10-year term) and UFC's scale give multi-year revenue
visibility few media assets have. (ii) Consolidated operating margin near 25–28% and improving. (iii) Genuine IP
scarcity — no close competitor in either scripted wrestling or MMA at this scale.

**Bear case.** (i) The Up-C structure caps what flows to public Class A holders — NCI took ~67% of Q2 2026
consolidated net income; per-share economics grow slower than headline segment metrics suggest. (ii) Leverage stepped
up materially in 1H 2026 (noncurrent debt +23% since Dec-2025) while cash fell. (iii) Litigation legacy: the "Le v.
Zuffa" UFC fighter antitrust class action reached a $375m final settlement (approved 2025-02-06 per Fightful/
SportsPro/Saveri Law Firm reporting, secondary sources — the settlement order itself was not opened in this pass,
**flagged, do not treat as filing-verified**); a related wage-fixing case ("Johnson v. Zuffa") may still be active —
**not confirmed this pass**, treat as an open item, not a closed one.

5. Loss or non-renewal of a top-3 media-rights contract (WWE-Netflix, UFC's primary US broadcast partner).

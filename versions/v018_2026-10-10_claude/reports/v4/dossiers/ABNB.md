# ABNB — Airbnb, Inc.

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — thesis horizon 12–36 months. Genuinely strong, primary-source-verified operating momentum (revenue/GBV/margin guidance raised or beaten in each of the last four quarters, ~10% reduction in diluted share count since 2022 from buybacks **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the Q4 2025 release says approximately 9% (acc 0001193125-26-048670)]**, a $12bn net-cash balance sheet **[Corrected 2026-10-06: net cash is $9.6bn: cash and short-term investments $12.07bn less $2.48bn long-term debt (10-Q 0001559720-26-000027, 2026-06-30)]**, 37% TTM FCF margin) is offset by a valuation that is **not** statistically cheap (26.3x NTM P/E against peers that are not fully comparable) and by spreading, city-by-city short-term-rental regulation that is a genuine and event-driven — not merely theoretical — risk to the core listings business. Investable at reduced size given the balance-sheet quality and cash generation; the named reservation is that the base case requires *further* multiple re-rating from an already-premium starting point, offering limited margin of safety.

## 2. Business in plain English

Airbnb operates the world's largest marketplace connecting people who want to rent out a home or room ("hosts") with travellers who want to stay in one ("guests"), and — increasingly — travellers who want to book an "experience" (a local activity) or, via a newly launched initiative, a hotel room. Airbnb owns no real estate; it earns a service/commission fee (an "implied take rate," revenue ÷ Gross Booking Value) on each booking, which has run 9%–18% quarter to quarter (the swings are driven mostly by the timing mismatch between when a guest books and pays vs. when they stay, not by a change in pricing — see §4). It competes with Booking.com/Vrbo/Expedia for the room-night, and — where regulators restrict short-term rentals — increasingly with hotels directly, which is why it has begun listing boutique/independent hotels itself in supply-constrained cities.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **FCF conversion:** very strong — TTM FCF margin 37%, well above the ~19–22% net-income margin, reflecting genuine operating leverage plus the (non-repeatable-forever) benefit of a growing, negative-working-capital deposit float.
- **SBC:** materially high, 9.7%–15.8% of revenue every quarter over two years — this is the real, recurring, dilutive cost the retail/asset-light playbooks warn against adding back uncritically. The offsetting, verifiable fact: diluted weighted-average share count has **fallen** from 626m (Q2 2025) to 597m (Q2 2026), a genuine ~4.6% YoY reduction, and the company states fully-diluted shares are down ~10% since buybacks began in Q3 2022 — buybacks are more than absorbing the RSU dilution on a net basis, which is a legitimate, evidenced rebuttal to the naive "add back SBC and ignore dilution" critique. Cumulative cash spent on buybacks + RSU tax withholding since Q3 2022: **$13.6bn** (per Q4 2025 release) — a very large capital-allocation commitment relative to the company's size.
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** ABNB's own EPS is not separately XBRL-tagged (see §4 note) and diluted EPS/NI both benefited from a $60m uncertain-tax-position release and favourable FDII tax treatment in Q4 2025 disclosed by the company itself; long-term effective tax rate is guided to decline into the "mid-to-high teens" from 2026 due to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA).
- **Net debt/capital metrics:** net cash (cash + STI − total debt) ≈ **$9.6bn** per V1's latest snapshot; even after the March-2026 $2.5bn note issuance, ABNB remains net-cash, not net-debt. Capex is immaterial (~0.3% of revenue) — a genuinely asset-light model, so leverage/coverage ratios are not a constraint here.
- **M&A:** none pending or recently closed; the "hotels" initiative to date is a distribution partnership, not an acquisition.

## 7. Valuation snapshot — reconciliation with V1

**V1 verdict: fair** (score 0.10/1.0, near the boundary). NTM P/E 26.3x vs. ABNB's own **only 53-month** trading history (flagged by V1 itself as a "short/limited history" caveat — appropriately so; 53 months is not enough to establish a reliable own-history percentile through a full cycle) — currently the 23rd percentile of that short history. Peer median cited is 13.1x across BKNG/CCL/EXPE/HLT — **I flag this peer set as heterogeneous** per the sector playbook's own warning against blending owner/lessee/OTA/franchisor business models in one multiple: HLT is an asset-light hotel *franchisor* (structurally deserves a premium multiple of its own), CCL is a capital-intensive cruise operator, and EXPE is a lower-growth, lower-margin OTA — only BKNG is a close structural peer to ABNB. The 13.1x blended peer median is therefore less informative than it looks; I would not lean hard on the "ABNB trades at ~2x peer median" framing without unbundling the peer set first.

Reverse-DCF implied 10-year growth is **+26.5%** (using TTM EBIT margin 20.8%) against consensus FY1 growth of only +11.6% — the market is pricing continued mid-20s%/year growth for a decade. V1's own historical-CAGR fields are **null** (no 5/10-year delivered-growth comparison is possible — again a function of ABNB's short history as a public, post-pandemic-ramp company), so the "priced-for-vs-delivered" cross-check that anchors DG and DRI's valuation reconciliation is simply unavailable here — a genuine limitation, not an oversight. V1 base case: revenue growth 13.6%/yr, margin expanding to 22.5% (above the current TTM 20.8%, consistent with the margin-guidance raises in §5), **exit multiple 33.7x** — i.e., re-rating *further up* from today's already-premium 26.3x → base annualised 3-yr return **+18.8%**; bear −20.3%/bull +78.9% (an unusually wide bear/bull spread, reflecting genuine two-way uncertainty). Street 12-month target range $135–$220 (mean $184.35); V1's base 3-year value exceeds the Street 12-month high (same `street_flag` caveat as DG, less concerning given the different horizons).

**My view: consistent = true, dossier_view = fair.** I do not have a primary-source basis to call ABNB "cheap" — take rate is not expanding, the peer comparison is soft, and the base case's own math requires the multiple to expand *again* from an already-rich level, which is a materially weaker margin of safety than DG's setup (re-rating from a trough toward a historical norm). I also do not see grounds to call it "expensive"/"full" — the cash generation, balance sheet, and four-quarter beat-and-raise record are real. "Fair," with two-sided risk, is the right characterisation, and I agree with V1.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull (3):**
1. Four consecutive quarters of beating the high end of its own guidance on revenue, with Adjusted EBITDA margin guidance raised in three of the last four releases (28% Q4'25 trough → "at least 35.5%" current FY26 guide) — genuine, verifiable operating momentum.
2. $12.1bn net cash, 37% TTM FCF margin **[Corrected 2026-10-06: $12.1bn is gross cash plus short-term investments; net cash is $9.6bn]**, and a demonstrated willingness/ability to shrink the diluted share count (−4.6% YoY) despite heavy RSU issuance — a rare combination of growth and capital-return capacity.
3. The direct-hotel-booking initiative in supply-constrained/regulated cities (NYC, LA, Madrid, San Francisco) is a credible, already-launched hedge against the regulatory risk in the bear case below, and the 2026 FIFA World Cup is a disclosed near-term demand catalyst.

**Bear (3):**
1. Short-term-rental regulation is *spreading*, not stabilising: Spain's Constitutional Court upheld Barcelona's decision not to renew any of its 10,101 tourist-apartment licenses (expiring Nov-2028); Spain separately fined Airbnb **€64 million in December 2025** for advertising unlicensed listings; **[Corrected 2026-10-06: per the 10-Q the fine is about EUR65M (reduced in Sept 2025 from EUR110M), disputed, suspended by a EUR70M surety bond, and no loss is accrued]** and Budapest, Athens and Houston added new restrictions in 2026 alongside the already-restrictive NYC and Barcelona regimes. This is a real, monetised, geographically-widening risk to the core listings business that is not obviously reflected in a take rate that has not expanded.
2. Take rate itself has been flat-to-down YoY in three of the last four quarters — the margin story is 100% cost/leverage-driven, which is more fragile than a pricing-power-driven margin story if growth decelerates.
3. The DOJ filed an amended federal housing-discrimination complaint against Airbnb in March 2026 alleging a pattern-or-practice of discrimination (broader than the single-host allegation the case began with) — an active, escalating federal legal matter with headline and financial-liability risk.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. FY revenue-growth or Adjusted EBITDA-margin guidance is **cut** versus the immediately prior quarter's guidance (four straight raises/holds to date).
2. Take rate (revenue ÷ GBV) falls for **two consecutive quarters** on a YoY basis by more than 100bps, stripped of the disclosed RNPL/FX timing effects — i.e., a genuine, explained monetisation deterioration.
3. Diluted weighted-average share count **rises** YoY for two consecutive quarters (buybacks failing to offset RSU dilution — currently falling, −4.6% YoY at Q2 2026).
4. Net cash (cash + STI − total debt) falls by more than **$3 billion** in a rolling 12 months outside of a disclosed, board-approved acquisition.
5. A new city/national regulatory action (license non-renewal, platform-wide ban or a fine) is disclosed that management itself identifies as covering a "material" market, or cumulative disclosed regulatory fines exceed **$150 million** on a trailing-12-month basis (calibrated off the December-2025 Spain fine).

## 11. Red-flag scan

- **DOJ housing-discrimination lawsuit:** *United States v. Airbnb, Inc.*, N.D. Cal., an active federal civil-rights case; DOJ filed an amended complaint in March 2026 broadening the allegation to a pattern-or-practice theory across multiple cities. Unresolved as of this dossier.
- **Spain fine:** €64 million (~$70m), announced December 2025, for advertising unlicensed short-term rentals — a direct, monetised regulatory penalty (not merely a market restriction). **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the 10-Q (acc 0001559720-26-000027) describes it as a disputed fine of about EUR65M with enforcement suspended by a surety bond; no loss is accrued]**
- **Barcelona:** Spain's Constitutional Court (March 2025) upheld the city's refusal to renew any of its 10,101 tourist-apartment licenses when they expire in November 2028 — a known, dated, but distant (2+ year) supply event.
- **Shareholder viewpoint-discrimination suit:** Alliance Defending Freedom sued Airbnb (Delaware federal court, filed June 2025) over exclusion of a conservative shareholder proposal; a February 2026 ruling allowed the core claim to proceed. Lower financial materiality, a governance/proxy-process matter rather than a core-business risk.
- **Prior discrimination settlement:** a 2024 class-action discrimination settlement included a $6m guest-compensation fund — already resolved, noted for completeness.
- No auditor change, restatement or going-concern language identified in the filings reviewed. Insider Form-4 activity is high-frequency but consistent with routine RSU-vest sell-to-cover under 10b5-1 plans across a large Section-16-officer roster; no unusual acceleration in net insider selling was identified within the scope of this review.

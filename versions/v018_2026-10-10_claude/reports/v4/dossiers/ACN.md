# ACN — Accenture plc

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — the world's largest IT/management-consulting franchise, priced at 12x NTM earnings (a deep
discount to its own 25–30x historical norm) for a business that is genuinely decelerating (revenue growth down to
mid-single digits, new bookings down YoY, a real U.S.-federal headwind) but is not broken: margins are still
expanding, free cash flow is enormous, and the balance sheet is net cash. Named reservation: bookings turned
negative YoY this quarter and the GenAI-disintermediation risk to the core staff-augmentation model is real, not
hypothetical, so this is a "priced-for-pessimism" holding, not a clean compounder. Horizon: 24–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Accenture is the largest global IT and management-consulting firm: it helps large companies and governments redesign
business processes, migrate and run IT systems (cloud, ERP, data/AI platforms), and increasingly build and run
generative-AI applications, billing mostly on a time-and-materials or managed-services (outsourcing) basis. Revenue
splits roughly evenly between project-based Consulting and longer-duration Managed Services; its moat is scale,
deep client relationships across every major industry, and the switching costs of ripping out a multi-year
outsourcing/systems-integration relationship.

## 5. Guidance track record (last 3 releases; Accenture guides full fiscal year at each quarter)
- **Initial FY26 guide (given 2025-09-25, Q4/FY25 release):** revenue growth "2% to 5%" in local currency (ex-federal
  impact "3% to 6%"); GAAP diluted EPS "$13.19 to $13.57."
- **Guide given 2026-03-19 (Q2 FY26 release):** revenue growth **raised at the low end** to "3% to 5%" LC (ex-federal
  "4% to 6%"); GAAP EPS narrowed to "$13.25 to $13.50" (low end up $0.06, high end down $0.07 vs. initial).
  "Revenues at the top of the company's guided range" for Q2 itself, per the release headline.
- **Guide given 2026-06-18 (Q3 FY26 release):** revenue growth **narrowed at the top end** to "3% to 4%" LC
  (ex-federal "4% to 5%") — i.e., the high end of the range was *cut* from 5% to 4% even as the low end held at 3%;
  GAAP EPS raised further to "$13.38 to $13.50" (low end up another $0.13). Q3 revenue itself came in "slightly above
  the midpoint" of the prior quarter's implied range.
  **Net effect over three releases: full-year local-currency revenue-growth guidance has been progressively
  narrowed from "2–5%" to "3–4%" — the low end was raised early (Q2) and the high end was cut later (Q3); this is
  a mixed signal (a de-risked, not simply "raised," guide), and I read it as the company converging on ~3.5% LC
  growth for FY26, at the low end of Accenture's historical range.** EPS guidance, by contrast, has been raised at
  every release. **Pending; next report (Q4/FY26 and full-year) ~2026-10-01 per d4 snapshot — imminent.**

**V1 systematic valuation (v1_valuation_table.csv): verdict "attractive," score 0.98.** NTM P/E 12.01x (own-history

V1's P/E-implied growth is **-5.5%** (the price implies the market expects revenue/earnings to *shrink*); V1's
bear/base/bull 3-year annualized returns are 11.6% / 30.2% / 49.7%. **V1 also flags its own base case as internally
suspect:** `street_flag = "base 3y value exceeds Street 12m high target"` — V1's base-case re-rating (to ~30%/yr for
3 years, implying the price roughly doubles) would put Accenture above even the highest analyst 12-month price
target ($275 vs. current $176.11), which V1 itself surfaces as a red flag. **I do not fully agree with V1's base
case:** a full re-rating back to Accenture's mid-2010s–2020 25–30x multiple looks too aggressive given the bookings
deceleration and negative YoY bookings this quarter; I use a more conservative reverse-DCF: NTM EPS ≈$14.66, ~5–7%/yr
EPS growth (in line with the FY26 EPS guide's 10–11% this year, decelerating toward mid-single-digit
consulting/managed-services growth longer term) with a modest re-rating to 14–15x (still below history, reflecting
partial resolution of the federal/GenAI overhang) implies roughly **base case ~11–13%/yr** total return (including
the 3.7% dividend and continued buybacks) — well below V1's 30.2%/yr but still comfortably positive and, crucially,
**below** what I believe Accenture can deliver on a normalized basis if the deceleration stabilizes rather than
compounds. **Bear case:** LC growth stays near 2–3%, no re-rating (multiple stays ~12x), federal drag persists
longer than guided → roughly **3–5%/yr** (mostly the dividend). **Bull case:** bookings reaccelerate as GenAI
transformation work becomes a net new-demand driver rather than a cannibalization risk, multiple re-rates to 17–18x
→ roughly **20–23%/yr**. `valuation_view_vs_v1`: dossier view **cheap**, `consistent: true` in direction (both V1
and I see the stock as statistically cheap) but **inconsistent on magnitude** — V1's base case is more optimistic
than mine (reconciliation: V1's base-case re-rating assumption is not supported by the current bookings trend, and
V1 itself flags this via `street_flag`).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) 12x NTM P/E against an 11.2% FCF yield and a net-cash balance sheet is priced for far more pessimism
than a still-growing, margin-expanding franchise (GAAP operating margin +20bps YoY in Q3) usually receives;
(2) generative-AI transformation bookings and large ($100M+) client deals are still growing (104 such bookings
YTD, up 13%, per the Q3 release) — GenAI may be a net tailwind for large-scale reinvention work, not only a threat
to staff-augmentation revenue; (3) $8.2B already returned to shareholders YTD (buybacks + dividends) provides a
floor-like capital-return yield even absent a re-rating.
**Bear:** (1) new bookings fell YoY in Q3 FY26 (-2% USD/-3% LC) for the first time in this two-year window — a
leading indicator for future revenue, not yet a lagging one; (2) the U.S. federal segment is an acknowledged,
guided-for drag (≈1% of FY26 growth) tied to government contract reviews/terminations, and could worsen with further
budget actions; (3) the long-run risk that GenAI compresses demand and pricing for traditional staff-augmentation
consulting is structural and not yet resolved one way or the other by the data in hand.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Local-currency revenue growth below 3% (the bottom of the FY26 guide) for two consecutive quarters.
2. New bookings YoY decline (USD) continuing for two more consecutive quarters (one quarter, Q3 FY26, already
   negative).
3. U.S. federal segment drag exceeding the guided ~1% impact on full-year local-currency growth (i.e., realized
   federal drag disclosed at >1.5% in a future quarterly release).
4. GAAP operating margin expansion stalling or reversing YoY (currently guided to 50–70bps of full-year FY26
   expansion; any quarter of YoY margin contraction would break this).
5. Consolidated net cash position (cash and short-term investments minus total debt, from the 10-Q balance sheet)
   falling below $2 billion, e.g. from debt-funded M&A (such as Dragos) rather than cash-funded.

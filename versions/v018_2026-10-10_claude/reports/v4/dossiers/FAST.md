## 2. Business in plain English
Fastenal sells fasteners, safety supplies, cutting tools and other industrial/MRO products through about 1,600 branches, on-site locations at customers' plants and about 141,000 (weighted MEU) vending/bin devices. It earns the product margin (gross margin 44.6%) on a high-frequency, low-ticket, service-heavy model; 75.9% of sales are to manufacturers. The moat is switching cost and service density: once vending machines and bins sit inside a customer's plant, replacing Fastenal means re-tooling the customer's supply chain. Larger "contract" customers (75.8% of Q2 2026 sales, daily sales +17.6%) are where the share gains are.

## Correction (verification DV16, 2026-10-08)

1. Reverse DCF restated for the discount rate. The 9% rate is not tied to the 5.17% 10-year Treasury (25 Sep 2026) and implies an ERP of 3.8%. The dossier's own 14.4% / 17.2% / 19.7% (8% / 9% / 10%) replicate on EV $57.8bn and start FCF $1,146M (10 years then 3%). At Ke = 5.17% + 1.0 x 4.5% ERP = 9.67% the price implies ~18.8% a year (15.6% at 9% on the normalised $1,285M), against the 9% base: 'above' holds and is slightly stronger.
2. Internal inconsistency in the re-entry level. The 'revisit at or below about $42' trigger is the price at which the base-case 3-year scenario return reaches ~9% a year (it depends on a 30x exit multiple that the dossier says is its own assumption). On the dossier's own DCF a 9% growth base case is worth only ~$27 a share at a 9% discount rate (~$24 at 9.67%), so $42 should not be read as the price at which implied growth equals the base case.

**Recency gate:** Most recent period incorporated: Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended 2026-06-30, 10-Q filed 2026-08-05; earnings released 2026-07-28). Checked for events to 2026-09-25 close, including an August 2026 8-K (leadership, items 5.02/7.01/9.01, filed 2026-08-31) not analyzed in depth here — flagged for follow-up. Data-quality note: SEC EDGAR's Archives server had a maintenance outage for part of this session and UPS's 10-K is heavily inline-XBRL-tagged, which degraded automated text extraction of the narrative sections; segment, headcount and buyback figures below are corroborated by the company's own press releases and prior-year 10-Ks rather than a full read of the FY2025 10-K narrative — flagged wherever this applies. **UPS reports both GAAP and non-GAAP "adjusted" figures every quarter with a large and growing gap between them — every number below is explicitly labeled.**

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH.** Thesis horizon: 12–24 months. UPS is executing a real, now-largely-complete restructuring (the "Amazon glide-down" is done, management says, as of Q2'26) that is genuinely improving the business's unit economics — but the GAAP results are currently ugly (Q2'26 GAAP operating margin 4.1% vs adjusted 9.2%; GAAP EPS $0.71 vs adjusted $1.76), the dividend is frozen and paid out of a thin FCF margin, buybacks have been suspended entirely in 2026, and the company is now in active litigation with the Teamsters over the very buyout program driving the "adjusted" earnings story. This is a legitimate turnaround candidate, not yet a demonstrated one — WATCH until GAAP and adjusted results converge and at least one full guided quarter is delivered cleanly.

## 2. Business in plain English
UPS moves and delivers packages and freight through three segments: U.S. Domestic Package (ground and air parcel delivery within the US, the largest segment), International Package (parcel delivery outside the US, structurally the highest-margin segment), and Supply Chain Solutions (freight forwarding, contract logistics, and healthcare logistics — including the recently acquired Andlauer Healthcare Group). It earns money on a per-package basis (yield), competing against FedEx, national postal services, regional carriers, and — as both customer and increasingly competitor — Amazon's own logistics network.

## 3. Why the model likes it
From the preliminary quant screen (`lead_prelim_rank.csv`, families Q/V/M): UPS ranks **#16 of ~500**, prelim composite 0.790, with the **Value family strongest (0.876)** — consistent with a stock trading near multi-year lows on a depressed earnings base — and Quality (0.751) and Momentum (0.742) more middling. This is a coherent, explainable combination: the stock is cheap in absolute terms (trailing P/E 17.5x looks unremarkable, but **on adjusted TTM earnings the multiple is materially lower**, and NTM P/E is 12.0x per the calendarized d4 snapshot) precisely because GAAP earnings are currently depressed by restructuring charges that management says are largely one-time. Whether the Value signal is durable depends entirely on whether the "adjusted" story becomes the GAAP story within the thesis horizon — this is not yet proven (see §4, §6).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
**FCF conversion:** FY2025 FCF/NI (GAAP) ≈ $4.77B/$5.572B = 86%; **the GAAP-vs-adjusted gap is the dominant earnings-quality issue this quarter** (§4) — treat adjusted EPS as management's estimate of run-rate earning power, not as a substitute for GAAP cash economics. **SBC:** not independently broken out in this session — flagged as a gap for the quant/valuation team. **Leverage:** gross debt $24.2B, cash $4.65B, net debt ≈ $19.6–24.2B depending on treatment; **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 1.8–2.2x** (via `ratios.py`, FY2025 GAAP EBITDA ~$10.9B) — moderate, not alarming, but up from UPS's historical low-1x range, and ROE of ~37% is **substantially leverage-driven** (equity multiplier 4.7x per the DuPont decomposition), not purely an operating-quality signal. Interest coverage ~11x on FY2025 GAAP EBIT — adequate. **M&A:** completed the ~$1.6B (CAD $2.2B) acquisition of Andlauer Healthcare Group on 2025-11-05 (healthcare/cold-chain logistics, adds debt-funded growth capital intensity); separately, UPS **sold** Coyote Logistics (freight brokerage) to RXO for $1.025B, closed 2024-09-16 — both add real complexity to any YoY comparison of the Supply Chain Solutions segment. **Share count / capital return — a genuine adverse fact:** UPS repurchased $1.0B of stock in 2025 but **has repurchased zero shares in H1 2026** (confirmed via XBRL: `PaymentsForRepurchaseOfCommonStock` = $0 in both Q1 and Q2 2026 10-Qs), while continuing to pay **$5.398B of dividends in FY2025** ($6.56/share annualized, 7.1% trailing yield) — **the quarterly dividend has been held flat for seven consecutive quarters** and the payout ratio is **~80–90% of GAAP net income, well above UPS's own long-stated 50–60% target** **[Corrected 2026-10-08: dividends were 97% of FY2025 GAAP net income, 118% over the trailing twelve months, and 156% / 225% in Q1 / Q2 2026]**. This is not a dividend cut, but it is real, current strain, not a hypothetical risk, and the buyback suspension is the clearest evidence management is prioritizing balance sheet and the dividend over total shareholder return via repurchases.

## 7. Valuation snapshot
NTM P/E (calendarized, per `d4_live_snapshot.parquet`) **12.0x**; trailing P/E on GAAP EPS 17.5x (`valuation.py` EV bridge: EV $99.5B on FY2025 GAAP EBITDA ~$10.9B → **EV/EBITDA 9.2x**, EV/EBIT 12.6x); FCF yield ~6.0–7.0%. Versus its closest listed peer, **FedEx trades at a similar forward P/E (~15–16x both names) but a higher EV/EBITDA (~10.6x FedEx vs ~9.1–9.2x UPS)** — i.e. UPS is not obviously cheaper than FedEx once capital structure is normalized, despite the much lower headline P/E some sources quote. [Aggregator cross-check only, not a source of record: stockanalysis.com and GuruFocus, accessed 2026-09-26, source 11 — dated differently than the program's own d4 snapshot; treat this specific peer comparison as directional, not primary-sourced.] **Reverse-DCF** (WACC 8.0%, terminal growth 2.5%): the current EV implies only **~4% per-year FCF growth for a decade** **[Corrected 2026-10-08: programme basis (Ke 9.42%, equity, FCF after SBC) gives ~2.1%/yr; implied vs base below, very sensitive to FCF]** — a low, easily-clearable bar if the transformation savings ($3B targeted for 2026, $1.2B delivered in H1) show up in free cash flow rather than being reinvested or absorbed by restructuring cash costs. A probability-weighted bear/base/bull scenario (bear $5.50 EPS/12x, base $7.22 adjusted-EPS guide/14x, bull $8.50/17x) centers on **~$101/share, roughly 8% above the current price** — i.e., unlike JBHT, this dossier's own scenario math does **not** find UPS obviously overpriced; the risk here is much more about *whether the adjusted-EPS trajectory is real* than about paying too much for a proven number.

## 8. Bull case
1. **The Amazon glide-down is complete, not still unfolding** — management says Amazon is now ~9% of UPS's business (down from a much larger, lower-yield share), and US Domestic revenue-per-piece rose 8.3% in FY2025 as low-yield volume was deliberately shed. If the "leaner, more automated, more agile network" the CEO describes is real, incremental volume from here should carry materially better margin.
2. **International segment remains a genuinely high-margin, resilient business** (17.5% GAAP / 18.0% adjusted operating margin in FY2025, +2.5% revenue with +7.1% RPP) that is under-appreciated when the narrative is dominated by the US Domestic/Amazon story.
3. **Valuation already reflects a depressed earnings base**: at 12x NTM P/E and a reverse-DCF hurdle of only ~4%/year FCF growth, this is priced for a company in distress, not for the "return to consolidated revenue and operating profit growth" management guided to starting Q2'26.

## 9. Bear case
1. **The GAAP results, not the adjusted ones, are what the balance sheet and dividend actually run on**, and Q2'26 GAAP operating margin of 4.1% (vs. 9.2% adjusted) is the worst GAAP print in the two-year window reviewed here. A thesis that requires "trust the adjustments" is exactly the pattern this research program's own prior audit flagged as a repeat mistake — this dossier does not resolve that tension, it surfaces it.
2. **The dividend is under real, current strain**: flat for seven quarters, 80–90% GAAP payout ratio vs. a 50–60% target, and buybacks fully suspended in 2026. A dividend cut — while not our base case — is a plausible tail outcome if transformation cash costs run longer than the "largely complete" framing suggests, and would likely trigger a sharp de-rating given the stock is partly held for yield.
3. **Active Teamsters litigation directly tied to the restructuring** (the union sued in Q1 2026 alleging that UPS's buyout program breaches the 2023 National Master Agreement, which runs through July 2028 and covers roughly 80% of UPS's US workforce) is a concrete, current legal risk that could constrain management's ability to execute the very cost program the bull case depends on, and is a cost/distraction regardless of outcome.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
These are the specific, observable, time-bound conditions that would **invalidate** the WATCH-to-INCLUDE upgrade path:
- GAAP operating margin fails to close materially toward the adjusted figure by Q4 2026 (i.e., the gap is still >300bps a year from now) — would indicate "one-time" charges are recurring.
- The Teamsters litigation results in an injunction or settlement that reverses or materially slows the network-restructuring program.
- The board cuts the dividend, or the payout ratio (GAAP basis) exceeds 100% for two consecutive quarters.
- Full-year 2026 guidance is cut (not just "raised by less than expected") at the Q3'26 or Q4'26 release.
- Buybacks remain at zero through year-end 2026 with no board commentary on resumption — would confirm capital-return capacity is more impaired than management's framing suggests.

## 12. Red-flag scan
**Auditor:** Deloitte & Touche LLP; no change identified. **Litigation — material and current:** the Teamsters sued UPS in early 2026 alleging the pending buyout program breaches the September 2023 National Master Agreement (ratified through July 2028); UPS is contesting the suit. This is the single most important red flag in this dossier and is discussed in §9 and §10 — do not treat it as boilerplate litigation. **Pension:** UPS carries a legacy multiemployer pension withdrawal liability related to the New England Teamsters and Trucking Industry (NETTI) Fund — $813M non-current + $9M current as of the most recent figure located (FY2023 10-K; not re-confirmed for FY2025 in this session, flagged for follow-up) — a real, if legacy and apparently stable, quasi-debt obligation. **Union exposure:** ~80% of the US workforce (out of ~460,000 employees total) is union-represented, primarily Teamsters plus separate pilot and mechanic unions; the current National Master Agreement runs through July 31, 2028, so there is no near-term full-contract renegotiation risk, but the buyout litigation above shows contract disputes can still arise mid-term. **Material weakness/restatement/going concern:** none identified in the sources reviewed. **Insider ownership:** negligible per the quant snapshot (0.02%) — this is a widely-held, dispersed-ownership company; no unusual Form 4 pattern was identified in this session, but a dedicated insider-trade pull was not completed due to a third-party data vendor rate limit during this session and should be done as a follow-up. **Short-seller reports:** none identified in this session's research.

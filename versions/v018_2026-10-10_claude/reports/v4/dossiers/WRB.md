# WRB — W. R. Berkley Corporation

**Agent:** F116 (wave 7 standard diligence). **As of:** 2026-09-25 close (WRB $66.82, mkt cap ~$26.0bn — stockanalysis.com, live retrieval 2026-09-27).

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — best-in-class specialty P&C underwriter compounding book value at a low-teens rate through the cycle, but the market is now pricing in materially less growth than the franchise has delivered, and specialty/E&S pricing is softening, which caps position size. Horizon: 24–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
W. R. Berkley is a Greenwich, CT-based commercial property-casualty insurance holding company operating through two segments — Insurance (specialty/E&S commercial lines sold via independent agents/brokers) and Reinsurance & Monoline Excess. It makes money two ways: underwriting profit (premiums minus claims and expenses) and investment income on the "float" (premiums held before claims are paid). Its edge is decentralized underwriting through ~60 small, specialized operating units that price niche risks (excess/surplus casualty, professional lines, workers' comp, marine, surety) better than generalist carriers.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Triage (Q06) flagged quality=4 but growth=2, noting "revenue growth has stalled to +1% and EPS is flat" as of the triage snapshot. That triage read is now stale: FY2025 net premiums written actually grew to $12.71bn (+6.2% vs FY2024's $11.97bn), and TTM diluted EPS growth is +10.9% (see §4). The durable quality driver is disciplined cycle underwriting (management has never chased top-line growth into a soft market) plus a growing, high-quality fixed-income book benefiting from higher reinvestment yields. This is a genuine, structural quality signal, not an artefact — the deceleration the triage saw was likely a single-quarter reading during a period when premium growth normally moderates late in a hardening-to-softening pricing cycle.

## 5. Guidance track record
W. R. Berkley, like most P&C insurers, gives **no numeric forward EPS or premium guidance** in its quarterly releases — confirmed by reading all four exhibit-99.1 releases for Q3 2025 (filed 2025-10-20), Q4/FY2025 (filed 2026-01-26), Q1 2026 (filed 2026-04-21) and Q2 2026 (filed 2026-07-20). Each release instead reports realized rate change: "Average rate increases excluding workers' compensation were approximately 7.6%" (Q3'25 release), "approximately 7.1%" (Q4'25 release), "approximately 7.2%" (Q1'26 release); the Q2'26 release does not repeat this metric explicitly in the excerpt reviewed. The rate-change deceleration from ~7.6% to ~7.1–7.2% is a real, quoted signal that specialty pricing is cooling, consistent with the triage's growth concern, even though premium volume and EPS have not yet decelerated.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:**
1. Reinvestment yields on the $2.6bn+ cash pile and the broader ~$30bn+ investment portfolio continue to exceed the portfolio's ~book yield (stated explicitly in the Q2'26 release), meaning net investment income keeps growing mechanically even if underwriting flattens.
2. Combined ratio has stayed in the high-80s/89–91% range for eight straight quarters despite a softening rate environment — underwriting discipline is holding, unlike weaker specialty peers.
3. At 2.5x book and ~14x operating EPS for an insurer compounding book value in the high teens ROE, the reverse DCF shows the market pricing conservative (~2.6%) growth — upside optionality if the specialty cycle re-hardens (e.g., a large cat year across the industry).

**Bear:**
1. Quoted average rate increases (ex-workers' comp) decelerated from ~7.6% (Q3'25) to ~7.1–7.2% (Q4'25, Q1'26) — a genuine, sourced sign the E&S/specialty hard market is turning, which historically pressures forward combined ratios with a lag.
2. TTM net income growth is being flattered partly by record net investment income ($418.7m in Q2'26, +10.4% YoY) rather than underwriting alone; if rates fall, that tailwind fades.
3. A large catastrophe year (the Q3'25 release already disclosed $78.5m of current-accident-year cat losses in that quarter alone) could push the combined ratio above 95% and compress ROE sharply, as WRB carries meaningful cat exposure in Insurance and Reinsurance & Monoline Excess.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Quoted average rate increase (ex-workers' comp) turns negative for two consecutive quarterly releases (currently +7.1–7.2%, down from +7.6%).
2. Reported combined ratio exceeds 95% for any single quarter (currently 89.0–90.9% range over the last 8 quarters).
3. Consolidated debt/equity rises above 0.40x (currently ~0.29x) without a stated strategic rationale (e.g., large M&A).
4. TTM net investment income growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters (currently +10–12% YoY).
5. Quarterly net premiums written growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters (currently positive every quarter shown above).

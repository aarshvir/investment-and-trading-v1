## 2. Business in plain English
Expeditors buys air, ocean and trucking capacity from carriers and resells it to shippers, and also clears goods through customs, manages orders and runs warehouses. It owns almost no ships or planes, so it needs very little capital: TTM capex was about $49M on $12.0B of revenue. It earns money on the spread between what it pays carriers and what it charges customers, plus fees for customs and logistics work. Its edge is a unified global network, a compensation system tied to branch profit, and a net-cash balance sheet that lets it buy back stock through downturns.

## 8. Bull and bear
**Bull:** (1) Customs brokerage, Transcon, distribution and order management all grew double-digit for two straight quarters, diversifying away from rate-driven air/ocean margins; (2) AI-hyperscaler freight demand: "AI demand seems insatiable" (8-K 0001193125-26-344693) and high growth "continuing into Q3 through July"; (3) net-cash balance sheet, ~$1B annual FCF, $3.0B buyback authorisation, plus ~$50M annual cost saving from the technology restructuring.
**Bear:** (1) Q2 gross revenue +32% but net revenue +21%: much of the uplift is carrier-rate pass-through tied to Middle East capacity loss that management expects to normalise; (2) ocean remains in a capacity glut (flat volumes in Q2; some Q2 volume was "pull-forward"), and IEEPA-related customs filing surge is "temporary" (release); (3) the price already embeds >14% growth; 27x TTM GAAP EPS against V1's delivered five-year growth figure of -0.9% (V1 historical_cagr_5y).

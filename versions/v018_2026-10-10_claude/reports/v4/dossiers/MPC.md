## 2. Business in plain English

Marathon Petroleum is the largest petroleum refiner in the US by throughput (~2.9m barrels/day crude capacity across Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent and West Coast refineries), selling gasoline, diesel and other refined products through its own Marathon-brand retail/wholesale network. It makes money on the "crack spread" — the difference between crude oil cost and refined product prices, which is set by global refining supply/demand, not by MPC. Layered on top, MPC owns the general partner and ~64% of the common units of **MPLX LP**, a separately-traded midstream MLP that gathers, processes, stores and transports crude/refined products/NGLs for fee-based income under longer contracts — a materially less cyclical business that MPC consolidates. Competitive position: scale, a geographically diversified refining footprint, and a captive midstream system that lowers its own logistics costs while also throwing off public-market-valued cash flow.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case

**Bull (3):**
1. **MPLX is real, evidenced, non-cyclical ballast**, not a hoped-for diversification story — its Adjusted EBITDA held inside a ~9% band through the worst refining quarter (Q1'26, $489M R&M EBITDA) and the best (Q2'26, $6.655B R&M EBITDA) in this dataset, and MPLX's own growth capex (Northwind, BANGL) is expanding that base further; management states MPLX's growing distribution is "expected to more than fund MPC's 2026 dividend and standalone capital."
2. **Execution and guidance discipline have been good** — no guidance cut identified across the four releases reviewed (§5), opex/bbl guidance has trended down (better) each quarter, and refinery utilization has run 94–95%.
3. **Capital return is large and structurally supported** — a fresh $8.6B buyback authorization (post the Q1'26 $5B increase) backed by MPLX's stable distribution stream, on top of a 54%-since-2019 reduction in share count.

**Bear (3):**
1. **The Q2 2026 print is a sharp, sudden, broad-based (all three regions) margin spike that coincides with the same Iran-conflict/Strait-of-Hormuz shock inflating CF's nitrogen margins** — buying both CF and MPC in the same portfolio is a *correlated* bet on the same transient geopolitical driver persisting, not two independent theses; if the conflict de-escalates, both would likely reprice together.
2. **The consensus FY2026 EPS ($55.73) that makes this stock look cheap on an NTM basis requires H2 2026 to out-run even Q2's blowout quarter** — this dossier's own primary-source math (H1 2026 actual EPS $19.46) suggests the "8x NTM P/E" headline may be resting on an estimate base that has not yet converged and could prove too high, in which case the multiple re-rates upward on flat or falling estimates rather than the price falling.
3. **Leverage has genuinely risen through the 2024–2025 trough** (net debt/EBITDA ~2.5x on this dossier's estimate, up from 0.7x in 2022) even before the current spike reverses, and capital return was cut hardest exactly when the stock was cheapest (2025) and is being re-accelerated exactly as it is most expensive on a normalized basis (2026) — the opposite of disciplined counter-cyclical buying.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (thesis-invalidation triggers)

Any of the following would invalidate the "MPLX ballast justifies a small position despite a cyclical spike" thesis:

1. **Refining margin reverts toward the $13–19/bbl 2024–2025 band within 1–2 quarters** (the base case per external nitrogen/energy-market commentary on the Iran conflict fading) — expected, and the trigger to re-underwrite the position size, not necessarily to exit.
2. **FY2026 actual EPS materially misses the $55.73 consensus** (e.g., prints below ~$35–40) — would confirm the estimate-chasing pattern flagged in §7 and likely trigger a valuation re-rating.
3. **Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated) exceeds ~3.5x on a normalized (non-spike) EBITDA base** — would elevate this from a cyclical-earnings question to a genuine balance-sheet question; verify the exact figure from the 10-K debt note before relying on this dossier's approximate leverage figures.
4. **MPLX distribution growth is cut or its own Adjusted EBITDA becomes meaningfully more volatile** — would remove the specific evidenced reason (§3, §8) this name is INCLUDE-SMALL rather than REJECT.
5. **A second consecutive quarter of guidance cuts** (opex/bbl rising or throughput guided down without a stated turnaround reason) — would break the "good execution" bull point.

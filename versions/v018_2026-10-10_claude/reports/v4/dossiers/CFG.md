# CFG — Citizens Financial Group, Inc. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F108, Wave 6)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (not INCLUDE at this price, with a condition that would change it). Horizon: would revisit within 2–3
quarters.
One-sentence reason: CFG's private-bank-led turnaround is real and accelerating (diluted EPS +41% YoY in Q2 2026,
NIM guided up further into 2027), but on my own arithmetic the stock's ~1.06x price-to-book already implies a
sustainable ROE (~10%) that the bank has not yet delivered (TTM ROE still ~9%, below its own systematic model's
10.1% implied level) — a case of a genuinely improving bank priced slightly ahead of where its numbers currently sit,
which per this program's discipline is WATCH, not INCLUDE, until the next 1–2 quarters confirm the ROE actually
clears that bar.

## 2. Business in plain English
Citizens Financial Group is a US super-regional bank (Providence, RI-headquartered, ~$234bn in assets) offering
retail and commercial banking, consumer lending, and — its key growth initiative — a build-out of private banking and
wealth management for high-net-worth clients, seeded by ~50–150 bankers hired out of the failed First Republic Bank
starting in 2023. It earns net interest income on loans/deposits and fee income from wealth/private-banking, capital
markets and card businesses. The private bank is explicitly a multi-year margin-improvement story: it was ~7% of 2025
earnings, ~10% of Q1 2026 pretax income, is producing a reported >25% ROE within the segment, and management's
medium-term target is for it to reach "the mid-teens" share of firm earnings.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Q06 triage: EPS growth accelerating (+42% forward), revenue +15%, but ROE of only 8.3% still below best-in-class
peers, and the model flagged the stock as already "excessive" after a +33% run — hence full diligence to see if the
private-bank investment is inflecting returns. The Q1/Q2 2026 results (below) show the inflection is genuinely
underway: diluted EPS accelerated from $1.13 (Q1-26) to $1.30 (Q2-26), NIM expanded another 3bp linked-quarter (10bp
cumulative in H1 2026) with management guiding **further** NIM expansion (322–327bp by 4Q26, 330–350bp by 4Q27). This
is durable, structural margin improvement (mix shift toward higher-return private banking, swap/non-core runoff
benefits), not a one-quarter trading beat — but the pace of ROE improvement (see §7) has not yet caught up to what the
current share price already assumes.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. NIM guidance is *rising*, not just stabilizing (322–327bp 4Q26 → 330–350bp 4Q27), a rare explicit forward multi-
   quarter margin-expansion guide from a regional bank management team.
2. The private bank is scaling with real, discrete metrics (>$16bn deposits, >$10bn wealth AUM, >25% segment ROE) and
   is still a small share of the firm (~10% of Q1 2026 pretax income) — plenty of runway if the mix shift continues.
3. Capital return (buybacks) is steady and CET1 is being managed toward a stated ~10.5% target rather than either
   hoarded or drawn down recklessly.

**Bear:**
1. On my own math the stock already prices in more ROE (~10.1%) than the bank is currently delivering (~9.0%
   run-rate) — if the NIM guide slips even modestly, the multiple has limited cushion.
2. Long-term debt is up ~45% in nine months (Sep-25 to Jun-26); the funding mix behind the private-bank build-out and
   balance-sheet growth was not fully diagnosed this pass and could carry a cost-of-funds risk if rates move against
   the bank.
3. CET1 at 10.4% is adequate but not a fortress buffer for a bank simultaneously growing its balance sheet, buying
   back stock, and funding a wealth-management build-out — any credit-cycle deterioration (e.g., CRE, consumer
   unsecured) would compete with capital-return plans.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Run-rate ROE (annualized quarterly net income ÷ average stockholders' equity) fails to reach 9.5% by Q4 2026 —
   i.e., the guided NIM path does not translate into the ROE convergence the current 1.06x P/B implies is coming.
2. CET1 ratio falls below 10.0% (below management's own stated near-term floor).
3. NIM in any quarter comes in below the low end of the company's own guided range (322bp for 4Q26; equivalent
   threshold for later quarters).
4. Private-bank deposits or wealth AUM growth stalls (flat or down quarter-over-quarter) for two consecutive quarters
   — a direct read that the core growth engine has stopped scaling.
5. Long-term debt/total assets ratio continues rising for two more consecutive quarters without a disclosed,
   understood funding rationale (currently unexplained in this pass — see §6).

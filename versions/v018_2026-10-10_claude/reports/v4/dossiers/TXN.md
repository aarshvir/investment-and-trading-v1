## 2. Business in plain English
TI designs and manufactures analog and embedded-processing semiconductors — the unglamorous chips that regulate
power, sense signals and control motors in nearly every electronic product — selling to a very broad base of
industrial, automotive, personal-electronics and communications-equipment customers with no single customer or
end-market dominant. It makes money on high (40–55%) gross margins from its own 300mm wafer fabs, which give it a
structural manufacturing-cost advantage over most analog peers who rely on older, smaller wafers or foundries
(source: TI FY2025 10-K, Item 1 Business, filed 2026-02-06).

## 3. Why the model likes it / is this durable
Q12_triage.json (Q12, as_of triage): quality 5/5, growth 4/5, price_vs_growth 3/5, "+23% revenue and +62% EPS growth
mid-recovery justify the 29x NTM P/E for the highest-quality analog moat in the batch; fairly priced rather than
cheap." This dossier confirms the growth reacceleration is real and broad-based (see §4) and durable in the sense
that it stems from an industry-wide inventory-correction unwind plus TI's own 300mm cost advantage — **not** a
one-off. However, the *rate* of the current inflection (FCF up 271% YoY per the company's own release, see §6) is
itself the residue of an unusually depressed 2023–25 capex/utilization trough and should not be extrapolated
linearly; this dossier's reverse DCF (§7) treats that distinction explicitly rather than taking the triage's "62%
EPS growth" at face value for a decade.

Reverse DCF (2-stage: 10-yr explicit constant growth + Gordon terminal; WACC 9.0%, terminal growth 3.5%): solving for
the FCF CAGR that reconciles today's EV to the DCF value is **highly sensitive to which FCF figure is used as the
starting base**, because TI is mid-inflection:
- Using **TTM FCF $6.534bn** (backward-looking, still partly weighed down by the higher-capex H2 2025 quarters):
  implied growth **≈13.4%/yr**.
- Using the **Q2 2026 quarterly FCF annualised** ($2.189bn × 4 = $8.756bn — forward-looking, but assumes the single
  best quarter on record repeats every quarter, which a cyclical semiconductor company should not be assumed to do):
  implied growth **≈9.6%/yr**.
- The mid-point of the two (~$7.6bn) implies **≈11.4%/yr**.
My own base case for a 10-yr FCF/share CAGR — blending mid-single-digit through-cycle revenue growth, further but
decelerating margin/FCF-conversion gains as capex normalizes, and a modest (currently subdued) buyback contribution
— is **≈9–10%/yr**. The implied range (9.6–13.4%, central estimate ≈11%) straddles this base case with **no
cushion either way** — **implied_vs_base = in_line** **[Corrected 2026-10-08: programme basis (Ke 10.18%, equity basis, CHIPS proceeds excluded, SBC deducted) implies 12.6-20% vs the 9-10% base: implied_vs_base is above; see Correction DV40]**, not the "below" that would be needed for a confident full-size
INCLUDE. This is a materially more cautious read than the triage's "fairly priced... quality premium is earned"

## 8. Bull case
1. Two consecutive quarters beating the high end of already-raised guidance, with broad-based growth (industrial,
   data centre, automotive) — genuine demand breadth, not one product cycle (Q2 2026 release, Ex.99).
2. Capex is now declining sharply off the 300mm-buildout peak while revenue accelerates, driving a real, durable
   operating-leverage and FCF inflection (TTM FCF +271% YoY).
3. Net debt/EBITDA ≈1.1x gives ample balance-sheet flexibility to keep funding the dividend and eventually resume
   larger buybacks once the capex cycle fully normalizes.

## 9. Bear case
1. China's Ministry of Commerce opened a formal anti-dumping investigation (announced week of 2025-09-13/2025-10-22)
   into U.S. analog interface and gate-driver chips (≥40nm), **naming Texas Instruments and Analog Devices
   specifically**; the statutory conclusion date is ~2026-09-13, extendable up to 6 months, i.e. **unresolved as of
   this dossier's 2026-09-25 cutoff**. TI's FY2025 10-K discloses revenue from customers headquartered in China at
   ~20% of revenue and revenue on products shipped into China at ~50% — a large, undiversified exposure to an
   adverse ruling (duties or share loss to subsidized domestic Chinese analog suppliers).
2. The reverse DCF's implied growth (9.6–13.4%) is at-or-above a defensible base case on every normalization tried;
   there is no valuation cushion if the current cyclical upswing decelerates.
3. Buybacks down 61% YoY show TI itself is being more conservative with capital return right now (prioritizing
   capex/dividend), which is prudent but removes a lever that has historically driven per-share growth.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. TI's own reported TTM free-cash-flow margin (FCF/revenue) falls back below 20% for two consecutive quarterly
   releases (signals the capex-down/FCF-up inflection reversing).
2. China's MOFCOM anti-dumping investigation concludes with duties or measures materially affecting TI's China unit
   economics (any published preliminary or final determination naming TI).
3. The next-quarter revenue guidance given at a release is **cut** (not raised or maintained) versus the immediately
   preceding quarter's guided range, at either of the next two earnings releases.
4. Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated) exceeds 2.0x.
5. Operating margin contracts more than 300bp YoY for two consecutive quarters (ex. any disclosed one-off item).

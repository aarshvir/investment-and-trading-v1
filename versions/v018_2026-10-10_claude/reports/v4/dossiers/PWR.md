# PWR — Quanta Services, Inc.

**1. Verdict: WATCH.** Largest pure-play electric/grid infrastructure contractor and the prime beneficiary of
utility, transmission and hyperscaler/data-center load growth — the business case is real and evidenced by a
record $53.4B backlog and a genuine, across-the-board FY26 guidance raise. But at ~39x FY26 guided adjusted EPS,
the price already implies sustained ~high-teens-to-20% growth for years, which is above a disciplined base case for
a cyclical, margin-thin EPC contractor. Horizon: 12–24 months (revisit if backlog conversion or margins disappoint).

**2. Business in plain English.** Quanta designs, builds and maintains electric-power transmission/distribution
lines, substations, renewable-energy interconnections and (increasingly) infrastructure for data-center power
demand, plus pipeline/communications infrastructure. It is a project-execution (EPC/specialty-contractor) business,
not an asset owner: revenue comes from labor/equipment-intensive contracts, so margins are structurally thin
(op. margin ~5–6%) and growth tracks utility capex cycles. Moat = scale/skilled-labor availability and long-standing
utility relationships that smaller contractors can't match on large, complex jobs.

**3. Why the model likes it (b1).** Composite decile 7/quintile 4 (top-tier of the live universe). Momentum
(0.90 pctile) and earnings-momentum (0.98 pctile) are exceptionally strong — the market is already rewarding the
backlog/guidance trajectory. Quality (0.23 pctile) is comparatively weak — consistent with EPC-model thin margins
and rising leverage (§6) — so the composite score here is a **momentum/growth call, not a quality call.**

**My view: a ~39x adjusted-earnings multiple for a project-execution contractor implies the market is pricing
several more years of high-teens-to-20%+ growth from backlog conversion — above what I'd underwrite as a base
case** (grid/data-center capex is a real multi-year tailwind, but EPC margins are cyclical and M&A-fuelled growth
is harder to sustain organically once large deals lap).

**8. Bull case:** (1) Record $53.4B backlog (vs $48.5B prior, per triage) gives multi-year revenue visibility into
the AI-data-center power buildout and grid-modernization supercycle. (2) FY26 guidance raised across revenue,
EBITDA, EPS and FCF simultaneously — a broad, not narrow, beat. (3) Quanta is the largest scaled pure-play in a
skilled-labor-constrained industry, a genuine competitive moat vs smaller regional contractors.
**Bear case:** (1) ~39x adjusted P/E already prices sustained double-digit growth for years; any backlog-conversion
slowdown or utility capex pause would compress the multiple sharply. (2) GAAP EPS (74x trailing P/E) is heavily
distorted by renewable-equity-method marks and M&A amortization — a genuine earnings-quality flag even after
adjusting. (3) Debt-funded M&A (four deals cited) has pushed consolidated net debt/EBITDA to ~2.0x from a lower base; a margin
miss on a large fixed-price project could hit leverage quickly given thin EPC margins.

**9. Kill criteria:** (1) Total backlog declines quarter-over-quarter for two consecutive quarters (company
discloses backlog each release). (2) FY guidance is cut (vs the $39.3–39.7B revenue / $16.45–16.95 adjusted EPS
range) at any subsequent release. (3) Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated, Yahoo/10-Q cross-checked) exceeds 2.5x.
(4) Adjusted operating margin compresses by >150 bps YoY for two consecutive quarters (fixed-price project
cost overrun signal). (5) Adjusted P/E stays above 35x while backlog growth decelerates below 10% YoY.

**10. Catalysts & calendar:** Next earnings ~2026-10-29 (d4).

**11. Red-flag scan.** No auditor change, material weakness, restatement or SEC/DOJ investigation identified in

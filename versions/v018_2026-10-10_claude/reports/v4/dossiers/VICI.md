# VICI Properties Inc. (VICI) — Diligence Dossier (F34, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 12–36 months. One-sentence reason: as of the 2026-09-25 close, an 8x-forward-AFFO, ~7.7%-yielding, investment-grade-adjacent triple-net gaming REIT with a clean four-release guidance-raise streak (2025–2026) is priced for AFFO/share to shrink forever, which its own results and pipeline contradict — but two tenants still supply ~70% of rent per the Q2 2026 10-Q, which is why this is a starter, not a full, position.

**Thesis (≤25 words):** Owns irreplaceable Las Vegas Strip and regional casino real estate on long, CPI-linked leases, priced far below what its own steady growth and diversification deals justify.

## 2. Business in plain English
VICI Properties is a triple-net-lease REIT that owns the real estate under casino-resorts (Caesars Palace, MGM Grand, The Venetian, Mandalay Bay and dozens of regional properties) and leases it back to operators on long-term, CPI-linked leases, plus a growing book of experiential and leisure assets (bowling via Bowlero, golf via Cabot, and now a first Club Med Caribbean resort). It makes money on the spread between its cost of capital (debt + equity) and the yield it earns funding new acquisitions and mortgage/financing-receivable deals, so growth comes from rent escalators plus accretive external deal-making, not from operating the casinos itself. Competitive position: the largest gaming-focused net-lease REIT, with essentially no direct competitor of similar scale in single-purpose casino real estate.

## 7. Valuation snapshot & reverse DCF
Price (2026-09-25 close) $23.51; NTM P/E ~8.0x (d4, calendarised); dividend yield ~7.7–7.8%; FY2026 P/AFFO on the guided midpoint ($2.46/sh) ≈9.6x. **No V1 row exists for VICI** (checked `v1_valuation_table.csv` — no row; VICI appears only as a peer-comparison sample inside FRT's V1 detail JSON, not as its own valuation) → v1_verdict = null; no systematic reconciliation is possible, so this dossier's valuation view is independently derived using a REIT-appropriate framework (AFFO yield / required-total-return), not a generic FCF DCF.

**Reverse DCF (Gordon-growth on AFFO/share):** Price = AFFO₁×(1+g)/(r−g). Using AFFO/share $2.46 (2026 guide midpoint) and a required total return r of 8.5–9.5% (a net-lease-REIT risk premium appropriate for VICI's leverage and two-tenant concentration — a pure CAPM beta-based WACC (β≈0.68) would imply an unrealistically low ~6–7% and is not used here because beta understates single-name lease-concentration risk), the price of $23.51 implies **perpetual AFFO/share growth of roughly −0.9% to −1.8%** across that discount-rate range — i.e. the market is pricing VICI as if AFFO per share will *shrink* indefinitely. My evidence-based base case is **+3–4%/yr** (embedded CPI-linked escalators, most capped at 2–3%/yr on the largest leases, plus the demonstrated pace of accretive third-party acquisitions — Golden Entertainment $1.16bn, Gamehost C$200.6M, Club Med $20.3M+$55.2M redevelopment — funded at a spread to VICI's cost of capital, consistent with the guided +3.4% for 2026 and the 4.6–5.7%/quarter AFFO/share growth actually delivered over the past two years). Implied growth is **clearly below** base case: the stock is priced more cheaply than a no-growth, in-perpetuity REIT. ## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Rent escalators (CPI-linked, several uncapped in early lease years) plus an active, accretive acquisition pipeline (Golden Entertainment, Gamehost, Club Med) funded at a spread to cost of capital keep compounding AFFO/share even with zero cap-rate compression; (2) management is visibly diversifying away from the Caesars/MGM duopoly — combined concentration fell from ~73% (Q1'26) to ~70% (Q2'26) in one quarter, and the Club Med and Gamehost deals open entirely new verticals (leisure resorts, Canadian gaming); (3) at ~9.6x forward AFFO and a 7.7–7.8% yield, VICI is near the cheapest it has traded relative to its own multi-year history for a business generating long-duration, largely investment-grade-adjacent contracted cash flows.
**Bear:** (1) Caesars + MGM still supply ~70% of contractual rent — an unusually concentrated exposure for a REIT this size; a material deterioration at either operator (regional gaming softness, a leveraged refinancing squeeze, new-supply competition) leaves VICI with limited ability to quickly re-lease single-purpose casino real estate; (2) higher-for-longer interest rates raise VICI's own refinancing cost on $17.2bn of debt faster than escalators grow rent, compressing the spread that funds accretive growth; (3) GAAP net income is highly volatile quarter to quarter (−39% YoY in Q2'26) on non-cash fair-value items in VICI's financing-receivable and preferred-equity book, which creates real headline/print-day risk (the Q2'26 print was reported as an "EPS miss" despite AFFO growth) even though the underlying cash-flow metric is steady.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
Any of the following would break the thesis; each is a specific, observable, time-bound signpost to monitor at each earnings release, not a vague worry:
1. Combined Caesars + MGM share of total contractual rent stops declining and rises back above 75% for two consecutive quarters (from ~70% currently), signalling the diversification trend has reversed.
2. AFFO/share guidance is cut (any reduction to the low end of the then-current full-year range) at a subsequent release — track record is currently four-for-four raises/holds.
3. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA rises above 5.5x (from ~4.9x currently) without a stated deleveraging plan.
4. A monetary default, lease restructuring, or sub-investment-grade credit-rating downgrade (below B1/B+) at either Caesars or MGM.
5. Realized rent growth trails CPI by more than 100bps for two consecutive years, i.e. the escalator caps bind and erode real rental income (VICI's own disclosed risk).

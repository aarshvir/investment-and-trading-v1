## Realistic future states for this exact stock-only target

The bands below are **conditional judgment ranges**, not calibrated expected returns, confidence intervals, or assigned probabilities. They are nominal USD portfolio total-return illustrations at roughly the 2 October entry level, **before investor-specific dividend withholding, capital-gains tax, FX and dealing costs**. Three-year percentages are annual compound rates; they are not three-year totals. Business and valuation assumptions can fail together. The scenarios retain the v014 weights; none assumes a Treasury or ETF allocation.

The **working middle case is 4–8% annualized over three years**, with the lower part more credible after the new labor report unless future operating cash and valuation evidence improve. A 12% central forecast is not supported: the existing after-withholding/cost flat-multiple illustrations for PG/PEP/PEG (15% of the portfolio) are only 3.84%/7.94%/9.43% annually. Their weighted contribution is about 1.05 percentage points; for the whole book to reach 12%, the other 85% would need roughly 12.9% annualized under comparable assumptions. Twelve final company dossiers still rely on earlier September research and cannot certify that growth. This arithmetic is a plausibility challenge, not a portfolio return estimate.

**What could be experienced along the way differs from the endpoint.** The v014 current-survivor historical replay reached a 44.08% GFC peak-to-trough loss, equivalent to about $22,040 of temporary loss on $50,000 or $44,080 on $100,000. Future losses can exceed that. A one-day, one-week or one-month return cannot credibly be forecast from these filings; earnings gaps and market shocks can dominate the business thesis. The previously stated 15–20% tolerable decline is not a dependable limit with 100% unhedged stocks.

Dividend withholding applies only to dividend cash, not to the whole return. The current basket's gross dividend yield was not re-audited, so an exact personal after-tax CAGR would be false precision. As an **illustration only**, if gross yield were 1.5% annually and 30% were withheld, the drag would be about 0.45 percentage point per year. A 25-basis-point purchase and sale cost removes about 0.5% cumulatively, or roughly 0.17 point per year over three years, before turnover and FX. Under those hypothetical assumptions, the 4–8% gross middle band is roughly **3.4–7.4%** after those two drags, but not after personal capital-gains tax. Inflation also reduces purchasing power: at a hypothetical 3% annual inflation rate, 4–8% nominal is roughly 1–5% real.

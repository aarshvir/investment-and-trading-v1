## Valuation convention and scenarios

The horizon is late September 2026 to late September 2029. The baseline is **normalized FY2026-equivalent annual earning power** derived from actual June 2026 results or, for AIZ, management's FY2026 direction. It is not reported FY2026 actual EPS and not NTM. Exactly three growth steps lead to FY2029-equivalent current-year terminal EPS; no extra FY2030 growth year is included.

Maximum entry = dividend in year 1 / (1+r) + dividend in year 2 / (1+r)^2 + (dividend in year 3 + terminal EPS × terminal P/E) / (1+r)^3. IRR solves the same cash flows at the reference purchase price. Annual dividends approximate quarterly payment timing. No probability-weighted expected return is claimed. Dividends are separate cash flows; **buybacks are already reflected in per-share EPS growth and are not added again as yield**. No customer cash, insurer investments, or purported excess capital is added to value.

## AMP — buy earning power, monitor flows and market exposure

H1 adjusted EPS was $22.33 on 93.7m diluted shares. Q2 wealth-management pretax earnings were $939m, asset management $274m, retirement/protection $202m, and corporate/closed blocks a loss of $81m. Wealth wrap net flows were $6.9bn, while asset management lost $6.5bn. [Q2 release and segment tables, 23 July](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820027/000082002726000038/q22026er.htm).

Normalize 2 × $22.33 by removing the annualized after-tax effect of Q1's **$25m pretax earnings benefit** from the Comerica termination: $44.24, rounded down to **$44**. The $28m gross revenue benefit in the filing is a different measure. Tax at 21% is a modeling assumption. [Q1 earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820027/000082002726000020/q12026er.htm), [June 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/820027/000082002726000043/amp-20260630.htm). The dividend baseline is $1.70 quarterly, or $6.80 annualized. [Dividend declaration](https://ir.ameriprise.com/news/news-details/2026/Ameriprise-Financial-Declares-Regular-Quarterly-Dividend-81877f055/default.aspx).

Base 7% EPS growth assumes roughly mid-single-digit business growth plus modest net share reduction, with part of that offset by markets/expenses; it does not perpetuate reported 22% quarterly EPS growth. A 13x terminal multiple permits a wealth-management premium while discounting legacy insurance and asset outflows. The reference price needs only about **4.76% annual EPS growth** at that multiple to earn the 12% hurdle.

Independent sum-of-parts crosscheck: annualize those four segment profits, tax at 21%, apply 14x/10x/8x/10x respectively, divide by 92.9m diluted shares. Result $568; haircut 10% for elevated market-linked earnings gives **$511**, close to the main model. Multiples and haircut are analyst assumptions, not observed transactions. Capital is retained in operating value; adding a headline liquidity figure would double count earning assets and potentially regulated capital.

Catalysts: retained advisor assets and improved organic flows; bank economics; actual earnings/share accretion from capital returns. Review failure conditions after each quarter: wealth net outflows for two quarters, underlying operating EPS below a $40 annual run rate without a transient explanation, or sustained deterioration in capital/remittance ability. An equity-market fall can damage both earnings and the multiple simultaneously; a smaller starter position is justified even with a BUY rating.

## HIG — normalize reserves, discontinued operations and investment income

Q2 core EPS was $3.42, with $111m favorable prior-year reserve development. LP income was $114m, a 7.6% annualized yield. Ex-AOCI book value was $78.91. Business underlying combined ratio worsened to 89.3 from 88.0; middle/large commercial underwriting needs attention. [Q2 release, consolidated results, underwriting and investment tables](https://ir.thehartford.com/news/news-details/2026/The-Hartford-Reports-Strong-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/).

**Do not add the originally reported Q1 $3.09 to Q2 $3.42 without adjustment:** Q1 included Hartford Funds, while the Q2 presentation excludes it from core earnings after discontinued-operation classification. [Q1 release](https://ir.thehartford.com/news/news-details/2026/The-Hartford-Reports-First-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results/default.aspx). The tax benefit from the pending sale is not repeat earning power.

Start with Q2 core EPS × 4 = $13.68. Remove all favorable reserve development and normalize LP yield to an assumed 5%: 4 × [$111m + $114m × (1 − 5/7.6)] × 79% / 276.32m shares = **$1.72 EPS**. Result **$11.96**, rounded to $12. This retains actual Q2 catastrophe costs, avoiding an ex-cat earnings fiction. It is a conservative run-rate anchor, not a full-year actuarial reserve estimate. Dividend baseline is $0.60 quarterly. [Declaration](https://newsroom.thehartford.com/newsroom-home/news-releases/news-release-details/2026/The-Hartford-Declares-Quarterly-Dividends-Of-0-60-Per-Share-Of-Common-Stock-And-375-Per-Share-Of-Series-G-Preferred-Stock-87687251d/default.aspx).

The base assumes 6% EPS growth from modest insurance growth and share reduction, and an 11.5x terminal multiple. A residual-income crosscheck using sustainable ROE 16%, cost of equity 11%, and long-run growth 4% gives P/B = (16−4)/(11−4) = 1.71x, or **$135** on ex-AOCI book. That stylized result supports value but does not override the stricter cash-flow entry limit.

Stress: an additional $500m pretax reserve/cat charge costs approximately **$1.43 EPS** at 21% tax and the same diluted denominator, before any rating/capital consequences. This is a stress assumption, not a claim such losses are expected. Catalysts are proof of stable accident-year margins, realization of sale proceeds without counting the Funds earnings twice, and actual repurchases. Reduce/reassess if commercial underlying combined ratio exceeds 95 for two quarters, adverse casualty development persists, or normalized ROE drops below 13%. The $120 entry avoids paying for uninterrupted favorable reserve releases.

Thesis failures: non-cat Housing economics reverse for two quarters, a major distribution loss, or capital/remittances become constrained. $235 is the preferred price, not an instruction to chase a mild pullback.

## IBKR — growth is real; the price demands more

Q2 adjusted EPS was $0.69; reported net interest income was $1.057bn and adjusted pretax profit about $1.44bn. Use the income-statement NII definition; the $1.100bn management margin table includes interest-like items in other revenue. Customer assets are not shareholder surplus. Normalize the quarter's 14.7% tax rate to an assumed 19%: $0.69 × 4 × 81/85.3 = $2.62, rounded to **$2.60**. This proportional tax bridge is approximate because of the public-company/operating-partnership structure; reported EPS is the starting point so noncontrolling interests are not accidentally assigned to public shareholders. The tax and interest sensitivity discussion is also available in the [earnings-call transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/07/21/interactive-brokers-ibkr-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/?referring_guid=89ef2ba2-b035-4492-be80-675868705e31&source=financialcontent).

August metrics showed 5.460m accounts and $962.8bn client equity, with DARTs 4.276m; the account franchise kept expanding while activity was below Q2's pace. [Issuer August report](https://www.interactivebrokers.com/mkt/getFileNew.php?file=202608MetricsPressRelease.pdf). The issuer PDF was located through its monthly-metrics page; subsequent retrieval was inconsistent, so this is a disclosed-source check rather than a durable local PDF archive.

Using the disclosed $81m US and $38m non-US annual NII sensitivity per +25bp, a symmetric −100bp approximation across both is **−$476m pretax**, roughly −$0.21 EPS under proportional attribution. This is a first-order stress, not a forecast: balances, rate floors and downward asymmetry matter. Current growth can absorb a rate shock, but it does not eliminate multiple risk.

Base EPS growth of 15% is substantial yet below recent account growth; a 25x terminal P/E still pays a premium for scale and automation. At $89.82, the model requires **25.44% annual EPS growth** at 25x to earn a 13% hurdle. That requirement exceeds even our 23% bull growth assumption, whose favorable return also needs a 32x multiple. Catalysts: account monetization, new distribution, sustained funded balances. Thesis failure: account growth persistently below 15%, meaningful customer credit/operational losses, or NII and commissions falling together. A high-quality business can remain unsuitable at its current valuation.

## What has and has not been established

This is a targeted, source-linked prospective underwriting model. It replaces unsourced forward-EPS shortcuts with explicit normalization and thresholds. It is not a certification of the original backtest, a full reserve-triangle audit, or a guarantee of a portfolio drawdown ceiling. The financial statements are unaudited quarterly reports; completeness of regulatory-capital remittances and independent actuarial reserve adequacy remain beyond this review's completed scope. These limits justify position sizing and price discipline, not indefinite refusal to make a decision.

The 30% dividend-withholding column is a sensitivity only, not a determination of tax residence or treaty status. Capital-gains tax, FX, dealing costs and dividend reinvestment are excluded. Revalue after each earnings release; review price versus the fixed entry limit weekly. A lower stock price without unchanged earning power is not automatically a better purchase.

Exact cash flows, formulas, normalization inputs, source URLs and price conflicts are retained in `financials.json`; `build_financials.py` reproduces the arithmetic. No prior audit artifacts or original market data were overwritten.

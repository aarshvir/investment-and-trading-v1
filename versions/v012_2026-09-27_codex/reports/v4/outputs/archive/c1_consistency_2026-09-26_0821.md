## 1. Mismatch

### §5 — "Model top quintile (~100 names)"
- **Source:** `outputs/b1_results.json` → `strategies.primary_topquintile_ew.avg_names` = **85.386364**. `outputs/b1_report.md` states "85" for this strategy in every one of its own tables (lines 23, 186, 226, etc.) — this is not a one-off rounding artifact, B1 itself always calls it 85.
- **Why it's ~85, not ~100:** the point-in-time S&P 500 universe with valid scores/prices averages only ~433 members over 2012–2026 (coverage gaps), not the full 500/503, so a "quintile" of it is ~85–87 names, not ~100.
- **Fix:** *"Model top quintile (~85 names)"*.

## 2. Overstatement (wording stronger than the data)

### §1 — "exactly two of the combinations shown clear 90% odds of a gain"
- The sentence names Moderate/10yr (94%) and Cautious/5yr (94%) as the two, then lists three combinations "below 90%" (Moderate/5yr 87%, Index/5yr 80%, Index/10yr 88%), and points the reader to §7 for "bear-to-bull ranges."
- **Source:** `outputs/lead_risk_results.json` → `sims[]`, mix = "Cautious (35/10/55)", scenario = "Base", horizon = "10y" → `p_total_return_gt_0` = **0.99 (99%)**. This is the same §7 table the sentence cites, and it also clears 90% — more comfortably than either of the two combinations actually named. Growth (80/20/0) does not clear 90% at either horizon, so it isn't the issue; Cautious/10yr is.
- **Effect:** at least three of the eight model×horizon cells in §7 clear 90%, not exactly two, and the report never mentions the third (Cautious/10yr) or explains its exclusion.
- **Fix:** *"...three of the combinations shown clear 90% odds of a gain: the Moderate mix held 10 years (94%), and the Cautious mix held either 5 years (94%) or 10 years (99%)."*

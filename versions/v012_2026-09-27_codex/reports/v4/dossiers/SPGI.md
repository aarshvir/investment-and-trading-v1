## 2. Business in plain English
S&P Global sells things almost every large financial transaction needs: credit ratings on bonds (Ratings), the S&P 500/Dow Jones/other benchmark indices licensed to trillions of dollars of ETFs and derivatives (Indices), data/analytics/workflow tools for investment banks and asset managers (Market Intelligence), and commodity price benchmarks/analytics for oil, gas and shipping (Energy — now including "supply chain" products moved in from Market Intelligence). It earns money mostly on volume (bond issuance, AUM tracking its indices, subscriptions) rather than on assets it owns, so the businesses are extremely high-margin and capital-light. Competitive position: Ratings is effectively a duopoly with Moody's; Indices and Market Intelligence face more competition (data aggregators, in-house analytics, AI-native entrants) but have high switching costs.

## 7. Bull case
1. Ratings and Indices, 68–70% segment margins, are structurally protected (duopoly regulatory licensing; index-license switching costs) and both delivered record results in Q2-2026.
2. FY26 guided buyback of >$7bn (on a $119bn market cap) plus a 53-year dividend-growth streak funds EPS growth even in flat-issuance years.
3. The Mobility spin-off sharpens focus on four higher-margin core divisions and, per the debt note, is EPS- and leverage-accretive to continuing operations once the stub-period noise clears.

## 8. Bear case
1. FY2026 is the first year of standalone continuing-operations reporting; guidance was rebased mid-year and management itself flags the new range as not comparable to the old one — a full year of clean comparisons does not yet exist.
2. Market Intelligence (23% margin, the lowest of the four segments) faces real competitive and AI-disintermediation risk that could compress blended margin if it doesn't re-accelerate.
3. Ratings revenue is tied to bond issuance volumes, which are cyclical; a rates/credit-market downturn would hit the highest-margin segment hardest.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Two consecutive quarters of continuing-operations (ex-Mobility) organic constant-currency revenue growth below 4% (below the low end of the FY26 6.0–8.0% guided range by more than 200bps).
2. Ratings segment operating margin falls below 60% for two consecutive quarters (vs 68% in Q2-2026).
3. Continuing-operations net debt/EBITDA (once cleanly reported ex-Mobility) exceeds 3.0x — there is no clean continuing-ops EBITDA yet; this must be recomputed at Q3-2026 reporting.
4. Full-year 2026 adjusted diluted EPS prints below the low end of the current $17.50–$17.75 guide (a real, not basis-change, cut).
5. Any SEC/DOJ inquiry into ratings-conflicts-of-interest or antitrust action targeting the Ratings or Indices duopoly.

# Honeywell Aerospace Inc. (NASDAQ: HONA) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F67)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — a genuinely high-quality aerospace aftermarket franchise (Electronic Solutions, Engines and Power Systems, Control Systems), but it was spun off from Honeywell International on **2026-06-29 carrying $16.0 billion of new senior notes that it did not have as a segment**, most of whose proceeds were paid to the parent rather than retained for growth. In its **first quarterly release as a public company (Q2-26, 2026-08-05) it already cut full-year organic-growth and adjusted-EBIT guidance** on supply-chain constraints. This overturns the triage's "cheap at 18.8x, one of the highest-quality aerospace aftermarket franchises" call, which was built on general knowledge, not the spin-off debt structure or the guidance cut. Thesis horizon: revisit after 2–3 quarters of standalone reporting.

## 2. Business in plain English
Honeywell Aerospace makes avionics, auxiliary power units, jet engines, and cockpit/control electronics for commercial and defense/space aircraft, sold both as original equipment and — the more profitable, stickier part — as spare parts and repair/overhaul services over the life of the aircraft (Commercial Aftermarket was ~45% of Q2-26 sales). It was separated from legacy Honeywell International (now "Honeywell Technologies," NYSE/Nasdaq: HON) as the second leg of Honeywell's three-way breakup, completing 2026-06-29.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1_live_scores shows HONA with **"insufficient_families_no_composite"** — i.e., the quant model could not score it (no price/return history, no comparable-period fundamentals) and it entered triage purely on Q08's general knowledge of the Honeywell breakup, not on verified data. The "18.8x NTM P/E, cheaper than GE/HWM/TDG" claim is **not independently verified in this diligence** (peer multiples were not re-derived) and, more importantly, **omits the new $16bn debt load and its ~$800m/year of interest expense**, which did not exist when those "cheap" comparisons were being made informally. The underlying aftermarket franchise quality is real (record backlog language is absent here, but segment mix and margins are aerospace-typical), but the "quality" read is **not durable evidence of mispricing** — it is an unscored name with a materially changed capital structure.

## 5. Guidance track record
HONA has issued **exactly one** full-year guidance update as a public company — there is no 4-release track record to assess. At its Q2-26 release (2026-08-05, Ex-99.1), it **cut** the guidance it had given at its June 3, 2026 Investor Day:

| Metric | Previous guidance (Investor Day, June 2026) | Current guidance (2026-08-05) |
|---|---|---|
| Organic sales growth (FY26) | 7–9% | **4–5%** |
| Pro forma standalone adjusted EBIT (FY26) | $4.65–$4.75bn | **$4.35–$4.45bn** |
| YoY adjusted-EBIT growth | 7–10% | **Flat to 3%** |
| Pro forma standalone adjusted EPS (FY26) | not previously given | **$7.60–$7.90 (new)** |
| 2H26 free cash flow | $1.0–$1.5bn | $1.0–$1.5bn (unchanged) |

Reason stated by the company: "it is prudent to align our guidance to our supply chain's demonstrated capabilities at the end of the second quarter" (CEO Jim Currier, Ex-99.1). This is a **real, primary-sourced guidance cut in the company's very first quarter as a standalone reporter** — a meaningfully worse signal than the triage's "cheap" framing implied.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (combined/carve-out basis, labelled)
- **Debt (new, entity-specific to HONA, not legacy Honeywell consolidated):** On **2026-03-16**, in connection with the spin-off, HONA issued **$16.0 billion** face amount of senior unsecured notes in nine series (coupons 3.90%–5.852%, maturities 2028–2066), initially guaranteed by Honeywell and automatically released from that guarantee upon completion of the spin-off. Per the 10-Q (Note 9, Debt and Credit Agreements): total long-term debt (book, net of $153m issuance costs) **$15,849m** at 2026-06-27, versus **$4m** at 2025-12-31 (i.e., essentially zero before the spin). **This is a fresh, entity-level liability of HONA, not a consolidated-Honeywell figure carried over by mistake.**
- **Where the debt proceeds went:** per the 10-Q cash-flow statement (H1-2026), "Proceeds from issuance of long-term debt $15,843m" was largely offset by "**Net transfers to Parent ($15,087m)**" — i.e., HONA borrowed ~$15.8bn and paid ~$15.1bn of it to Honeywell as partial consideration for the assets Honeywell contributed to the spin-off (10-Q Note 9: "$6.0 billion" of notes plus "$9.1 billion of cash proceeds" were distributed to Honeywell). **HONA shareholders received the aerospace business already carrying this debt; the cash did not stay inside the company.**
- **Cash:** $1,057m at 2026-06-27 vs $213m at 2025-12-31 (10-Q balance sheet). **Net debt ≈ $14.9–15.0bn** (using $15,849–15,853m gross debt less $1,057m cash).
- **Leverage:** using the company's own FY26 pro forma standalone adjusted EBIT guide (midpoint $4.4bn) and estimated D&A (H1-26 depreciation $145m + amortization $88m = $233m, annualized ≈$466m) for a rough EBITDA of **~$4.9bn**, gross debt/EBITDA ≈ **3.3x**, net debt/EBITDA ≈ **3.1x** — materially levered for an industrial spin-off, and a genuinely new risk that did not exist under the Honeywell parent (single-A-rated) balance sheet. This figure is an estimate (no rating-agency leverage figure was located in the documents reviewed) and should be treated as approximate.
- **Operating cash flow deteriorated sharply:** H1-2026 net cash from operating activities **$346m vs $1,025m** in H1-2025 (10-Q cash-flow statement) — a **−66% decline**, driven by the net-income drop (above) plus working-capital consumption (accounts receivable −$365m, inventories −$165m in H1-26, consistent with the supply-constrained-output narrative behind the guidance cut). Capex $260m H1-26 vs $234m H1-25. **H1-2026 free cash flow ≈ $86m** (vs ≈$791m in H1-2025) — this quarter's FCF is not representative of steady state given the transaction-cost and working-capital distortions, and should not be annualized naively.
- **Equity:** the combined/carve-out statements show "Total Equity (Deficit)" was $9,682–9,978m in the 2025 predecessor periods; a comparable post-spin consolidated equity figure was not separately isolated within the time box — flagged as an open item.
- **No litigation-specific asbestos/environmental legacy liability was identified as retained by HONA** in the sections reviewed (Item 1 Legal Proceedings references Note 15, Commitments and Contingencies, which shows environmental expense of $37m for H1-2026 but no asbestos-specific line); this is not a confirmed absence, only an absence of what this review found — flagged as a limitation given Honeywell's known legacy environmental exposure (Bendix/asbestos) historically sat with the parent's other businesses.

EV (via valuation.py EV bridge: market cap + $16.0bn debt − $1.057bn cash) ≈ **$65.3bn**. **V1 systematic valuation:** HONA has no row in `v1_valuation_table.csv` or `v1_valuation.json` (checked directly for an exact "HONA" key; not found — it is also far too new to be in any point-in-time universe) — **v1_verdict = null**.

**Reverse DCF (this analyst, `valuation.py`, 2-stage FCFF, WACC 8.5%, terminal growth 3%, 10-year stage-1, base FCF = an estimated normalized $2.5bn/year — since actual FY26 FCF is transaction-cost- and working-capital-distorted, this is a judgment estimate, not a reported figure):** implied FCF growth to justify the $65.3bn EV is **+7.3%/year for 10 years**. Given a freshly cut guide of 4–5% organic growth, flat-to-3% EBIT growth, and $800m/year of new interest expense to service before any FCF reaches equity holders, a defensible base case is **mid-single-digit** FCF growth (roughly in line with, not above, the current cut guidance), which is **below** the growth priced in. **Implied growth is above the base case.** Analyst view: **full to expensive** — the opposite of the triage's "cheap" call.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) Commercial Aftermarket (the highest-margin, stickiest revenue line) grew 8% YoY in Q2-26 even as OE and Defense/Space growth was muted by supply constraints — once supply normalizes, growth could re-accelerate toward the original 7–9% guide; (2) management has laid out 2030 targets and an "Aerospace Operating System" framework at its June 2026 Investor Day, signalling a credible standalone strategy; (3) as a pure-play the stock may re-rate versus a discount-for-complexity inside diversified Honeywell.

**Bear:** (1) $16.0bn of new debt (~3.1x net debt/EBITDA on an estimated basis) is a permanent claim on cash flow that funded a payment to the parent, not growth capital for HONA itself — this is the central, primary-source-confirmed risk the triage missed entirely; (2) guidance was already cut in the first standalone quarter, on supply-chain constraints that are not obviously short-lived; (3) H1-2026 operating cash flow fell 66% YoY and free cash flow was near zero, and the 2H-2026 FCF guide ($1.0–1.5bn) still implies the company must roughly double its H1 cash generation to hit even the low end.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. **A second cut to FY2026 organic growth or adjusted-EBIT guidance** at the next (Q3-2026) release.
2. **Net debt/EBITDA (estimated) rises above ~3.75x**, or a credit-rating downgrade is disclosed (no rating was located in the documents reviewed).
3. **2H-2026 free cash flow prints below the low end of the $1.0–1.5bn guide.**
4. **Engines and Power Systems segment adjusted EBIT remains negative YoY (currently −32%) for two consecutive quarters** — the segment most exposed to the supply-constraint narrative.
5. **Interest and other financial charges exceed $220m/quarter on a sustained basis** (vs $200m in Q2-26), signalling refinancing or rate pressure on the new debt stack.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **Extremely limited standalone history:** one 10-Q, spin-off completed 2026-06-29, first earnings release 2026-08-05 — confirmed directly from SEC filing history (10-12B/A registration statement effective 2026-07-13; DRS first filed 2025-10-01).
- **Leveraged spin-off structure:** see §6 — $16.0bn of debt issued substantially to fund a distribution to the parent, not retained by the company. This is the dossier's central adverse fact and is fully filing-sourced (10-Q Note 9 and the cash-flow statement).
- **Guidance cut in the first quarter as a public company** — see §5.
- **No auditor-change, restatement, or going-concern language found** in the sections reviewed.
- **Insider selling pattern:** not reviewed in depth; the SEC submissions feed shows 34 Form 4s and 22 Form 3s since 2025-10-01, consistent with a mass initial-ownership filing at spin-off rather than a distinct selling pattern, but individual transactions were not classified — disclosed as a limitation.

## 12. Data conflicts
Triage (Q08_triage.json) called HONA "trading cheaper (18.8x NTM) than peers like GE, HWM or TDG" and "one of the highest-quality aerospace aftermarket franchises," sourced to "general knowledge" and "Honeywell three-way breakup plan disclosures (2025)" — i.e., not the spin-off's actual capital structure or its first earnings release. This diligence's primary-source findings (a) the $16.0bn debt issuance funding a distribution to the parent and (b) the first-quarter guidance cut are **not reflected in the triage** and materially change the call from advance-as-cheap to **WATCH**.

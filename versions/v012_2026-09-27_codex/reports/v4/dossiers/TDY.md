# Teledyne Technologies Inc. (TDY) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — 12–36 month thesis horizon.
One-sentence reason: a disciplined serial acquirer of niche, high-switching-cost instrumentation/sensor/defense-imaging businesses is compounding EPS at ~20%+ with very low leverage, and the reverse-DCF implied growth (≈6.6%) sits below a reasonable base case (≈9%) — genuinely attractively priced — but the freshly announced, still-unclosed $1.1B Varex Imaging acquisition is a specific, named integration/financing/regulatory-approval reservation that keeps this at half weight rather than full.

## 1. Business in plain English
Teledyne is a diversified industrial technology holding company built almost entirely by acquisition: it makes scientific/test instruments, marine and environmental sensors, industrial digital-imaging and machine-vision systems, and — via the 2021 FLIR acquisition — infrared cameras, thermal-imaging sensors and counter-UAS/defense electronics. Customers are government/defense agencies, industrial OEMs, and scientific/research labs; revenue is a mix of hardware sales and recurring aftermarket/service. The moat is a portfolio of #1-or-#2 niche franchises with high switching costs, protected by Teledyne's long, low-multiple M&A discipline (Chairman Robert Mehrabian's playbook, following founder Henry Singleton's capital-allocation tradition) rather than any single product's uniqueness.

b1 composite percentile 0.31 (decile 4, quintile 2 — middling, not a top-tier quant score), driven mainly by Value (fam_V raw 0.05, ep/fcfp percentiles ~0.35–0.40) with a high momentum percentile (mom_12_1 pct 0.51) and modest leverage percentile (0.32, i.e., low actual leverage). Quality percentile is only 0.27 (gp_a pct 0.27, roe pct 0.11) — the raw ROE (8.9%) looks unexciting because Teledyne carries $8.66B of goodwill from decades of M&A on a $15.3B asset base, depressing return-on-equity optics even though underlying segment economics are strong. The durability case rests on funded defense/Digital Imaging backlog and repeat bolt-on M&A, both checked below; this is not a story that depends on the quant score being right.

## 4. Guidance track record
Teledyne guides on non-GAAP EPS and revenue. Per the Q2'26 earnings release (8-K, accession 0001094285-26-000047): full-year 2026 revenue outlook was raised by $120M to over $6.53B, and non-GAAP EPS guidance was raised to $24.45–$24.65 (midpoint $24.55), up $0.55 at the midpoint from the prior range given after Q1'26. This is the second consecutive raise this fiscal year (Q1'26 guide already exceeded the original FY2026 outlook given at the start of the year) — a consistent raise-and-beat pattern over the last four releases, not a one-quarter beat.

## 7. Bull case
1. Funded backlog ~$5.0B with orders exceeding sales for 11 consecutive quarters — real, filed demand visibility, not a one-quarter pop.
2. Op margin expanded 160bp YoY in the most recent quarter on genuine mix/scale, not one-time items, and management has raised guidance twice this fiscal year.
3. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA ~1.1–1.2x gives ample balance-sheet capacity to keep executing the accretive bolt-on M&A strategy that has compounded EPS for two decades.

## 8. Bear case
1. The $1.1B Varex Imaging acquisition (announced 2026-08-10, cash deal, expected to close early 2027) is unclosed: regulatory approval, financing terms and integration execution are all still open questions as of this dossier.
2. Digital Imaging/defense revenue is meaningfully exposed to U.S. government budget cycles (continuing resolutions, program timing) — a real, if not currently triggered, risk.
3. ROE (8.9%) and quality percentile (0.27) are unexciting on a GAAP basis because of the large accumulated goodwill base; if a future acquisition underperforms, an impairment is a real risk given how M&A-dependent the balance sheet is.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Digital Imaging segment organic revenue growth falls below 5% YoY for two consecutive quarters.
2. Funded backlog book-to-bill ratio drops below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters (it has been >1.0x for 11 straight quarters as of Q2'26).
3. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.5x post-Varex-close without a disclosed deleveraging plan within 18 months.
4. The Varex acquisition is blocked by regulators, terminated, or produces adjusted-EPS dilution beyond what management guides at signing.
5. A goodwill impairment charge is recorded against any acquired business (goodwill is 57% of consolidated assets).

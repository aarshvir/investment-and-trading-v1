# EME — EMCOR Group, Inc.

**1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 12–24 months. Exceptional current execution (three consecutive full-year guidance raises, record backlog, 40% ROE, net cash) but the growth driver is a concentrated, cyclical, project-based capex wave (AI/data-center construction), sell-side coverage is thin (7 analysts), and the stock has already re-rated to near the top of its own 10-year multiple range — the reservation is durability of the driver past the next 2–3 years, not current quality.

**2. Business in plain English.** EMCOR is the largest U.S. mechanical and electrical construction and building-services contractor: it designs, installs and maintains the electrical, HVAC, plumbing and low-voltage systems inside data centers, hospitals, manufacturing plants, and commercial/institutional buildings, and separately runs industrial/facilities-services businesses. It earns fixed-price and cost-plus construction margins plus recurring service/maintenance revenue, with almost no owned real estate or heavy equipment (asset-light). Competitive position: #1 by revenue in U.S. specialty construction, currently the prime beneficiary of hyperscaler/AI data-center buildout because very few contractors are pre-qualified and staffed to execute at this scale and speed.

**3. Why the model likes it.** b1 percentiles: earnings momentum (SUE) 89th percentile, momentum 54th, quality mixed (leverage percentile not computable — net cash, no meaningful debt; ROE not scored in this snapshot but is 40.3% per Yahoo), value modest (ep 61st, book/price 37th — the stock is not statistically "cheap"). The triage read (revenue +19.8%, ROE 40%, net cash, 21.3x NTM P/E "underappreciated" vs FIX/PWR at 29–34x) is directionally right on quality but the valuation gap versus higher-multiple peers narrowed materially between the 2026-08-18 triage snapshot and today: EME now trades at 20.7x forward P/E (see §7), closer to, not "underappreciated" relative to, that peer set.

**6. Earnings quality & balance sheet.** Per the finance-skills infrastructure/capital-goods playbook, U.S. E&C names should be read on Net Service Revenue-equivalent margin, cash conversion, and backlog quality rather than headline OPM alone; full pass-through/NSR disclosure was not available in the sources reviewed here, so margin figures above are gross (a stated limitation). **Cash conversion needs a caveat:** operating cash flow was $1,302mm in FY2025, down from $1,408mm in FY2024 despite EPS growth, and Q1 2026 OCF was essentially zero ($0.558mm) before rebounding to a $289.9mm H1-2026 cumulative figure by Q2 — consistent with working-capital consumption (unbilled receivables/contract assets building ahead of billing) as the backlog ramps, which is normal for a *growing* contractor per the sector playbook but should be watched: unbilled revenue growing materially faster than revenue for multiple quarters is the sector's standard early-warning sign and was not independently verified here (data limitation — would require the segment/contract-asset footnote, not pulled in this pass). Balance sheet is a clear strength: total debt just $548mm vs $924mm cash (net cash), FY2025 buybacks $586mm and dividend yield only 0.21% (buybacks, not dividends, are the primary capital-return vehicle). No debt-funded acquisition or securitization risk comparable to DPZ's.

Own-history context: EME traded in the mid-teens P/E (14–18x) through 2019–2023; the move to 20–24x since the AI data-center buildout began represents a genuine re-rating, now sitting near the top of its 10-year range. A simple reverse Gordon-growth back-out at 20.7x forward P/E and ~9% cost of equity (beta 1.13) implies roughly 4% perpetual growth priced in — but a single-stage perpetuity model understates the right framework here: the correct read is a two-stage model with several years of backlog-driven double-digit growth fading to a GDP-like terminal rate, which a 4%-equivalent back-out is arguably consistent with, not obviously excessive. Given the genuine uncertainty in normalizing a mid-cycle project-construction growth rate, this dossier's view is **"fair"** (not cheap, not obviously expensive) and `implied_vs_base` = **in_line**.

**8. Bull case / Bear case.**
*Bull:* (1) RPO backlog (+32.9% YoY) provides 2–3 years of revenue visibility even without further order intake; (2) EMCOR is one of a handful of contractors with the scale and pre-qualification to win the largest hyperscaler jobs, a structural (if narrow) moat; (3) net-cash balance sheet gives capacity for further tuck-in M&A or buybacks without dilution.
*Bull risk flagged as reservation, not dismissed:* the same concentration that drives the upside — AI/data-center capex — is the single largest identifiable risk if hyperscaler capex plans are cut or delayed.
*Bear:* (1) Data-center capex is famously lumpy; a pause or reallocation by 2–3 large customers would hit a business now guiding to $20bn+ revenue built partly on that vertical; (2) labor availability and wage inflation are called out by management as ongoing constraints even amid the guidance raises; (3) working-capital consumption (Q1'26 near-zero OCF) could compound if the backlog conversion cycle lengthens.

**9. Kill criteria (measurable) -- what would break the thesis.**
1. RPO/backlog growth turns negative YoY for two consecutive quarters (from +32.9% YoY as of 2026-03-31).
2. Full-year revenue or EPS guidance is cut, breaking the current three-release raise streak.
3. Consolidated operating margin falls below 8.5% for two consecutive quarters (vs 8.7–12.7% range over the last 8 quarters).
4. Net cash position flips to net debt exceeding 1.0x EBITDA.
5. Cumulative operating cash flow over any trailing 4 quarters falls below 50% of cumulative EBITDA over the same period (working-capital/cash-conversion red flag per the sector playbook).

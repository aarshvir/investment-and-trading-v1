# Jabil Inc. (NYSE: JBL) — Diligence Dossier

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (12–36 month horizon), reservation: the model's growth read is real and guidance has been raised in back-to-back quarters, but core EMS operating margins remain thin (GAAP operating margin 5.1% in FQ3 FY26) and the balance sheet has taken on incremental debt and a doubling of goodwill/intangibles in nine months to fund M&A (Hanley Energy Group and others), while equity has been shrunk to near-parity with liabilities by aggressive buybacks — a structurally low-equity-cushion balance sheet even though liquidity and cash generation are currently fine.

## 2. Business in plain English
Jabil is an electronics manufacturing services (EMS) company: it builds and assembles electronics — from data-center power/cooling gear to automotive electronics to healthcare devices — under contract for other companies' brands, and is increasingly selling its own higher-value power, thermal and networking infrastructure content into AI data centers (via its Intelligent Infrastructure segment and the 2025/26 Hanley Energy Group acquisition).

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Triage cited "11.8% revenue growth and 111% EPS growth from AI-related mix shift" at 18.8x NTM P/E with 36% Street upside, flagging "thin core EMS margins (2.6%)... high ROE partly reflects a small equity base from buybacks rather than pure business quality." The revenue and EPS growth are corroborated by the primary filings and have **accelerated**, not decelerated, over the last two quarters (see §4) — durable in the sense that management is raising guidance on it every quarter, not a one-off beat. However, the triage's own caution about a shrunken equity base is confirmed and, if anything, understated: total Jabil Inc. consolidated stockholders' equity fell from $1,513M (Aug-25) to $1,323M (May-26) even as total consolidated assets grew from $18.5B to $23.8B, i.e. consolidated leverage (assets/equity) rose from ~12.2x to ~18.0x in nine months — this is a genuine artefact risk for any ROE-based factor score.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF conversion:** nine-month (cumulative, not quarterly) FY26 adjusted FCF $991M vs. nine-month core earnings $933M ≈ 106%; vs. nine-month GAAP net income attributable to Jabil Inc. $644M ≈ 154% (core earnings itself already strips SBC and other non-cash items, so the GAAP comparison is the more conservative one and it's still comfortably >100%).
- **SBC:** nine-month SBC and related charges $115M / $25,338M revenue ≈ 0.45% of revenue — low relative to typical software/tech comparables, consistent with an EMS business model.
- **GAAP-vs-core gap:** FQ3 FY26 core operating income $504M vs. GAAP $445M — the $59M bridge is amortization of intangibles ($23M), SBC ($25M) and acquisition/divestiture charges ($3M) plus a small business-interruption/impairment item — reasonably clean, no large one-off swings.
- **Leverage — entity is Jabil Inc. and Subsidiaries consolidated balance sheet (per the FQ3 FY26 8-K Ex-99.1 Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets):** notes payable and long-term debt, less current installments, rose from $2,386M (Aug-25) to $2,879M (May-26); current installments flat at ~$499-500M. Total debt ≈ $3,378M (May-26) vs. cash $1,360M → net debt ≈ $2,018M. **Total Jabil Inc. stockholders' equity fell to $1,323M (May-26) from $1,513M (Aug-25)** even as retained earnings grew (+$618M) — the gap is explained by treasury stock rising from $(7,899)M to $(8,849)M, i.e. buybacks ($891M in nine months) outpacing net income accretion to equity. Total liabilities/total equity ≈ 17.0x as of May-26 (entity: consolidated).
- **M&A and financing:** nine-month cash paid for business/intangible acquisitions was $852M (mostly funded by new borrowings of $2,144M against repayments of $1,884M) — goodwill and intangible assets, net, roughly doubled from $1,114M to $1,855M over nine months, consistent with the Hanley Energy Group deal (referenced in the FQ3 release's tax-adjustment footnote: *"include $8 million of gains on forward foreign exchange contracts in connection with the acquisition of Hanley Energy Group"*).
- **Share count:** diluted weighted-average shares fell from 111.5M (nine-month FY25) to 107.2M (nine-month FY26) on continued buybacks — a genuine EPS tailwind independent of operations, exactly the dynamic the triage flagged.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) guidance raised in both of the last two quarters on revenue, margin, EPS and FCF simultaneously; (2) core operating margin trend is improving (4.5%→5.3%→5.8% quarter over quarter into FQ4 guide); (3) FCF conversion stayed >100% of core earnings even while investing heavily in M&A.
**Bear:** (1) core EMS margins remain structurally thin and cyclical — a swing in AI-capex sentiment would hit Intelligent Infrastructure hard; (2) consolidated stockholders' equity has been run down to $1.3B against $22.5B of consolidated total liabilities, an unusually thin cushion that limits room for a downside surprise without a covenant or rating concern; (3) debt-funded M&A (Hanley Energy Group and others) roughly doubled consolidated goodwill/intangibles in nine months, adding both integration risk and future amortization/impairment risk.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Core operating margin (Non-GAAP) falls below 5.0% for two consecutive quarters (vs. 5.8% FQ3 FY26 actual and 5.8% FY26 full-year guide).
2. Total Jabil Inc. stockholders' equity (consolidated) falls below $1.0 billion at any quarter-end.
3. Consolidated net debt (notes payable and long-term debt, less cash) exceeds $3.5 billion at any quarter-end (vs. ≈$2.0B at May-26).
4. FY26 or FY27 guidance is cut (not raised) versus the immediately prior range at any quarterly release.
5. Adjusted free cash flow conversion (adjusted FCF / core earnings) falls below 70% for two consecutive quarters.

# PLD — Prologis, Inc.

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (24–48 month horizon), with a named reservation: **leverage and rate sensitivity** (consolidated net debt ≈$34.7bn against $53.7bn of Prologis-attributable equity) mean the thesis depends on continued industrial-demand re-acceleration, not just supply thinning. This **confirms and strengthens** the triage's "advance" call — the fundamentals have improved further since the triage was written, and guidance has been raised in each of the last three quarterly releases.

## 2. Business in plain English
Prologis is the largest owner and operator of logistics/warehouse real estate globally, leasing modern distribution space to e-commerce, retail and manufacturing tenants near major population centers and ports. It earns rental income plus fee income from managing third-party capital ("strategic capital" — its co-investment/asset-management platform) and profits from developing and selling stabilized properties. Its moat is irreplaceable infill land positions in supply-constrained markets, built up over decades, which smaller REITs and new entrants cannot easily replicate.

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse DCF
PLD has **no row in `v4/outputs/v1_valuation_table.csv`** — per the addendum, `v1_verdict` is set to **null**.

At the 25 Sep 2026 close of **$133.05** (mkt cap ≈$124.05bn per `b1_live_scores.csv`, implying ≈932.2M shares — closely matching the 10-Q's 933.1M weighted-average basic share count), and FY2026 Core FFO/share guidance midpoint of **$6.26**, P/FFO ≈ **21.3x**; FFO yield ≈**4.7%**.

I ran a simplified two-stage reverse DCF (10 years of FFO/FCF growth at rate *g*, 3% terminal growth, ~7.8% cost of equity appropriate for a large, well-capitalized industrial REIT) and solved for the implied *g*: **≈2.8–3.0%** — essentially in line with (barely above) terminal/GDP-level growth. **This is materially below** the growth rate the business is currently guiding to (Core FFO/share growth of roughly high-single-to-low-double-digits in 2026 per the guidance track record above) and below the 8.5% same-store cash NOI print. **My view: valuation is "cheap"** relative to the company's own near-term guided trajectory — the market appears to be pricing a reversion to trend growth rather than crediting the multi-quarter guidance-raise streak. The gap between "priced growth" (~3%) and "guided growth" (high-single-digit-plus) is the core of the bull case; the risk is that 2026's above-trend growth is itself partly cyclical (supply glut clearing) and a 10-year view should not extrapolate it.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Three consecutive quarterly guidance raises (Core FFO, occupancy, same-store NOI all up) is a genuinely rare, verifiable pattern showing the 2023–25 supply glut thinning into real pricing power; (2) reverse DCF implies the market is pricing only ~3% long-run growth against a business currently compounding Core FFO/share at double-digit rates — a real valuation cushion if even part of the current acceleration persists; (3) unmatched infill-location scale is a structural moat that smaller REITs cannot replicate, supporting share gains as demand recovers.

**Bear:** (1) Consolidated net debt of ~$34.7bn against $53.7bn of attributable equity leaves less room to maneuver if long rates rise or refinancing costs increase — I could not complete a normalized net-debt/EBITDA cross-check within the time-box, which is itself a gap worth closing before sizing up; (2) GAAP EPS is heavily gain-dependent (Q2 FY26's 85% YoY EPS jump was ~40% attributable to one-time disposition gains), so headline EPS momentum should be discounted relative to Core FFO; (3) the 2026 acceleration coincides with an expanding development pipeline ($4.5–5.5bn of starts guided) — if the current demand recovery stalls, PLD would be left with more spec/pipeline exposure into a softer market, a classic REIT-cycle risk.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Full-year Core FFO/share guidance is **cut** (vs. the current $6.22–$6.30 range) at the next quarterly update — would reverse a three-quarter raise streak.
2. Period-end occupancy falls **below 95.0%** for two consecutive quarters (currently 95.5%, trending up).
3. Same-store cash NOI growth decelerates **below 5%** YoY (currently 8.5%).
4. Consolidated net debt rises **above $40bn** without a commensurate increase in stabilized NOI (signals funding growth with leverage rather than operating cash flow).
5. Development stabilizations fall **materially below** the guided $2.25–2.75bn range for the full year (signals the leasing environment is not absorbing the expanded pipeline as expected).

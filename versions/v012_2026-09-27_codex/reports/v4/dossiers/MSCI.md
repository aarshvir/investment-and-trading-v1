# MSCI — MSCI Inc.

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE** — thesis horizon 24–36 months. A high-margin (56%+ operating margin), high-retention (95.3% Retention
Rate) index/data/analytics compounder whose Q2 2026 release raised both expense and free-cash-flow guidance on
genuine business momentum (Index Run Rate +17.4%), not one-offs. A reverse DCF on the 25-Sep-2026 close implies
roughly 9.7% ten-year FCF growth — at or slightly below an evidence-based ~10–11% base case — so the stock is not
priced for more than the business plausibly delivers. Main watch-items are share-count-funded buybacks partly on
debt and a real, if modest, sensitivity to global AUM/market levels (asset-based fees are ~1/3 of Run Rate).

## 2. Business in plain English

MSCI sells subscriptions and asset-linked licenses for equity/fixed-income indexes (used as benchmarks and as the
basis for trillions of dollars in ETFs and index funds), portfolio and risk analytics tools, ESG/climate data, and
private-assets data — mainly to asset managers, asset owners, banks, hedge funds and insurers. It earns recurring
subscription fees (the majority of revenue) plus fees that scale with the assets under management (AUM) benchmarked
or linked to MSCI indexes (asset-based fees, the fastest-growing and most market-sensitive piece). Its moat is
extreme switching cost: index methodologies are embedded in client mandates, benchmarks, fund names and regulatory/
compliance workflows, so clients rarely switch once an index is adopted, and MSCI's own analytics and ESG data
reinforce the same client relationships.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?

b1_live_scores (live_rank 250/~2,300, decile 5, quintile 3, composite 0.498 — a middling composite, not a top-decile
quant score): the standout percentiles are **quality/growth**, not cheapness. pct_gp_a (gross profit/assets) 96th
percentile, pct_asset_growth 91st percentile, pct_sue (earnings-surprise trend) 83rd percentile, pct_mom_12_1
(12-1 month momentum) 65th percentile. By contrast the **value family is weak**: pct_ebit_ev only 5th percentile,
pct_bp (book/price) 35th percentile, pct_fcfp (FCF yield) 28th percentile. **Durability judgement:** this is a
genuine "quality/momentum, not value" profile — the model likes MSCI because it is a highly profitable, structurally
growing business, not because it is statistically cheap. That is durable (confirmed by the Run Rate/Retention data
below) and is not an artefact of a one-off or a cyclical peak.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (all figures: MSCI Inc. consolidated — single reporting entity, no
subsidiary/segment debt breakout)

**FCF conversion is strong and stable:** FY2025 FCF $1,458.6M vs GAAP net income (FY2025, sum of quarters) ≈$1,364M
→ conversion **~107%** (typical for a low-capex, subscription business; capex was only $129.9M for all of FY2025).
**Adjusted EBITDA margin ~62%** (Q4 2025 62.2%, Q2 2026 62.1% — stable, no erosion). **Debt (10-Q, Note 7 "Debt",
consolidated Condensed Balance Sheet, as of 30 Jun 2026):** $6.0bn in Senior Notes (tranches due 2029–2036) + $475m
drawn on the Revolving Credit Facility = **~$6.475bn total debt**; cash **$352.7m** → **net debt ≈$6.12bn**. Against
TTM Adjusted EBITDA of roughly $2.1–2.2bn (annualised from the ~62% margin on ~$3.4bn TTM revenue), **net debt/
Adjusted EBITDA ≈2.8–2.9x** — consistent with MSCI's long-stated target leverage band and not a red flag. **Share
count is shrinking fast:** weighted-average diluted shares down **5.9% YoY** in Q2 2026 (74.7m in Q4 2025 → 72.9m in
Q2 2026); $145.0m repurchased in Q2 2026 alone at an average $557.34/share, ~$1.6bn still authorized as of 20 Jul
2026. Buybacks are funded by FCF and some incremental debt issuance (debt has grown across the two years shown) —
worth watching but not yet a leverage problem at ~2.8x net. **Pending M&A:** First Street Technology (physical
climate-risk data), $120m cash at closing plus contingent earn-outs, expected to close Q3 2026 subject to
regulatory approval — small relative to the balance sheet (~0.3% of market cap) and guidance is explicitly
conditioned on this closing on schedule.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case

**Bull:** (1) Index segment Run Rate growth (17.4% YoY, driven by asset-based fees) continues as passive/index
investing keeps taking share globally; (2) Private Assets and Climate/Sustainability data (First Street) become
new multi-hundred-million-dollar recurring lines, diversifying away from AUM-linked revenue; (3) continued ~5–6%/yr
buybacks compound per-share value on top of organic growth.
**Bear:** (1) A sustained equity/fixed-income bear market shrinks AUM linked to MSCI indexes, directly cutting the
~1/3 of Run Rate that is asset-based fees (this happened in 2022, when asset-based fee growth slowed sharply);
(2) Retention Rate (95.3% today) erodes as clients consolidate index providers or in-house benchmark construction
becomes more feasible with AI tools; (3) Debt-funded buybacks at a rich multiple destroy value if organic growth
decelerates below the ~10% guided/consensus range.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria

1. Organic recurring subscription Run Rate growth falls below **6%** for two consecutive quarters (currently 8.1%,
   Q2 2026).
2. Company-wide Retention Rate falls below **92%** for two consecutive quarters (currently 95.3%, Q2 2026; 93.4% a
   year ago in Q4 2025 — the metric itself has some seasonal/measurement noise, so two-quarter persistence is the
   trigger). **[Corrected 26 Sep 2026: the year-ago comparator (Q2 2025) is 94.4%; 93.4% was Q4 2025. Threshold unchanged. See the Correction section below.]**
3. Net debt/Adjusted EBITDA rises above **3.5x** (currently ~2.8–2.9x).
4. Asset-based fee Run Rate growth turns **negative** for two consecutive quarters (a direct read on AUM-linked
   revenue sensitivity to a market downturn).
5. FY guidance is **cut** (as opposed to raised/reaffirmed) at any subsequent quarterly release — track record is
   currently initiate → reaffirm → raise.

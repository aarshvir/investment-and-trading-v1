# Hewlett Packard Enterprise Co. (HPE) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F75, Wave 4)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon: 18–36 months. Reservation: leverage from the debt-funded Juniper acquisition (net debt/EBITDA ≈2.0x) and heavy exposure to the AI-capex cycle, plus an unusually large GAAP/non-GAAP gap in FY2025 that is not yet fully explained from primary sources reviewed this session.

## 2. Business in plain English
HPE sells enterprise IT infrastructure: servers (increasingly AI-optimized systems), storage/hybrid-cloud (GreenLake), and — since closing its $14bn acquisition of Juniper Networks in 2025 — enterprise/data-center networking (including Juniper's Mist AI-driven Wi-Fi and routing). A Financial Services arm leases and finances this equipment for customers. It competes with Dell, Cisco, and the hyperscalers' own in-house infrastructure build-out, and is positioning itself as a full-stack AI-infrastructure vendor.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Per `b1_live_scores.csv`: composite decile 6/10, quintile 3, live_rank 211/~500 — the best-ranked of this batch's three names. The score is driven by **exceptional momentum** (fam_M 0.83, raw 12-1m return 1.16 — one of the largest in the universe) and **exceptional earnings surprise** (fam_S 0.83, raw SUE 1.15), with a genuinely decent **value** read too (fam_V 0.58 — high book/price and FCF-yield percentiles), offset by weak raw **quality** (fam_Q 0.44, dragged by a very low gross-profit/assets percentile of 0.04 — a low-margin hardware-distribution-like balance sheet metric). **Judgement: more durable than most momentum names in this batch.** The momentum and SUE are the product of four consecutive quarters of guidance raises and large EPS beats (see §5) tied to a real, multi-quarter AI-server and networking demand acceleration — but a meaningful share of the reported YoY growth is the Juniper acquisition entering the comparison base, not pure organic demand (the primary sources reviewed do not cleanly disclose an organic-ex-Juniper growth rate — **data gap, flagged**).

Guidance was raised at **every one of the last three releases**; the FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guide roughly **doubled** (Δ +$1.50 at the midpoint, +65%) in nine months, and management stated on the Q3 FY2026 call that the current-year outlook now **exceeds what had been targeted for FY2028** in the October-2025 long-term plan. A new FY2027 framework was also issued: revenue growth 13–17%, non-GAAP EPS growth 16–20%. Separately, the company's own Q3 guide (given at Q2 2026) had called for revenue of $11.5–12.1bn and non-GAAP EPS of $0.88–0.93 for the quarter just reported — **actual results beat the top end of EPS guidance by ~19%** ($1.11 vs $0.93), an unusually large beat that cuts both ways: strong momentum, but a sign guidance-setting is struggling to keep pace with an accelerating, less-predictable demand curve.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Balance sheet (consolidated, 10-Q as of 2026-07-31, filed 2026-09-03):** cash $6,216M; short-term borrowings/current debt $2,901M ($2,137M current portion of LT debt + $2,901M is the combined ST figure per the filing's own line, see source); long-term debt $17,343M; total debt ≈ **$20.24bn**; stockholders' equity $26,514M; total assets $83,596M.
- **Net debt ≈ $14.0bn.** Against TTM EBITDA of ~$6.89bn (d4 snapshot), **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 2.0x** — consistent with the triage flag on the debt-funded $14bn Juniper deal; manageable given FY2026 FCF now guided ≥$3.5bn, but a real constraint if the AI-server cycle slows before the debt is paid down.
- **FCF:** TTM ≈ $4.74bn (fcf_yield 5.7%, d4 snapshot), tracking toward the company's own raised ≥$3.5bn FY2026 guide.
- **GAAP vs non-GAAP:** the gap is unusually wide (FY2025: -$0.04 GAAP vs $1.94 non-GAAP EPS) — non-GAAP addbacks (per the company's own guidance language) are "primarily amortization of intangible assets, stock-based compensation, and acquisition/disposition and other charges," i.e. Juniper-deal-related items are a structural, not one-off, feature of the next several years of reported non-GAAP EPS.
- **Debt maturities:** the 10-Q shows a maturity ladder running 2026 through 2030, including a Senior Unsecured Delayed Draw Term Loan Credit Facility (Juniper-deal financing); exact per-year amounts were not extracted from the primary source in this session — follow-up item.

My own base case is **≈13%/yr** (near-term high-teens per management's own FY2027 framework of 16–20% EPS growth, decelerating over the following years as the AI-server cycle normalizes and Juniper synergies are fully captured). **`implied_vs_base = "below"`** — the market is pricing in less growth than a reasonable base case, i.e. some of HPE's demonstrated execution is not yet in the price, which is why the verdict leans INCLUDE rather than WATCH despite the leverage and AI-cycle risk.

## 8. Bull case
1. A rare, verified pattern of guidance raised at every recent quarterly release — FY2026 revenue-growth guidance roughly doubled and non-GAAP EPS guidance up ~65% at the midpoint within nine months, with margins expanding materially (GAAP operating margin +870bps YoY in fiscal Q3).
2. Full-stack AI-infrastructure position (AI servers plus Juniper/Mist AI-driven networking) is differentiated versus pure-play server vendors.
3. Reverse DCF implies less growth (≈9.6%/yr) than management's own newly-issued FY2027 framework (16–20% EPS growth) — a real margin of safety if even a few years of that framework are delivered.

## 9. Bear case
1. A large share of reported growth, especially Networking's +75% YoY, reflects Juniper entering the year-over-year base rather than pure organic demand; an organic-ex-Juniper growth rate was not available from the primary sources reviewed.
2. Net debt/EBITDA of ~2.0x from debt-funded M&A leaves less room for error if the AI-capex cycle that is driving results decelerates — a live 2026 market debate across the AI-hardware complex generally, not specific evidence of an HPE slowdown.
3. FY2025's underlying GAAP economics were weak (net income of just $57M on $34.3bn of revenue, a full-year GAAP operating loss of -$437M) — almost all reported profit is non-GAAP, and the addbacks (intangible amortization, SBC, acquisition charges) are structural post-Juniper, not transitory.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Non-GAAP EPS guidance cut from the current FY2026 $3.75–$3.85 range in any subsequent quarterly release.
2. Networking segment (Juniper) growth decelerates sharply (e.g., below 20% YoY) in a quarter where the Juniper base is now fully in both comparison periods.
3. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA (per the 10-Q's own debt schedule) rises above ~2.5x without a disclosed deleveraging path.
4. Any disclosed evidence of AI-server order/backlog growth stalling, or loss of a major hyperscale customer (customer-level concentration is not separately disclosed in the sources reviewed — a data gap, not a current red flag).
5. Reopening of the Juniper merger review (e.g., a successful appeal of the August 2026 Tunney Act approval) that reimposes conditions beyond the already-completed Instant On divestiture.

## 12. Red-flag scan
- **DOJ/antitrust (largely resolved):** the DOJ sued in January 2025 to block the $14bn Juniper acquisition; a June 2025 settlement required HPE to divest its global Instant On campus/branch WLAN business and license Juniper's Mist AI Ops source code. Thirteen state attorneys general (CA, NY, MA, IL and others) intervened in the Tunney Act review in October 2025, arguing the remedy was inadequate; press reporting found the Instant On divestiture drew bids as low as $1–15 million, and the presiding judge (N.D. Cal., P. Casey Pitts) said at a March 2026 hearing the divestiture "might have been designed merely to create the appearance" of a structural fix — but **the court approved the settlement in August 2026**, finding the Tunney Act does not let it block a settlement on process grounds alone. **Legally resolved; a residual reputational/antitrust-scrutiny data point for future HPE M&A, not a current financial exposure.**
- **Open/unexplained:** the FY2025 Q2 (Feb–Apr 2025) GAAP net loss of $1.05bn is large relative to any single quarter in the two-year table and was not fully explained from primary sources reviewed this session — follow-up item before increasing conviction.
- No auditor change, material weakness or going-concern language found in the sections of the 10-Q reviewed (Note 14/legal-proceedings full text was not retrieved in this session — flagged as incomplete).

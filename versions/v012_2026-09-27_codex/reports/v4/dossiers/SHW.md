## 1. Verdict
**WATCH.** Thesis horizon: would revisit at 12–24 months, or sooner if the market re-prices. One-sentence reason: this is a genuinely excellent, category-leading business, but the 25-Sep-2026 close prices in roughly **11.2%/yr** of 10-year earnings growth against an evidence-based base case of **~8%/yr** given management's own words ("no meaningful improvement in demand," guiding only ~5% adjusted EPS growth for FY2026) — a great business priced for more than the evidence supports is a WATCH, not an INCLUDE, per this program's verdict discipline.

## 2. Business in plain English
Sherwin-Williams makes and sells paint and coatings — mostly through its own ~5,000 company-run Paint Stores (contractors, DIY, industrial), plus consumer brands (Valspar, sold through retailers) and performance/protective coatings (industrial, marine, packaging, auto refinish). The store network and direct contractor relationships are the moat: professional painters return to the same store for credit, color-matching and same-day product, which is hard for big-box retail or e-commerce to replicate.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Triage (Q13): quality 5, growth 4, price_vs_growth 3, "no disqualifying red flags," but flagged its own reservation — "net debt/EBITDA 3.1x, elevated partly from buyback funding — confirm this stays disciplined." b1 quant: fam_Q strong (pct_gp_a 0.50, pct_roe 0.84, pct_ocf_a 0.42), value percentiles weak (pct_ep 0.19, pct_fcfp 0.33 — not statistically cheap), momentum modest (pct_mom_12_1 0.58). ROE of ~65% (per d4, `y_returnOnEquity`) is real but is inflated by a thin/negative equity base from years of buybacks funded partly by debt (see §5) — this is partly an artefact of capital structure, not purely operating excellence, and should be read alongside net debt trends, not in isolation.

## 5. Balance sheet, cash conversion, capital return (consolidated, 30-Jun-2026, 10-Q)
Total debt outstanding $12,072.1m ($9,825.7m long-term incl. current portion + $2,246.4m short-term borrowings) vs $10,871.3m at 31-Dec-2025 — **up ~$1.2bn in two quarters**, driven by continued buybacks and a $500m draw on a credit facility "for general corporate purposes" (10-Q, Credit Agreements note). Cash is thin at $293.5m. **Stockholders' equity fell from $4,598.3m (31-Dec-2025) to $3,854.7m (30-Jun-2026)** — a $743.6m six-month decline (XBRL `StockholdersEquity`, both figures from the same 10-Q's comparative balance sheet), consistent with buybacks exceeding retained earnings; SHW has run with a thin/negative book-value cushion for years, which is a known feature of the model, not a new discovery, but worth stating plainly.

**Independent leverage check vs the triage card's "3.1x":** using the company's own EBITDA reconciliation tables (8-K Ex-99.1 exhibits, four most recent quarters: Q3-2025 adjusted EBITDA $1,359.0m + Q4-2025 $993.1m + Q1-2026 $998.2m + Q2-2026 $1,458.6m = **$4,808.9m TTM adjusted EBITDA**) against net debt of $12,072.1m − $293.5m cash = **$11,778.6m**, net debt/adjusted EBITDA ≈ **2.45x**, not 3.1x. This is a **data conflict** with the triage card (flagged per the wave-3 rule, not resolved by assuming either figure is authoritative) — my figure is built directly from the two most recent 8-K reconciliation tables and the Q2-2026 10-Q debt-summary table; the triage card cited no source beyond "triage_cards.csv (general knowledge)." Either way, leverage is rising, not falling, funded largely by buybacks in a soft-demand year — the direction of the triage's concern is confirmed even if the precise multiple differs.

## 6. Valuation (own reverse DCF — no V1 row exists for SHW in this wave)
Basis: price $329.10; forward earnings base = FY2026 guided adjusted diluted EPS midpoint $12.00; r (CAPM, rf 5.17% + beta 1.089 × ERP 4.14%) = 9.68%; terminal growth 3.0%. Solving for the growth rate that reconciles today's price to a 10-year DCF gives **implied 10-year EPS growth ≈ 11.2%/yr**.
- Peer check: PPG Industries (closest scaled coatings peer) trades at 12.7x NTM P/E (d4) vs SHW at ~24.9x NTM P/E (d4) / ~27.4x on the FY26 GAAP guide midpoint — SHW trades at roughly **double** its most direct peer's multiple, which the market has historically paid for SHW's superior store-network moat and margin, but it raises the bar for what "priced in" means.
- My evidence-based scenarios: **bear 3%** (prolonged housing/repaint stagnation, no volume recovery, "no meaningful improvement" per management persists for years) → fair value ≈ $183; **base 8%** (near-term guide of only ~5% EPS growth, but repaint/replacement demand is non-discretionary over a full cycle and eventually recovers, plus continued margin/buyback accretion — landing at high-single-digit EPS growth over 10 years) → fair value ≈ $261; **bull 13%** (housing/DIY volume recovery + raw-material deflation + continued aggressive buybacks) → fair value ≈ $375.
- Implied growth (11.2%) sits **above** my base case (8%) → `implied_vs_base = "above"`. Dossier valuation view: **full/expensive** relative to the near-term evidence, even though the long-run business quality is not in question.

## 7. Bull case
1. Store-network/contractor-relationship moat is real and durable; new-account wins and share-of-wallet gains continued even in a soft macro (Q2-2026 same-store sales +4.2% against management's own description of "no meaningful improvement in demand").
2. A U.S. housing/renovation cycle recovery (rate cuts, existing-home turnover) would flow disproportionately to Paint Stores Group volume, the highest-margin channel.
3. FY26 guidance was genuinely raised twice in 2026 (Q1 reaffirm, Q2 raise) — management is not currently missing its own numbers.

## 8. Bear case
1. Management's own language ("no meaningful improvement in demand," "little to no recovery in most end markets") describes a business growing EPS mainly through pricing, cost control and buybacks, not volume — that combination is exactly what makes an 11%/yr 10-year growth assumption hard to underwrite from here.
2. Leverage is rising (net debt +$1.2bn in two quarters per the 10-Q) while equity keeps shrinking; a demand downturn combined with continued buyback-funded leverage would compress the safety margin.
3. SHW trades at roughly 2x PPG's NTM multiple; any multiple compression toward peers, even with earnings on plan, would be a meaningful drawdown.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Two consecutive quarters of Paint Stores Group same-store sales growth below 2% (vs +4.2% in Q2-2026).
2. Net debt/adjusted EBITDA (per the company's own EBITDA reconciliation) exceeds 3.0x at any quarter-end.
3. FY2026 adjusted diluted EPS guidance is cut below $11.50 (the current low end) at any subsequent quarterly release.
4. Stockholders' equity turns negative (it fell from $4.60bn to $3.85bn in H1-2026; a continuation of that trend at the same pace would approach zero within roughly 18 months).
5. Any adverse ruling or settlement in the pending lead-pigment/lead-based-paint litigation (see §11) that results in an accrued liability being recorded (currently none is accrued).

## 11. Red-flag scan — the one substantive finding
**Lead pigment and lead-based paint litigation is real, ongoing, and not accrued.** From the Q2-2026 10-Q (Note on Contingencies, Item 1 Legal Proceedings): SHW is a defendant in multiple Wisconsin "risk contribution" cases (Gibson, Valoe, Beal) alleging injury from historical lead pigment/lead-based paint. Per the filing's own dates: in the **Gibson** case, discovery is "scheduled to be completed by December 15, 2026"; in **Valoe**, the federal court on **April 6, 2026** stayed all proceedings pending resolution of certain issues; **Beal** continues in Milwaukee County Circuit Court with cross-claims against landlord defendants. The company states it "currently [has] not accrued any amounts for the pending lead pigment and lead-based paint litigation... because the Company does not believe it is probable that a loss will occur, or the Company believes it is not possible to estimate the range of potential losses," and separately warns any adverse outcome "may have a material adverse effect." This is a decades-old, well-known overhang for SHW specifically (distinct from ordinary-course litigation) and is disclosed here with filing-sourced dates per the wave-3 mandatory rule. No auditor change, restatement or going-concern language found.

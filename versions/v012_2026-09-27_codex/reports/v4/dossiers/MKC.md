# MKC — McCormick & Company, Incorporated (Diligence dossier, agent F69, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (condition to revisit: McCormick's shareholders approve, and U.S./EU/U.K. antitrust authorities clear without material forced divestitures beyond the already-announced Colman's mustard sale, the pending combination with Unilever's Foods business — expected to close **mid-2027**). This overturns the triage's "advance" call: the triage's own flagged "data conflict" (trailing P/E 8.0x vs. NTM P/E 14.7x with a headline -45% forward EPS growth) is now fully explained and resolved (§6), but doing so surfaced the far larger, previously-unpriced-in-triage fact — a nearly **$45bn, majority-dilutive merger** that will leave legacy McCormick shareholders owning only **roughly 35% of the combined company**, funded partly by a new **$15.7bn cash outlay** MKC does not currently have the balance sheet to cover without a major debt raise. The standalone business is a genuine high-quality flavor/spice leader, but the stock today is effectively an option on a specific, binary, 9-plus-month-away corporate event, not a claim on the standalone company the reverse DCF below prices.

## 2. Business in plain English
McCormick is the world's largest branded spice, seasoning and flavor company (McCormick, Frank's RedHot, French's, Cholula, Lawry's), selling both to grocery consumers (Consumer segment) and to food manufacturers/restaurants (Flavor Solutions segment). Its moat is category leadership, shelf presence, and manufacturing/flavor-science scale that let it defend pricing even as private-label competes on cost. It has historically grown mid-single-digit organically and compounded returns through disciplined bolt-on M&A (it also owns a manufacturing/private-label sourcing capability, per the triage note).

b1 (2026-09-25): composite decile 2/10 but composite_qvm 0.204 (better than the raw composite) — driven almost entirely by value percentiles that look extreme (pct_ep 0.99, pct_fcfp 0.78, pct_ebit_ev 0.73, pct_bp 0.78) against weak quality/profitability percentiles (pct_gp_a 0.21, pct_accruals 0.04) and very low momentum (pct_mom_12_1 0.07). **Largely an artefact, now explained**: the extreme earnings-yield cheapness (pct_ep 0.99) is a direct product of the one-time ~$850–900M non-cash gain from the McCormick de Mexico step-acquisition inflating trailing GAAP earnings (§6) — not evidence of a mispriced quality compounder. Once normalized to guided adjusted EPS, MKC trades at a much less extreme (though still reasonable) ~15.5x (§7).

## 5. Guidance track record
FY2026 guidance was **reaffirmed, not changed**, at the Q2 FY2026 release (2026-06-25) relative to the prior (Q1 FY2026, 2026-03-31) guide: net sales growth 13–17% reported (12–16% constant currency), of which McCormick de Mexico contributes 11–13 points and organic growth is only **1–3%**; adjusted operating income growth 16–20% reported; **adjusted EPS $3.05–3.13** (2–5% reported growth / 1–4% constant-currency growth) (Source 1). No cut or raise to assess across the two most recent releases reviewed at standard depth (limitation: only two FY2026 guidance releases were available to compare, since the fiscal year began with the January 2026 Q4-FY25/FY26-initial-guide release, which is a reasonable third data point but was not independently re-verified against the primary document at standard depth).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
**Entity scope: McCormick & Company, Incorporated consolidated (which since 2026-01-02 includes 100% of McCormick de Mexico's revenue/assets with a ~25% non-controlling-interest deduction in net income — NCI net income was $10.1M in Q2 FY2026 vs. $1.0M in Q2 FY2025, confirming the new consolidation), from the 10-Q for the quarter ended 2026-05-31 (filed 2026-06-25).**
- **GAAP vs. adjusted gap:** Q2 FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS **fell 13.8%** YoY ($0.65→$0.56) while adjusted diluted EPS **rose 15.9%** ($0.69→$0.80) — the gap is driven by Unilever-deal and de Mexico transaction/integration costs plus higher interest expense and NCI, per the release's own explanation (Source 1). This is a real, disclosed, and currently widening divergence — flag for continued monitoring, not (yet) an irregularity.
- **Balance sheet (2026-05-31, 10-Q):** stockholders' equity $6,995.6M (up from $5,736.5M at 2025-11-30, reflecting the retained one-time de Mexico gain); long-term debt (noncurrent) $3,597.4M + current portion $9.5M + short-term borrowings (commercial paper) $1,326.6M = **total debt ≈ $4.93bn**; cash $331.2M; **net debt ≈ $4.6bn**. Debt rose sharply from $3.11bn (noncurrent, 2025-11-30) as the $750M de Mexico purchase was funded via cash and commercial paper (Source 4).
- **FCF (fiscal year ended 2025-11-30, the last full year available):** OCF $962.2M vs. $921.9M prior year (+4.4%); dividends paid $483M vs. $451M. Capex figure was not cleanly isolated from XBRL at standard depth for the most recent year (tag discontinuity); estimated FCF ≈ $700–750M using MKC's typical ~3–4%-of-sales capex run-rate (limitation: estimate, not a directly disclosed figure).
- **Completed M&A:** McCormick completed the purchase of an additional 25% of McCormick de Mexico (a 78-year joint venture with Grupo Herdez) for **$750M cash** on **2026-01-02**, raising its stake to 75%; the company stated the deal is accretive to net sales, adjusted operating margin and adjusted EPS in 2026, with "minimal impact" on net debt/adjusted EBITDA (Source 4).
- **Pending M&A — the dominant fact for this dossier:** on **2026-03-31**, McCormick agreed to combine with the majority of Unilever's Foods business (Knorr, Hellmann's; **excludes** Lipton ready-to-drink tea, Buavita juices, Unilever's lifestyle-nutrition assets, and Unilever's food operations in India, Portugal and Nepal) in a deal valuing the combination at **~$44.8bn enterprise value**. McCormick will pay **$15.7bn in cash and $29.1bn in stock**. **Unilever's own shareholders will receive 55.1% of the combined company's shares, with Unilever (the corporate parent) retaining a 9.9% stake — implying legacy McCormick shareholders are diluted to roughly 35% economic ownership of the combined entity.** The deal requires McCormick shareholder approval (status not confirmed as held or scheduled in sources reviewed) and antitrust clearance in the U.S., E.U. and U.K.; the **U.K. Competition and Markets Authority opened a Phase 1 investigation with a decision deadline of 2026-11-11**, and Unilever is preemptively divesting its Colman's mustard brand to address U.K. competition concerns. Expected close: **mid-2027**. McCormick will retain its NYSE primary listing and add a secondary London Stock Exchange listing on close (Source 5/6/7).
- No auditor change, restatement, or going-concern language found.

  - The implied ~4.1%/yr sits **at the top of the base case**, i.e., roughly `in_line` on a standalone basis. **But** the standalone valuation is not the operative risk: the pending merger's shareholder dilution (to ~35% ownership) and $15.7bn new-debt financing need dominate the actual forward-return math for a holder today, and neither is captured in this DCF.

## 8. Bull case
1. The underlying, standalone business is executing well: organic growth +1.7% (Q2 FY2026), adjusted operating income +29.7% YoY, and adjusted EPS growth of +15.9% YoY, with FY2026 guidance reaffirmed (not cut) at the most recent release.
2. The completed McCormick de Mexico step-acquisition (to 75% ownership, $750M, closed 2026-01-02) is a proven, accretive bolt-on with a 78-year operating history behind it — a real example of MKC's disciplined M&A track record working as advertised.
3. If the Unilever Foods combination closes as structured (mid-2027) and delivers the "significant earnings per share accretion" management claims, the combined entity would be a much larger, more diversified global flavor leader — the strategic logic (flavor-focused portfolio, Knorr/Hellmann's brand strength) is plausible, not obviously value-destructive, if execution and integration go to plan.

## 9. Bear case
1. Existing McCormick shareholders are set to be diluted to **roughly 35% ownership** of the combined company in exchange for taking on Unilever's Foods business and a large new debt load — this is a fundamentally different security than the one being bought today, and the reverse DCF above (implied ~4.1%/yr, standalone) does not price this dilution or the combined entity's prospects at all.
2. The **$15.7bn cash portion** of the deal consideration dwarfs MKC's current balance-sheet capacity (current total debt ≈ $4.9bn); a major new debt raise is required before close, and the combined company's pro forma leverage has not been disclosed in the sources reviewed — a material, currently unquantified balance-sheet risk.
3. The deal is not yet closed and faces multiple live regulatory gates: the U.K. CMA's Phase 1 antitrust decision is due 2026-11-11 (with a possible Phase 2 referral), plus separate U.S. and E.U. reviews, and a McCormick shareholder vote whose scheduling/outcome was not confirmed in sources reviewed. Unilever is already divesting the Colman's mustard brand to pre-empt U.K. concerns — a signal regulators see real competitive overlap, not a rubber-stamp review.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable — what would break this thesis / invalidation triggers)
1. The U.K. CMA's Phase 1 decision (due **2026-11-11**) refers the deal to a Phase 2 in-depth investigation, or the U.S./E.U. authorities impose material forced divestitures beyond Colman's.
2. McCormick's shareholder vote on the transaction (once scheduled) fails, or is postponed beyond calendar 2027.
3. Organic sales growth (ex-acquisitions/FX, as reported each quarter) falls below 1% for two consecutive quarters (vs. the 1–3% guided for FY2026 and +1.7% delivered in Q2).
4. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($3.05–3.13) is cut below $3.00 at any subsequent quarterly release.
5. Disclosed pro forma net debt/adjusted EBITDA for the combined company (once published, expected ahead of the shareholder vote) exceeds 4.5x, or MKC's own standalone net debt/adjusted EBITDA rises materially above its current low-single-digit level as bridge financing is arranged ahead of close.

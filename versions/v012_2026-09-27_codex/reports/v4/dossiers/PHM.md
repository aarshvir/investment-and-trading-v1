# PHM — PulteGroup, Inc. — Diligence Dossier (F97, Wave 6)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — a well-run, move-up/active-adult-tilted homebuilder priced at a trough-cycle multiple that already looks cheap on depressed earnings, BUT this cycle it funded buybacks partly with **new debt** while operating cash flow collapsed — a leverage-direction change the Q02 triage's "low leverage" framing did not capture. Thesis horizon: 12–24 months (housing-cycle dependent).

## 2. Business in plain English
PulteGroup builds and sells single-family homes across ~45 U.S. markets under the Pulte, Centex and Del Webb (active-adult) brands, plus a captive mortgage/title/insurance financial-services arm. It makes money on the spread between land+construction cost and home sale price (gross margin), levered to community count and absorption pace; a more diversified buyer mix (first-time, move-up, 55+) than pure entry-level peers is its stated differentiator.

## 3. Why the model likes it / is it durable?
Quant score: composite decile 4/quintile 2 (mid-pack, not top-decile), with strong quality percentiles (ROE pct 0.79, OCF/assets pct 0.89) but a mediocre valuation percentile (bp pct 0.16, ep pct 0.56) reflecting the market already pricing in some housing weakness. The Q02 triage flagged "Revenue -9.6% YoY - sector-wide downturn" as the key red flag and "low leverage (0.4x)" as a mitigant. **This diligence finds the leverage mitigant is weakening in real time**: notes payable rose from $1,631M (Dec-25) to $1,820M (Jun-26) as the company issued $795M of new debt in H1 2026 while operating cash flow fell to $177M from $422M a year earlier (10-Q Cash Flow Statement, R5.htm) — the "low leverage" framing is a snapshot, not a trend, and the trend moved the wrong way this half.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (consolidated; source: 10-Q R2/R3/R5.htm, period 2026-06-30 vs. 2025-12-31)
- **FCF conversion / OCF:** H1 2026 operating cash flow **$176.8M** vs. **$421.7M** in H1 2025 (−58%) — inventory build (house-and-land inventory rose to $13.72B from $12.93B) is absorbing cash faster than earnings generate it. Capex is small ($55.3M H1'26) for a builder; the real cash drain is working capital (land/inventory), which is normal at this point in a market where builders keep building spec homes to hit incentive-driven pricing.
- **Balance sheet:** Total assets $18,263M (Jun-26) vs. $18,048M (Dec-25); Notes payable **$1,820.3M vs. $1,631.1M** (+11.6%); Financial Services debt $477.9M vs. $532.3M; Shareholders' equity $13,013.7M vs. $12,985.4M (essentially flat despite $819M of net income — buybacks offset most of the equity build).
- **Cash:** $1,335.7M (Jun-26) vs. $1,980.9M (Dec-25) — down $645M, consistent with a debt-to-capital ratio the company states at 12.3% (still low in absolute terms, but rising from a lower base).

- **Litigation/authorization:** 10-Q Item 1 (Legal Proceedings) states "no material developments" vs. the FY2025 10-K; remaining share-repurchase authorization $1.80B as of June 2026 (10-Q Item 2 table).

## 7. Valuation snapshot & reverse DCF
- Price (2026-09-25 close): **$119.85**. TTM diluted EPS (FY2025 $11.12 − H1'25 $5.60 + H1'26 $4.27) = **$9.79** ⇒ TTM P/E ≈ **12.2x** (consistent with triage's ~11.1x NTM tag, since consensus NTM EPS is presumably below TTM given the ongoing downturn).
- **Reverse DCF (earnings-yield Gordon-growth sense-check):** TTM earnings yield = 9.79/119.85 = **8.2%**. At an assumed cost of equity r = 10.5% (assumption: cyclical homebuilder, beta materially >1, no primary-source WACC available — analyst estimate, not a filed number), implied perpetual growth **g ≈ 10.5% − 8.2% = 2.3%**. Base case for a mid-cycle housing recovery (mid-single-digit unit/ASP growth plus buyback-driven EPS accretion) is ~4–5%/yr — **implied growth is below base case** (implied_vs_base = below), i.e. the stock is priced for a trough that persists roughly at current depressed levels, not for full recovery. This looks statistically cheap **if** the housing downturn does not deepen materially further and if buybacks are not curtailed by rising leverage.

## 8. Bull / Bear
**Bull:** (1) mortgage-rate relief reopens affordability and reaccelerates closings/ASP without requiring incentive-driven margin giveback; (2) diversified buyer mix (move-up/Del Webb active-adult) is more resilient than pure entry-level peers, evidenced by orders still up 6% YoY even as revenue fell; (3) continued buybacks at a trough multiple (12.2x TTM) are accretive if funded from a recovering FCF base rather than incremental debt.
**Bear:** (1) gross margin keeps compressing (already −200bp YoY) if incentives escalate further to move inventory; (2) the debt-funded buyback pattern seen in H1 2026 continues or accelerates, eroding the "low leverage" case the model originally rewarded; (3) housing downturn is not cyclical but structural (affordability, insurance costs, migration patterns) and revenue keeps declining beyond the current -9.6% to -10.9% run-rate.

## 9. Kill criteria (measurable)
1. Home sale gross margin falls below 22% for two consecutive quarters (vs. 25.0% in Q2 2026).
2. Notes payable (consolidated) rises above $2.2B (vs. $1.82B Jun-26) without a matching reduction in buyback pace.
3. Operating cash flow is negative for a full fiscal year (vs. +$176.8M H1 2026).
4. Net new order units decline YoY for two consecutive quarters (vs. +6% in Q2 2026).
5. Debt-to-capital ratio (company-reported) exceeds 20% (vs. 12.3% at Jun-26).

# BR — Broadridge Financial Solutions, Inc.

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE** — thesis horizon 12–36 months. A ~90%-recurring-revenue processor of proxy voting, mutual-fund/annual-meeting communications and multi-asset trading/post-trade technology, compounding recurring revenue at high-single digits and adjusted EPS at low-double digits for a decade, trading at 15.2x NTM P/E and a 6.3% FCF yield — a valuation that (on a conservative reverse DCF) prices in only ~2.9%/year 10-year FCF growth against a company that has never grown FCF that slowly and has just guided to another year of 6–8% recurring-revenue / 8–12% adjusted-EPS growth. Four straight quarters of raised guidance in FY2026, a clean balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA ≈1.7x), and a 14th double-digit dividend increase in 15 years support the quantitative "cheap growth compounder" read from the triage. The reservation that keeps this from a larger weighting: BR's own recurring-revenue growth is itself decelerating on a multi-year view (FY24 +6.6%, FY25 +5.9%, FY26 +8% cc — noisy, not a clean trend) and a portion of FY2026's growth and cash flow was flattered by non-recurring digital-asset mark-to-market gains that will not repeat every year.

## 2. Business in plain English

Broadridge is the plumbing behind shareholder democracy and a large piece of the back office of Wall Street. When a public company or mutual fund needs to send proxy materials and collect votes from millions of retail and institutional shareholders, Broadridge does it — its ProxyEdge platform handles the vote processing for the large majority of US institutional share volume. That is the **Investor Communication Solutions (ICS)** segment ($5,561m of FY2026's $7,477m revenue, 74%). The **Global Technology and Operations (GTO)** segment ($1,916m, 26%) sells outsourced technology and operations platforms — trading, clearing, wealth/investment-management middle- and back-office systems — to banks and broker-dealers, recently expanded via the CQG acquisition (multi-asset futures/options execution and connectivity, completed 1 May 2026) into global futures/options trading infrastructure. Both businesses monetize on long-term, multi-year contracts with high switching costs (a bank does not re-platform its clearing operations casually), which is the source of the ~90%+ recurring-revenue mix.

## 3. Why the model likes it — and is it durable?

B1 composite is upper-mid (live_rank 223/~2,300, decile 6, quintile 3; composite 0.553). Family drivers: **Value (fam_V ≈0.81, very high)** — cheap on earnings yield, FCF yield and EBIT/EV; **Quality (fam_Q ≈0.71, high)** — strong gross-profit/assets and ROE (39.6%); **Momentum (fam_M ≈0.12, weak)** — the stock has *not* re-rated (12-month price momentum is actually negative, mom_12_1 ≈ −0.22), consistent with the triage's "22% pullback" framing; **Earnings momentum (fam_S ≈0.46, average)**.

Durability check: the value signal is corroborated by four consecutive quarters of guidance raises through FY2026 (see §5) built on real, filed-and-verified operating results (recurring revenue growth, closed sales, adjusted margin), not an accounting artefact. The weak momentum is the honest counterweight — the market has not yet rewarded the beat-and-raise pattern, which is exactly the "genuine mispricing" the triage flagged. One durability caveat the model cannot see: FY2026 GAAP net income and free cash flow benefited from a **$227m full-year gain on digital-asset holdings** (offset by an $11m Q4 non-cash loss) related to Broadridge's tokenization/blockchain initiatives — a real but non-operating, mark-to-market item that inflated the reported growth rate this year and should not be extrapolated.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **FCF conversion:** company-reported FY2026 free cash flow $1,233m against GAAP net income $1,124.3m — conversion >100%, consistent with the FY2027 guide of ">100% FCF conversion." Genuinely strong, low-capex model (capex only $32.1m in Q4, ~1.4% of quarterly revenue).
- **SBC:** modest and stable, $13.5m–$27.2m per quarter (0.7%–1.4% of revenue) via `AllocatedShareBasedCompensationExpense` — far lower than typical software/platform names, consistent with a services/utility-like recurring-revenue model.
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** adjusted EPS excludes amortization of acquired intangibles and acquisition/integration costs; FY2026's GAAP-adjusted convergence at $9.60 (noted in §4) is the one unresolved data conflict in this dossier.
- **OCF seasonality:** Broadridge's proxy/mutual-fund billing cycle produces a highly seasonal cash-flow pattern — Q1 (Jul–Sep) operating cash flow has been *negative* in three of the last four fiscal years (e.g., −$125.5m in Q1 FY26) and Q4 (Apr–Jun) carries the bulk of annual OCF ($677.4m in Q4 FY26 alone, out of ~$720m implied full-year run levels in prior years). Never read a single BR quarter's OCF without adjusting for this calendar.
- **Net debt/leverage:** total debt $3,520.5m, cash $402.9m (at FYE) → net debt ≈$3.12bn against TTM EBITDA $1,809m = **net debt/EBITDA ≈1.7x** — comfortably investment-grade, no covenant stress evident.
- **Capital allocation:** FY2026 buybacks $250.8m (Q4) after $150.5m (Q1) and $130.7m (prior-year Q4); dividend raised 12% to $4.36/share annualized — the company states this is the 14th double-digit dividend increase in the past 15 years, a genuinely long, verifiable track record. New $1.5bn buyback authorization announced with Q4 results.
- **M&A:** CQG (futures/options execution and market connectivity) completed 1 May 2026; deal size not disclosed, explicitly stated by the company as not expected to be material to results — a bolt-on, not a bet-the-company deal.

## 7. Valuation snapshot — no V1 row; own reverse DCF

No systematic V1 valuation row exists for BR (`v1_valuation_table.csv`/`v1_valuation.json` cover a different 92-name subset); `v1_verdict` is null in the summary JSON per program convention.

**Quant snapshot (d4, as of 2026-09-25 close $163.77):** NTM P/E 15.17x (calendarised, `pe_ntm`), TTM FCF yield 6.3%, EV/EBITDA ≈12.0x (EV $21,663m / EBITDA $1,809m). Consensus targets: mean $213.38, range $176–250 (8 analysts, "buy" average rating).

**Own reverse DCF (10-year explicit FCF growth + 2.5% terminal growth, 8.5% discount rate — a mid-single-digit spread over the 10-year Treasury, appropriate for a low-beta, investment-grade recurring-revenue business):** solving EV = $21,663m for a constant annual FCF growth rate on a $1,233m (FY2026 company-reported) FCF base implies **≈2.9%/year for ten years**. This is far below every base-case growth reference available: FY2027 guidance alone calls for 6–8% recurring-revenue growth and 8–12% adjusted-EPS growth, and BR has not grown revenue as slowly as 2.9%/year in any of the last ten fiscal years.

**Bear/base/bull cases (own construction, 3-year horizon, exit-multiple method):**
- **Bear:** adjusted EPS growth slows to 4%/year (a macro/event-driven-revenue air-pocket; ICS event-driven revenue is genuinely cyclical), exit multiple compresses to 12x NTM (below the current 15.2x, reflecting a growth scare) → EPS ≈$12.15, price ≈$145.8, **annualised total return ≈−1.0%** including dividends.
- **Base:** adjusted EPS growth 10%/year (midpoint of the FY2027 8–12% guide, consistent with FY2026's delivered 12%), exit multiple flat at 15x (no re-rating credit taken) → EPS ≈$14.37, price ≈$215.6, **annualised total return ≈+11.8%**.
- **Bull:** adjusted EPS growth 13%/year (top of range plus CQG/tokenization upside), exit multiple re-rates to 18x (closer to Broadridge's historical average multiple, rewarding four years of consecutive delivery) → EPS ≈$15.58, price ≈$280.5, **annualised total return ≈+21.5%**.

**valuation_view_vs_v1 / consistency:** dossier view = **cheap**. The implied 10-year growth (2.9%) sits well *below* my own base case (10%/year), which is the INCLUDE test this program requires: the market is not paying for the growth the evidence-based base case expects the company to deliver.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull (3):**
1. Four consecutive FY2026 quarters of guidance raises that were still exceeded at year-end (delivered 8% recurring-revenue growth and 12% adjusted-EPS growth, both at or above every range issued during the year) — a rare, clean, filed-and-verified beat-and-raise record.
2. >100% FCF conversion, net debt/EBITDA ≈1.7x, and a 14-of-15-year streak of double-digit dividend increases — a genuinely shareholder-friendly, low-risk capital-allocation record that funds bolt-on M&A (CQG) without straining the balance sheet.
3. High-switching-cost, regulatorily-anchored recurring revenue (proxy voting/vote-processing scale, multi-year bank/broker outsourcing contracts) gives real visibility into the FY2027 guide, and tokenization/digital-asset initiatives (mutual-fund/repo infrastructure) are a disclosed, early-stage option not embedded in the base case.

**Bear (3):**
1. 12-month price momentum is negative (the stock has *not* rewarded the beat-and-raise pattern) — either the market is right to discount recurring-revenue growth that is itself decelerating on a multi-year view (FY24 +6.6% → FY25 +5.9% → FY26 +8%, a noisy rather than clean acceleration), or this is a genuine, durable mispricing; the base case in this dossier assumes the latter, which is a real judgment call, not a certainty.
2. A real, disclosed chunk of FY2026's reported growth was non-operating: a $227m full-year gain on digital-asset holdings inflated GAAP net income and cash flow, and the unexplained convergence of GAAP and adjusted EPS to exactly $9.60 for FY2026 is an unresolved data conflict this dossier could not fully reconcile from public disclosure alone.
3. ICS "event-driven" revenue (M&A/reorg communications, contested proxy contests) is inherently market-cycle-dependent — a sharp slowdown in corporate-action volume would hit the segment that carries the bulk of BR's revenue and margin, and is the most plausible mechanism for the bear case above.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable) -- signposts that would change our verdict

1. Recurring-revenue growth (constant currency) falls below **5%** for two consecutive quarters (below the low end of every guidance range issued in FY2026/FY2027).
2. Adjusted EPS growth guidance is **cut** versus the immediately prior quarter's range (zero cuts to date across FY2026's four releases).
3. Net debt/EBITDA rises above **3.0x** (from ≈1.7x currently) absent a disclosed, board-approved acquisition financing plan.
4. Full-year FCF conversion (FCF/net income) falls below **90%** for a full fiscal year (FY2026: >100%).
5. Digital-asset/tokenization mark-to-market losses exceed **$50 million** in any single quarter (Q4 FY26: −$11m), which would signal the strategic initiative is a source of earnings volatility rather than a disclosed side item.

## 11. Red-flag scan

- **Litigation:** management estimates an aggregate range of reasonably possible losses for ordinary-course litigation matters of **up to $5.0 million** in excess of established reserves (per FY2026 filings) — immaterial relative to BR's scale ($7.5bn revenue). No SEC/DOJ investigation, restatement, auditor change, or going-concern language identified in the filings and news reviewed.
- **Data conflict (disclosed, unresolved):** FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS and adjusted EPS both equal exactly $9.60 for the full year despite differing at the quarterly level (Q4 GAAP $3.44 vs adjusted $3.82) — the annual convergence appears to be an arithmetic/rounding coincidence across the four quarters' adjustment items netting to roughly zero for the year, but this dossier could not independently verify the exact reconciling items from the public earnings release alone. Treat FY2026 "adjusted EPS growth of 12%" as approximately, not exactly, equal to GAAP growth for the year.
- **Insider activity:** not separately reviewed in this pass (no adverse Form-4 pattern surfaced in the searches performed); scope limitation disclosed.
- **CQG acquisition:** financial terms undisclosed by the company; treated as immaterial per management's own statement, but the lack of disclosed purchase price is itself noted for completeness.

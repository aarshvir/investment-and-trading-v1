# Omnicom Group Inc. (OMC) — Diligence Dossier

**1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Reservation: 12 months of post-merger integration evidence is not yet available; the $1.5bn three-year synergy target (33%+ of the combined base's TTM EBITA) is unproven and merger-accounting noise has made trailing GAAP figures unusable for a clean read. Thesis horizon 12–36 months.

**2. Business in plain English.** Omnicom is a holding company of advertising, PR, media-buying and marketing-services agencies (BBDO, DDB, TBWA, OMD, and now, post-merger, McCann, FCB, IPG Mediabrands and others) that plans and buys advertising and runs marketing campaigns for large corporate clients, earning fees/commissions on billings. On 26 November 2025 it completed an all-stock ~$13.5bn acquisition of The Interpublic Group (IPG), becoming the world's largest advertising/marketing-services company by revenue.

**3. Why the model likes it.** b1_live_scores.csv: OMC scores well on Value family (pct_ep 0.85, pct_fcfp 0.97 — cheap on trailing earnings/FCF yield) with weaker Quality (pct_gp_a 0.14, pct_roe 0.19 — currently depressed by merger costs), live_rank 378/1172, triage price_vs_growth 5/5, quality 3/5, growth 4/5. The extreme trailing metrics (triage flagged "205.8x trailing P/E and 3,074% EPS growth") are **merger-accounting noise, not durable value**: FY2025 GAAP EPS was **‑$0.27** (a loss) because of one month of low-margin newly-consolidated IPG plus transaction/severance/integration costs recognised in Q4 2025, and the 2026 YoY EPS "growth" figures compare against that artificially low base. The real, durable driver is organic growth acceleration (2.6% Q3'25 standalone → 3.9% Q1'26 → 6.1% Q2'26 core-ops) plus real, if unquantified, cost synergies — this is partly durable (secular ad-market recovery + synergies) and partly a comp-base artefact that will normalise by Q4 2026.

**5. Guidance track record.** OMC does not issue formal numeric revenue/EPS guidance in any of the last four releases (Q3 2025, Q4/FY2025, Q1 2026, Q2 2026 — confirmed absent in each earnings-release exhibit). The nearest equivalent is the **cost-synergy target**, which *was* explicitly raised: the Q4/FY2025 investor presentation (2026-02-18) states the three-year cost-synergy target was **doubled to $1.5bn from an initial $750M estimate**, phased $900M (2026) / $1,300M (2027) / $1,500M (2028) — a genuine, dated, numeric commitment that should be tracked each quarter. Organic growth (the closest thing to an operating KPI) has accelerated each quarter reported so far in 2026 (3.9% → 6.1%), though this is only two data points against a low prior-year base and is not yet a guided range.

**6. Earnings quality & balance sheet.**
- FCF/cash conversion: OMC's operating cash flow is deeply seasonal (agencies collect client cash and pay media bills on different cycles) — OCF is reliably negative in Q1–Q3 and strongly positive in Q4 every year in this data (e.g., 9M-2025 OCF ‑$99.2M, FY2025 full-year OCF **+$2,938.2M**). FY2025 OCF of $2.94bn (vs $1.73bn FY2024) is flattered by one month of IPG's own year-end cash collection; a clean read requires waiting for a full IPG-inclusive annual cycle (FY2026).
- SBC and capex are both small relative to revenue for this asset-light business (FY2025 capex $149.8M on $17.27bn revenue, <1%).
- GAAP vs adjusted gap: driven by $150.6M of severance/integration/disposition costs in H1 2026 alone (Q2'26 $87.1M pre-tax + Q1'26 residual), explicitly reconciled in the earnings releases — not concealed, but a genuine, still-running cash cost.
- Balance sheet: long-term debt roughly doubled post-merger to **$9.27bn** at 2025-12-31 (from $6.07bn a year earlier) as OMC assumed/refinanced IPG obligations; cash+restricted cash was $4.29bn at 2026-03-31. Net debt of roughly $5bn against a combined TTM adjusted EBITA of ~$2.7bn (per the Q4/FY2025 investor presentation's "LTM Sept-2025" combined figure) implies leverage in the low-2x area — not currently a concern, but disclosed net-debt/leverage was not itself stated in any release we read (data gap, flagged).
- Dividend raised to $0.80/share (from $0.70 YoY); "$3.5bn" cumulative share-repurchase reference in Q1 2026 release (amount/timeframe basis not fully specified — treat as approximate).
- No pending M&A beyond the now-closed IPG deal.

**8. Bull case / Bear case.**
- Bull: (1) $1.5bn three-year synergy target (doubled from $750M) is large relative to the combined cost base and, if even half-delivered, would be highly EPS-accretive from a currently depressed base; (2) organic growth is accelerating (2.6%→3.9%→6.1%) as the combined entity wins new business ("agentic marketing" AI tooling positioning); (3) the FTC consent order, while a compliance obligation, did not block or materially restructure the deal — regulatory risk is now largely resolved.
- Bear: (1) integration risk is real and ongoing — $150M+ of severance/integration costs in H1 2026 alone, and holding-company mergers of this scale have a mixed history of client attrition during integration; (2) AI-driven disintermediation (in-house/agentic ad creation and programmatic buying) is a structural threat to the agency-fee model that neither release quantifies; (3) net debt roughly doubled post-merger with no disclosed leverage target, and the FTC-order compliance/reporting burden (viewpoint-based ad-placement documentation) is a new, ongoing cost and reputational-risk vector.

**9. Key risks & kill criteria.**
1. Organic growth (Core Ops) falls below 3% for two consecutive quarters.
2. Net debt / adjusted EBITDA rises above 3.0x (from an estimated ~2x currently) at any quarter-end.
3. Cumulative disclosed 2026 cost synergies come in below $600M (vs the $900M 2026 target) by year-end.
4. A second consecutive year of full-year GAAP net income below FY2024's $1.48bn level (i.e., integration costs fail to taper as promised).
5. Any adverse ruling or expanded damages exposure in the *A.A. et al v. Omnicom Group* Qatar World Cup migrant-labor "sportswashing" litigation (S.D.N.Y., 1:25-cv-03389; motions to dismiss for lack of jurisdiction denied in 2026) that a reasonable analyst would judge reputationally or financially material.

**11. Red-flag scan.** FTC consent order (finalised 23 June 2025, amended 26 Sept 2025) restricts Omnicom's ability to steer ad dollars away from publishers based on political/ideological viewpoint and imposes documentation/annual-reporting obligations — an ongoing compliance cost and a novel type of regulatory overhang for the industry. Litigation: *A.A. et al v. Omnicom Group, Inc. et al* (S.D.N.Y. 1:25-cv-03389) — migrant Qatar World Cup workers allege Omnicom/Ogilvy PR work aided human-trafficking-adjacent conditions; court denied motions to dismiss for lack of subject-matter and personal jurisdiction in 2026, so the case proceeds — reputational risk exceeds likely financial exposure but is unquantified. Also an immaterial $2.45M ERISA 401(k) fee settlement. No auditor change, restatement or going-concern language identified. Insider Form-4 pattern not reviewed at standard depth (data gap).

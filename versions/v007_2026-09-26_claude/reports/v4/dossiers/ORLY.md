# ORLY — O'Reilly Automotive, Inc.

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** — thesis horizon 24–36 months, half weight for a valuation reservation. O'Reilly is a
33-consecutive-year comparable-store-sales grower with a low-leverage, high-FCF, buyback-funded compounding model
that just **raised** full-year 2026 guidance on both comp sales and EPS (the second raise of the year). A reverse
DCF on the 25-Sep-2026 close implies a ten-year FCF CAGR of roughly 11.6%, essentially **in line** with this
dossier's evidence-based ~11% base case — a proven business, but not obviously on sale at the current price, hence
the half-weight rather than full-weight sizing.

## 2. Business in plain English

O'Reilly is one of the largest specialty retailers of automotive aftermarket parts, tools, supplies and accessories
in the US, Mexico and Canada, serving both do-it-yourself (DIY) consumers and professional service-provider (DIFM)
customers such as repair shops and dealers. It makes money through a dense store network plus a tiered distribution
system that lets it stock more SKUs closer to the customer than most competitors, driving same-day/same-hour parts
availability. Demand is largely non-discretionary (vehicles need repairs regardless of the economic cycle) and the
aging US vehicle fleet is a structural tailwind. The moat is store density, inventory depth/availability and
scale in procurement — a "long tail of hard-to-find parts, fast" logistics advantage that is expensive for smaller
rivals to replicate.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet (all figures: O'Reilly Automotive, Inc. consolidated — a single reporting
entity; O'Reilly has no separately regulated or reported subsidiary/segment balance sheet)

**FCF conversion is strong; capital return is the core of the model.** FY2025 net cash from operating activities
**$2.8bn** (Q4 2025 release); FY2026 guided OCF **$3.1–3.5bn**, guided capex **$1.3–1.4bn**, guided FCF **$1.8–2.1bn**
(Q2 2026 release). **Negative shareholders' equity (a deficit of $1.836bn as of 30 Jun 2026, 10-Q balance sheet) is
a deliberate, long-standing feature of ORLY's capital structure** (aggressive, sustained buybacks funding return of
capital in excess of retained earnings), **not a distress signal** — confirmed by continued strong profitability,
positive and growing FCF, and an investment-grade credit profile (below). **Debt (10-Q Note 7 "Financing," 30 Jun
2026 vs 31 Dec 2025):** long-term debt (includes commercial paper) **$7,014.5M** (vs **$6,016.9M**, +16.6% in six
months — mostly a **$655m increase in commercial paper** used to help fund buybacks); cash **$262.2M** (vs $193.8M).
Net debt ≈**$6.75bn**. Against an estimated TTM EBITDA of roughly $4.6–4.7bn (FY2025 operating income $3.46bn plus
~$1.0–1.1bn of D&A, adjusted for H1 2026 growth), **net debt/EBITDA ≈1.4–1.5x** — low, conservative leverage for a
company with this cash-flow stability; the six-month debt increase funds buybacks, not operating weakness.
**Buybacks:** $2.43bn repurchased in H1 2026 (26.7m shares at an average $91.17), a further $632m (7.3m shares)
after quarter-end through the release date; $1.33bn remained authorized as of the Q2 2026 release. Total repurchased
since 2011: **1.50 billion shares for $30.42bn** at an average $20.32 — the single largest driver of ORLY's
long-run per-share earnings growth.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case

**Bull:** (1) Comp-sales momentum is *accelerating*, not decelerating (Q2 2026 +6.0% vs +4.1% a year ago), with two
guidance raises in 2026 alone; (2) the aging US vehicle fleet and rising average vehicle age are structural, multi-
year tailwinds for aftermarket parts demand largely independent of new-vehicle sales cycles; (3) buybacks at ~1.5x
net debt/EBITDA leverage have ample room to continue or even accelerate if FCF keeps growing.
**Bear:** (1) SG&A cost inflation (health care, casualty claims) has twice exceeded management's own expectations
recently — a real, recurring margin risk, not a one-off; (2) at ~24x NTM earnings with implied growth already in
line with the base case, any deceleration in comp sales removes the valuation cushion quickly; (3) commercial-paper-
funded buybacks increase refinancing/rate risk if short-term rates stay elevated for longer.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria

1. Comparable store sales growth falls below **2%** for two consecutive quarters (from +6.0% currently).
2. Gross margin falls below **51.0%** of sales for two consecutive quarters (currently 51.5–51.8%).
3. Net debt/EBITDA rises above **2.5x** (from ~1.4–1.5x currently) without an offsetting reason (e.g. large M&A).
4. SG&A growth outpaces sales growth for **three consecutive quarters** (as already flagged once by management in
   Q4 2025 — "SG&A expenses again exceeded our expectations").
5. Net new store openings miss the guided **225–235** range for FY2026.

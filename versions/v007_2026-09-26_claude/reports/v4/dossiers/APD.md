# APD — Air Products and Chemicals, Inc. (NYSE: APD)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — 24–36 month horizon. Core industrial-gas franchise is executing (guidance raised three consecutive quarters), but two large green-hydrogen impairments in five quarters ($2.33B pre-tax in FQ2 FY25; up to $2.9B pre-tax in FQ3 FY26) are a real capital-allocation red flag, and leverage has roughly doubled since FY22 to fund the now-partly-written-off megaproject program. Reservation: size small until management shows one full year without a fresh write-down.

## 2. Business in plain English
Air Products produces and sells industrial gases (oxygen, nitrogen, hydrogen, argon, helium) under long-term, often take-or-pay contracts to refining, chemicals, electronics, metals and food customers, plus builds and operates large "on-site" gasification and hydrogen plants. Revenue is largely contracted and cost-pass-through (energy costs), which smooths the base business; the volatility of the last two years comes from a separate, discretionary bet on multibillion-dollar clean-hydrogen megaprojects (Louisiana blue/green hydrogen, NEOM in Saudi Arabia) that the company is now scaling back. Competitive position: one of four global industrial-gas majors (with Linde, Air Liquide, Messer) in an oligopoly with high customer switching costs (on-site plants built next to the customer's fence line).

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Quant factor scores (b1_live_scores.csv, 25 Sep 2026): quality/value composite decile 2 of 10 (i.e., statistically cheap-looking), but momentum is very weak (mom_12_1 percentile 6%) and the model's overall rank is 432/~500 — the triage flagged this as a "screen might be hiding a good business" case given the poor rank is driven almost entirely by price momentum, not by the underlying industrial-gas economics. That is **partly an artefact**: the stock's 12-month drawdown is a direct, rational market reaction to the impairments, not evidence the core business is broken — but the model's "cheap" P/E readings on trailing GAAP EPS are themselves distorted by the same impairments (GAAP EPS was negative in two of the last eight quarters), so neither the momentum penalty nor the naive value signal should be taken at face value.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **GAAP vs adjusted gap:** driven almost entirely by the two impairment/restructuring charges — FQ2 FY25 GAAP EPS $(7.77)$ vs an otherwise-normal quarter, and FQ3 FY26 GAAP EPS $(6.47)$ vs adjusted $3.47. Company labels these clearly as non-representative; I did not find evidence of aggressive adjustment elsewhere in the quarterly bridge.
- **Cash flow:** Q1 FY26 operating cash flow $900.7M vs $811.7M Q1 FY25 (+11%); capex has been running $1.2–2.1B **per quarter** (Q1 FY26 capex $1,251.2M vs $2,117.6M Q1 FY25 — capex is now decelerating as megaprojects wind down).
- **Balance sheet:** total debt + finance leases grew from $7.63B (FY22) to $17.66B (FY25) — roughly 2.3x in three years, funding the now-partly-abandoned hydrogen program. Cash was only $980.5M as of 30 Jun 2026. Using FY25 adjusted operating income ($2.9B, per company release) plus ~$1.56B D&A as a rough adjusted EBITDA proxy (~$4.46B) against ~$16.7B estimated net debt gives **net debt/adjusted EBITDA ≈ 3.7x** (my estimate, not company-disclosed) — elevated for the industrial-gas peer group (typically 2.5–3.5x) and a genuine leverage flag given the LCEC write-off destroyed capital that added to this debt without a matching return.
- Stockholders' equity fell from $17.0B (FY24) to $13.9B (Jun-2026), reflecting the cumulative impairments plus buybacks/dividends, not primarily share repurchases.
- Dividend: $1.81/quarter declared (≈$7.24 annualized), ≈2.6% yield at the 25-Sep-2026 close — a 40+ year dividend-growth record, still intact.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Core industrial-gas oligopoly economics are intact — 25.6% core operating margin even in the write-off quarter, and guidance has been raised three times this fiscal year.
2. Capex is stepping down (FY26 guide cut to ~$3.5B from $4.0B) as the failed megaprojects are abandoned, which should improve free-cash-flow conversion from here.
3. NEOM/Yara renewable-ammonia marketing deal (announced 30 Jun 2026, being finalized) shows the company can still monetize part of the green-hydrogen build-out rather than a total loss.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Two impairments in five fiscal quarters (total pre-tax charges >$5B combined) is a repeated pattern, not a one-off — capital-allocation discipline on new "growth" projects is unproven going forward.
2. Debt roughly doubled in three years to fund a program that is now being partly unwound; net debt/EBITDA is estimated at ~3.7x, above historical norms for the sector.
3. Reported operating loss and negative GAAP EPS in 2 of the last 8 quarters make headline "cheap" P/E screens (on trailing GAAP earnings) unreliable — a risk this triage/model process itself is exposed to.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. A third impairment/asset write-down exceeding $500M pre-tax within the next four fiscal quarters.
2. Adjusted EPS guidance is **cut** (vs. the immediately prior range) at any of the next four quarterly releases.
3. Core segment (ex-impairment) operating margin falls below 22% for two consecutive quarters (vs. 25.6% cited by triage / ~23-24% implied by Q1–Q3 FY26 adjusted operating income/sales here).
4. Estimated net debt/adjusted EBITDA rises above ~4.25x (from ~3.7x now).
5. FY2027 capex guidance re-accelerates above $4.5B without a named, contracted, return-hurdle-cleared customer (signals reversion to undisciplined megaproject spending).

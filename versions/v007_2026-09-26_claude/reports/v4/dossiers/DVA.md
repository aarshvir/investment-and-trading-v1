# DaVita Inc. (DVA) — Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F15 (replaces F9, which hung before writing anything — this is a fresh review) | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close | **Price:** $178.47

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — thesis horizon **24–36 months**.

DaVita is statistically cheap (10.8x NTM P/E, 9.6th percentile of its own 177-month history) and its earnings-per-share growth has genuinely compounded. But that growth is **majority financially engineered** (debt-funded buybacks against a negative-equity balance sheet), not organic — normalized non-acquired treatment growth was −0.6% in FY2025 and only +0.1–0.3% in H1 2026. Named reservation: leverage (3.37x company-defined ratio, rising) on a negative-equity balance sheet, concentrated in a single ~45%-owner (Berkshire Hathaway) that has been trimming even as its percentage stake mechanically rises, at 44% one-year volatility — the highest of any name in this batch and near the mandate's 45% ceiling.

## 2. Business in plain English

DaVita operates ~2,700 outpatient dialysis centers in the US (plus a smaller international business), providing life-sustaining hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis to patients with end-stage kidney disease (ESKD). About two-thirds of US patients are reimbursed at government (Medicare/Medicaid) rates; the remaining roughly one-third of revenue comes from commercial insurers, who pay materially higher rates per treatment — this small commercial cohort disproportionately drives profitability (source 8). Competitive position: a near-duopoly with Fresenius Medical Care in US dialysis, with high switching costs (dialysis is a recurring, life-critical service tied to a physical location) and real regulatory/reimbursement dependence.

## 3. Why the model likes it

B1/live-rank 14 (the highest-ranked of this batch's three names) reflects strong per-share earnings growth and a very low valuation multiple. The quant signal is **partly an artefact of aggressive, debt-funded share buybacks compressing the share count** rather than pure organic quality — real caution is warranted before reading the composite score as "the business is accelerating." Treatment-volume growth (the true organic-demand metric) has been flat to slightly negative through the period reviewed.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **Negative equity, confirmed:** Total DaVita Inc. shareholders' deficit **$(651,082)K** at 2025-12-31 (10-K) — the result of over a decade of debt-funded buybacks exceeding retained earnings. This is DaVita's normal structure, not a new distress signal, but it means there is no equity cushion against a downturn.
- **Leverage:** Total debt $10,345M / net debt $9,668M at YE-2025 → $10,694M (Q1-26, leverage ratio 3.34x) → $10,848M total / $10,162M net (Q2-26, leverage ratio **3.37x**) — company-defined ratio drifting up as debt funds continued buybacks.
- **Debt actions:** Two credit-agreement amendments in the review period — an **8th Amendment** (2025-11-24) refinancing a $2.0bn term loan and $1.5bn revolver (5-year, replacing facilities maturing April 2028), and a **9th Amendment** (2026-06-08) adding an **incremental $500M** Term B-2 loan (SOFR+175bps, maturing May 2031) explicitly to repay revolver draws and fund "general corporate purposes" (i.e., continued buybacks) (source 6, 7).
- **Buybacks:** $465M (Q3-25, 3.3M sh) → part of $1.78bn FY2025 (12.7M sh at avg $140.09) → $403M (Q1-26, 3.0M sh at $133.70) → **$348M (Q2-26, 2.2M sh at $154.95)**. Shares outstanding fell from 68.5M (Dec-25) toward the low-to-mid 60s millions by mid-2026 — **note:** the Q1-26 "68.875M diluted" and Q2-26 "63.955M outstanding" figures as reported are not on a fully consistent basis (diluted weighted-average vs period-end outstanding); V1's own diluted-share input (66.09M) sits between the two and is the more defensible reference for per-share valuation. Flagged as a data-presentation inconsistency, not a restatement.
- **No dividend** — DaVita's entire capital-return program is buybacks.
- **FCF conversion:** TTM OCF $2.19bn vs TTM NI (DaVita Inc. portion) $847M per D3/V1 — a high multiple, partly reflecting minority-interest carve-outs in joint-venture dialysis centers, not a clean FCF/NI ratio; treat with caution.

Peer set (CI, CVS, DVA, LH, DGX) is a loose "Health Care Services" GICS bucket; DaVita's truer direct peer, Fresenius Medical Care, is a foreign private issuer not in this universe and was not independently re-benchmarked here.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:**
1. Treatment-volume growth has turned positive for two straight quarters (+0.1%, +0.3%) after a negative FY2025 — tentative evidence the GLP-1 volume scare has not materialized as feared; DaVita's own clinical data (presented at ASN Kidney Week / AHA) shows GLP-1 use associated with a **9% reduction in hospitalization** and improved mortality among existing ESKD patients — a potential net positive (longer patient lifetime value) rather than a pure headwind (source 9).
2. Buyback math is real and legal: 12.7M shares retired in FY2025 alone (≈18% of the Dec-2025 share count over a multi-year lookback), directly compounding per-share value for remaining holders.
3. Cheap on both an absolute (9.6th percentile) and peer-relative (at the peer median) basis, with no obvious near-term catalyst needed for a re-rating — a change in narrative alone could work.

**Bear:**
1. Leverage (3.37x and rising) sits on a **negative-equity** balance sheet with **no dividend cushion**; two credit-facility amendments in ten months show reliance on external financing to keep buying back stock.
2. Berkshire Hathaway holds **~45%** of DaVita (28.7M shares per its Q1-2026 13F) and has been an active seller (e.g., 182,980 shares sold 2026-07-31 at $199.55; other periodic trims reported) even as its percentage stake mechanically rises from DaVita's own buybacks shrinking the float — a concentrated holder that has been reducing its dollar exposure is a real overhang/liquidity risk if it accelerates (source 10, 11).
3. An active qui tam False Claims Act suit (*U.S. ex rel. Bayne v. DaVita*) survived a motion to dismiss (denied 2025-04-29; company answered 2025-07-21), and multi-year, unresolved regulatory inquiries into DaVita's American Kidney Fund premium-assistance practices (California DOI subpoenas since 2020; DC Attorney General antitrust CID since Jan-2023) remain open with no disclosed resolution (source 12 — sourced via secondary summary of the company's own legal-proceedings disclosure; not independently re-read in full within this time box).

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. Normalized non-acquired treatment growth is **negative for two consecutive quarters** (reversing the +0.1%/+0.3% seen in H1 2026).
2. Company-defined leverage ratio rises above **4.0x** (from 3.37x in Q2 2026).
3. Berkshire Hathaway's ownership falls below **25%** via an accelerated sale (versus a gradual, buyback-driven drift), signalling loss of confidence rather than mechanical float shrinkage.
4. Full-year adjusted EPS or adjusted operating-income guidance is **cut** from a prior range (breaking the pattern of raises/maintains through Q1–Q2 2026).
5. A material adverse ruling or settlement in the *Bayne* qui tam case, or a formal charge (not just a subpoena/CID) arising from the AKF investigations, is disclosed.

## 11. Red-flag scan

- **Litigation:** $34M whistleblower kickback settlement already paid (2024, resolved); *Bayne* qui tam case active (see above); AKF-related CA DOI and DC AG inquiries open for 3–6 years with no disclosed resolution.
- **Insider activity:** COO David Maughan's September 2026 disposal was routine tax-withholding on RSU vesting; board members received routine annual stock grants (Aug-2026); the one large, directionally meaningful transaction was **Berkshire's** July-2026 sale as a 10%+ owner (source 10).
- **Negative equity** is structural (buyback-driven), not a going-concern flag, but leaves no balance-sheet cushion.
- **Auditor:** KPMG ratified at the June-2026 annual meeting; no auditor change or qualification identified.
- No restatements or material-weakness disclosures identified in the filings reviewed.

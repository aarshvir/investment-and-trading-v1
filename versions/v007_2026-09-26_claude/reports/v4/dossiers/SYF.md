## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — A well-funded, transparently-guided, statistically cheap private-label lender whose recent earnings growth has been disproportionately credit-cost- and buyback-driven rather than organic, with a plateauing NCO rate and a concentrated partner base; the valuation cushion justifies a position, but credit-cycle timing risk argues for sizing it small rather than at full conviction. **Thesis horizon:** 12–24 months, reassessed at every print against Section 9.

## 2. The Business
Synchrony is the largest US private-label/co-brand credit card issuer, originating card and installment receivables through exclusive retailer, healthcare (CareCredit) and platform partnerships rather than its own brand [2]. Economics run: yield on receivables → net interest income → **Retailer Share Arrangements (RSA)**, a contractual profit/loss-share paid back to partners that scales with program performance — a partner-alignment tool that also caps how much credit-cycle upside SYF keeps [2,4]. Funding comes from Synchrony Bank, a federally chartered **savings association** (not a national bank, not an industrial loan company), regulated by the OCC, FDIC and CFPB [2]: direct retail deposits fund 93% of total deposits ($75.2B of $81.1B, FY2025-end), and deposits are 83–84% of total funding [2,4]. The five largest programs (Amazon, Lowe's, PayPal/Venmo, Sam's Club, TJX) generated 54% of FY2025 interest-and-fee income and 52% of receivables, three of them individually above 10% [2] — real concentration, addressed in Sections 8–9.

## 3. Why the Model Likes It
The pipeline's Quality (0.96) and Value (0.958) percentiles, driving the 0.80 composite [17], are real but partly cycle-flattered. ROE of ~21–25% and P/E of ~7.2–7.5x on trailing/NTM EPS [17,18] partly reflect a genuine low-cost, retail-funded franchise with expanding NIM (15.08%, Q2 2026, +30bp YoY [4]). But part is timing: provision fell from $1,491M (Q1 2025) to $1,146M (Q2 2025) as NCO dropped 68bp in one quarter [7], and allowance coverage has declined every quarter for five straight quarters (10.87%→10.09% [4,7]) — reserve releases have been a real EPS tailwind that is now shrinking ($163M release in Q2 2026 vs $265M a year earlier [4]). This is the nbfc.md pattern of a lender "looking cheapest right before the loss cycle turns," tempered by transparency: NCO has plateaued at ~5.4% for three straight quarters, just below, not deep inside, management's own 5.5–6.0% through-cycle target [4,14].

Rebuilding both from SYF's own Statements of Earnings [2,4,7] resolves it exactly: TTM-through-Q2 2026 "total net revenue" as defined above = $15,032M (Q3 2025 $3,823M + Q4 2025 $3,793M + Q1 2026 $3,698M + Q2 2026 $3,718M); FY2025 = $14,981M (audited: NII $18,466M + RSA −$4,005M + other income $520M [2]) — both match consensus almost to the dollar. Net income: TTM $3,518M [7,4] vs. net income available to common $3,435M [18] — the ~2.4% gap is **preferred dividends** ($1,716M of preferred stock at Q2 2026, incl. a new $500M 7.25% issue [4]), not a timing artifact. Credit cycle: NCO fell from 6.45% (Q4 2024) to a low of 5.16% (Q3 2025), then **plateaued** at 5.37–5.43% for three straight quarters — the easy-improvement phase looks over.

## 7. Valuation Snapshot
**Suppressed and why:** EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, generic FCF yield, D/E, ROCE and current ratio are all undefined or inverted for a deposit-funded lender — debt (deposits/borrowings) is raw material, not financing, and CFO is dominated by loan/deposit balance-sheet churn [nbfc.md, banks.md]. **Primary method — P/TBV vs sustainable ROTE**, P/B = (ROE − g)/(COE − g): at price $72.94 and disclosed tangible book value/share $42.01 [4], SYF trades at **1.74x P/TBV**. Using COE 11% (above the DM-bank 9–11% range given SYF's 1.308 beta [18] and monoline card-cycle concentration) and g 4% (management's own mid-single-digit receivables growth guidance [14]): raw FY2025 ROE (21.3%, still credit-cost-flattered) implies a justified 2.47x; a through-cycle-normalized ROE — migrating NCO from ~5.4% to the mid-point of management's own 5.5–6.0% band, roughly a 1.5–2 point ROE haircut — implies **1.86x–2.00x**. Current 1.74x sits at or slightly below even this more conservative normalized band, i.e. the market is already pricing in most of the credit-normalization risk, not ignoring it. **Secondary — P/E on normalized credit cost:** TTM diluted EPS ≈ $9.76 (sum of last four quarters [4,7]) at today's below-target NCO; normalizing to the 5.5–6.0% band cuts net income by an estimated 8–13%, giving normalized EPS of roughly $8.5–9.0 and a normalized P/E of **~8.1–8.6x** vs. a raw trailing ~7.5x [17,18] — still not statistically demanding. Peers (secondary cross-check, Tier 2/4): Bread Financial (BFH), the closest pure private-label peer, trades at 1.54x P/TBV with 22.6% ROTCE [19]; Capital One (COF) at 2.05x P/TBV / 13.2x trailing P/E [20], though COF's ROE is currently distorted by Discover-integration charges and it is now a much larger, more diversified franchise. **Discover (DFS) is excluded as a peer**: Capital One completed its ~$35B acquisition of Discover on 2025-05-18 [13], so DFS is no longer an independently listed comparable.

**Valuation reconciliation with V1 (added 2026-09-26):** V1 rates SYF **"attractive"** (score 0.81) with a base-case 3-year annualized return of **+31.2%**, built on ROE settling at the 50th percentile of its own history (19.1%, versus 20.8% actual TTM) combined with aggressive buyback-driven per-share book-value growth and a P/B re-rating from 1.44x to an exit 1.61x — a 3-year price (~$160.83) that V1's own street-flag notes **"exceeds Street 12m high target"** ($104; mean $89.43, low $78). This dossier's own valuation read above is that SYF's 1.74x P/TBV already sits at or slightly below its own normalized band (1.86x–2.00x, using COE 11%/g 4%) and its normalized P/E (~8.1–8.6x) is "still not statistically demanding" but not a bargain either — i.e., **"the market is already pricing in most of the credit-normalization risk, not ignoring it."** **This dossier's more measured "fair" read is better supported:** NCO has plateaued at 5.37–5.43% for three straight quarters — just below, not deep inside, management's own 5.5–6.0% through-cycle target — and allowance coverage has fallen every quarter for over a year as reserve releases shrink (§3/§6/§9). That is exactly the mechanism that would prevent the further ROE improvement and re-rating V1's base case requires, and it is why this dossier sizes the position small (INCLUDE-SMALL) rather than at full conviction despite agreeing the shares are not expensive.

## 8. Bull Case and Bear Case
### Bull case
1. Genuinely low-cost, retail-funded balance sheet (93% direct deposits [2]) with expanding NIM (+30bp YoY to 15.08% [4]) and a management team giving, and largely meeting or beating, explicit forward NCO/RSA/EPS ranges every quarter [14] — a real transparency edge the v3 process's own audit flagged as often missing elsewhere.
2. Valuation already screens as reasonable even after normalizing credit cost to management's own stated through-cycle range (Section 7), with a large, ongoing, uncapped buyback (16% share-count reduction in under two years [3,7]) compounding per-share value.
3. The CFPB late-fee rule that would have cut fee income was vacated in April 2025 [2,11,12], removing a previously live overhang; no new rule is currently in effect.

### Bear case
1. **Credit-cycle timing.** NCO improvement has stalled — three straight quarters at 5.37–5.43% [4] — right near the low end of management's own 5.5–6.0% "entry of cycle" target [14], and ACL coverage has fallen every quarter for over a year (10.87%→10.09% [4,7]) as reserve releases shrink; the next leg for NCO is more likely up than down, and FY26 guidance already assumes normalization.
2. **Partner concentration.** Five programs are 54% of interest-and-fee income, three of them (Lowe's, PayPal/Venmo, Sam's Club) individually above 10% [2]; loss or repricing of any one is a single-event risk with no comparable offset disclosed.
3. **Muted organic growth.** Total net revenue has been range-bound at $3.65–3.82B/quarter for two years (Section 4); most of the FY2025 EPS growth came from a falling provision line, not core spread income, and RSA is running near the top of its 4.0–4.5% target range [14], structurally capping how much of any credit outperformance SYF keeps versus its partners. Sell-side Q3 2026 EPS estimates have been cut roughly 9% over the trailing 90 days (consensus $2.362 vs. $2.603 ninety days earlier) [17,18], consistent with [15], despite the Q2 2026 beat, consistent with the market pricing in this normalization.

## 9. Key Risks & Kill Criteria — What Would Invalidate This Thesis
1. NCO rate exceeds 6.00% (top of management's own through-cycle band) for two consecutive quarters [4,14].
2. Holding-company CET1 falls below 11.5% for two consecutive quarters (vs. 12.6–13.7% over the last four quarters) [4,7].
3. Non-renewal or material repricing of any of the five largest partner programs (54% of interest-and-fee income) [2].
4. RSA exceeds 4.5% of average receivables for two consecutive quarters with no offsetting NIM gain [14].
5. CFPB finalizes and implements a new late-fee rule that SYF discloses as expected to materially reduce late-fee income [2,11].

Regulatory: the Bank's two legacy CFPB consent orders (2013, 2014, from the GE Capital Retail Bank era — $259M total consumer redress plus a $3.5M penalty) were both terminated, most recently on 2025-05-12 [2,10] — a closed legacy matter, not a new action; two smaller 2026 consumer class actions (a TCPA debt-collection-calls suit and an FCRA credit-reporting suit) were found via WebSearch but are unverified beyond docket-level secondary reporting and look consistent with routine, industry-standard consumer litigation, not a systemic issue. A 2018-vintage securities class action (disclosure of retailer "pushback" on underwriting changes) settled for $34M in 2023 [16] — closed, pre-dates this window. Insider selling: checked a bounded sample of the 12 most recent Form 4 filings (all dated 2026-08-19, transactions dated 2026-08-17) — all were routine dividend-equivalent-unit accruals on outstanding RSU/PSU awards (Code A, small share counts), not open-market sales; a comprehensive multi-year Form 4/144 tally (719 Form 4s and 71 Form 144s on file since 2022 [1]) was not performed and is out of scope for this pass. No short-seller report was found via WebSearch.

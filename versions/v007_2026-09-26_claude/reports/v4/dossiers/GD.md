## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE.** Thesis horizon: 12–36 months. Of the three companies in this batch, GD has the cleanest and most currently-confirmed positive story: three consecutive quarters of raised full-year guidance, a record $136.5B backlog with a 1.4x book-to-bill, broad-based growth across all four segments (including a Marine Systems margin that is *improving*, not deteriorating, contrary to the multi-year execution-risk narrative this dossier set out to test), strong free cash flow, and the lowest leverage of the batch. The main reason it screened with only a middling Momentum score is that the quant snapshot underlying this batch appears to **predate GD's Q2 2026 report** (see the data-quality note below) — the fundamental picture as of this writing is stronger than the screen shows.

## 2. Business in plain English
General Dynamics is a diversified US defense and aerospace prime operating four segments: **Aerospace** (Gulfstream business jets — design, manufacture, and after-market support), **Marine Systems** (nuclear submarines — Columbia-class and Virginia-class — built at Electric Boat, plus surface ships at Bath Iron Works and NASSCO), **Combat Systems** (armored vehicles including the Abrams tank and Stryker, plus munitions), and **Technologies** (GDIT — federal IT/mission-systems services, plus Mission Systems electronics). It earns money primarily from multi-year contracts with the US government (Navy, Army, federal civilian agencies) and, in Aerospace, from private and corporate buyers of business jets.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
**FCF conversion:** very strong — H1'26 FCF/NI ≈ $3.598B/$2.285B (Q1+Q2 NI) ≈ 157%; FY2025 FCF (CFO $5.120B − capex $1.161B) = $3.959B vs NI $4.210B ≈ 94%. **GAAP vs. adjusted:** minimal gap — no material non-GAAP EPS adjustment was identified in the sources reviewed (unlike UPS in this batch), which is itself a quality signal, though this should be confirmed against the FY2025 10-K's own non-GAAP reconciliation note as a follow-up. **Effective tax rate** is a modest ~17.9% (FY2024–25 blended, per `ratios.py`), below the US statutory-plus-state expectation — plausibly R&D credits and export incentives (FDII) common for an aerospace/defense exporter; not flagged as aggressive, but noted for the record. **Leverage:** gross debt ~$9.8B, cash $3.65B, **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 1.0x, interest coverage 12–17x** — the strongest balance sheet of the three names in this batch, and debt is being actively reduced ($498M paid down in Q2'26 alone; long-term debt fell from ~$7.0B (Q3'25) to ~$6.3B (Q1'26) per XBRL). **Working-capital / percentage-of-completion caution (per the sector playbook):** this dossier did not independently verify GD's unbilled-revenue growth vs. revenue growth or cumulative CFO/EBITDA over a 3–5 year window — both are the sector's standard lie-detector for POC-accounting aggressiveness and should be run before a large position is sized; flagged as a gap, not a finding either way. **M&A:** none material identified in 2025–2026. **Capital return:** dividends $834M and buybacks $319M in H1'26 (well covered by FCF); dividend yield ~1.8%, payout ratio ~35–38% — comfortable, with room to grow, a clear contrast to UPS in this batch.

## 7. Valuation snapshot
NTM P/E (calendarized, per `d4_live_snapshot.parquet`) **18.5x**; trailing P/E **21.0x** (via `valuation.py` EV bridge: EV $97.3B on FY2025 EBITDA ~$6.3B → **EV/EBITDA 15.5x**, EV/EBIT 18.2x); FCF yield ~6.5%. The sector playbook's cited range for global defense primes is **~15–22x P/E with a "visibility premium"** for backlog-driven revenue — GD's 18.5x NTM sits comfortably inside that band, neither obviously cheap nor stretched. **Reverse-DCF** (WACC 8.0%, terminal growth 3.5%): using a $5.9B normalized FCF base, the current EV implies **slightly *negative* FCF growth over the next decade** — i.e., the market's implied assumption looks conservative relative to a company that just raised guidance for the third straight quarter and is sitting on a record, growing backlog; this is the clearest quantitative support for the Value-family screen result. A probability-weighted bear/base/bull scenario (bear $15.50 EPS/16x, base $16.85/19x, bull $18.50/22x) centers on **~$324/share, close to fair value (-3.8%)** — this dossier does not find GD dramatically mispriced either way; the case here rests on business quality and trend durability, not a valuation anomaly. (A separate forward-DCF run using a more aggressive 10%/5yr growth assumption produced an implausibly high per-share value and is not reported as a serious estimate — flagged here so the lead does not mistake scenario sensitivity for a price target.)

## 8. Bull case
1. **A verified, three-quarter pattern of guidance raises with record, growing backlog** ($136.5B, +32% YoY, 1.4x book-to-bill, funded backlog $104.1B/76% of total) — this is about as close to a "show me" thesis being actually shown as this batch contains.
2. **Marine Systems — historically the segment most exposed to execution risk on multi-decade submarine programs — is currently improving, not deteriorating**: margin +40bps YoY, Columbia-class labor hours earned +37% YoY, sequence-critical material deliveries +65% YoY. If this holds, it removes the single largest historical overhang on the stock.
3. **Balance sheet and capital allocation are the strongest of the three names in this batch**: net debt/EBITDA ~1.0x, actively deleveraging, dividend well covered at ~35–38% payout with room to grow, no dependence on non-GAAP adjustments to tell the earnings story.

## 9. Bear case
1. **Multi-decade submarine programs (Columbia/Virginia-class) carry real, structural execution risk that a few good quarters does not retire** — the sector playbook is explicit that these programs have historically produced multi-year margin surprises (favorable and unfavorable) via estimate-at-completion adjustments; this dossier did not independently audit GD's EAC adjustment history and recommends that as a follow-up before treating the current improvement as durable.
2. **Budget/appropriations risk is live, not hypothetical, right now**: the government is funded only through a continuing resolution to 2026-12-11 (signed 2026-09-01); the FY2027 defense appropriations process is far from complete (House has passed 3 of 12 spending bills, Senate none, as of this session), and a further shutdown or a CR-driven funding gap remains a real near-term risk to contract obligations and cash timing, even though GD's multi-year backlog buffers the revenue line better than most industrials.
3. **Gulfstream (Aerospace) is a large and growing share of the growth story (+15.1% revenue in Q2'26) but is the segment most exposed to a general economic or corporate-profit downturn** — business-jet demand is discretionary and cyclical in a way the defense segments are not; a re-acceleration narrative built partly on Gulfstream deliveries is not immune to a macro shock.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
These are the specific, observable, time-bound conditions that would **invalidate** the INCLUDE thesis above:
- Marine Systems margin reverses back below ~6.5% for two consecutive quarters, or a disclosed unfavorable EAC adjustment on Columbia or Virginia-class work exceeds a low-single-digit-percent of segment revenue.
- Book-to-bill falls below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters (backlog visibility deteriorating).
- FY2027 appropriations lapse into a full-year continuing resolution or shutdown that measurably delays contract funding or payment (watch specifically around the 2026-12-11 CR expiry).
- Full-year guidance is cut, or fails to be raised, at the Q3'26 or Q4'26 release — breaking the three-quarter raise streak.
- Gulfstream order intake or deliveries decelerate for two consecutive quarters alongside broader signs of corporate-capex weakness.

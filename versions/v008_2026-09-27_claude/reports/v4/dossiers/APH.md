# Amphenol Corporation (APH) — Diligence Dossier (F27, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — 24–36 month horizon. Named reservation: total debt roughly doubled (from $6.89bn to $18.81bn between FY2024 and 2026-06-30) to fund the transformational $10.5bn CommScope (Connectivity & Cable Solutions) acquisition that closed 2026-01-09; the underlying business and execution are excellent (four consecutive quarters beating the high end of its own guidance, book-to-bill >1.2x), and estimated net debt/EBITDA (~1.4–1.5x) is manageable for an investment-grade industrial, but this is real, recently-added leverage and integration risk that argues against a full-weight position until at least two post-close quarters confirm the deal is performing to plan.

## 2. Business in plain English
Amphenol designs and makes electronic and fiber-optic connectors, cable assemblies, sensors and interconnect systems used inside almost every category of electronics — data centers/AI servers, telecom/IT networks, automobiles, industrial equipment, defense systems and mobile devices. It sells to a highly diversified base of OEM customers across those end markets and grows both organically and through a long, disciplined track record of bolt-on and, occasionally, large (CommScope) acquisitions, run by a famously decentralized, general-manager-owned operating model.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
b1_live_scores.csv: composite decile 4/quintile 2, live_rank 315/1172; Quality strong (gp/assets 38th but roe 67th, ocf/assets 42nd), Value mixed (bp 62nd — not particularly cheap), Momentum 32nd (below-median 12-1m), SUE 78th (strong earnings surprises). The reported "55% revenue growth" in the prior triage is **partly an M&A artefact**, not purely organic: management itself attributes growth to "strong organic growth... as well as contributions from the Company's acquisition program" in every one of the last four earnings releases, and FY2026 guidance explicitly separates out CommScope's now-expected $4.6bn of incremental FY2026 sales. This does not make the growth low-quality — it is real, cash-funded, disciplined M&A that the company has executed successfully for decades — but it is durable in a different sense than organic-only growth, and the diligence should not treat 55% as a clean organic run-rate.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) a 30+ year track record of successfully integrating acquisitions at scale, now applied to the largest deal in company history (CommScope), already tracking above initial expectations; (2) record backlog/book-to-bill (1.23–1.24x in the last two quarters) evidences real, broad-based end-market demand, not just M&A math; (3) highly diversified end-market exposure (IT datacom, auto, defense, broadband, industrial) reduces single-customer/single-cycle risk relative to pure AI-networking peers.
**Bear:** (1) total debt nearly tripled in 18 months specifically to fund M&A — if the AI-datacom capex cycle or auto/industrial demand disappoints, deleveraging slows and equity-value creation stalls; (2) a one-time inventory step-up charge already shows up as GAAP earnings noise in Q1'26 — more such charges are likely as CommScope integrates; (3) the growth rate itself is not fully organic, so multiple-expansion re-rating on a "55% growth" headline risks disappointment once M&A-driven growth normalizes.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.5x in any quarterly report (vs. an estimated ~1.4–1.5x today) without a specific deleveraging plan disclosed.
2. Organic (non-M&A) revenue growth, as separately disclosed by management, falls below 5% for two consecutive quarters.
3. GAAP-to-adjusted EPS gap (acquisition/integration-related costs + amortization of acquired intangibles) exceeds 20% of GAAP EPS for two consecutive quarters, signalling integration is costing more than communicated.
4. Company misses (rather than beats) the high end of its own quarterly revenue guidance for two consecutive quarters (vs. 4-for-4 beats over the last year).
5. A credit-rating downgrade is announced by Moody's or S&P specifically citing the CommScope-related leverage.

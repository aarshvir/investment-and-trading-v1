# Everest Group, Ltd. (EG) — Diligence Dossier (F22, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 18–30 months. Reason: the market is pricing continued, permanent earnings decline (implied 10-year growth is deeply negative — see §7) despite two consecutive clean quarters of results since the company capped its worst known casualty-reserve exposure with a $1.2bn reinsurance cover; the reservation is that reserve adequacy is unproven beyond two quarters and premium is still shrinking sharply, so this is a statistically cheap, evidence-supported turnaround, not a proven one.

**Thesis (≤25 words):** Global reinsurer that twice surprised the market with US casualty reserve charges, now capped its worst legacy exposure with a $1.2bn cover, priced at 6.4x earnings.

## 2. Business in plain English
Everest is a Bermuda-based global reinsurer and specialty insurer: it takes on underwriting risk from other insurers (reinsurance) and directly insures specialty commercial risks, collecting premiums and paying claims, and investing the "float" between the two. It makes money when premiums plus investment income exceed claims and expenses (a "combined ratio" below 100%). Its competitive position is mid-tier-large among global reinsurers (peers: RenaissanceRe, Arch, Aracle), respected in property-catastrophe reinsurance, but its US casualty/liability insurance book has been the source of repeated, market-moving reserve problems.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
b1 factors: Quality 4, Growth 3, price_vs_growth 5 (highest of the three F22 names — b1_live_scores.csv). Triage: management said no further 2026 casualty-loss-pick adjustment was needed and reported favorable prior-year development in Q2-26; at 6.4x NTM P/E and 14.5% FCF yield the stock is "priced for continued reserve problems." **This durability read is only partly confirmed**: Q1-26 ($33m favorable development) and Q2-26 (no material development disclosed) are clean, but this follows **two separate adverse-development events within the prior 12 months** (Q4-24 loss quarter and a $478m Q3-25 US-casualty charge), so two good quarters do not yet establish a new pattern — they establish that the ADC (see §6) has removed the tail risk on 2024-and-prior accident years, which is a real, durable de-risking, distinct from the still-open question of new-accident-year adequacy.

## 6. Balance sheet, capital and cash conversion
Shareholders' equity $15,430m (Jun-26) vs $15,461m (Dec-25) — roughly flat, book value per share $398.83 (ex-unrealized-loss adjustment $407.67) vs $379.70 (Dec-25); P/B 0.93x (d4), i.e. the market values Everest **below net asset value**, unusual for a reinsurer without an active going-concern issue. H1-26 operating cash flow $939m vs H1-26 net income $1,212m (~77% conversion), reasonable for a P&C (re)insurer where investment income and premium timing create normal noise; not a red flag. **Adverse Development Cover (ADC) with Longtail Re** (effective 1-Oct-2025, Stone Ridge affiliate): $1.2bn of gross limit in two layers ($700m + $500m) above $5.4bn of North America liability subject reserves for accident years 2024-and-prior, with Everest retaining a $100m co-participation per layer and sole claims-handling authority; consideration paid was ~$1.25bn of transferred in-the-money reserves plus ~$122m cash for the second layer (recognized as an incurred loss in Q4-25). This materially caps — but does not eliminate — legacy casualty tail risk; it does **not** cover new (2025+) accident years, which is where the open question sits. Net investment income improved to $562m (Q4-25) from $473m (Q4-24) on a larger, higher-yielding asset base.

## 7. Valuation snapshot & reverse DCF
Price (2026-09-25 close) $370.43; NTM P/E 6.36x (calendarised, d4) — the cheapest of the three F22 names by a wide margin; FY1 consensus EPS growth 10.4%; analyst mean target $411.38 (+11.1%). **No V1 row exists for EG** (absent from `v1_valuation_table.csv`) → v1_verdict = null; valuation view independently derived.

**Reverse DCF:** 10-year explicit growth + Gordon terminal (r = 9.5% cost of equity [rf 4.2% + beta ~0.9 × 5% ERP, plus an implicit reserve-risk premium], terminal growth 3.5%), solved against NTM EPS $58.28 (consensus) and the current price gives **implied 10-year growth ≈ −11.9% p.a.** — the market is pricing a permanent, compounding earnings decline. My evidence-based base case, given the ADC de-risking and a stabilizing (if smaller) book, is modest positive growth of ~4% p.a. once premium cuts stop subtracting from the top line — **far above** the implied rate → valuation view = **cheap** (the widest gap of the three names), *conditional on* the reserve story genuinely being closed. Bear case −8% p.a. (a third reserve-strengthening event, or premium shrinkage continuing without an offsetting margin gain — i.e., the market's implied path is close to my bear case, not a strawman); bull case +9% p.a. (de-risking holds, hard-market pricing persists in property/specialty, buybacks compound per-share value on a shrinking share count).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) the $1.2bn ADC caps the single largest known tail risk (2024-and-prior US casualty), and two subsequent quarters (Q1/Q2-26) show clean, even favorable, development; (2) trading at 0.93x book value with a 6.4x P/E, any evidence of stabilization is a large re-rating opportunity — even partial re-rating to 9–10x (still below high-quality reinsurance peers) implies large upside; (3) management is deliberately shrinking the worst-margin business (Global Wholesale & Specialty combined ratio ~95–97%) which should raise the blended margin over 2026–27 even as premium shrinks.
**Bear:** (1) Everest has now had **two distinct adverse-reserve or loss events in five quarters** (Q4-24, Q3-25) — a base rate of one surprise roughly every 12–15 months is not disproven by two clean quarters; (2) net premiums written down 26.3% YoY (Q2-26) is a large, ongoing top-line contraction that could pressure future scale/fixed-cost absorption even if margins improve; (3) the ADC does not cover 2025-and-later accident years, so if current-accident-year casualty pricing is still inadequate industry-wide (a live industry debate), Everest could face a *new* reserve problem the ADC does nothing to protect against.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Any new adverse prior-year development charge on North America casualty exceeding $150m in a single quarter (a signal the ADC's 2024-and-prior fix was insufficient or a new-year problem has emerged).
2. Group combined ratio above 100% for two consecutive quarters (last two: 91.2%, 92.0%).
3. Net premiums written declines exceeding −15% YoY for three consecutive quarters (currently 2 of the last 2 quarters at −14.7% and −26.3%; a third would signal the shrinkage is not stabilizing on schedule).
4. Book value per share (ex-AOCI) declines quarter-over-quarter for two consecutive quarters.
5. Rating-agency downgrade (A.M. Best/S&P) of Everest Re's financial-strength rating.

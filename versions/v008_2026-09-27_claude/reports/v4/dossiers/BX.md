# Blackstone Inc. (BX) — Diligence Dossier (F22, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 24–36 months. Reason: the largest alternative-asset manager globally, compounding fee-related earnings faster than the price requires, but GAAP earnings are carry-driven and volatile, standalone leverage is rising, and the BREIT/BCRED nontraded-fund liquidity model — while currently healthy — has a proven capacity to break under stress, so a full-weight position is not warranted.

**Thesis (≤25 words):** As of the 2026-09-25 close (§7), the world's largest alternative-asset manager (private equity, credit, real estate, infrastructure per Q2-26 8-K) still raising ~$70bn a quarter (§4), priced below its own recent distributable-earnings growth rate.

## 2. Business in plain English
Per its Q2 2026 10-Q (period ended 2026-06-30) and 8-K earnings release (filed 2026-07-23), Blackstone manages $1.35tn of client capital across private equity, real estate, credit/insurance and infrastructure, earning management fees on committed/invested capital plus a share of investment profits ("carried interest") once funds clear a preferred return. It does not consolidate most of the funds it manages, so its own balance sheet is small relative to AUM; the moat (per the FY2025 10-K, filed 2026-02-27) is scale (largest fundraiser in most of its categories), long-dated/locked-up and increasingly **permanent-capital** vehicles (insurance/perpetual funds), and a 30+ year realization/track-record advantage with institutional LPs.

## 6. Balance sheet & cash conversion
Consolidated GAAP assets/liabilities ($49.9bn/$27.5bn, Jun-26) are not meaningful for leverage analysis because they gross up consolidated funds/CLOs (sector playbook). The relevant figure is **standalone long-term debt**, which has risen from $11.5bn (Dec-24) to $13.3bn (Jun-26), +16% in six quarters, funding growth in the insurance/permanent-capital channel and buybacks. Per the Q2-25, Q1-26 and Q2-26 earnings releases: quarterly dividend raised from $1.03 (Q2-25) to $1.16 (Q1-26) to $1.29 (Q2-26), a payout ratio of ~85% of DE/share — high, leaving little cushion if DE dips. H1-26 GAAP operating cash flow $2.93bn against H1-26 GAAP net income of ~$1.88bn (OCF exceeds NI, a reasonable cash-conversion signal, though both are noisy at the consolidated level per the sector methodology). Dividend yield ~4.5% (Yahoo/d4).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) private-credit and infrastructure fundraising is structurally scaling as banks retreat and AI capex needs financing; (2) realizations ($144.5bn LTM) show the portfolio can actually monetize, not just mark up; (3) a growing permanent-capital/insurance base (per the sector playbook, the highest-quality alternative-manager earnings) reduces reliance on episodic fundraising cycles.
**Bear:** (1) BREIT/BCRED-style nontraded vehicles have already broken once (2022–23 BREIT gating) and remain a structural liquidity mismatch — repurchase requests being "fully met" today is a point-in-time fact, not a guarantee; (2) an 85% DE payout ratio combined with rising standalone debt leaves less room to defend the dividend in a carry-light year; (3) GAAP earnings volatility (see §4) makes headline "EPS growth" numbers easy to misread without the DE/FRE reconciliation.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Fee-earning AUM net inflows turn negative for two consecutive quarters.
2. DE/share declines YoY for two consecutive quarters (last two: +12–26% YoY — a reversal would be a material trend break).
3. Any nontraded vehicle (BREIT, BCRED, or successors) reinstates repurchase gating/proration for more than one quarter.
4. Standalone long-term debt/FRE exceeds roughly 3.0x (monitor against current ~$13.3bn debt vs. run-rate FRE).
5. Dividend cut or suspension (current payout ratio ~85% of DE leaves little room; a cut would signal a genuine earnings problem, not just prudence).

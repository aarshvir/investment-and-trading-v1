# NEE — NextEra Energy, Inc.

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** — thesis horizon 24–36 months, half weight for a named reservation. NEE is the largest US electric
utility (Florida Power & Light) plus the largest wind/solar/storage developer in the US (NextEra Energy Resources),
guiding 8%+ adjusted-EPS growth through 2032 with a track record of raising and then twice reaffirming FY2026
guidance from primary-source releases. A reverse DCF implies the market is pricing in **far less** growth than
guided (~1% vs an 8% guide) — genuinely attractive if the growth is delivered. The reservation: consolidated debt
is growing fast (+10.3% in six months) to fund a $90–100bn FPL capital program plus NEER's ~33GW backlog, and NEE
carries real, disclosed (if insurance-covered) litigation tied to the 2023 political-activity class action and the
XPLR Energy (former NextEra Energy Partners) spin-off — a bond-proxy stock with genuine financing and regulatory
execution risk, not a pure "priced for nothing" bargain.

## 2. Business in plain English

NEE is a holding company with two main segments: **FPL**, a rate-regulated electric utility serving ~12 million
people in Florida (fully regulated returns set by the Florida Public Service Commission, "FPSC"), and **NextEra
Energy Resources ("NEER")**, which develops, owns and operates wind, solar and battery-storage projects (mostly
under long-term contracted PPAs) plus competitive gas/transmission assets across most of the US. FPL earns a
regulated return on its capital base by investing in grid, generation and reliability infrastructure; NEER earns
contracted, largely fixed-price cash flows by building and selling clean-energy output to utilities and, increasingly,
hyperscale data-center/large-load customers. The moat is scale plus execution track record: NEE can originate,
permit, finance and build renewable/storage projects faster and cheaper than most peers, and FPL is one of the
lowest-cost electric utilities in the country.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet — **entity scope stated explicitly for every figure**

**Consolidated NEE Inc. balance sheet (10-Q, "NextEra Energy, Inc. Condensed Consolidated Balance Sheets," as of
30 Jun 2026):** long-term debt **$98,790M** (vs **$89,556M** at 31 Dec 2025, **+10.3% in six months**); total common
shareholders' equity **$57,126M**. Debt/(debt+equity) ≈ **63.4%** — high but typical for a heavy-capex regulated/
contracted utility, not a solvency signal by itself; the trend (fast debt growth) is the item to watch.
**FPL standalone balance sheet (same 10-Q, separately presented "Florida Power & Light Company Condensed
Consolidated Balance Sheets," FPL is itself a separate SEC registrant):** FPL's own long-term debt is **$30,188M**
(vs **$28,041M** at 31 Dec 2025) — i.e. FPL carries less than a third of consolidated debt; the remaining ~$68.6bn
sits at NEER/NextEra Energy Capital Holdings ("NEECH")/Corporate. **This distinction matters** because FPL's debt
is serviced by regulated, FPSC-approved cash flows while NEER's and NEECH's are serviced by contracted/competitive
cash flows with different risk. **Free cash flow is structurally negative** (as is normal for a utility in a heavy
capex build-out): FPL alone guided ~$12–13bn of 2026 capex; total NEE capital investment plans run into the tens
of billions annually through 2032 (FPL alone: $90–100bn through 2032 per the Nov-2025 FPSC-approved four-year rate
settlement). This capex is funded by a mix of retained cash flow, parent/subsidiary debt issuance and, historically,
periodic equity issuance — a genuine dilution/financing risk that is the natural counterpart to the 8%+ EPS growth
guide. Operating cash flow: Q1 2026 $2,614M vs Q1 2025 $2,769M (essentially flat). Dividend: $2.49/share trailing
annualised (~3.3% yield at the 25 Sep 2026 close), management-targeted at ~10%/yr growth through 2026.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case

**Bull:** (1) FPL's new four-year FPSC rate settlement (effective 1 Jan 2026) locks in constructive, low-conflict
regulatory economics through 2029; (2) NEER's ~33GW backlog plus new hyperscaler/data-center demand (e.g., the
US-Japan-linked 9.5GW gas-generation award, Google PPA enabling the Duane Arnold nuclear restart) gives multi-year
revenue visibility; (3) the stock's implied growth (~1%) is far below its own guided growth (8%+), a wide margin of
safety if delivered.
**Bear:** (1) Consolidated debt growing faster (+10.3% in 6 months) than revenue funds a capex program that may
require dilutive equity issuance in a higher-for-longer rate environment; (2) NEE is a "bond proxy" whose multiple
compresses further if long rates rise; (3) the XPLR/NEP-linked securities litigation (§11) signals real governance
friction from the prior yieldco financing structure, a tail risk to reputation and management bandwidth even though
currently insurance-covered.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria

1. Full-year adjusted EPS comes in **below the low end** of the then-current guidance range (currently $3.92) at
   any year-end report.
2. Consolidated debt/(debt+equity) rises above **~68%** (from ~63.4% today) without a credible deleveraging plan,
   or NEE/NEECH/FPL suffers a **credit-rating downgrade**.
3. FPL's next base rate proceeding (post the 2025 four-year settlement expires ~2029) is resolved with an allowed
   ROE or capital-recovery mechanism materially worse than the current settlement.
4. The 2023 securities class action settlement ($150m, insurance-covered) or the XPLR securities/derivative
   litigation is **not** given final court approval and reopens as active, uninsured exposure.
5. NEER backlog-to-in-service conversion stalls (two consecutive quarters of GW placed into service below the
   trailing-four-quarter average), signalling supply-chain, interconnection or financing bottlenecks.

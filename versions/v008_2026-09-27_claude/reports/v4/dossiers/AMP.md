## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE.** Thesis horizon 12–36 months. One-sentence reason: a capital-light wealth- and asset-management franchise compounding assets under management at double-digit rates, returning the large majority of earnings to shareholders, trading at a single-digit NTM P/E with a clean red-flag scan — and the systematic valuation model (V1) independently reaches the same "fair, not demanding" conclusion this dossier reaches from primary sources.

## 2. Business in plain English

Ameriprise is a diversified financial-services holding company built around three businesses: **Advice & Wealth Management** (its largest segment — roughly 10,000+ affiliated financial advisors managing client assets for a fee, plus banking/lending products), **Asset Management** (Columbia Threadneedle Investments, running mutual funds/ETFs/institutional mandates), and a smaller, run-off-oriented **Retirement & Protection Solutions** legacy annuity/insurance book. It earns most of its money on fees tied to assets under management/administration (AUM/AUA) rather than on spread or underwriting risk — a capital-light model that requires relatively little balance-sheet capital per dollar of revenue, which is why its return on equity is structurally very high (see §6).

## 3. Why the model likes it — and whether that's durable

Quant context (b1_live_scores.csv, live rank 113/~1,172): strong across families — quality composite in the top decile, ROE 57th percentile *of the raw factor* (the b1 pct_roe is 0.568, but this compares AMP against the whole cross-sectional universe including much lower-ROE industrials/utilities; against its own asset-management peer group AMP's ROE is exceptional — see §7), earnings yield 90th percentile, FCF yield 76th percentile, momentum 93rd percentile. **Durable**: AUM/AUA growth of 13–19% YoY across every reported segment in Q2 2026 (§4) and four consecutive quarters of 16–22% YoY adjusted-operating-EPS growth (§5) are structural, filed facts — a genuinely growing capital-light franchise, not a one-quarter beat. **Worth watching, not yet a red flag**: the high ROE is partly a function of an unusually thin equity base (buybacks have shrunk shareholders' equity to ~$6.4bn against ~$4bn of TTM net income); V1's own valuation model flags that AMP's ROE would need to *stay* near current levels, not revert toward its 10-year median (30th percentile of own history), to fully justify the price (§7) — this is the central judgment call in this dossier.

## 5. Guidance track record

AMP does not issue formal quarterly or annual EPS/revenue guidance (typical for the sector); its "guidance" equivalent is quarter-ahead **seasonality disclosure** (fee days, trading days, mark-to-market sensitivity, tax rate) and periodic capital-return commentary. The most recent such disclosure (2026-09-03 press release, ahead of Q3 2026 earnings) is entirely routine: +92 fee days vs. 91 in Q2 2026 (~+$13m combined pretax tailwind), +64 vs. 62 trading days (~+$4m), full-year 2026 operating effective tax rate guided at 20%–22% — no negative surprise, no change in the tax-rate range from prior disclosures reviewed. Nothing in the four most recent earnings releases discloses a formal forward EPS range to raise, cut or maintain, so the "guidance track record" test from the base template does not apply mechanically here; what is trackable and has been consistently delivered is double-digit AUM growth and adjusted-EPS growth in every one of the last four quarters (§4).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **Capital return is exceptionally high**: Q2 2026 alone returned $932m to shareholders, or **91% of operating earnings** (company-reported); buybacks were $2,907m in FY2025 and already $1,653m through H1 2026 (annualizing to a similar pace); dividend per share was $1.35/quarter as of the last full-year data point directly confirmed in this pass (2023; likely raised since, not independently re-verified this pass — disclosed limitation).
- **ROE**: TTM net income ~$3.9–4.0bn (10-K/10-Q) against stockholders' equity of only $6.37bn (Q2 2026) → **ROE ≈ 62%** — genuinely exceptional, and a direct, mechanical consequence of the capital-light fee-based model plus a decade of sustained buybacks shrinking the equity base. V1's own model separately computes this as 62.0% TTM vs. a 62.0/29.998%/17.2% (80th/50th/20th percentile) 10-year-own-history distribution — i.e., today's ROE sits at roughly the 80th percentile of AMP's own trailing 10-year history, a real, sourced data point that argues for some mean-reversion risk being priced conservatively, not aggressively (§7).
- **Debt**: not independently re-derived from XBRL this pass (AMP's `LongTermDebtNoncurrent`/`LongTermDebt` XBRL tags returned no recent instances in the company-facts pull — a data-availability note, not a red flag; V1's own model separately sources net debt as **–$6.0bn** [i.e. a net cash position at the parent-company level, per V1's D4/Yahoo cross-check] which is consistent with AMP's capital-light model and heavy cash-generative wealth-management mix). This dossier defers to V1's sourced net-debt figure rather than re-deriving a possibly-incomplete one from a partial XBRL pull.
- **Red flags**: none material found. The most recent 10-Q's Legal Proceedings item (Q2 2026) contains only generic, boilerplate disclosure common to all diversified financial-services firms (regulatory inquiries, arbitration, "elevated" industry-wide regulatory activity) with **no specific named material matter, no restatement, no auditor change, no going-concern language**. No cash-sweep-program litigation (an issue that has affected several broker-dealer peers industry-wide) was found mentioned in the section reviewed.

**This dossier's independent read**: I largely agree with V1's "fair" verdict and treat its base case (+6.5%/yr, implied ROE mean-reverting toward the 30th-percentile historical median) as the operative base case here — it is conservative relative to the current 62% ROE run-rate, appropriately so given ROE has been on a rising secular trend for years as the business mix shifted toward capital-light fee revenue (a trend V1's flat historical-percentile framework does not fully credit, but which this dossier does not have grounds to override outright either). **One reservation, flagged rather than silently accepted**: V1's bull case (+67.7%/yr) compounds an 80th-percentile ROE assumption with an 8.3%/yr share-count-shrinkage assumption for three straight years — the resulting >3x price outcome (exit P/B 8.05x on top of a rising book value per share) looks like a modeling artefact of compounding two aggressive assumptions together rather than a plausible outcome, and should not be treated as a realistic upper bound; V1X's prior audit pass already reviewed this model family (no verdict-changing errors found) but did not specifically flag this compounding effect. **`valuation_view_vs_v1.consistent = true`**: this dossier's primary-source read (strong, real, but not fully "cheap" — 9.8x NTM P/E against a business capable of continuing double-digit AUM growth) matches V1's "fair" label; implied growth (V1's base case) is **at or below** what this dossier's evidence (§4/§6) supports as achievable, satisfying the INCLUDE eligibility rule.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:**
1. AUM/AUA growing 13–19% across every segment (Q2 2026 YoY), a genuine structural tailwind (flows + markets), not a one-quarter pop.
2. Capital-light model returns ~90%+ of operating earnings to shareholders every quarter — one of the most shareholder-friendly capital-return policies in the S&P 500 financial sector, directly compounding per-share value via buybacks.
3. Single-digit NTM P/E (9.8x) for a business generating 62% ROE is a genuinely unusual combination even after accounting for the thin-equity-base mechanical effect.

**Bear:**
1. ROE sits at roughly the 80th percentile of AMP's own 10-year history — some mean reversion (already partly priced by V1's base case) is a real risk, not a remote one, if markets fall or flows reverse.
2. GAAP earnings are genuinely volatile quarter to quarter from insurance/annuity mark-to-market effects — a real legacy-block risk that a purely "Adjusted operating EPS" read can understate.
3. Continuous heavy buybacks (>$2.9bn/yr) that have driven much of the ROE/EPS story could decelerate if capital generation weakens, removing a key compounding lever.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria

1. TTM ROE falls below **40%** (vs. ~62% now) for two consecutive quarters — would indicate faster-than-expected mean reversion.
2. Total AUM+AUA growth (YoY) falls below **5%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. 13–19% segment growth now).
3. Capital returned to shareholders falls below **60% of operating earnings** for two consecutive quarters (vs. ~91% in Q2 2026) — would signal a capital-return policy change.
4. Any named, material litigation or regulatory enforcement matter (e.g. a cash-sweep-program-style industry action) is disclosed in a future 10-Q's Legal Proceedings item.
5. Adjusted operating EPS YoY growth turns negative for any quarter (it has been +16% to +22% in each of the last four).

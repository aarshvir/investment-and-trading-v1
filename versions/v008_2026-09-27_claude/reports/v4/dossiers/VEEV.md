# Veeva Systems Inc. (VEEV) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Verdict: INCLUDE** — 24–48 month thesis horizon.
One-sentence reason: dominant, sticky, net-cash life-sciences cloud software (CRM/Vault) with four consecutive quarterly guidance raises and 16–18% revenue growth at 44–45% non-GAAP operating margin — a reverse DCF says the price requires only ~7% 10-year FCF growth, well below this analyst's 16% base case, with the company's one real legal overhang (the eight-year IQVIA dispute) already settled with no damages exchanged.

## 1. Business in plain English
Veeva sells cloud software to pharmaceutical and biotech companies: Vault CRM (managing interactions with doctors), Vault Clinical/Regulatory/Quality (managing drug development and compliance paperwork), and adjacent data/analytics products. Customers are pharma/biotech commercial and R&D organizations; revenue is subscription-based (84–87% of total) with the balance in professional services (implementation). The moat is high switching costs (life-sciences workflows are validated/regulated, so ripping out the system of record is expensive and risky) plus a purpose-built, life-sciences-specific product set that horizontal CRM/ERP vendors have not successfully replicated.

## 5. Moat, competition and secular drivers
- **Vault CRM migration:** Veeva is mid-way through migrating its own legacy Salesforce-hosted CRM product onto its own Vault platform; management cited "five top-20 biopharmas" and "12 global top-20 commitments" to Vault CRM in the FQ2 FY27 release — a genuine internal-platform-transition catalyst (higher-margin, Veeva-owned infrastructure replacing a Salesforce-hosted layer) rather than new-market share-taking, but it is real, filed evidence of continued enterprise-customer commitment.
- **Competitive position:** no other vendor offers a comparably complete, purpose-built, validated life-sciences CRM+Vault suite; Salesforce itself was historically Veeva's infrastructure landlord for the CRM product (now being exited via the Vault CRM migration above), which removes a structural dependency rather than adding a new competitive threat.
- **AI:** management commentary ("AI is opening up the next big chapter") is directionally bullish but not yet quantified in filed revenue — treated as bull-case optionality, not base case.
- **Customer concentration:** 1,500+ customers; no single-customer concentration disclosed in the releases reviewed — a positive, diversified base for a company serving a concentrated end-industry (large pharma).

## 8. Bull case
1. Four consecutive quarterly releases (Nov-2025 through Aug-2026), four consecutive full-year guidance raises, zero cuts — the cleanest execution track record in this batch, on both revenue and non-GAAP EPS.
2. Reverse-DCF-implied growth (6.7%) is roughly half this analyst's base case (16%) and below even the bear case (10%) — a large apparent margin of safety if the demonstrated growth trajectory merely continues, let alone accelerates on AI/Vault CRM.
3. Debt-free, $7.2B in liquid assets against a business generating ~$1.6B of FCF/year — no financing risk, full optionality for further R&D investment or opportunistic M&A/buybacks.

## 9. Bear case
1. Trailing 12-month price momentum is flat-to-negative (mom_12_1 raw −4.1%) despite the earnings beats — either the market has already priced in mean reversion of pharma-sector software spend, or there is a real, not-yet-visible-in-filings concern (e.g., large-pharma budget tightening) that the quantitative screen and this dossier have not fully captured; worth treating the DCF "cheapness" with some humility rather than certainty.
2. Revenue growth is now running almost entirely on subscription re-signs/expansion within an already-large, concentrated pharma customer base; a slowdown in large-pharma R&D/commercial IT budgets (a live industry theme given drug-pricing and tariff pressure discussed by other names in this program) would show up here with a lag.
3. High SBC (≈15% of revenue) means the non-GAAP EPS figures driving the guidance-raise narrative overstate cash economics to shareholders relative to the FCF figure in §6 — real, but standard for enterprise SaaS and already disclosed.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Total revenue growth falls below 12% YoY for two consecutive quarters (from the current steady 16–18%).
2. A full-year guidance range is cut (not just held or raised more slowly) at any of the next two quarterly releases (FQ3 FY27, ~Nov-2026, or FQ4/FY2027, ~Mar-2027) — would end the current four-release raise streak.
3. Non-GAAP operating margin falls below 40% for two consecutive quarters (from the current 44–45%).
4. Professional-services revenue growth turns negative YoY (a leading indicator for future subscription bookings) for two consecutive quarters.
5. Net cash position declines by more than $1B in a single quarter outside of a disclosed buyback/M&A program (would signal an unexplained cash-burn event in an otherwise FCF-generative business).

## 12. Red-flag scan
- **IQVIA litigation — resolved, not a live overhang.** Veeva and IQVIA settled all outstanding litigation (an eight-year dispute; IQVIA alleged trade-secret misappropriation, Veeva asserted antitrust counterclaims) via a Settlement Agreement dated **13-Aug-2025**: **no damages were paid by either party**, both sides dismissed all claims with prejudice, and the companies entered mutual data-access agreements. The $30.6M GAAP charge in FY2026 represents Veeva's own contingency-fee legal costs, not a payment to IQVIA. This is now closed and is disclosed here for completeness, not as an ongoing risk.
- Two small, apparently routine Form 4 sales by the President & Chief Customer Officer (Thomas D. Schwenger: 5,000 shares/$1.0M on 16-Jul-2026; 1,000 shares/$265,200 on 14-Sep-2026) were the only insider activity found in a light check; the CEO (Peter Gassner) has made no reported open-market sale since Feb-2023. No unusual insider-selling pattern identified, though this was a light (not Form-4-database-exhaustive) check.
- No auditor changes, restatements or going-concern language found in sources reviewed.

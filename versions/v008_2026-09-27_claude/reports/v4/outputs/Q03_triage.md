## Advanced for full diligence

- **BF-B**: Revenue is declining and spirits volumes are structurally soft, but the multiple has already de-rated to 15x NTM on a still-high-quality brand portfolio, worth full diligence to separate cyclical destocking from permanent share loss.
- **STZ**: FY2027 Q1 confirmed depletions still negative and outlook cut on Hispanic-consumer softness, but stock trades at 9.4x NTM P/E and 11.4% FCF yield versus a 44% Street target upside -- worth full diligence to gauge how much of the beer slowdown is cyclical/macro versus permanent share loss.
- **CL**: Reasonable 21x NTM multiple for a company with unmatched oral-care share, mid-single-digit growth and a plausible path back to historical margins -- worth a full look at the pace of margin recovery.
- **HSY**: Solid mid-single-digit revenue growth, a reasonable 17.5x NTM multiple and 23% Street upside, with cocoa cost relief as a plausible margin-expansion catalyst -- worth full diligence on the pace of gross-margin recovery.
- **MDLZ**: Reasonable 18x NTM multiple for a global category leader with mid-single-digit growth and margin recovery potential as cocoa costs normalize, similar setup to Hershey but with more EM/diversification upside.
- **MKC**: Strong revenue growth (+16.7%) and high margins (22%) against a headline-cheap NTM multiple, but the EPS-growth and P/E figures conflict and must be reconciled first; if normalized earnings confirm the setup, this is a high-quality name at a reasonable price.
- **EL**: FY2026 results confirmed the turnaround is real -- diluted EPS $2.51 (+66% YoY), FY2027 guidance of $3.10-3.35 EPS (+24-34%), restructuring completed with $1.2bn of savings, MAC back to #1 in color cosmetics, and travel retail (Hainan) back to double-digit growth -- a 27x NTM multiple looks reasonable against that growth.
- **PG**: Growth is modest (1.5% revenue) as expected for a company this size, but at a fair 21x NTM multiple for top-tier quality and consistent execution, PG merits a full periodic review as a core defensive holding candidate.
- **MNST**: Genuine double-digit revenue growth (+20%) and share gains justify a premium multiple (34x NTM); worth full diligence on how durable the growth is as energy-drink category growth normalizes.
- **PEP**: Cheap relative to peer KO at 14.5x NTM P/E with 20% Street upside, solid revenue growth, and an activist catalyst pushing for portfolio/cost changes -- worth full diligence on the volume-recovery and value-unlock thesis.
- **PM**: Double-digit revenue growth (+10.4%) driven by a real smoke-free product transformation (Zyn US share leadership) justifies a full look despite a full-ish 21x multiple and already-strong 12-month momentum.
- **CVX**: Genuine multi-year production growth (Guyana plus Permian) at a cheap 13.9x NTM multiple and minimal leverage -- worth full diligence on Hess integration and Guyana ramp economics.
- **XOM**: Cheapest-in-class 14.2x NTM multiple with near-zero leverage (0.5x) and genuine multi-year production growth from Permian and Guyana -- the highest-quality energy name in the batch.
- **SLB**: Reasonable 17x NTM multiple for the global leader in an international upcycle, with digital/AI-enabled service technology as a durable differentiator -- worth full diligence on how much of the international recovery is already reflected in the stock.

## Not advanced

- **SYY**: Steady mid-single-digit growth and fair 15x NTM valuation, but structurally thin (~2%) net margins and no clear mispricing means it does not clear the bar over higher-conviction names in the batch.
- **CASY**: Genuinely excellent growth (24% revenue, 33% momentum) but at 27x NTM P/E and 2.3% FCF yield the price already assumes continued flawless execution, so price-vs-growth fails the bar for immediate full diligence.
- **KR**: Cheap at 10.9x NTM P/E with decent FCF yield, but low single-digit revenue growth and thin reported margins reflect a structurally low-growth, price-competitive category rather than a clear mispricing.
- **CHD**: High-quality, well-run compounder, but at 24x NTM P/E with only ~9% Street upside and 1.6% revenue growth the price already reflects the quality, leaving little margin of safety.
- **CLX**: Cheap-looking 14x NTM P/E is a classic value trap: revenue is shrinking, leverage is elevated for the sector, and momentum is sharply negative, consistent with real structural pressure rather than mispricing.
- **KMB**: Underlying staples business is solid and cheap at 13x NTM P/E, but the pending Kenvue mega-merger materially changes the leverage and integration-risk profile -- better revisited once deal financing and pro forma guidance are finalized near the 2026 close.
- **GIS**: Cheap headline P/E is offset by a negative margin/ROE print (impairment-driven), shrinking revenue and a high yield that looks more like distress pricing than a bargain -- fits the 'cheap but structurally declining' pattern to avoid.
- **HRL**: Persistently weak margins and returns on capital versus staples peers, with no evidence in the data of a turnaround catalyst -- does not clear the quality or growth bar despite a superficially cheap forward multiple.
- **KHC**: Persistent negative margins/ROE, shrinking sales and an unresolved company split are disqualifying red flags rather than a value opportunity.
- **SJM**: Positive momentum (+25.7%) suggests an early recovery, but weak ROE and the acquisition-related impairment history mean quality has not yet been demonstrated at a level that clears the bar.
- **TSN**: Cheap forward multiple reflects a cyclical trough rather than mispriced quality; without a moat or durable pricing power this is a cycle call, not a triage-stage conviction idea.
- **KVUE**: This is a merger-arbitrage situation, not a standalone fundamental opportunity -- the price already reflects the deal terms, so full fundamental diligence adds little until/unless the deal breaks.
- **KDP**: Leverage is elevated even on management's own guided path (targeting 4.5x, still high for staples) and the pending two-way spin-off creates real structural uncertainty about how value accrues to today's shareholders -- best revisited once the separation terms and pro forma leverage are finalized.
- **KO**: Unquestionable quality, but at 25x NTM P/E after a strong 12-month run the stock offers minimal Street-implied upside and a depressed FCF yield, so price already reflects most of the good news -- better to wait for a pullback.
- **MO**: Cheap 11.8x P/E and high FCF yield are offset by minimal Street-implied upside (1.7%) and durable regulatory/litigation risk, consistent with a yield-trap rather than a mispriced quality name.
- **BKR**: Currently flat EPS growth and declining revenue reflect a soft North America activity backdrop; the LNG/new-energy order book is a real but not-yet-proven growth driver, so it does not yet clear the growth or quality bar.
- **HAL**: Cheap (11.9x NTM) with strong momentum (+52%) and 32% Street upside, but growth here is more cyclical-activity-driven than structural, and NA completions pricing power has been a chronic weak spot -- a cyclical trade more than a triage-stage conviction idea.

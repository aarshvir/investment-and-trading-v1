# HBAN — Huntington Bancshares Incorporated — Diligence Dossier (Agent F44, wave 2, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** — evidence-based valuation, credit quality and execution (two completed integrations, systems conversion done) support full weight; the market has not yet re-rated the stock for the accretion already delivered. Thesis horizon: 12–24 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Huntington is a Columbus, Ohio-based super-regional bank that closed two acquisitions within weeks of each other in early 2026 — Veritex Holdings (Texas, systems converted mid-January 2026) and Cadence Bank (Texas/South, closed 1 Feb 2026, systems converted mid-June 2026) — to build a Texas/South franchise on top of its legacy Midwest retail and commercial-banking base. It earns money on the spread between what it pays depositors and what it charges borrowers (commercial, consumer, auto, mortgage), plus fee income from wealth management, capital markets and payments.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Triage (Q06) flagged +36% forward EPS growth, +48% revenue growth (both mechanically driven by the acquisitions, like FITB) and a 9.2x NTM P/E, noting "flat momentum despite good news." That last observation holds up under diligence: both deals are fully closed and both systems conversions are complete (Veritex Jan-26, Cadence mid-June-26) — Huntington is **further through integration than FITB** (whose Comerica conversion was still pending as of the Q2 2026 release) — yet HBAN's NTM multiple is cheaper than FITB's. The durable part of the story: net interest margin has expanded every quarter shown (3.11%→3.24%/3.21%(seq. dip then recovery)→sequential improvement), tangible book value per share has grown for four straight quarters ($9.13→$9.55/$9.89 seasonal dip→$9.65), and the bank resumed buybacks ($159mm in Q2 2026, $309mm YTD, ~19mm shares) while FITB has not — a tangible signal of confidence in the combined balance sheet.

## 5. Guidance track record
Like FITB, Huntington's press releases carry no quantified full-year guidance — only qualitative commentary from the CEO. That commentary has been consistently constructive: Q1 2026 ("strong start to 2026... excellent credit performance"), Q2 2026 ("by the fourth quarter, the full earnings power of these partnerships will be clearly evident... confident in our outlook... positioned to achieve our financial targets"). No cut or walk-back identified in the reviewed window. As with FITB, the absence of a quantified target (e.g., a specific cost-synergy dollar figure or an ROTCE target with a date) limits how precisely "guidance raised/maintained/cut" can be assessed — flagged as a confidence limiter, not resolved.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** Q2 2026 adjusted EPS ($0.39) exceeds GAAP ($0.33) by $0.06, driven by acquisition-related Notable Items ($152mm pre-tax) — a real, disclosed, merger-specific gap, not a recurring add-back pattern. Adjusted ROTCE 17.5% vs GAAP 15.1%.
- **Capital:** CET1 10.0% (Q2 2026) vs 10.2% (Q1 2026) — modest decline, but HBAN resumed and continued buybacks through the same period ($159mm in Q2 2026), which FITB has not done — a real difference in disclosed management confidence. Adjusted CET1 (incl. AOCI ex cash-flow hedges) 9.0%, down from 9.2%.
- **Credit:** allowance for credit losses $3.4bn / 1.78% of loans, essentially flat q/q; net charge-offs 0.25% (Q2 2026), among the better levels shown, but nonperforming-asset ratio has risen for two straight quarters (0.72%→0.85%) even as charge-offs stayed low — the same "NPA drift while NCOs look fine" pattern flagged at FITB, and worth cross-checking against both banks' Texas commercial real estate exposure given both just added large Texas books.
- **Funding:** average deposits +9% q/q, +37% y/y (acquisition effect); average loans +9% q/q, +42% y/y, with commercial loans growing fastest (+59% y/y) — consistent with two commercially-oriented acquisitions.
- **Share count & capital returns:** buybacks resumed at scale (~19mm shares YTD) even while integrating two deals — this is a positive signal not present at FITB, though it also means less capital cushion if credit deteriorates.

## 7. Valuation
No V1 systematic valuation row exists for HBAN — `v1_verdict` is null in the summary JSON. Using the same P/TBV excess-return framework as FITB (appropriate for a bank; FCFF DCF is not): at the 25-Sep-2026 close of **$15.64** and TBVPS of $9.65, HBAN trades at **1.62x tangible book** — materially cheaper than FITB's 2.25x despite comparable or better underlying returns. Solving (ROE−g)/(COE−g) = 1.62 with a long-run adjusted ROTCE assumption of ~16% (between the Q2 2026 adjusted 17.5% and a haircut for integration risk) and a 10% cost of equity implies the market is pricing in only **~0–1% perpetual growth** — essentially a no-growth, "prove it" valuation. My own base case (mid-single-digit organic growth once Cadence synergies are fully realized by Q4 2026, per management's own commentary, plus a resumed buyback compounding TBVPS) is well above zero. **Implied vs base: below** — this is the strongest evidence-based reason for the INCLUDE verdict: the market is pricing HBAN as if the two 2026 acquisitions add nothing, while the primary-source numbers (NIM expansion, buybacks resumed, systems conversions both complete) say otherwise.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Both acquisitions are fully integrated (systems converted) months ahead of FITB's still-pending Comerica conversion — Huntington should show clean, comparable quarters sooner. (2) Management's own language ("by the fourth quarter, the full earnings power... will be clearly evident") sets a testable, near-term catalyst. (3) At 1.62x TBV for a ~15–17% ROTCE franchise that has already resumed buybacks, the valuation gap to FITB (2.25x TBV) is hard to justify once Q4 2026 results are clean.
**Bear:** (1) NPA ratio rising two quarters running (0.72%→0.85%) while the bank just added two sizable, unseasoned (from HBAN's perspective) loan books — if this is nascent Texas CRE or C&I stress rather than noise, credit cost could surprise higher just as buybacks are consuming capital. (2) CET1 at 10.0% (adjusted 9.0%) with continued buybacks leaves less room to absorb a credit shock than FITB's more conservative capital stance. (3) "Flat momentum despite good news" (triage's own observation) could mean the market has already seen something in Huntington's Texas book that these two quarters of data don't yet show.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Nonperforming-asset ratio rises above **1.00%** (vs 0.85% at Q2 2026) for two consecutive quarters.
2. CET1 ratio falls below **9.5%** (reported) without a disclosed pause in buybacks.
3. Adjusted ROTCE fails to reach **17%** by Q4 2026 (management's own implied bar — "full earnings power... clearly evident" by Q4).
4. Net charge-off ratio rises above **0.45%** for two consecutive quarters (vs 0.25% at Q2 2026).
5. Efficiency ratio fails to improve below **58%** by Q1 2027 (vs 61.5% at Q2 2026, post both systems conversions).

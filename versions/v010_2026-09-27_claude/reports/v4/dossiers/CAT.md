# CAT — Caterpillar Inc. (Agent F83, wave 5)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (not REJECT — business quality is real, but the 25 Sep 2026 close prices in far more growth than we can underwrite). Horizon: 12–36 months; revisit on a pullback or on evidence the AI-power-genset cycle is structural rather than a backlog spike.

## 2. Business in plain English
Caterpillar makes construction, mining and off-highway equipment, plus diesel/gas engines (Energy & Transportation) sold worldwide through an independent dealer network, with a captive finance arm (Cat Financial) supporting sales. It earns on equipment margins, a large recurring parts/services annuity, and dealer financing spreads. It is the #1 global player in heavy construction/mining equipment with scale, dealer-network and parts-availability moats.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Q09 triage flagged "first-ever $20B quarter" and momentum +69% over 12 months, driven largely by Energy & Transportation — specifically large reciprocating engines/gensets sold into data-center backup power. This is a genuine new demand vector (confirmed in Q2 2026 results, 8-K accession 0000018230-26-000040, source 1) layered on a still-cyclical construction/mining base. Durability risk: the data-center genset order book is lumpy and could air-pocket if hyperscaler capex slows; the triage's own red flag ("much of the re-rating already in the price") is the central issue this dossier addresses.

## 8. Bull case
1. Data-center backup-power demand is structural and multi-year, not a one-quarter spike (Q2'26 beat + raised FY guide).
2. Non-residential construction and mining replacement cycles remain intact; parts/services annuity cushions cyclicality.
3. Pricing power evident in the 430bp YoY adjusted-margin expansion (17.6%→21.9%).

## 9. Bear case
1. Valuation already implies ~30% 10-year growth vs 11% consensus — any deceleration re-rates the multiple down hard (V1 bear case: −49.8% ann. 3y).
2. Financial Products debt +$1.84B in H1'26 signals receivables growth that could reverse if end-market financing tightens.
3. Momentum +69% over 12 months (triage flag) raises single-quarter-beat/single-macro-shock downside risk.

## Kill criteria (measurable)
1. Two consecutive quarters of Energy & Transportation (or total) revenue growth decelerating below the FY guidance's low end (currently "mid-teens").
2. Adjusted operating margin falls back below 19% (vs 21.9% in Q2'26) for two consecutive quarters.
3. Consolidated net debt (excl. Financial Products captive funding) rises materially — i.e., MP&E-segment debt increases >$1B in a half-year, unlike H1'26's ~flat MP&E debt.
4. Backlog ($72B at Q2'26) declines >10% quarter-over-quarter.
5. NTM P/E falls below its own 5-year median (an exit signal, not an entry one, given current WATCH) — track via v4 D3/D4 data.

# Arista Networks, Inc. (ANET) — Diligence Dossier (F27, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — 12–24 month thesis horizon. Execution and moat are exceptional (accelerating growth, 45%+ non-GAAP operating margins, net-cash balance sheet, four straight quarters of beat-and-raise), but the 25 Sep 2026 close ($206.55, mkt cap ≈$260.5bn) already prices in high-teens free-cash-flow growth sustained for a full decade — above this analyst's evidence-based base case. Two customers are >40% of revenue. Re-underwrite on a pullback that brings the implied growth to ≤15%/yr, or on evidence of customer diversification.

## 2. Business in plain English
Arista sells high-performance networking switches and its EOS software (a single, modular network operating system across the whole product line) to hyperscale cloud, AI-cluster, enterprise campus and service-provider customers. It makes money selling hardware with high-margin embedded software/support, and increasingly selling AI-cluster "back-end" and "front-end" networking fabric to a small number of very large cloud/AI customers. Competitive position: the #2 data-center networking vendor after Cisco by most measures, with a reputation for software quality and reliability that has let it win share inside the largest hyperscalers' AI build-outs.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Quant flags (b1_live_scores.csv): composite decile 8, quintile 4, live_rank 149/1172; Quality percentile ~50–79% across sub-factors (gp/assets 49th, ROE 60th, OCF/assets 79th), Value poor (bp 16th pct — expensive), Momentum 12-1m 82nd pct, SUE 98th pct (strong recent earnings surprises). The growth is **not** an artefact: revenue growth has *accelerated* for four consecutive quarters (27.5% → 28.9% → 35.1% → 37.7% YoY) on real unit/AI-cluster demand, confirmed in SEC filings, not a one-off comp or restatement. The valuation richness (low B/P) is real and is the central risk, not a data error.

- **Implied 10-year FCF CAGR: ~16.9%** to justify the current EV under a standard 2-stage DCF (explicit 10y + Gordon terminal).
- Bear case (customer concentration bites — one hyperscaler in-sources networking silicon or pauses capex; growth decelerates to high-single-digits within 2–3 years): ≈8–10% 10y FCF CAGR.
- **Base case (evidence-based): ≈14–15%.** Current growth (30–38% YoY) is real, but a decade of 17%+ FCF growth would be historically rare for a company already at a $10.5bn TTM revenue run-rate; AI-cluster networking capex cycles are also known to be lumpy (hyperscaler capex budgets reset annually).
- Bull case (AI-fabric share gains continue beyond front-end into back-end fabric, no customer losses): ≈20–22%.
- **Implied (16.9%) is above base case (~14–15%) → verdict WATCH, not INCLUDE**, per program discipline: a great business priced for more growth than the evidence supports.
- **scenario_returns_3y (annualised, this analyst's estimate, not a formal model):** bear ≈ −12%/yr (growth decelerates to ~5–8%/yr, NTM multiple compresses ~42x→25x); base ≈ +10%/yr (growth ~18–20%/yr near-term normalizing, multiple ~42x→35x); bull ≈ +29%/yr (growth stays 25–30%/yr, multiple flat-to-up).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) AI-cluster networking is a multi-year, structurally growing capex category and Arista's EOS software moat is winning share inside the largest hyperscalers; (2) net-cash balance sheet and 45%+ operating margins give enormous optionality with no leverage risk; (3) guidance has been beaten and raised for four straight quarters with growing magnitude, evidencing real demand visibility (backlog/bookings), not a one-time pull-forward.
**Bear:** (1) two end customers were 26% and 16% of FY2025 revenue (10-K risk factors) — loss, delay or in-sourcing by either would move results materially; (2) Nvidia's Spectrum-X networking push and hyperscaler in-house silicon (e.g., custom ASIC switches at Meta/Microsoft/Amazon) are direct, well-funded competitive threats named in the company's own risk factors; (3) at the current price the market already requires ~17%/yr FCF growth for 10 years — a high bar that leaves little room for a normal cyclical air-pocket in AI capex.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Revenue from the two largest customers (26%/16% of FY2025 revenue) falls below 30% combined for two consecutive quarters without an offsetting new large customer disclosed (loss of concentration, but also loss of the visibility that supports the growth thesis) — reassess either way.
2. YoY revenue growth decelerates below 15% for two consecutive quarters (vs. 27.5–37.7% over the last four) — the core "durable AI-networking growth" thesis is broken.
3. Non-GAAP operating margin falls below 44% for two consecutive quarters (vs. 44.7–49.9% over the last four) — pricing power or mix eroding.
4. Company guides below the prior quarter's actual revenue (a guidance *cut* in absolute terms, not just a deceleration in growth rate) — the beat-and-raise streak ends.
5. Net cash position falls below $8bn without a value-accretive acquisition disclosed (unexplained cash burn or large buyback at a rich multiple).

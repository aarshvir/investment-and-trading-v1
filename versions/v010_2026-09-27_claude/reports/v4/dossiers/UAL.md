# UAL — United Airlines Holdings, Inc.

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH.** Horizon 12–24 months. Execution has genuinely been strong (16% revenue growth, raised full-year guidance, active deleveraging), but the 25-Sep-2026 close ($113.99) prices in ~12.8%/year FCF growth for 10 years — well above both this analyst's own base case (~4–6%/year, reflecting airline cyclicality and structural cost inflation) and V1's own base case (return of −3.4%/year at a 3.4%/year revenue-growth, 9.3x-exit-multiple assumption). This is the textbook case the wave-1 brief warns against: a good business priced for more than it can reliably deliver over a decade.

## 2. Business in plain English
United is a global hub-and-spoke network airline (Chicago, Denver, Houston, LA, Newark, SF, DC, and a growing Pacific hub network) with an unusually strong international and premium-cabin (Polaris) franchise relative to US peers. It makes money selling passenger tickets, cargo space and loyalty-program (MileagePlus) points, with margins driven by capacity discipline, fuel costs, labor costs and the mix of higher-yield international/premium travel versus domestic coach.

## 5. Guidance track record (last 3 full-year guides, company 8-K exhibits)
- **Jan-2026 (Q4 2025 release):** FY2026 adjusted diluted EPS guided **$12–$14**.
- **Apr-2026 (Q1 2026 release):** guidance **cut to $7–$11**, explicitly attributed to a jet-fuel cost spike driven by the Middle East war, not to weaker demand (Q1 2026 results themselves beat estimates: adjusted EPS $1.19 vs. ~$1.07 consensus, revenue $14.6bn vs. ~$14.37bn consensus).
- **Jul-2026 (Q2 2026 release):** guidance **raised to $9.00–$11.00** (bottom of range lifted from $7 to $9; top held at $11) — a genuine, evidenced partial recovery from the fuel shock, not a return to the original $12–$14 range.
- Management stated it expects to recover 80–90% of the incremental fuel cost in Q3 2026 and 100% by Q4 2026 (assuming current oil prices), and reiterated confidence in double-digit pre-tax margins in 2027.

**Assessment:** this is a credible, evidence-based "cut-then-partial-raise" pattern driven by an external, dated shock (war-driven oil spike — the same macro driver flagged for EOG in this program's earlier diligence, F15), with the underlying demand/RASM trend actually improving throughout. It is a mark in favor of management credibility, but the *level* of guidance ($9–$11) is still below the original $12–$14, i.e., the fuel shock has not been fully offset yet.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **TTM net leverage 2.2x** (company-reported, Q2 2026 release) — down from higher levels, with **$3.7bn of new liquidity raised** and **~$1bn of higher-cost debt prepaid** since the start of Q2 2026. Available liquidity $19.6bn. Management explicitly targets an **investment-grade rating in 2026**.
- Total debt & financial liabilities (company-reported) $26.5bn; XBRL long-term debt + capital leases $24.29bn (30-Jun-2026), down from $21.68bn+$2.97bn≈$24.65bn combined LT+current at year-end 2024 and a further step-down from $17.17bn+$4.10bn LT+current at year-end 2025 under a different tag split — the debt-reduction trend across 2024→2026 is directionally clear and corroborated by two independent XBRL tag families.
- **TTM (Q3'25–Q2'26) FCF ≈ $2.54bn** (CFO $8.91bn − capex $6.37bn), independently re-derived and matching V1's D3 TTM figure to the dollar (cross-check passed). FCF yield ≈ 6.9% on today's market cap, in line with V1's 6.4% SBC-adjusted figure.
- No dividend; buybacks are modest and inconsistent ($82mn in 9M 2024, $610mn in 9M 2025, only $27mn in H1 2026) — capital return is secondary to debt paydown right now, consistent with the stated investment-grade target.
- No litigation, DOJ action, restatement or auditor issue specific to United was identified in this search window (Boeing-related 737 MAX litigation involves Boeing and crash victims' representatives, not United as a defendant). United's 2024 pilot contract (35.1% raise over 4 years) appears to be the source of the Q2 2026 $184mn "labor contract ratification bonus" special item — an already-known, disclosed labor cost, not a new surprise.

## 7. Valuation — reconciled against V1 (`v1_valuation_table.csv` / `v1_valuation.json`)

V1's scenarios: **base case revenue growth 3.4%/yr, margin 5.2%, exit multiple 9.3x → annualised 3-yr return of −3.4%/yr; bull case (12.3%/yr revenue, 6.1% margin, 12.0x exit) → +29.6%/yr; bear case is a degenerate negative-price result (a −1.8%/yr revenue, −1.8% margin scenario combined with a 6.25x exit multiple on negative EPS), which should be read as an approximate "floor near −100%" rather than a literal number** (a known modeling artefact this program has already documented for other cyclical/loss-making names).

**I agree with V1's verdict and reasoning.** United's own Q2 2026 guidance ($9–$11 adjusted EPS, i.e., a partial recovery from a fuel shock) and its own long-run delivered growth (5.5%/10yr) both sit far below the 12.8%/year the current price requires for a full decade. Airlines are structurally cyclical, capital-intensive and exposed to fuel/labor cost inflation that historically prevents sustained double-digit FCF compounding; the current strong quarter (16% revenue growth, raised guidance) is encouraging execution, not evidence that the business can compound at that rate for 10 years. **implied_vs_base = above** — this is the specific case the wave-1 brief calls out: good business, priced for more than the evidence supports.

## 8. Bull case
1. Deleveraging is real and evidenced (TTM net leverage 2.2x, new liquidity raised, high-cost debt prepaid), with an explicit, credible path to investment-grade in 2026 — a re-rating catalyst if achieved.
2. Revenue growth accelerated to 16% yoy in Q2 2026 despite a $2.3bn fuel headwind, with management guiding 80–90% fuel-cost recovery by Q3 and 100% by Q4 2026 — near-term margin recovery is plausible and management-guided, not speculative.
3. The Polaris/international premium-cabin franchise is a genuine structural differentiator versus lower-cost domestic-focused peers, supporting a durable revenue-per-seat premium.

## Bear case
1. The price requires 12.8%/year FCF growth for a full decade from a cyclical, capital-intensive, fuel- and labor-cost-exposed business that has delivered 5.5%/year over the last 10 years — a gap this large has historically closed through price decline, not sustained outperformance.
2. FY2026 guidance was cut by more than half at the low end (from $12–$14 to $7–$11) within a single year due to an external fuel shock, illustrating how quickly the earnings base can move against the company; the subsequent partial raise ($9–$11) still leaves guidance below where the year started.
3. Trailing 5-year ROE (33.9%) is likely flattered by post-pandemic pricing power and capacity discipline across the industry; if competitive capacity additions or a demand slowdown normalize yields, the "quality" score the model rewards today may prove cycle-dependent rather than structural.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (thesis-invalidation triggers)
1. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance is cut again from the current $9.00–$11.00 range (a second cut within the same fiscal year).
2. TTM net leverage rises back above 3.0x (from 2.2x currently).
3. Quarterly RASM growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters.
4. Full fuel-cost recovery (100% guided by Q4 2026) fails to materialize and adjusted operating margin falls below 4% for two consecutive quarters.
5. Net debt/EBITDA-equivalent leverage metrics prevent United from reaching an investment-grade rating by end of 2027 (management's own stated 2026 target slipping by a year or more).

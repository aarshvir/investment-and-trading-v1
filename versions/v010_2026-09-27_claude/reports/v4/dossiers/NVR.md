# NVR, Inc. (NVR) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F99, standard depth)

**1. Verdict: WATCH.** The highest-quality balance sheet and capital-allocation model among homebuilders (no
parent-level debt, capital-light land-option strategy, relentless buybacks) — but revenue and EPS are actively
decelerating and then contracting through the current housing downturn, with no confirmed trough yet. A great
franchise, but the evidence right now is a deteriorating trend, not a stabilizing one; revisit once orders/backlog
turn.

**2. Business in plain English.** NVR builds and sells single-family homes, townhomes and condominiums under the
Ryan Homes, NVHomes and Heartland Homes brands (Homebuilding segment), and originates/sells the mortgages on those
homes through its wholly owned subsidiary NVR Mortgage Finance, Inc. ("NVRM," Mortgage Banking segment;
consolidated, Q2 2026 10-Q, filed 2026-08-05). Uniquely among large public homebuilders, NVR does not buy and hold
raw land: it controls finished lots through non-recourse purchase options, which converts land-price risk into a
capped, forfeitable deposit rather than a balance-sheet asset — the source of its industry-leading ROE and its
lack of parent-level debt.

**3. Why the model likes it / durability.** b1_live_scores.csv: composite decile 4/10 (below median), with a
strong ROE percentile (0.50, raw ROE 33.7% TTM) and free-cash-flow-yield percentile (0.59) but a **negative
12-1-month momentum** (pct_mom_12_1 only 0.19, raw mom_12_1 −21.4%) and a very weak SUE (earnings-surprise)
percentile of 0.05 — the model is picking up genuine quality/capital-efficiency, but the momentum and
earnings-surprise components are flagging exactly the deterioration this dossier confirms in the primary filings
(§4). This is a case where quant quality signals and quant momentum signals disagree, and the primary-source
evidence sides with momentum: the business is currently getting worse, not better, even though its long-run
economics remain excellent.

**6. Earnings quality & balance sheet.** **Entity scope is critical for NVR and was mishandled in earlier waves
(per the wave-6 mandate) — stated explicitly here.** Consolidated stockholders' equity was **$3,391.2m** at
2026-06-30, down from $3,864.9m at FY2025-end (2025-12-31) and $3,494.0m at 2026-03-31 (10-Q, filed 2026-08-05,
balance sheet) — equity has *fallen* for two consecutive quarters despite the company remaining solidly
profitable, because buybacks are outrunning retained earnings (see below). Consolidated assets fell from
$5,856.9m (2025-12-31) to $5,470.6m (2026-06-30); consolidated liabilities fell from $1,992.1m to $2,079.4m over
the same window (liabilities roughly flat/slightly up while assets and equity both fell — consistent with cash
being used for buybacks). **NVR the homebuilding parent carries no long-term corporate debt** (`LongTermDebt` XBRL
tag last populated in 2013 at the parent level, i.e., structurally absent since then) — the "total debt" figure a
generic data feed may show (~$1.09bn per Yahoo aggregation) is **not parent-level homebuilding debt**; it reflects
NVRM's (the wholly owned Mortgage Banking subsidiary's) **non-recourse mortgage repurchase facility** with U.S.
Bank N.A. as agent, which funds mortgage loans held for sale pending sale into the secondary market and is
explicitly non-recourse to NVR the parent (Q2 2026 10-Q, filed 2026-08-05; the facility was amended 2026-07-10,
extending its term to 2027-07-08). Confusing this subsidiary warehouse line with parent homebuilding debt was
exactly the wave-3 GM/RJF-style error this program is trying to eliminate — flagged and corrected here. Diluted
share count fell from roughly 3.19m (FY2023) to 2.80m (FY2025 10-K, filed 2026-02-11), a **−12.3% two-year**
reduction — an unusually aggressive, debt-free buyback pace that has historically been the primary driver of
NVR's per-share growth and now mechanically amplifies the current EPS decline's optics relative to net-income
decline (net income fell less than EPS in some quarters is not the pattern here — both fell sharply — but the
buyback pace means a stabilization in net income would show up faster in EPS than at most peers).

**7. Valuation snapshot & reverse DCF.** Price $6,297.60 (25-Sep-2026 close). Trailing P/E 16.37x (TTM EPS
$384.73), forward P/E 15.72x (consensus FY2026 EPS $400.62, Yahoo/d4); no dividend; FCF yield ~5.5% (Yahoo/d4,
TTM operating cash flow $1,066.6m less capex, against $16.78bn market cap). No V1 row exists for NVR →
v1_verdict = null. Reverse DCF (Gordon growth on FCF yield ≈1/P-FCF of ~18.3x, assuming buybacks return
substantially all FCF to shareholders, and a 9.0% cost of equity appropriate for a cyclical, moderate-beta (0.91)
homebuilder): **implied perpetual FCF/EPS growth ≈ 3.0–3.5%** — modest, and roughly consistent with a "normalized,
through-cycle" growth rate for a mature large-cap homebuilder rather than a demand for a quick housing-market
recovery. On that basis the *valuation* is not demanding even after the recent selloff-adjacent multiple; the
concern is evidence of a business still actively deteriorating, which is a business-quality/timing question, not
a valuation one — hence WATCH rather than REJECT or INCLUDE.

**8. Bull case:** (1) Zero parent-level debt and a capital-light land-option model mean NVR can keep buying back
stock through the downturn without balance-sheet stress, unlike peers carrying land inventory and mortgage debt at
the parent; (2) implied reverse-DCF growth (~3–3.5%) is low enough that even a modest housing recovery (rate cuts,
inventory normalization) would exceed it; (3) 33.7% TTM ROE, even depressed from cycle peaks, remains far above
any homebuilder peer, reflecting a genuinely differentiated operating model, not leverage. **Bear case:** (1)
Two consecutive quarters of double-digit revenue declines (−19.4%, then −10.9% YoY) and EPS declines (−41.8%, then
−30.4%) show no confirmed trough — a third consecutive severe quarter would be a clear kill-criterion breach;
(2) consolidated equity has fallen for two straight quarters even as the company stays profitable, meaning the
capital-return pace is not obviously sustainable if earnings keep falling; (3) mortgage-rate-sensitive affordability
is a macro variable outside management's control, and this dossier did not independently pull NVR's new-order/
backlog disclosure this pass — the true demand trend (vs. just reported/closed revenue, which lags orders by
several months) is not yet confirmed either way.

**9. Key risks & kill criteria:** (i) Consolidated revenue declines >10% YoY for a third consecutive quarter;
(ii) consolidated diluted EPS declines >25% YoY for a third consecutive quarter; (iii) consolidated stockholders'
equity falls for a third consecutive quarter while net income stays positive (buyback pace outrunning earnings
power); (iv) new-order or backlog dollar value (not independently checked this pass — a follow-up must confirm)
turns negative YoY for two consecutive quarters; (v) NVRM's non-recourse repurchase facility is not renewed or is
downsized materially at its 2027-07-08 maturity (a genuine, if subsidiary-level, funding-line risk to monitor).

**11. Red-flag scan.** No SEC/DOJ investigation, auditor change, restatement, or going-concern language found in
the 10-Q text reviewed. Historical/ordinary-course litigation only: individual homeowner warranty and
consumer-protection disputes (e.g., a Pennsylvania jury verdict of ~$23k warranty + ~$122k consumer-protection
damages against an NVR-affiliated brand in a prior year, per National Law Review coverage), and scattered BBB
complaints — routine for a large homebuilder and reserved for in the ordinary course (warranty reserve
methodology disclosed in the 10-K); no evidence found this pass of an active, certified class action or a
material (>$50m) single claim. Not checked this pass: Form 4 insider-selling pattern, short-seller reports.

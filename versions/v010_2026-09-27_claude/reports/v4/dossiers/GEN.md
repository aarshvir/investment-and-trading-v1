# Gen Digital Inc. (GEN) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F28, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — not now, despite genuinely cheap headline valuation, because of a live, unresolved, potentially transformative
M&A situation disclosed one day before this dossier's cutoff. Condition to revisit: GoDaddy talks are confirmed dead (revert
to INCLUDE-SMALL/INCLUDE case below) **or** a signed agreement discloses concrete financing terms that can be underwritten.
Thesis horizon if resolved favourably: 12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Gen Digital sells consumer cybersecurity, identity-protection and privacy subscriptions under the Norton, Avast, LifeLock,
AVG and (since fiscal 2026) MoneyLion brands — a recurring-revenue, high-margin subscription business monetised mostly
through auto-renewing annual plans and OEM/retail bundling. It is the product of the 2022 NortonLifeLock/Avast merger and
has spent the years since integrating and deleveraging that deal.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1_live_scores.csv: composite 0.71 (live_rank 144/503), driven by a high V-family score (FCF yield 12.0%, earnings yield
8.1%) and strong earnings momentum (SUE 0.65). Triage (Q12_triage.json) called it "a cash cow… priced for outright decline"
at 7x NTM P/E / 12% FCF yield, flagging two red flags: elevated leverage from the Avast deal (3.2x net debt/EBITDA) and the
historical Avast/FTC data-selling settlement (resolved).
**Verified:** deleveraging is real and evidenced in primary filings — SEC XBRL long-term debt has fallen from $10.06bn
(Sep-2022) to **$8.16bn (Jul-2026)**, and the company states it hit its **3.0x net-leverage target a full year ahead of
schedule** in fiscal 2026, funded by $2.4bn of debt paydown, alongside $1.3bn of buybacks and ~$1.1bn of tuck-in M&A
(primarily MoneyLion) (Gen Digital Q4 FY26 earnings materials, May-2026; Investing.com summary of the same). Revenue growth
is genuinely accelerating: Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 3-Jul-2026) revenue grew **11% YoY, the fastest since Gen was created**,
and full-year FY27 guidance was **raised** at that release (see §5). So the "cheap cash cow" read holds up on growth and
leverage. **What the triage missed entirely, because it happened after the triage ran:** a preliminary ~$12bn takeover
approach for GoDaddy, reported 24-Sep-2026 — see §11. This is not an artefact of the scoring model; it is a live corporate-
action risk that changes the capital structure and strategic direction question entirely, and it arrived one day before
this dossier's data cutoff.

## 5. Guidance track record
Full-year non-GAAP guidance was **raised at the most recent release**: FY27 revenue guide moved from **$5.325–5.425bn to
$5.375–5.475bn** (9–11% growth) and FY27 non-GAAP EPS from **$2.85–2.95 to $2.87–2.97** (14–18% growth), both in the Q1 FY27
release (6-Aug-2026), after Q1 actual EPS of $0.71 beat the high end of the $0.68–0.70 guided range. This is the one release
independently verified in this pass; the addendum's request for four releases was not fully met inside the standard-depth
time box — prioritised per the addendum's own fallback order (verdict → results → balance sheet → valuation → kill criteria
→ red flags).

- **Litigation costs are running through the P&L** ($23m in Q1 FY27 alone) — a legacy TCPA (robocall) class action was

- Share count trend: buybacks running (~$100m in Q1 FY27) alongside continued deleveraging — a shareholder-friendly capital
  allocation pattern **that a debt-funded acquisition of GoDaddy would likely reverse.**

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Deleveraging is ahead of schedule and evidenced in primary filings (10.06bn→8.16bn LT debt over four years),
freeing cash for buybacks. (2) Revenue growth is accelerating, not decaying, and guidance was just raised. (3) 77–81% gross
margins and near-zero capex make this one of the highest-FCF-conversion names in the whole diligence set.

**Bear:** (1) **A preliminary ~$12bn takeover approach for GoDaddy (reported 24-Sep-2026, talks at an early, non-binding
stage) would need to be financed largely with equity given Gen's own ~$8bn net debt versus GoDaddy's ~$3.8bn** — this is
exactly the kind of "pending deal" the program's own sleeve-construction rules treat as an automatic exclusion for other
names (WBD, KVUE, TECH, AES, NSC, D), and it was invisible to both the quant screen and the original triage because it
broke the day before the data cutoff. (2) Operating margin has compressed roughly 8–10 points from its FY25 peak on
integration and litigation costs even before any GoDaddy deal. (3) A $900m note matures Sep-2027, inside the thesis horizon,
competing for capital with whatever the GoDaddy situation ultimately requires.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. A **signed, binding GoDaddy (or other >$5bn) acquisition agreement** is announced without disclosed financing terms
   that keep pro-forma net debt/EBITDA under ~3.5x within 12 months.
2. Net debt/EBITDA **rises above 3.5x** for any reason (deal-related or organic).
3. Quarterly organic revenue growth **below 5% YoY for two consecutive quarters** (current: accelerating, 11% last quarter).
4. FY27 guidance **cut** at any subsequent release (currently raised twice in the trailing year per the sources reviewed).
5. Buyback program **suspended or materially reduced** to fund M&A — a direct signal capital allocation has shifted away
   from the "cash cow" thesis this dossier is built on.

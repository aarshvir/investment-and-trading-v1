# APO — Apollo Global Management, Inc. (F107, agent F107)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — thesis horizon 24–36 months. Reservation: Apollo gated/capped a semi-liquid
private-credit vehicle in June 2026 after redemption requests hit ~17% (offshore investors 12.5%, U.S. onshore
~4.3%), a real liquidity-stress signal in exactly the product category the March-2026 press coverage flagged —
even though headline Fee Related Earnings (FRE) and Assets Under Management (AUM) growth remain excellent and a
large GAAP net loss in Q1 2026 was a one-time Bermuda tax item, not an operating problem.

## 2. Business in plain English
Apollo Global Management is a two-engine business: (1) an alternative-asset manager running private credit,
private equity and real assets funds for institutions and (increasingly) individual "wealth" investors, earning
management and performance fees; and (2) Athene, a retirement-services/annuity insurer whose general account
Apollo manages, earning a "spread" between what it credits policyholders and what its invested assets return.
The combination lets Apollo originate large amounts of private credit and fund a captive, long-duration liability
base (annuities) to hold much of it — a structurally different, more leveraged and more interest-rate/credit-
spread-sensitive model than a pure fee-based asset manager (e.g., Blackstone's or KKR's fee businesses alone).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Earnings-quality headline:** the FRE/SRE/ANI trio is Apollo's own primary non-GAAP earnings measure and is
  growing double-digit YoY (FRE +25%, driven by record fee-related revenue and margin expansion per the release);
  GAAP net income is far more volatile quarter to quarter because it includes investment mark-to-market
  (Investment related gains (losses) line item: **$(2,078)M in Q1 2026, +$2,989M in Q2 2026** — a huge GAAP swing
  from insurance-portfolio marks, not operating performance) and the one-off Bermuda tax charge above. **Treat
  trailing GAAP EPS/P/E as unreliable for this name**; ANI is the more decision-useful metric, and the market's
  ~11.9x NTM P/E cited in triage likely uses an ANI-equivalent consensus estimate, not trailing GAAP EPS (which
  would look far more expensive/volatile given the Q1 2026 loss).
- **Balance sheet, consolidated** (10-Q as of 2026-06-30, filed 2026-08-10): total stockholders' equity **$21,006M**
  (up from $19,951M at Q1 2026-end, down from $23,341M at FY2025-end — the FY25-to-Q1'26 drop is largely the
  Bermuda tax charge flowing through retained earnings). This is the **consolidated** Apollo Global Management,
  Inc. balance sheet, which combines the asset-management HoldCo and Athene's insurance general account — a much
  larger, more complex, more leveraged balance sheet than a pure asset manager's; a line-by-line net-debt/EBITDA
  metric is not meaningful for this entity type (per the standard playbook for holdco/insurance hybrids, the
  relevant capital metrics are Athene's own capital/RBC ratios and HoldCo leverage, neither of which was
  independently pulled this pass — **limitation**, flag for next cycle).
- **Capital return:** LTM (through Q2 2026) share repurchases ~$1.6bn (including $285M "opportunistic"), LTM
  dividends >$1.0bn; Q2 2026 alone: buybacks $729M, dividends $654M (both up materially YoY per the earlier XBRL
  pull, though those XBRL figures pre-date full Q2 ingestion and should be treated as directional).
- **Private-credit liquidity stress (the triage red flag, updated):** per Bloomberg/CNBC reporting dated late
  June 2026, Apollo capped/curbed withdrawals from a private-credit fund after redemption requests reached
  approximately **17%** of NAV in aggregate, with a regional split of **~4.3%** from U.S. onshore investors versus
  **~12.5%** from offshore investors; Apollo's own disclosure characterized expected net outflows from the
  relevant vehicle (ADS) at roughly **$400M YTD, ~3% of NAV**, for Q2 2026. This is a **confirmed, dated,
  company-specific liquidity event** (not merely industry-wide press speculation) and is the single most
  important adverse fact in this dossier — gating provisions exist precisely for this scenario and are contractual
  (not a default), but repeated or escalating gating would be a genuine credibility and flow-growth risk for the
  wealth-channel distribution strategy Apollo is counting on for future AUM growth.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Record FRE ($785M, +25% YoY) and record SRE ($877M) in Q2 2026 show both engines growing simultaneously; AUM
   crossed $1.05 trillion (+25% YoY) with broad-based inflows across Institutional, Global Wealth and Retirement
   Services channels — the growth engine is firing on both cylinders, not just one.
2. The Q1 2026 GAAP loss was a one-time, clearly disclosed, non-cash tax-valuation-allowance item tied to a
   Bermuda tax-law change — not a sign of deteriorating core profitability, and Adjusted Net Income was stable
   through the same quarter.
3. Trades at a persistent, large valuation discount to Blackstone and KKR on a core-earnings basis, giving
   room for re-rating if the private-credit/Athene concerns prove overstated relative to peers with less
   insurance exposure.

**Bear:**
1. The June 2026 fund-gating event (17% aggregate redemption requests, 12.5% from offshore investors) is a
   **confirmed** liquidity-stress signal in exactly the semi-liquid private-credit wealth-channel product Apollo
   is counting on for future distribution growth — if it recurs or spreads to other vehicles, it directly damages
   the wealth-channel AUM-growth thesis that underpins the bull case.
2. The business model's structural leverage to Athene's insurance balance sheet means GAAP earnings will
   continue to be volatile (large investment mark-to-market swings, as seen in the $(2,078)M → $2,989M swing in
   "Investment related gains (losses)" from Q1 to Q2 2026) — this is a genuine source of earnings-quality risk
   that a clean ANI multiple does not fully capture, especially in a credit-stress scenario where Athene's
   invested assets (much of it originated by Apollo's own private-credit platform) could see real impairments,
   not just mark-to-market noise.
3. The quant factor model ranks APO in the bottom decile (decile 1) on the pre-registered composite — while this
   dossier attributes that mostly to GAAP noise (§3), a persistent bottom-decile quant score is itself a signal
   worth respecting rather than fully explaining away, especially given the model's designed indifference to
   sector-specific "story" arguments.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. A **second** fund-gating or redemption-cap event at Apollo (any vehicle) disclosed within the next four
   quarters, or aggregate redemption requests exceeding **20%** of NAV in any Apollo semi-liquid vehicle.
2. Fee Related Earnings growth turns **negative** YoY in any quarter (from +25% YoY in Q2 2026).
3. A GAAP net loss in any quarter **not** attributable to a clearly disclosed one-time item (i.e., a loss driven
   by core operating or credit losses rather than a tax/accounting adjustment).
4. Athene-related credit losses or downgrades: any public disclosure of a **downgrade** to Athene's financial-
   strength ratings, or realized (not just mark-to-market) credit losses on Athene's invested-asset portfolio
   exceeding a materially adverse threshold disclosed in a future filing.
5. Total AUM growth decelerates below **10%** YoY for two consecutive quarters (from +25% YoY in Q2 2026).

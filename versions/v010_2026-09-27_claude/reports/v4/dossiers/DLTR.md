# DLTR — Dollar Tree, Inc. (CIK 935703) — v4 diligence dossier

**Recency:** latest reported period incorporated: Q2 FY2026 (13 weeks ended 2026-08-01), published 2026-08-27 (8-K Ex-99.1 and 10-Q); events checked to the 2026-09-25 close, including the 2026-09-24 by-law 8-K.
**Reporting basis:** consolidated, USD millions unless stated. The P&L figures are **continuing operations** (the Dollar Tree U.S. and Dollar Tree Canada banners). Family Dollar is shown as discontinued operations in every period presented. Balance-sheet totals are the real consolidated totals on each date.
**Price used:** $114.07 at the 2026-09-25 close (FMP profile endpoint). 187.673M shares from the Q2 FY2026 10-Q, giving a market cap of **$21,407M**.

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL (half weight). Horizon 12–36 months.** The Dollar Tree banner itself is sound and fairly priced: comps were positive in all 8 quarters, FCF is solid, lease-adjusted leverage is moderate, and normalized NTM P/E is about 15.7x. But the model's near-top Quality score is largely an accounting artefact. Family Dollar impairments and buybacks shrank the equity base. The latest quarter is also about half one-off (a tariff refund). So the factor composite overstates the case, and the position should be sized below the model's weight.

## 2. Business in plain English
Dollar Tree runs 9,436 small-box variety stores in the U.S. and Canada (at 2026-08-01, 84.0M selling sq ft). They sell consumables, seasonal goods, party supplies, crafts and household basics to value-seeking shoppers. It is a 1P principal: it buys, owns and marks up its inventory, and occupies leased stores. Its historic edge was a single fixed price point ($1.00, then $1.25). The current strategy is a "multi-price" (Dollar Tree 3.0) assortment. About 6,600 stores had it by Q2 FY2026, and the CEO said in December 2025 that 85% of the assortment is priced at $2 or less. Competitors are Dollar General, Five Below, Walmart and online retail. Family Dollar was sold on **2025-07-05**. Before that, the combined company had 16,500 stores (at 2025-02-01).

## 3. Why the model likes it, and whether that is durable
The pre-registered QVM percentiles are Q 0.962, V 0.917, M 0.836, composite 0.905. **The Quality score is largely an artefact.**
- **ROE of about 47% (d4) comes mostly from the equity denominator.** ratios.py gives 45.1% on average equity, using actual TTM NI of $1,618.6M. Equity fell from **$8,751.5M (FYE Jan-2023) to $3,425.3M (2026-08-01)**. The bridge, in round numbers:
  - +$4.4bn of continuing-ops earnings
  - −$6.3bn of after-tax Family Dollar discontinued-ops losses, most of them impairments: Q4 FY2023 −$2,242.2M and Q4 FY2024 −$4,096.1M
  - −$3.6bn of buybacks
- **Continuing-ops ROE on average equity** went from ~16% (FY2023) to ~18% (FY2024), ~32% (FY2025) and ~45% (TTM). Over the same run, continuing-ops NI went $1,265.8M → $1,042.5M → $1,225.3M → $1,618.6M. TTM NI is about $1.37bn excluding the after-tax tariff refund.
- At the FY2023 average equity level, TTM ROE would be about **20% (17% ex-refund)**. Roughly **25 of the ~29 points of ROE expansion are denominator.**
- ratios.py's DuPont credits asset turnover (0.94x → 1.46x). But **fixed-asset turnover is flat (3.90x → 3.99x)**. The turnover gain is the balance sheet shrinking after Family Dollar left, not more productive stores. ROIC excluding goodwill (17.5% TTM, 12.8% FY2025) is the better gauge: good, not elite.
- **GP/assets of 0.503** uses d4's gross profit of $7,050.7M. That is the **FY2025 fiscal-year** figure, not TTM (see data conflicts). The asset denominator fell 25% when Family Dollar was deconsolidated.
- **The Value inputs are flattered too.** d4's FCF yield of 10.27% rests on understated capex. The company's own tables give a TTM FCF yield of **9.2% reported and ~7.5% ex-refund**. The earnings yield of 7.53% includes a $1.31/share one-off; ex-refund it is about 6.1%.
- **Momentum (+34.1% 12-1) is real but fading.** The stock is $114.07 against a 52-week high of $142.40. It was about $131 on 2026-09-04 (the Form 4 price).
- **What is durable:** comp growth, a modest organic margin gain (TTM EBIT margin about 9.4% ex-refund vs 8.3% in FY2024), cash conversion, and buybacks.

## 6. Earnings quality and balance sheet
- **FCF conversion:** FCF/NI of 0.86x in both FY2024 ($892.8M) and FY2025 ($1,056.7M). TTM is 1.22x ($1,978.1M), inflated by refund cash. The Sloan accrual ratio is negative (−6% to −11%), which is favorable.
- **SBC** was 0.30% of FY2025 revenue ($58.9M), down from 0.61% in FY2024.
- **GAAP vs adjusted gap:** small, and it runs both ways. Adjusted strips out an insurance gain as well as costs. The larger issue is one-offs *inside* GAAP and adjusted alike: the $1.31 refund (Q2 FY26), ~$0.20 tariff timing (Q2 FY25) and ~$0.60 of refund in FY26 guidance.
- **Working capital:** inventory $2,452.2M, down 8.6% YoY on sales up 7%. DIO fell from 86 to 73 days and the cash-conversion cycle is +27 days (it is *not* negative working capital). FY2025 markdowns included a **$56.0M slow-SKU write-off**. The 10-K calls shrink "historically high".
- **Leverage (lease basis stated):**
  - Funded net debt is $1,875.4M, which is 0.65x TTM EBITDA of $2,881.4M (about 0.73x ex-refund).
  - Operating lease liabilities are **$4,737.3M**, larger than funded debt.
  - Under US GAAP ASC 842, operating-lease cost stays **one opex line** (FY2025 fixed lease cost $1,131.9M, variable $367.0M). So EBITDA was *not* mechanically inflated the way it is for IFRS 16 filers, but the lease liability is still debt-like.
  - **Lease-adjusted** (net debt + leases) / EBITDAR at FYE Jan-2026 was 1.85x. On the 8×-rent convention it was 3.1x ("manageable" band).
  - ratios.py's lease-inclusive net debt/EBITDA fell from 3.31x to 2.29x; fixed-charge cover was 2.8x.
- **Debt maturities (10-K):** $1,250.0M in FY2028 and $1,200.0M thereafter. The $1.0B 4.00% notes were repaid on 2025-05-15. A new credit agreement was signed on 2026-03-19 (8-K Items 1.01/1.02/2.03), after which long-term debt rose about $502M to $2,933.5M. Revolver pricing is SOFR + 1.000%, and covenants were in compliance.
- **Family Dollar M&A:**
  - Agreement signed 2025-03-25 with 1959 Holdings, LLC, backed by Brigade and Macellum; announced 2025-03-26.
  - Base price $1,007.5M in cash. The buyer's termination fee was $55.4M.
  - Closed **2025-07-05**. Cash monetized was about $800M: $665M at closing, $22M post-closing, and about $113M of working capital released before closing. The expected tax benefit is about $425M.
  - The pro-forma **loss on sale was only about $17.0M**. The carrying value had already been written down: Q4 FY2024 took a $1,400.0M trade-name impairment, a $490.5M goodwill impairment, a $3,438.7M held-for-sale write-down and $79.6M of store impairments (pre-tax), and FY2023 had earlier impairments.
- **Share count:** 221.2M (Jan-2023) → 187.7M (Aug-2026), down 15%. Buybacks were $1,548M in FY2025 and about $1.2B in H1 FY2026, with $2.5B of authorization remaining.

## 8. Bull case and bear case
**Bull case**
1. **Multi-price is working.** Comps were positive in all 8 quarters (+1.8% to +6.5%) and +5.3% for FY2025. About 70% of stores are converted. Traffic turned positive (+0.4%) in Q2 FY26.
2. **Cash and capital return.** FY2025 FCF was $1.06B. Share count is down 15% since Jan-2023. Lease-inclusive leverage has fallen (3.3x → 2.3x), and $2.5B of buyback authorization remains.
3. **The valuation is modest.** Normalized P/E is about 15.7x, the reverse DCF prices in about 1% FCF growth, and every guidance revision in the last 18 months has been a raise or a beat.

**Bear case**
1. **The Quality score is overstated.** It rests on a shrunken equity base (−$6.3B of after-tax FD losses and −$3.6B of buybacks) and a flat fixed-asset turnover. Management also carries a capital-allocation scar: Family Dollar ended in a ~$1.0B sale after roughly $6.3B of after-tax losses.
2. **Comps are ticket-dependent.** Traffic was −0.3%, −1.2% and −1.0% in Q3 FY25 through Q1 FY26, and H1 FY26 traffic was −0.3%. Comps have slowed from +6.5% to +3.5–3.7%, and FY26 comp guidance of 3–4% has not been raised. Price creep risks the "dollar" value proposition with low-income shoppers.
3. **One-offs and cost creep.** Q2 EPS was about 49% one-off. FY26 guidance includes about $0.60 of refund, and Q3 guidance includes a −$0.50 reinvestment drag. The SG&A rate is rising: FY2025 was 28.2% vs 27.5% (adjusted 28.1% vs 26.9%) on store payroll, wages, general-liability claims and depreciation. TSA income ($39M in H1 FY26) ends within about 18 months of July 2025. The tariff regime is still unsettled, including anti-dumping duties such as the $25.0M paper-plate accrual.

## 9. Key risks and kill criteria (thesis-invalidation triggers)
1. Comparable-store sales **below +1.0% for two consecutive quarters**, or traffic negative for two consecutive quarters while ticket growth is below +2%.
2. **Ex-refund gross margin down more than 150 bps YoY for two consecutive quarters.**
3. **Any cut** to FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance (now $7.70–8.05), or FY2027 guidance implying less than 5% ex-refund EPS growth.
4. Lease-adjusted (net debt + operating leases) / EBITDAR **above 2.5x** (1.85x at FYE Jan-2026), or funded net debt / EBITDA above 1.5x (0.65x now).
5. Normalized (ex-one-off) TTM FCF **below $1.0B**.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **Audit:** KPMG LLP gave an unqualified opinion on the FY2025 financial statements and on ICFR (10-K 2026-03-16). There is **no material weakness, no auditor change, no going-concern language and no error restatement**. The FY2024 re-presentation was a discontinued-ops reclassification, not an error correction.
- **West Memphis, AR distribution center:**
  - Nature: this is an **FDA/DOJ food-safety (FDCA) case, not an OSHA case.** It concerned a rodent infestation.
  - Plea: Family Dollar Stores LLC pleaded guilty. The plea agreement is dated 2024-02-26, with a **$41.675M** fine and forfeiture and a 3-year compliance and reporting term for Family Dollar and Dollar Tree (DOJ release).
  - Current disclosure: the plea is still Exhibit 10.27 in the FY2025 10-K, but it is not discussed as a contingency in Legal Proceedings.
  - Related news: a civil settlement of $5.5M and an Arkansas state settlement dated 2026-05-21 appear in local news. Amounts and payer were not verified in filings.
  - **Open item:** the post-divestiture allocation of residual obligations between Dollar Tree and Family Dollar's new owners is not disclosed.
  - Separate OSHA store-safety history was *not verified* in this pass.
- **Litigation:** the 10-K lists routine product-liability cases, including talc and acetaminophen, and calls them not material. A consumer suit over tariff pricing and refunds was reported by Supermarket News; it was **not verified**.
- **Insider selling (Form 4, via news summaries; FMP insider data unavailable):**
  - The CIO sold 2,500 shares at about $126.01 on 2026-08-31 (about 9% of direct holdings).
  - A director sold 1,185 shares at $131.47 on 2026-09-04.
  - Other dispositions were RSU tax withholding.
  - This is small and not a cluster.
- **Short sellers and activists:** no short-seller report was found. An aggregator reports that activist Paul Hilal cut a 6.5% 13D stake (Jul-2025) to about 0.1% by Jun-2026; the 13D/A was not verified.
- **Governance:**
  - 2026-09-24: the by-laws were amended to tighten advance-notice and special-meeting procedures (mildly defensive).
  - CFO change on 2025-03-30.
  - The Chief Legal Officer and the IR head also changed.
- **Share price:** down about 15% from its post-earnings level in September. Press attributes this to the unchanged sales guidance and to refund-driven EPS (aggregator; not verified).

# VMC — Vulcan Materials Company (F107, agent F107)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — thesis horizon 24–36 months. Reservation: growth in FY26 is pricing-led, not
volume-led (aggregates shipments +1% YoY in Q2), and the 24–28x TTM-earnings multiple leaves little room if
public/infrastructure demand does not offset continued private-construction softness; also monitor the July 2026
NAFTA-arbitration decision (found Mexico in violation but awarded only "negligible" damages) as a resolved-but-
disappointing legacy item.

## 2. Business in plain English
Vulcan Materials is the largest U.S. producer of construction aggregates (crushed stone, sand and gravel), with a
smaller Asphalt and Concrete ("non-aggregates") segment. It sells to public infrastructure (roads, highways),
private nonresidential and residential construction customers, monetizing via a local-oligopoly, high-fixed-cost,
low-substitutability model (aggregates are heavy/low-value-per-ton, so transport cost creates durable regional
moats around quarry locations). Growth comes from mid-single-digit-plus annual price increases, bolt-on
acquisitions of quarries/reserves, and operating leverage on volume.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Consolidated leverage (1.9x total debt/EBITDA) sits **below** management's own 2.0–2.5x target range, giving
   real balance-sheet capacity for accretive bolt-on M&A without equity dilution.
2. Pricing discipline is working: mix-adjusted price/ton +5% YoY in Q2 FY26 even as volumes were merely flat-to-up
   1%, and cash gross profit/ton hit a record $12.02 — margin expansion is happening independent of a volume
   recovery, which is the highest-quality kind of growth for this business model.
3. Public/infrastructure demand (federal and state highway funding) is a multi-year tailwind that is largely
   insulated from private-construction cyclicality, and Vulcan's reserve positions near population centers are
   effectively irreplaceable (permitting is the moat).

**Bear:**
1. Aggregates shipment growth of only **+1% YoY** in Q2 FY26 (with Texas/Southeast volumes hit by weather) shows
   the "volume recovery" the bull case depends on has not yet shown up — growth remains entirely price-led, and
   price increases eventually meet customer/contractor resistance if private construction stays weak.
2. 12-month momentum is sharply negative (**−11.7%** per b1 data) — the market is pricing in disappointment risk
   the headline EBITDA-guide-reiteration language does not fully address.
3. The NAFTA arbitration against Mexico (filed 2018) concluded in July 2026 with the tribunal finding Mexico
   violated the agreement but awarding only "negligible" monetary damages — an eight-year legal effort that
   resolved without the financial recovery Vulcan had been pursuing; while not large relative to market cap, it
   is a real, filed-and-dated example of a legacy legal exposure not paying off as hoped, worth watching for any
   further disclosure of write-offs tied to the underlying Mexican reserve asset.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Aggregates segment shipment growth **negative** (a decline in tons shipped YoY) for two consecutive quarters.
2. Consolidated total debt / TTM Adjusted EBITDA rises above the top of management's own stated target range,
   **2.5x** (from 1.9x as of 2026-06-30).
3. FY26 Adjusted EBITDA guidance **cut** below the current $2.4–2.6bn range at any subsequent quarterly release.
4. Freight-adjusted mix-adjusted aggregates pricing growth falls below **2%** YoY for two consecutive quarters
   (from +5% in Q2 FY26) — the signal that pricing power, the core of the thesis, is fading.
5. Cash gross profit per ton declines YoY for two consecutive quarters (from the current record $12.02/ton).

# Roper Technologies, Inc. (NASDAQ: ROP) — Diligence Dossier
Agent F98 | As of 2026-09-25 close (px $358.56) | Fiscal year ends Dec 31

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — best-in-class capital allocator with 90%+ recurring, vertical-market software revenue, trading at a genuine discount to its own history (16.1x NTM adjusted EPS vs a historical 20–25x per triage) — but flag: consolidated long-term debt has nearly doubled in five quarters (from $6.46B to $10.6B) to fund an aggressive buyback, and GAAP earnings are now dominated by a volatile, non-cash minority-investment mark-to-market that must be excluded to read the business correctly. Reservation named: rising leverage funding buybacks rather than growth capex/M&A is a change in capital-allocation character worth monitoring for two more quarters before sizing up. Thesis horizon: 12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Roper owns ~25+ mostly-standalone, mission-critical vertical-market software and technology-enabled businesses (permitting/licensing software for governments, insurance-agency management systems (Vertafore), medical/dental practice software, network/test equipment, water-metering technology) acquired and held for the long term with minimal cross-integration. Revenue is >90% recurring/subscription in the software segments; growth comes from a mix of organic pricing/volume and disciplined tuck-in/larger M&A funded by strong free cash flow.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF conversion:** Q2'26 adjusted operating cash flow $469M (GAAP OCF, +16% YoY), adjusted free cash flow $447M (+11% YoY) vs adjusted net earnings $542M — FCF/adjusted-NI ≈ 82% for the quarter, a normal software-model conversion rate given working-capital timing.
- **GAAP vs adjusted gap driver:** almost entirely the Indicor minority-stake fair-value swing (not SBC or routine intangible amortization, though amortization of acquisition-related intangibles, $164M in Q2'26, is also added back and is a recurring, real economic cost of Roper's M&A-heavy model worth keeping in mind even though it's a standard non-cash add-back).
- **Balance sheet (consolidated, Roper Technologies Inc. and subsidiaries; source: SEC XBRL companyfacts CIK0000882835, 10-Q balance sheets):** cash and equivalents $364.9M at 2026-06-30 (10-Q filed 2026-07-31 per companyfacts filing metadata — note: accession for this 10-Q is 0000882835-26-000036, filed date recorded as 2026-07-31 in XBRL), up modestly from $297.4M at 2025-12-31. **Long-term debt (consolidated) $10,601.1M at 2026-06-30, vs $6,457.0M at 2025-03-31 — a $4.1B increase in five quarters.** Stockholders' equity (consolidated) $18,700.3M at 2026-06-30, down from $19,881.5M at 2025-12-31 despite reported net earnings, because buybacks ($1.2B in Q2'26 alone — 3.6 million shares) exceeded net income added to retained earnings.
- **Leverage:** using Q2'26 adjusted EBITDA of $815M (quarterly; company's own Table 1 reconciliation) annualized to ~$3.26B, net debt (consolidated LT debt $10.6B less cash $0.36B ≈ $10.24B) / annualized adjusted EBITDA ≈ **3.1x** — up materially from Roper's historically conservative (~2x or lower) leverage. This is the single most important change in the credit/capital-structure profile this cycle and is why this dossier is INCLUDE-SMALL rather than INCLUDE.
- **M&A/divestitures:** CD&R sale of majority stake in the former Industrial Technology segment (now Indicor, minority-held) is the dominant corporate-action of the last two years; Indicor itself has a "pending divestiture of its instrumentation businesses" per the Q2'26 release, proceeds from which are explicitly excluded from Roper's own guidance (i.e., further complexity ahead, not yet in numbers).
- **Share count:** repurchased 9.0 million shares for $3.2B over the trailing three quarters as of Q2'26 (company's own disclosure) — "rolling our share count back to 2013 levels" per CEO Neil Hunn — buybacks now funded partly by rising debt, not just organic FCF.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. 90%+ recurring revenue, ~39% adjusted EBITDA margin, high-quality vertical-market moats (regulatory/workflow embeddedness in permitting, insurance-agency, medical-practice software) — genuinely hard to disrupt.
2. Valuation has de-rated to 16x adjusted NTM P/E against a 20-25x historical range, while adjusted DEPS guidance keeps getting raised (not cut) — a rare combination.
3. Aggressive buyback (>8% of shares in three quarters) directly and mechanically compounds adjusted EPS even before any operating improvement.

**Bear:**
1. Consolidated long-term debt has grown from $6.46B to $10.6B in five quarters (+64%) — largely to fund buybacks rather than growth investment; net debt/adjusted EBITDA has risen to ~3.1x from a historically much lower base. A credit-rating action or a slowdown in FCF growth would compound negatively with this leverage.
2. GAAP earnings are now dominated by a volatile, non-operating mark-to-market on the Indicor minority stake — a source of headline-EPS noise that could mislead less careful investors (and, per triage flag, has already produced one "flat guidance" misreading that this dossier had to correct against the latest release).
3. Indicor's own pending divestiture of its instrumentation businesses is an unresolved, off-balance-sheet-adjacent complexity whose proceeds and timing are explicitly excluded from Roper's guidance — a source of estimate uncertainty, not a settled asset.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Consolidated net debt / annualized adjusted EBITDA rises above **3.5x** (currently ~3.1x) without a corresponding acceleration in adjusted FCF growth.
2. Adjusted DEPS guidance is cut (not just reaffirmed/raised) in any quarterly release — none of the last four reviewed here showed a cut.
3. Organic revenue growth falls below **3%** YoY for two consecutive quarters (currently guided ~6%).
4. Any credit-rating downgrade action tied to the debt-funded buyback program.
5. A material adverse resolution or write-down of the residual Indicor minority stake.

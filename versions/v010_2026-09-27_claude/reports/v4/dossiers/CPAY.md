# CPAY — Corpay, Inc. (fka FLEETCOR) — Diligence Dossier (Agent F41, Wave 2, Standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — real double-digit organic growth at a reasonable adjusted multiple, but a named reservation: leverage (2.55x, above the company's own historical comfort zone), a fresh $100.9m preliminary FTC settlement charge (pending approval), and a wide, growing GAAP-vs-adjusted earnings gap. Thesis horizon: 12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Corpay (ticker changed from FLT to CPAY in 2024) sells corporate-payments software: fuel/fleet cards for trucking and field-service fleets, virtual corporate cards and AP automation ("Corpay One"/"Comdata"), and cross-border B2B payments (formerly Cambridge/AFEX). Customers are mid-market and large businesses that pay Corpay a recurring transaction/interchange-like fee for every fuel purchase, invoice payment or FX transfer it processes. It grows mostly by cross-selling its existing ~800k+ commercial customers into more products and by bolt-on acquisitions, funded with meaningful leverage. Competitive position: #1 or #2 in US fuel cards (WEX is the main competitor), a smaller but growing challenger in cross-border payments (versus Western Union Business Solutions, Convera) and corporate card (versus Amex, Brex).

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1/triage flags: Quality (ROE 32%, gross-profit/assets 0.076–0.28th pct in one direction, high leverage percentile pulling composite down slightly), Value (moderate — 42nd EP percentile), Momentum strong (12-1m return ~40%, 79th pct), Earnings-momentum (SUE) very strong (94th pct). Triage: quality 4/5, growth 4/5, price_vs_growth 4/5; "revenue +22%, margin 23%, ROE 29% and 13.1x NTM P/E is a rare combination of real growth and reasonable price."
This is **partly durable, partly an artefact**: organic revenue growth (double digits for 5 consecutive quarters per management) and ROE are real and confirmed in filings. But part of the reported "adjusted EPS" acceleration (+36% YoY in Q2 2026, +29% in Q1 2026) is inflated by (a) heavy share buybacks (diluted share count down 7.1% YoY in Q2 2026) funded by increasing leverage, and (b) a widening list of non-GAAP add-backs (see §6). The earnings-momentum (SUE) factor score is therefore flattered by a metric (adjusted EPS) that management, not GAAP, defines.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **GAAP-to-adjusted gap (Q2 2026, $m):** SBC 32.0; amortization 85.1; **legal settlements and litigation 100.9** (see red flags); loss on debt extinguishment 6.6; integration/deal costs 38.1; restructuring 2.8; equity-method adjustments 14.3; other 2.6; less gain on disposition (1.1) → total pre-tax add-backs $281.4m vs. GAAP pre-tax income. Adjusted net income $464.4m vs. GAAP $248.3m — adjusted is **87% higher** than GAAP this quarter, an unusually wide gap driven by the one-time litigation charge plus recurring heavy acquisition-intangible amortization.
- **FCF conversion (FY, OCF−capex, $m):** FY2023 $2,101.1−$153.8=$1,947.3; FY2024 $1,940.6−$175.2=$1,765.4 (−9.3%); FY2025 $1,499.9−$200.8=$1,299.1 (−26.4% YoY). Full-year OCF fell 22.7% in FY2025 despite revenue +13.9% and adjusted EPS growth in the mid-teens–20s — a real cash-conversion deterioration, not just a GAAP-vs-adjusted artefact. Partial offset: H1 2026 OCF ($1,413.5m) is already +32.6% vs. the comparable H1 2025 figure disclosed in the Q2 2026 10-Q, suggesting FY2025's dip was partly working-capital timing rather than a structural break — flagged as unresolved, not dismissed.
- **Leverage:** company-stated "2.55x" gross leverage exiting Q2 2026 (down from "2.7x" exiting Q1 2026, per the Q1 release), after refinancing and upsizing the revolver to $3.7bn. Both readings sit **above the 2.5x level the prior (v3) audit used as a red line**; balance-sheet debt (LongTermDebt, XBRL) rose from $8.0bn (FY24) to $10.6bn (Q2 2026) — a $2.6bn increase over 18 months funding both buybacks ($786m in Q1 2026 alone — 2.4m shares) and M&A.
- **Buybacks/share count:** diluted shares fell from 71.4m (Q2 2025) to 66.3m (Q2 2026), a real 7.1% reduction that mechanically boosts EPS growth independent of operating performance.
- **Cash:** grew from $1.55bn (FY24) to $3.16bn (Q2 2026), partly reflecting the $81m gain on a business sale in Q1 2026 and debt-facility upsizing.
- No dividend (buyback-only capital return).

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) Organic revenue growth in the double digits for five straight quarters, guidance raised twice in 2026; (2) reverse DCF shows the market pricing in only low-single-digit FCF growth against demonstrated double-digit growth; (3) buybacks are real and shrinking the share count, a lever management controls directly.
**Bear:** (1) Leverage (2.55x) is above the level the company itself flags as a self-imposed ceiling context and well above the level a prior audit used as a red line, funding both buybacks and M&A; (2) a fresh $100.9m preliminary FTC settlement (pending regulatory approval) plus an adverse January 2026 Eleventh Circuit ruling show the FTC/fee-disclosure legal overhang from the FLEETCOR era has not fully closed; (3) FY2025 operating cash flow fell 22.7% even as adjusted metrics grew — cash-conversion quality needs another 2–3 quarters to confirm the H1 2026 rebound is durable.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Company-reported gross leverage exceeds **3.0x** for two consecutive quarterly releases.
2. Organic (constant-currency) revenue growth falls below **10% YoY** for two consecutive quarters.
3. Cumulative litigation/regulatory settlement charges exceed **$250m** in any trailing 12 months (current: $100.9m preliminary, pending approval).
4. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance is **cut** versus the prior quarter's range (has been raised twice so far in 2026 — a cut would be a genuine reversal).
5. GAAP-to-adjusted pre-tax add-backs exceed **50% of GAAP pre-tax income** for two consecutive quarters (Q2 2026 was already close, at ~$281m of add-backs against ~$281m GAAP pre-tax income implied by a ~$249m net figure after tax — an earnings-quality trigger, not just a one-off).

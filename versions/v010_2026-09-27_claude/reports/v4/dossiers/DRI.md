# DRI — Darden Restaurants, Inc.

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE** (full weight) — thesis horizon 12–36 months. The cleanest evidence base of the three names reviewed in this batch: a modest but genuine valuation discount to own history and peers, a reverse-DCF that prices growth well below what the company has actually delivered, four consecutive quarters of guidance that was met or modestly raised (never cut), broad-based positive same-restaurant sales across every segment, and no unresolved securities litigation or regulatory-fine overhang. The one adverse fact that must be surfaced prominently: the most recently reported quarter (Q1 FY2027, filed one day before this dossier's cutoff) showed operating margin **compress** ~116bps year-over-year even as sales grew — a real, primary-source-verified data point that tempers, without breaking, the thesis.

## 2. Business in plain English

Darden owns and operates full-service ("sit-down") restaurants under nine brands — Olive Garden and LongHorn Steakhouse are by far the largest, alongside Cheddar's Scratch Kitchen, Yard House, Ruth's Chris Steak House, The Capital Grille, Chuy's (acquired 2024), Seasons 52 and Eddie V's — comprising 2,218 company-owned restaurants as of August 2026. It makes money on food/beverage sold to diners less the cost of ingredients, labour, restaurant occupancy and marketing; unlike many restaurant peers it owns and operates almost all of its restaurants directly (limited franchising), so it captures the full economics (and the full capital intensity) of the dining-room model rather than a royalty stream. Its moat is brand recognition, purchasing/supply-chain scale across ~2,200 units, and site selection built over decades.

## 3. Why the model likes it — and is it durable?

B1 composite is top decile (live_rank 46/~2,300; composite percentile ~91%; **gates: no disqualifying-red-flag gate is netted into this score — see §11 for the separate red-flag check**). Family drivers: **Earnings momentum (fam_S ≈0.93, very high)** — SUE has been consistently positive; **Momentum (fam_M ≈0.72)**; **Quality (fam_Q ≈0.65)**; **Value (fam_V ≈0.47, mid-pack)** — DRI is not a deep-value name, it is a modest discount to peers/history.

Durability check: the earnings-momentum signal is corroborated by four straight quarters of guidance that management met or narrowed upward (§5), and same-restaurant sales that were positive in **every** segment in the two most recently reported quarters (Q4 FY26: Olive Garden +2.4%, LongHorn +9.5%, Fine Dining +1.9%, Other +4.6%; Q1 FY27: Olive Garden +1.0%, LongHorn +6.8%, Fine Dining +1.0%, Other +4.5%, comparable-calendar basis) — a genuinely broad-based result, not one brand carrying the average. The counter-evidence, which I surface rather than average away: LongHorn's segment *margin* fell ~71bps YoY in FY2026 even as its sales grew +13.1% (fastest-growing brand, but its incremental sales are not flowing through at the historical rate), and Fine Dining's segment margin fell ~92bps. Consolidated operating margin in the single most recent quarter (Q1 FY27) was down ~116bps YoY. The SUE/momentum signal is real, but the margin-durability question is more open than the sales-growth headline suggests.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **FCF conversion:** volatile quarter to quarter (FCF/NI ranged 0.50x–1.47x over the last 8 quarters) but the two most recent quarters (Q4 FY26 0.94x, Q1 FY27 0.42x) show a downward trend, driven by working-capital timing (§4) and rising capex, not by a change in reported profitability.
- **SBC:** modest, 0.34%–1.26% of quarterly sales (Q1 FY25's 1.26% was an outlier, likely annual-grant timing); not a material distortion.
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** DRI discloses clean, itemised reconciliations every quarter (Chuy's integration costs, restaurant-closure/impairment charges, the Olive Garden Canada sale gain, tax items) — a transparency positive; I have used both reported and adjusted figures throughout and labelled which is which.
- **Balance sheet — a real, quantified deterioration worth watching:** short-term debt + current portion of long-term debt jumped from $693.6m (2026-05-31, fiscal year-end) to **$979.7m (2026-08-30)**, a $286.1m increase in one quarter, funding the capital-return gap noted in §4. Long-term debt $1,636.1m (roughly flat). **Total gross debt ≈$2,615.8m**, up from ≈$2,331.3m at fiscal year-end — a ~12% quarter-over-quarter increase. Cash is small and stable (~$220m). **Retained earnings are negative: $(259.5)m** as of 2026-08-30 (worsened from $(108.4)m at FYE), i.e. cumulative dividends + buybacks now exceed cumulative historical net income — common for a mature, buyback-heavy restaurant compounder, and the mechanical reason DRI's ROE (54.7% per the B1 dataset) looks extremely high: **it is flattered by a shrunk equity base, not by extraordinary returns on capital**, and should be read alongside segment-level margins (§4) rather than at face value, per the stock-analysis playbook's own warning about ROE manufactured by buybacks.
- **Net debt/EBITDA:** using FY2027 guided EBITDA ($2.26–2.29bn) against ≈$2.6bn gross debt less ~$220m cash ≈ $2.4bn net debt → **≈1.0–1.1x** — still low in absolute terms, but the trend (rising within a single quarter, debt-funded buybacks exceeding FCF) is the item to monitor, not the level.
- **M&A:** Chuy's Holdings acquired for ~$605m enterprise value / $649.1m total consideration, $37.50/share all-cash, announced 2024-07-17, closed 2024-10-11 — now 112 locations (from 108 a year ago), fully integrated into the "Other Business" segment and no longer flagged as a same-restaurant-sales exclusion after Q4 FY2026 (its 16-month anniversary). **Bahama Breeze wind-down:** the brand (28 units mid-2025) is being fully exited — ~14 locations closed around 2026-04-05, the remaining ~14 being converted to other Darden brands through Q4 FY2027; only 10 Bahama Breeze units remained at 2026-08-30. This is a small, well-telegraphed, already-substantially-executed restructuring, not a fresh risk.

## 7. Valuation snapshot — reconciliation with V1

**V1 verdict: attractive** (score 0.35/1.0). NTM P/E 17.2x vs. DRI's own 165-month history — **27th percentile**, a modest but real discount — and vs. peer median 18.9x (CMG/DPZ/MCD/SBUX). As with ABNB, I flag this **peer set as heterogeneous**: CMG is a much higher-growth, higher-multiple pure-play with a different unit-economics model (no full bar/table service), MCD and SBUX are global, franchise/royalty-heavy models with far less capital intensity than DRI's mostly-company-owned estate, and DPZ is franchise-heavy pizza delivery — DRI is the only "true" full-service, company-owned comparable in its own peer list. The resulting ~9% discount to that blended median is directionally fine to use but should not be read as a precise like-for-like gap.

Reverse-DCF implied 10-year growth is **+2.7%**, against consensus FY1 growth of +6.1% and delivered 5-year/10-year CAGRs of **+12.9%/+6.7%** — the market is pricing DRI for growth well below both what it has delivered and what the Street expects next year, which is the core of the "attractive" case and one I can corroborate: FY2027 guided total-sales growth (3.0–4.1% ex-53rd-week effects) and same-restaurant-sales growth (2.5–3.5%) are themselves modest, in-line-to-slightly-above the reverse-DCF-implied rate, not requiring any acceleration.

**A genuine data conflict I flag rather than pass over silently:** V1's own scenario `margin_assumed` values are bear 5.2% / base 7.8% / bull 9.2% — **all three below** the TTM operating margin V1 itself uses elsewhere in the same record (`ebit_margin_used` = 11.98%, which I independently confirm from D3/GAAP: $1,582.8m TTM EBIT ÷ $13,210.9m TTM revenue = 11.98%). I cannot fully resolve from the data available whether this is (a) an intentional, conservative fade toward a longer-run/mid-cycle margin (in which case the "attractive" verdict is, if anything, understated — even V1's bull case doesn't require margin expansion above what's already been delivered), or (b) a scenario-construction basis mismatch (e.g., a segment-profit-style margin excluding D&A/real-estate items being compared against a different EBIT base). Given the bear/base/bull returns V1 reports (−17.6%/+7.4%/+22.5% annualised) are still directionally sensible and roughly consistent with the multiple/growth inputs, I treat this as a modelling note for the lead/V1 agent to confirm rather than a reason to override the verdict, and record it in `data_conflicts`.

**My view: consistent = true, dossier_view = cheap (modest).** Base case assumes revenue growth 6.6%/yr (below the 5-year delivered 12.9%, in line with the 10-year delivered 6.7% — not aggressive) and an exit multiple of 20.1x (close to today's 17.2x and below the peer median 18.9x — a small, not heroic, re-rating). Combined with the guidance-accuracy track record in §5 and the broad-based same-restaurant-sales strength in §3, I agree with V1's "attractive" call and do not see a basis to downgrade it to "fair" — the main things working against the thesis (margin compression in the two most recent segment/consolidated readings, and the debt-funded capital return in Q1 FY27) are real but, on the evidence available, second-order relative to the valuation discount and demonstrated sales/guidance durability.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull (3):**
1. Every reportable segment posted positive same-restaurant sales in each of the last two quarters — a broad, not single-brand-dependent, recovery, and guidance has been met or modestly raised for four straight releases with no cut.
2. Valuation is a genuine, if modest, discount to both own history (27th percentile) and the reverse-DCF-implied growth rate is well below what the company has actually delivered over 5 and 10 years.
3. Disciplined, well-disclosed capital allocation: dividend raised 8% (to $1.62/quarter) in June 2026, a new $1.5bn buyback authorization, and a small, already-largely-executed brand rationalisation (Bahama Breeze exit) rather than a large, uncertain restructuring.

**Bear (3):**
1. Consolidated operating margin fell ~116bps YoY in the single most recently reported quarter (Q1 FY27), and LongHorn's and Fine Dining's full-year segment margins each fell 71–92bps in FY2026 despite strong sales growth — cost inflation (labour, restaurant expense, D&A) is outrunning sales growth in the two brands driving most of the comp strength.
2. Q1 FY27 free cash flow fell 39% YoY, and the company funded capital returns (dividends + buybacks, $405m) well in excess of free cash flow ($98.9m) with a $286m single-quarter increase in short-term debt — a real, quantifiable, and newly-emerging capital-discipline question, not yet a crisis (net debt/EBITDA still ~1x) but worth a full-year read before dismissing.
3. Ordinary industry-standard tip-credit/wage litigation (One Fair Wage-affiliated FLSA claims over the $2.13/hour tipped minimum wage) is an ongoing, if not unusual-for-the-industry, legal cost and headline-risk item.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. Consolidated same-restaurant sales growth turns negative for **two consecutive quarters** (currently +3.1–4.6% depending on calendar basis).
2. Olive Garden (the largest segment, ~42% of FY26 sales) same-restaurant sales turn negative for **two consecutive quarters** — it is already the slowest-growing segment (+1.0–2.4%).
3. Full-year diluted-EPS guidance is **cut** versus the prior quarter's range (no cut found in the four releases reviewed).
4. Net debt / TTM EBITDA rises above **2.0x** (currently ≈1.0–1.1x, but rising within the most recent quarter).
5. Cumulative incremental Chuy's-integration or Bahama-Breeze-wind-down charges exceed **$50 million** beyond what is already disclosed in the FY2026 10-K/8-Ks (scope-creep signal on the two known restructuring items).

## 11. Red-flag scan

- **Wage/labour litigation:** One Fair Wage (a restaurant-workers advocacy group) sued Darden alleging FLSA and state wage-and-hour violations tied to the tipped-minimum-wage model ($2.13/hour federal tip credit); an associated race-discrimination claim was **dropped**, wage claims reportedly remain. This is a real, ongoing legal item but structurally common across the full-service restaurant industry (tip-credit litigation is not unique to Darden) rather than a company-specific governance failure.
- **Debt-funded capital return in the most recent quarter:** see §6/§8 — a real, quantified, newly-emerging item to track for a second consecutive quarter before treating it as a trend.
- **53rd-week / fiscal-calendar effects:** real and disclosed by the company itself; already addressed via the "comparable calendar" same-restaurant-sales metric — a modelling nuance, not a governance or accounting-quality concern.
- No auditor change, restatement, going-concern language, or SEC/DOJ investigation was identified in the filings and news reviewed for this dossier.

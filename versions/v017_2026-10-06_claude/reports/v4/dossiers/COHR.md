# COHR — Coherent Corp. (Diligence agent F95, wave 6)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — 12–36 month thesis horizon, with a specific named reservation: **free cash flow is currently negative** despite the earnings turnaround and the 158%-in-12-months share-price run (triage data). This is a real photonics/laser leader riding the AI-optics (800G/1.6T transceiver) upgrade cycle with a genuine, multi-quarter beat-and-raise record, but until capex-heavy capacity investment converts to positive cash generation, size the position for the turnaround-execution risk it still carries.

## 2. Business in plain English
Coherent Corp. (the 2022 merger of legacy Coherent Inc. and II-VI, still SEC-registered as "iivi" in some filings) designs and manufactures photonics, lasers and optical components — including the optical transceivers and components that connect servers inside AI datacenters and the networking gear that connects datacenters to each other. Customers are hyperscale/cloud datacenter operators, telecom-equipment makers, industrial and materials-processing customers (a legacy, smaller business), and consumer-electronics/sensing customers. It makes money selling hardware components at the leading edge of optical speed (800G now, 1.6T ramping) where technology leadership commands premium pricing.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
b1_live_scores shows very strong momentum and value-adjacent (earnings) signals: composite ~0.44, live_rank 278, fam_M (momentum) very high, driven by 34% revenue growth and — critically — a real EPS recovery from a loss-making/near-breakeven state 18–24 months ago to consistently profitable, accelerating non-GAAP EPS. This durability case is credible: it is a genuine restructuring/product-mix turnaround (divestiture of the non-core Munich materials-processing unit closed end-January 2026, per the Q2 FY26 press release) combined with real AI-optics demand, not an accounting artefact. The main durability risk is the same one flagged in triage: cash conversion has not caught up with the reported earnings recovery.

- **FCF conversion — the central caution:** FY26 (year ended Jun 30 2026) operating cash flow was only **$79.5m** against **$1,102.9m of capex**, i.e. **FCF ≈ −$1,023.4m for the year**. The swing is capacity investment for AI-optics manufacturing scale-up (explicitly cited in the Q4 FY26 press release: "prioritizing investments to expand manufacturing capacity so we can efficiently fulfill the ongoing acceleration in customer demand"), not deteriorating operations — but it means the reported earnings recovery has **not yet** been matched by cash generation, exactly the triage red flag. This is the single most important thing to monitor.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3 points):** (1) three consecutive quarters of guidance beats/raises with the Q1 FY27 guide implying a further acceleration ($2.2–2.4bn vs $2.05bn Q4 FY26 actual); (2) the completed divestiture of the non-core Munich materials-processing business sharpens focus on the higher-growth photonics/AI-optics portfolio; (3) debt is being actively paid down ($3.69bn→$3.22bn YoY) even during the heaviest capex year, showing balance-sheet discipline alongside growth investment.

**Bull risk offset — Bear (3 points):** (1) FY26 FCF was **−$1.02bn**; if capex intensity does not moderate as the capacity ramp completes, the company may need external financing (equity or debt) before FCF turns durably positive; (2) the GAAP/non-GAAP EPS gap (~46% in Q4 FY26) means the "earnings" the market is paying 28x forward for still include material add-backs — a slower pace of restructuring completion would keep this gap wide; (3) COHR's AI-optics transceiver business faces the same customer-concentration and cyclicality risk as CIEN and LITE — a pause in hyperscaler 800G/1.6T capex would hit a name still working through negative FCF harder than one already generating cash.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. **FCF stays negative (OCF minus capex < $0) for two consecutive fiscal quarters after FY26 Q4** — the single most important test of whether the capex cycle is nearing its planned peak as management describes, or open-ended.
2. **Quarterly revenue guidance is cut (below the immediately prior quarter's stated range)** — breaks the three-quarter beat-and-raise pattern.
3. **Consolidated total debt (kill threshold, not a current figure) rises above $4.0bn** (actual: $3.22bn at Jun 30 2026, per the 10-K debt schedule) without a corresponding step-up in FCF guidance — would signal the capacity investment is outpacing self-funding capacity.
4. **Non-GAAP-to-GAAP EPS gap widens beyond 50%** for two consecutive quarters (vs ~46% in Q4 FY26) — would suggest the "recovery" is increasingly an adjustments story rather than an operating one.
5. **Organic revenue growth (ex-Munich divestiture effects) decelerates below 20% YoY for two consecutive quarters** — vs the current 22.5%(FY)/33.7%(Q4) run-rate.

# Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc. (TMO) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Verdict: WATCH** — condition: implied growth compresses toward base case (via a pullback or a further guidance raise that closes the gap) before adding.
One-sentence reason: this is genuinely the best-in-class life-science tools/services scale platform with a real, filed bioprocessing-and-academic-funding re-acceleration and a just-raised FY2026 guide, but the reverse-DCF implies ≈10.2% ten-year FCF growth versus a reasonable ≈8% base case — the price is already crediting more than this analyst's evidence-based base case delivers, so per the program's discipline this is a WATCH, not an INCLUDE, despite the strong business.

## 1. Business in plain English
Thermo Fisher is the largest scale player selling the tools, consumables, reagents and outsourced manufacturing (CDMO/CRO) services that pharma, biotech, academic and industrial labs use to do research, develop drugs and manufacture biologics. It makes money through instruments (one-time sale), consumables/reagents (high-margin, recurring, tied to instrument installed base) and services (analytical/CDMO, more variable). The moat is scale (largest distribution/manufacturing footprint in the industry), an enormous installed base that locks in consumables demand, and repeated bolt-on/large M&A (most recently Solventum's Purification & Filtration business) that keeps extending the platform.

## 4. Guidance track record
Per the Q2'26 earnings release (8-K, accession 0000097745-26-000138) and multiple transcript summaries: TMO beat revenue by ~$300M and adjusted EPS by ~$0.30 versus consensus, and **raised full-year 2026 guidance** to $47.4B–$48.1B revenue (6–8% reported growth) and $24.93–$25.33 adjusted EPS (9–11% growth), a $0.25 increase at the midpoint from the prior (post-Q1'26) range. This is a genuine raise off a beat, consistent across at least the last two quarters — management cited "broad-based end-market strength" and continued bioprocessing/academic-funding momentum.

## 7. Bull case
1. Bioprocessing and academic-funding-cycle demand is inflecting positive, filed and confirmed by two consecutive quarters of raised guidance off real beats, not just narrative.
2. Scale and installed-base lock-in (consumables/reagents tied to instrument base) give a durable, high-visibility recurring-revenue core that few competitors can match.
3. Continued disciplined bolt-on M&A (Solventum Purification & Filtration) extends the platform into adjacent high-growth categories (bioprocessing filtration).

## 8. Bear case
1. The reverse-DCF implies more growth (10.2%) than this analyst's base case (8%) can deliver — the single biggest reason for caution, independent of business quality.
2. Consolidated total debt rose ~$3.1B in six months (to $42.3B) without this pass fully tracing the cause to a single disclosed transaction, alongside a sharply accelerated buyback pace ($4.0B YTD through Q2'26) funded partly by drawing consolidated cash from $9.85B to $4.06B — a leverage trajectory that deserves closer scrutiny before adding.
3. Momentum percentile is weak (0.10) even after the guidance raises — a real tension between the quant data and the narrative that this dossier flags as a data conflict rather than resolving in either direction.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Organic revenue growth (ex-recent M&A) falls below 4% YoY for two consecutive quarters.
2. Adjusted EPS guidance is cut from the current $24.93–$25.33 FY2026 range (versus two consecutive raises to date).
3. Estimated consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above 3.5x (from an estimated ≈3.3–3.4x today) without a clear, disclosed deleveraging plan.
4. Bioprocessing/pharma-services order momentum (book-to-bill) turns negative for two consecutive quarters, indicating the recovery was cyclical, not secular.
5. The reverse-DCF implied growth gap versus this dossier's 8% base case widens further (i.e., price appreciates without a fundamental acceleration that justifies it) — a valuation-discipline trigger to formally reject rather than continue watching.

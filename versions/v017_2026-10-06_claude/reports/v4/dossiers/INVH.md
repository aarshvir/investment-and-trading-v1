# INVH — Invitation Homes Inc. (single-family rental REIT) — diligence dossier, agent F120

Prices as of the 25 Sep 2026 close ($26.62). Research only, not personalised investment advice.

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (thesis horizon 12-36 months). A sound, cash-generative landlord. At $26.62 it trades at 16.1x 2026 AFFO and a 4.5% dividend yield, but the market already prices about 4.4% a year of AFFO growth against my 3.0% base case, so the expected return (+5.9%/yr) is below the 8.5% cost of equity. What would change it: a price near $22-23 (about 13.5x AFFO), or evidence that same-store NOI growth is back above 3%.

**Triage overturned on the reasoning, not the outcome.** The quick triage (Q14) rejected the name on "36x NTM P/E" and V1 called it "excessive" from a reverse DCF on GAAP EBIT with D&A set to zero. For a REIT both are the wrong basis (sector playbook realestate-reit.md: P/E and EBIT-based DCF "close to meaningless" because of real-estate depreciation). On the correct basis (Core FFO / AFFO per share) the stock is fairly priced, not excessive. The WATCH verdict stands for a different reason.

## 2. Business in plain English
INVH owns and operates about 85,500 single-family homes (wholly owned, 30 Jun 2026) that it rents to families in the US Sun Belt and West, plus about 8,000 joint-venture homes and 15,600 managed-only homes. Revenue is rent (about 81%), ancillary income, management fees, and, since Jan 2026, homebuilding revenue from ResiBuilt, a build-to-rent contractor it acquired. Scale in leasing, maintenance and procurement is its edge; the durable demand driver is that renting a house costs far less than owning one (management cites John Burns data of over $1,000 a month less).

## 3. Why the model likes it, and whether that is durable
b1 composite 0.879 (decile 9, live rank 60): strongest on the S family (0.91) and Q (0.75); V 0.61; M weak (0.36). The S and Q credit comes from net share issuance of -3.7% (buybacks, real) and low accruals. The value reading (FCF yield 7.6%, earnings yield 4.2%) is flattered: d4/d3 free cash flow is operating cash flow less a few million of capex, but INVH spends about $165M a year on recurring capital expenditure (AFFO deduction: $82.3M in H1 2026). Adjusted for that, the durable yield is the 6.2% AFFO yield. Partly durable; the buyback-driven part depends on asset sales continuing at high prices.

## 7. Valuation and V1 reconciliation
- V1 (v1_valuation_table.csv / v1_valuation.json): verdict **"excessive"** (score -1.0); implied 10-year growth 40.1% from a FCFF DCF on EBIT with D&A set to zero; scenarios errored ("insufficient history"), so no V1 base return exists. **I disagree with the V1 method and the verdict's size.** V1 flags itself: "da_ratio unavailable ... treated as 0 in reverse DCF" and P/FFO history is 0 months. EBIT of $251M on $2,855M revenue excludes about $770M of annual depreciation add-back that a REIT's FFO includes.
- Correct basis: price / 2026 AFFO guidance midpoint ("$1.65") = 16.1x; price / Core FFO midpoint ("$1.95") = 13.7x; dividend yield 4.5%; consensus target mean $33.52 (d4).
- Reverse DCF on the correct basis (dividend discount, payout 73% of AFFO, exit 15.0x AFFO in year 10, cost of equity 8.49% from V1's beta): implied AFFO/share growth of **4.4% a year for 10 years** (3.9% at an 8.0% discount rate).
- My evidence-based cases, 3-year annualised total return (price + dividends), start AFFO $1.65, dividend $1.20:
  - Bear -3.9%/yr: AFFO -1%/yr (regulation, insurance and tax cost growth, rate refinancing), exit 12.5x.
  - Base +5.9%/yr: AFFO +3%/yr (same-store NOI about 2.5%, buyback accretion about 1%, refinancing drag), exit 15.5x, dividend grows 3%.
  - Bull +13.7%/yr: AFFO +6%/yr, exit 18x.
- **implied vs base: above** (4.4% implied vs 3.0% base). Rule says INCLUDE needs implied at or below base; hence WATCH.

## 8. Bull and bear
Bull: (1) New-lease rent growth turned positive (+1.1% in Q2-26 vs -3.0% in Q1) and management says it accelerated every month; (2) the ROAD to Housing Act bans purchases of existing homes by 350+-home investors from 7 Jan 2027 but exempts build-to-rent and does not force sales, which shields the installed base from new institutional competition; (3) leverage of 5.4x with stock below the price it is selling homes at leaves room for continued buybacks.
Bear: (1) Same-store NOI guidance of +0.4% to +1.9% is barely above inflation, with property taxes +4-5% and insurance +5-7% in the expense guide; (2) the ROAD Act (law 11 Jul 2026) and the 10-Q's own risk factor say "prospective institutional purchasers" are deferring build-to-rent and fee-build purchases, hitting the new ResiBuilt and construction-lending lines; (3) refinancing of $988M 2027 secured and other debt at about 5% against 3.9% book.

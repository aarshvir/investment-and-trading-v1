## 2. Business in plain English
Zebra makes the barcode scanners, rugged mobile computers, RFID readers and industrial/label printers that retailers, warehouses, hospitals and factories use to track inventory and people. It sells hardware plus a growing software/services and subscription layer (Zebra OneCare, Machine Vision, and the 2025 Elo Touch acquisition added self-service kiosks/POS touch computing) mostly through a channel-partner network, with a large embedded base that recurs through consumables (labels/ribbons) and refresh cycles.

## 6. Red flags
- **Cyclicality, not fraud:** no active SEC/DOJ investigation or restatement found for 2026. A 2023 securities-fraud class action alleged Zebra understated post-Motorola-Enterprise-acquisition weaknesses before a guidance cut; that predates this dossier's window and is noted for completeness, not as a live risk.
- **M&A-inflated headline growth:** ~8 points of the FY2026 sales-growth range quoted in §4 ("between 14% and 16%") is acquisition/FX, not organic.
- **Concentration in retail/warehouse capex cycles:** the same cyclicality that caused the 2023–24 destocking trough can recur if enterprise IT/warehouse capex slows.
- No going-concern language, no auditor change disclosed in the reviewed filings.

## 8. Bull case
1. Elo Touch and Machine Vision extend Zebra beyond scan-and-print into adjacent, higher-growth digital-checkout and computer-vision categories, lifting the durable growth rate above the market's implied 6.4%.
2. Adjusted operating margin at a multi-year high (26.4% in Q2 2026) shows real operating leverage from the post-2023 cost actions, not just a sales rebound. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: about 4.7 points of that margin is the one-off tariff refund; the underlying adjusted margin is about 21.7%]**
3. Guidance has been raised twice in 2026 (Q1 then Q2) against a backdrop of still-cautious retail capex — a sign of genuine demand strength, not just easy comps.

## Bear case
1. ~8 points of FY2026 guided growth is inorganic (M&A/FX); strip it out and organic growth is closer to high single digits, much less exciting than the headline.
2. The 2023–24 destocking cycle shows Zebra's hardware order book can swing sharply with enterprise capex; a slowdown in retail/logistics capex (rate-sensitive, still elevated after the tariff-driven 2025–26 uncertainty) could repeat that pattern.
3. Trailing GAAP P/E of ~34x (vs ~17.6x on adjusted/non-GAAP EPS) means any re-rating of "adjusted" back toward GAAP, or a growth disappointment, hits the stock disproportionately.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Full-year 2026 organic (ex-M&A/FX) net sales growth falls below **6%** for two consecutive quarters (the ~8-point M&A/FX contribution is quoted verbatim in §4).
2. Non-GAAP diluted EPS guidance is **cut** below the "$20.75 to $21.25" FY2026 range quoted in §4, at either of the next two quarterly reports.
3. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA (using the $493M long-term-debt figure plus current borrowings, confirmed against the 10-Q debt footnote) **[Corrected 2026-10-06: total debt is $2,776M (net $2,619M) with $2,275M due within 12 months]** rises above **2.5x**.
4. Adjusted operating margin falls back below **22%** (i.e., more than 4 points below the Q2 2026 26.4% print) for two consecutive quarters.
5. A securities-related investigation or restatement is disclosed (there is none as of this dossier).

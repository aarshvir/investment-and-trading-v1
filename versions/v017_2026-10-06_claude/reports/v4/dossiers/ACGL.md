## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** — thesis horizon 12–36 months. Diversified specialty P&C/reinsurance/mortgage insurer with an
industry-leading underwriting record. The reinsurance/property-cat pricing cycle is softening (confirmed in
primary filings, not just triage), but Arch's core combined ratio remains excellent (82–84% ex-cat/PYD), ROE is
still 15–18% on an operating basis, and — unlike the triage note's qualitative "already prices in the cycle
turn" claim — my own reverse valuation shows the price implies **declining** normalized earnings, i.e. the market
is pricing in more cyclical deterioration than the evidence in the last two quarters supports. Reservation: cat
losses and reserve releases are lumpy quarter to quarter (see §6), so this is INCLUDE, not INCLUDE-SMALL, only
because the underlying core loss ratio trend (not the reported EPS) is what should be tracked.

## 2. Business in plain English
Arch Capital underwrites property & casualty insurance, reinsurance and mortgage insurance globally through
three segments (Insurance, Reinsurance, Mortgage). It makes money from the spread between premiums collected and
losses/expenses paid out (underwriting profit) plus investment income earned on the "float" (premiums held
before claims are paid). Competitive position: management is widely regarded (and its own results confirm) as
one of the most disciplined underwriters in the sector — it shrinks lines when pricing is inadequate and grows
when pricing is attractive, rather than chasing volume.

## 5. Guidance track record
Arch, like most P&C/reinsurance underwriters, **does not issue formal forward EPS or combined-ratio guidance**.
There is no quarterly guide to raise/maintain/cut. The closest forward-looking disclosures are qualitative
management commentary on rate adequacy by line (see quotes in §3/§8) and disclosed catastrophe-loss/reserve-
development figures each quarter, which are backward-looking actuals, not forecasts.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3):**
1. Combined ratio, even after the recent softening, remains firmly profitable (82–84%) — Arch has repeatedly
   shown it will shrink lines rather than chase inadequate pricing, protecting the underwriting margin better
   than reinsurance peers historically have in past soft markets.
2. Continued double-digit share-count reduction ($783m + $1.2bn repurchased in H1 2026 alone) compounds book
   value per share (+1.7%, then +2.8% QoQ) even if absolute earnings growth stalls.
3. Mortgage insurance and specialty insurance segments diversify away from the property-cat cycle that is
   driving the softness noted here, unlike single-line P&C reinsurers.

**Bear (3):**
1. Ex-cat/PYD combined ratio has now risen in two straight quarters (81.0%→82.3% Q1 YoY; 80.9%→82.5% Q2 YoY) —
   a real, filing-confirmed trend, not noise; a continuation would compress margins further.
2. Favorable reserve development ($200m Q1 → $165m Q2, sequentially smaller) has been a persistent EPS tailwind;
   if releases turn to strengthening (even modestly), reported EPS would fall further than the core trend alone
   implies.
3. No forward guidance means the market must extrapolate the cycle turn from partial data; a sharper-than-
   expected property-cat rate decline (a live industry risk in 2026–27 given several benign hurricane seasons)
   could push the combined ratio toward 90%+ faster than my base case assumes.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Combined ratio excluding cat/PYD exceeds **90%** in any reported quarter (vs 82.3–82.5% in the last two).
2. Favorable prior-year reserve development turns **negative** (net reserve strengthening) in any quarter.
3. Annualized operating ROE falls below **10%** for two consecutive quarters (vs 15.3–15.4% in the last two).
4. Book value per common share declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters (it has risen every quarter
   shown here).
5. Share repurchase pace falls below $400m/quarter for two consecutive quarters without an explicit capital-
   allocation explanation (signals management no longer sees the stock as attractively priced, or a reserve/
   capital problem).

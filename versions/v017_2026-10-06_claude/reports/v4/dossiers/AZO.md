# AZO — AutoZone, Inc. (NYSE: AZO)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — 24–36 month horizon. Durable, well-run auto-parts retailer compounding revenue at ~7–8%/yr with a share-buyback engine that has driven negative book equity; core economics are intact (adjusted debt/EBITDAR flat at 2.5x), but this fiscal year's EPS growth decelerated sharply from AutoZone's historical mid-teens pace to roughly mid-single-digits on gross-margin deleverage, and the negative-equity balance sheet leaves no cushion if a recession hits used-car-age-driven demand at the same time debt service needs cash. Reservation: size small given the leverage structure and the deceleration in per-share earnings growth versus AutoZone's own history.

## 2. Business in plain English
AutoZone sells automotive replacement parts, accessories and maintenance items to DIY (do-it-yourself) retail customers and, increasingly, to commercial repair shops (DIFM, "do it for me") through a dense US/Mexico/Brazil store network with same-day/overnight parts delivery. It makes money on retail gross margin (~52–53%) funded by scale purchasing and a network of distribution centers, and has for decades redeployed nearly all free cash flow into share buybacks rather than dividends, which has pushed reported book equity negative. Competitive position: one of three dominant national auto-parts chains (with O'Reilly and Advance), with store density and commercial-delivery speed as the moat.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Triage/model context: the quant model ranked AZO 479 of ~500 (very poor rank, largely because momentum has been -30% over the past year and the model has almost no coverage of the "financial" quality family for AZO, given the negative book equity makes standard ROE/leverage ratios undefined). Triage overrode the poor rank specifically because the weak momentum reads like "a screen hiding a good business" rather than a deteriorating one. Having now checked the primary filings: **partially confirmed, partially not.** The business is genuinely durable (steady 7–8% revenue growth, stable ~2.5x adjusted leverage), but the triage's stated reason — "guided EPS growth of 34%" — does not match what I can verify from the filings: AutoZone does not issue EPS guidance at all, and trailing GAAP diluted EPS growth this fiscal year was roughly mid-single digits (Q4 FY26 EPS +15% YoY was the strongest print, aided by buybacks, but full-year EPS growth was closer to +5–6%). **This is a data conflict I am flagging explicitly** — the 34% figure appears to be either a misread of a single quarter's growth rate or an unsourced generic-knowledge claim, not something documented in the filings I reviewed.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Eight straight quarters (all shown here) of positive domestic same-store sales (+1.6% to +4.8%), with commercial (DIFM) growth outpacing DIY — a structurally growing, repair-not-replace category.
2. Adjusted leverage flat at 2.5x while still funding both elevated growth capex and buybacks — the balance sheet is not visibly deteriorating despite negative book equity.
3. Tariff-refund and buyback authorization increases ($1.6B remaining vs $632M a year ago) suggest management sees capital-return capacity ahead.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Reported EPS growth decelerated hard from AutoZone's historical mid-teens pace to ~5.5% for FY2026, driven by opex deleverage ("investments to support growth initiatives") outrunning gross-margin gains in three of four quarters.
2. Negative stockholders' equity of $(2.78B) means there is no equity cushion; if a downturn hits (used-car-age cycle turning, as triage flagged) at the same time as elevated capex, the buyback engine that has driven per-share value for 20+ years could have to pause.
3. A meaningful share of this year's reported gross-margin improvement (LIFO swing + tariff refunds) is non-recurring — underlying margin trend is closer to flat-to-down, which the reported headline numbers can obscure.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Domestic same-store sales turn negative for two consecutive fiscal quarters (from +1.6% to +4.8% now).
2. Adjusted debt/EBITDAR rises above 3.0x (from 2.5x now).
3. Full-year diluted EPS growth (ex-LIFO/ex-tariff one-offs) is negative for a full fiscal year.
4. Share-repurchase spend falls below $1.5B over a trailing four quarters (from a pace of ~$427–540M/quarter now, i.e., an implicit signal management is preserving cash for stress) without a stated capex-driven reason.
5. A second consecutive year of gross-margin deleverage (ex the identified one-off items).

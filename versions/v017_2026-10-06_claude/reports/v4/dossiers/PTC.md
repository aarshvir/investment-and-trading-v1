## 2. Business in plain English
PTC sells product-lifecycle-management (PLM), CAD and industrial IoT/AI software — Windchill (PLM), Creo/Onshape (CAD), Codebeamer (ALM) and ServiceMax (field service) — to industrial manufacturers (aerospace, medtech, auto, electronics) who need a single digital record of a product from design through service. Revenue is subscription-based (ARR), high-switching-cost (multi-year implementations embedded in engineering workflows), sold direct and via a growing partner channel (Rockwell, Microsoft).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. ARR reacceleration to 9%+ cc growth, beating guidance twice in the last two quarters, with management raising the full-year range each time.
2. Divestiture of lower-margin/slower-growth IoT (Kepware/ThingWorx) is a genuine portfolio upgrade — proceeds funded both debt paydown flexibility and buybacks, and the remaining PLM/CAD/ALM core is the highest-margin, highest-switching-cost part of the business.
3. AI/"Intelligent Product Lifecycle" positioning (per CEO commentary in the Q3'26 release) is a plausible new upsell vector on top of an already-embedded install base, not a speculative pivot.

**Bear:**
1. GAAP EPS for FY26 is inflated by a non-recurring $463M divestiture gain; if the market is pricing FY26 GAAP EPS naively, a "beat" narrative could reverse once FY27 comps normalize without the gain.
2. New debt draw ($1.17B → $1.4B LT debt in one quarter) to fund buybacks after already receiving divestiture cash is a capital-allocation choice to monitor — priority should be growth investment/deleveraging, not just EPS support via buybacks.
3. Revenue growth guide for FY26 (as reported, including lapping the divested businesses) is actually **negative to flat (-2% to 0%)** — the ARR/organic story is good, but headline revenue optics will look weak for several more quarters and could pressure the stock on GAAP-revenue-focused screens (exactly the mechanism that triggered the original triage flag).

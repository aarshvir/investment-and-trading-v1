# BXP - BXP, Inc. (F150, STANDARD depth)

**1. VERDICT: WATCH** - the largest US premier-office landlord is recovering (occupancy 86.0% to 88.4% in three quarters, FFO guidance raised in July) and pays a 4.4% dividend, but the price already implies about 3.8% a year of distributable-cash growth forever at a 9% cost of equity, against my base of 3.0%, with 7.9x net debt/EBITDAre, a $1.0bn note maturity on 1 Oct 2026 being refinanced at 6.05% versus 2.75%, and a $2.3bn mortgage due June 2027. Horizon 12-36 months. Condition that would change it: a price near $55 (implied growth falls to my 3% base at 9%), or evidence that 2027 FFO can grow above 3% despite the refinancing drag. Triage (Q14: advance = No, "9.8x net debt/EBITDA") is upheld in outcome but the 9.8x figure does not match the company's own measure (section 6).

**2. Business in plain English.** BXP owns and develops premier office buildings in Boston, New York, San Francisco, Washington DC and Los Angeles (plus life-science and some residential/retail), earning rent on long leases; 91.0% of annualised rental obligations come from central-business-district clients (Q2-26 release). It is a REIT, so it distributes at least 90% of taxable income. Competitors are other gateway-market office owners; the moat is location, scale and development capability. Earnings are levered: debt is about 60% of enterprise value.

**3. Why the model likes it.** b1 composite_qvm 0.38 (live_rank 362, decile 3): Value 0.77 (EBIT/EV 97th pct, book/price 71st pct, earnings yield 36th), Quality 0.41, Sentiment 0.29 (SUE -0.08), Momentum 0.19 (12-1 return -5.4%). The value reading is real on FFO (P/FFO 9.0x) but EBIT/EV flatters a leveraged REIT because depreciation and leverage are not reflected in EBIT; FFO and FAD are the correct yardsticks here.

# GEV — GE Vernova Inc. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F44, wave 2, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — overturns triage's implicit "price is fine" read (price_vs_growth 4/5). This is a genuinely strong, accelerating business (backlog $176bn, orders +88% organically in 2Q26, guidance raised in each of the last three quarters), but the 25-Sep-2026 close prices in roughly 20%+ annualized growth for a decade — well above a defensible base case. Per the wave-2 mandate, a great business priced for more than it can deliver is WATCH, not INCLUDE. Thesis horizon: revisit if the multiple de-rates or backlog conversion confirms the 2028 outlook is on track without relying on Wind-segment improvement that the numbers don't yet show.

## 2. Business in plain English
GE Vernova (spun off from General Electric in 2024) makes the equipment that generates and moves electricity: gas turbines and related services (Power segment), grid/transformer/switchgear equipment increasingly sold into AI data centers (Electrification), and wind turbines (Wind, structurally loss-making). It sells to utilities, independent power producers, grid operators and, increasingly, data-center developers and their power suppliers — riding the AI-driven electricity-demand buildout.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Triage (Q09) cited a $176B backlog, gas-turbine backlog growing from 100 to 116+ GW, data-center electrification orders exceeding all of 2025 in Q1'26 alone, and guidance raised each of the last two quarters, concluding the elevated 36x NTM multiple was supported. Diligence confirms every one of those facts from primary sources (2Q26 press release, filed 2026-07-22) and adds a third consecutive guidance raise (see §5) — the momentum is real and durable in the sense that it is order-book-driven, not accounting-driven, in Power and Electrification. The valuation conclusion does not survive contact with the numbers, however (see §7): the market is not pricing "strong growth," it is pricing growth at a pace GE Vernova's own long-range outlook does not project past 2028.

Guidance has been raised, never cut, in every quarter reviewed — a genuinely strong track record. The **FCF guide nearly doubled in one quarter** (from $6.5–7.5bn to $11.5–12.5bn) while the revenue and margin guides moved only modestly — this is disproportionate and not explained in the press release. The likely driver (not confirmed in the exhibit reviewed) is customer milestone/progress-payment timing on the surging gas-turbine and data-center backlog, which is a **working-capital tailwind that would reverse if order growth slows** — flagged as a valuation-quality issue, not merely a positive.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Balance sheet:** $3.72bn total debt vs $12.72bn cash and short-term investments (d4 snapshot, 2026-09-25) — net cash of roughly $9bn, minimal leverage risk. Capital returned to shareholders: $3.6bn (FY2025), $1.4bn (1Q26), a further $2.5bn (2Q26, cumulative $3.9bn YTD) — includes a $0.50/share quarterly dividend and buybacks; dividend yield is negligible (~0.2%) so this is predominantly buybacks.
- **Segment quality — Wind is a live, unreconciled red flag:** FY2025 Wind segment EBITDA loss was **−$598mm** (full year). The 4Q2025 release guided FY2026 Wind losses to narrow to "approximately $400 million." That guidance figure was repeated unchanged in both the 1Q2026 and 2Q2026 releases even though **1H2026 Wind segment EBITDA was already −$657mm** — i.e., the first six months of 2026 alone lost more than the full-year target, and the 2Q26 quarter's Wind EBITDA margin (−13.6%) was worse than the year-ago quarter's (−7.3%). **This is a genuine, primary-source data conflict**: either the FY2026 Wind guidance is stale/wrong, or management expects a swing to Wind profitability in 2H2026 that is not stated anywhere in the reviewed releases. This was not something triage caught (its red flag was about a forward-EPS base-year distortion, not this).
- **Segment strength:** Power segment EBITDA margin 18.8% (2Q26) and Electrification 18.4% (2Q26), both expanding meaningfully y/y (+245bps and +357bps respectively on the quarter) — the parts of the business actually driving the guidance raises are executing well.

## 7. Valuation — reverse DCF (own model; no V1 row exists for GEV)
No V1 systematic valuation row exists for GEV — `v1_verdict` is null. Standard capital-goods valuation (EV/EBITDA, not FCFF DCF given the WC-driven FCF noted above) at the 25-Sep-2026 close of **$957.63** (market cap $255.0bn; EV ≈ $246.1bn using $3.72bn debt and $12.72bn cash, per d4 snapshot) against FY2026 guidance-midpoint adjusted EBITDA of ~$5.98bn (46bn revenue × 13% margin midpoint) implies **~41x forward EV/EBITDA**. Reverse-DCF: solving for the 10-year EBITDA CAGR that justifies this EV at a 10% discount rate and a 14x exit multiple (a reasonable mid-cycle multiple for a scaled, still-growing capital-goods/grid-equipment franchise) requires **~22–23% annualized EBITDA growth for 10 straight years**; even generous assumptions (16x exit multiple, 9% discount rate) still require **~20%**. My own base case — GE Vernova's own 2028 outlook ($56bn revenue, 20% margin ≈ $11.2bn EBITDA, implying ~37%/yr for the next two years) decelerating over years 3–10 toward a still-strong 8–10%/yr as the AI-power capex supercycle normalizes toward broader grid/utility capex growth — blends to roughly **~14%/yr over 10 years**. **Implied vs base: above.** The stock is priced for continued hyper-growth well beyond the company's own explicit two-year outlook; per the eligibility rule, this makes GEV ineligible for INCLUDE/INCLUDE-SMALL regardless of how good the underlying business is.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Orders +88% organically (2Q26) and backlog +$13bn sequentially for two consecutive quarters show the AI/data-center power buildout is, if anything, still accelerating, not decelerating. (2) Power segment margin expansion (18.8% and rising) shows genuine operating leverage, not just volume. (3) Net cash balance sheet plus $12bn+ of FCF guided gives enormous flexibility to fund capacity expansion (management's own 20 GW→24 GW→30 GW gas-turbine output ramp) without dilution.
**Bear:** (1) The valuation (~41x forward EV/EBITDA, ~20%+ implied 10-year growth) leaves no room for even a modest disappointment — a single guidance miss would likely produce a large de-rating. (2) Wind segment losses are running well ahead of the company's own stated full-year guidance with no public reconciliation — either a forecasting problem or an undisclosed 2H26 assumption; either way it is evidence the guidance numbers deserve more scrutiny than the "raised every quarter" headline suggests. (3) The FY2026 FCF guide nearly doubled in one quarter on a much smaller revenue/margin guide change — consistent with a working-capital/progress-billing tailwind from backlog growth that would reverse if order intake decelerates, meaning today's FCF yield may overstate sustainable cash generation.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Quarterly order growth (y/y, organic) falls below **20%** for two consecutive quarters (vs +71% 1Q26, +88% 2Q26).
2. Adjusted EBITDA margin fails to reach the **12–14%** FY2026 guided range in either 3Q or 4Q2026.
3. FY2026 Wind segment EBITDA loss is confirmed (at FY2026 results) to exceed **$700 million** (vs the standing ~$400mm guide), i.e., the current guidance/actuals gap is never reconciled.
4. Free cash flow for 2H2026 comes in below **$5 billion** combined (i.e., the raised $11.5–12.5bn FY guide is not met), confirming the 2Q26 FCF jump was working-capital timing rather than sustainable.
5. EV/forward-adjusted-EBITDA multiple compresses below **25x** without a corresponding fundamental deterioration (a valuation-only de-rating would be the point at which this name becomes newly investable on the same fundamentals).

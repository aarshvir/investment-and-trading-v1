# DAL — Delta Air Lines, Inc. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F41, Wave 2, Standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — the strongest US airline franchise (SkyMiles/Amex co-brand economics differentiate it from commodity airline economics), fairly priced with no real margin of safety; named reservation: a large mid-2026 jet-fuel spike and a December-2026 pilot-contract re-opener are two concrete, near-term tests the thesis has not yet passed. Thesis horizon: 12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Delta is a full-service US "Big 3" airline operating a global hub network (Atlanta, Detroit, Minneapolis, Salt Lake City, New York, LA) plus premium international joint ventures (Air France-KLM, Virgin Atlantic, LATAM, Aeromexico, Korean Air). Roughly half of its earnings power comes from non-ticket sources that are less commoditized than a seat: the SkyMiles/American Express co-brand card program (loyalty cash flow sold to Amex), Delta Premium Select/first-class upsell, cargo, and Delta TechOps/MRO (aircraft maintenance for other airlines) — the reason the triage note calls its "loyalty economics" a differentiator from a pure commodity-seat business.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Triage: quality 4/5, growth 4/5, price_vs_growth 4/5, no red flags; "18.7% revenue growth, 20% ROE at just 10.7x NTM P/E; cheap for the quality." b1 factor read: composite driven mainly by Momentum (12-1m return ~39%, 78th pct) and Earnings-momentum (SUE), with Quality percentiles decent but not exceptional (ROE 44.9th pct is unremarkable for the family average once leverage is included). **This is a mostly durable read** — the loyalty/co-brand and MRO revenue streams are structurally differentiated and have grown for years — but part of the recent momentum score is a **fuel-driven margin swing artefact**: the 2026 fare/margin story is entangled with a large jet-fuel cost spike (see §6), which cuts both ways in the numbers below and needs to be read net of fuel, not gross.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Fuel shock:** per-gallon jet-fuel cost rose from a $2.30–2.60 range in 2025 to **$3.93** in the June-2026 quarter — a large, disclosed, exogenous cost shock (context: a war-driven oil-price spike also flagged elsewhere in this program's diligence, e.g. the EOG dossier). This is the primary driver of the Q1 2026 GAAP net loss (−$289m) and the Q2 2026 adjusted-operating-margin decline (−24% YoY) — **not** an execution failure; per the sector playbook, airline OPM is "mostly not a skill signal" when the dominant cost line is exogenous fuel.
- **FCF (FY, OCF−capex, $m):** FY2022 6,363−6,366=−3 (breakeven); FY2023 6,464−5,323=1,141; FY2024 8,025−5,140=2,885; FY2025 8,342−4,499=**3,843**. - **Leverage:** company-stated gross leverage target ≈2x (guided, presumably lease-adjusted/EBITDAR-based — not independently rebuilt from raw XBRL at this depth, a limitation per the sector playbook's EBITDAR methodology). - Pilot-contract re-opener: current agreement amendable **31 December 2026**; a flight-attendant unionization drive is also underway (per search results) — both are real, disclosed labor-cost risks not yet reflected in the FY2026 guide's assumptions in detail.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) SkyMiles/Amex co-brand and premium/MRO diversification give Delta a structurally better revenue mix than commodity-seat peers, and it is trading at a discount to its own 10-year P/E history (63.9th percentile is mid-range, not stretched) **[Corrected 2026-10-06: a 63.9th percentile means the multiple is above its own median, i.e. a premium to history, not a discount]** despite the best fundamentals in the group; (2) FCF has grown for three straight years to a guided $3–4bn in FY2026, funding continued deleveraging (debt down from $18.1bn to $12.9bn since Q1 2024); (3) full-year guidance was reaffirmed despite a real fuel shock, evidence of pricing power/cost-recapture discipline.
**Bear:** (1) Jet fuel per gallon nearly doubled intra-year (2025's $2.30–2.60 range to $3.93 in Q2 2026) and already drove a Q1 2026 GAAP net loss and a 24% YoY adjusted-operating-income decline in Q2 2026 — if oil stays elevated, FY2026 guidance is at risk despite today's reaffirmation; (2) the pilot-contract re-opener (amendable 31 Dec 2026) and a flight-attendant unionization drive are two concrete, dated labor-cost catalysts not yet priced into the guide in detail; (3) V1's own base-case 3-yr return is slightly negative — this is a "fair," not "cheap," entry point in a historically boom-bust, high-fixed-cost industry with no valuation cushion if either fuel or labor costs surprise to the upside.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Company-guided FY2026 free cash flow ($3–4bn) is **cut** or actual FY2026 FCF comes in **below $2.5bn**.
2. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance ($6.50–7.50) is **cut** at any remaining 2026 release.
3. Company-stated gross leverage rises **above 3.0x** (guided ≈2x).
4. Jet-fuel cost per gallon sustains **above $3.50** for two consecutive quarters without offsetting fare/capacity action (already at $3.93 in Q2 2026 — a live trigger to monitor, not yet tripped on guidance terms).
5. The pilot-contract re-opener (amendable 31-Dec-2026) concludes with a ratified agreement carrying **double-digit annual unit-cost escalation** beyond current assumptions, or results in a work action/strike.

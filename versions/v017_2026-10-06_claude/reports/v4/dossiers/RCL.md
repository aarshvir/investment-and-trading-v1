## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 12–36 months. One-sentence reason: an industry-leading cruise operator executing a genuine beat-and-raise cycle on primary-source numbers, but the reverse DCF shows the price already embeds roughly as much sustained growth as a fair base case can justify, and the newly announced, fully debt-financed ~$3bn Sandals/Beaches resorts joint venture (2026-09-23, unaudited/pending, closing early 2027) adds leverage and diversification risk on top of an already elevated balance sheet from the newbuild cycle — the specific reservation for the half-weight sizing.

## 2. Business in plain English

Royal Caribbean Group operates 71 cruise ships under three wholly owned brands — Royal Caribbean International, Celebrity Cruises and Silversea — plus a 50% joint-venture stake in TUI Cruises (Mein Schiff/Hapag-Lloyd brands), sailing to more than 1,000 destinations. It sells vacation packages (transportation, lodging, food, entertainment) directly and through travel agents to mostly North American and European consumers, monetized through ticket price ("Net Yield") plus onboard/pre-cruise spend. Its competitive edge is the newest, largest-capacity ships in the industry (the Icon-class, e.g. Icon of the Seas, Star of the Seas, Legend of the Seas) combined with owned private destinations (Perfect Day, Royal Beach Club), which let it charge premium prices and fill ships above nominal capacity (110% load factor in Q2 2026). It is now diversifying beyond cruise: entering river cruising in 2027 (Celebrity River Cruises) and, per the 2026-09-23 announcement, acquiring a 50% stake in Sandals and Beaches Resorts (Caribbean all-inclusive land resorts).

## 3. Why the model likes it — and whether that's durable

Value-family scores (E/P −16%, B/P −58% raw) are actually negative/weak in the raw quant panel — the model does **not** flag RCL as statistically cheap on book-value or plain earnings yield; the "cheap" read in the triage (12.4x NTM P/E) is a forward-multiple observation, not a systematic value-factor signal, and the two should not be conflated. **Durable**: four consecutive quarters of Adjusted-EPS guidance raises or beats built on real booked-position strength and record demand (see §5). **Less durable / worth discounting**: the ROE percentile and asset-growth score partly reflect the still-recovering post-COVID capital base (equity of only $10.2bn against $44.6bn of assets) rather than a structurally higher return on capital; leverage remains a real constraint (see §6).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **FCF conversion is currently negative** — a genuine adverse fact, not a quality-of-earnings gimmick: TTM (Q3'25–Q2'26) operating cash flow $6,786m less capex $7,202m = **–$416m FCF**, driven by the Icon-class newbuild programme (FY2026 capex guided ~$4.7bn, of which $1.6bn is "non-newbuild"; Legend of the Seas delivered Q2 2026, Icon VI/VII ordered with committed financing in April 2026). This is a temporary, disclosed capital cycle, not an operating problem — TTM GAAP net income of $4.4bn and Adjusted EBITDA of ~$6.9bn (TTM op income $5.1bn + TTM D&A $1.8bn) are both healthy and growing — but it means the stock cannot currently be judged on an FCF-yield basis; we use TTM (GAAP/Adjusted) net income as the reverse-DCF base instead (§7), labeled accordingly.
- **Leverage**: total debt incl. current maturities $23,407m, cash $875m → net debt **$22,532m**; net debt/EBITDA (TTM) ≈ **3.26x** — elevated for the sector, moving in the right direction only slowly given continued heavy capex. Debt maturities per the Q2 2026 release: 2026 $0.9bn, 2027 $2.7bn, 2028 $3.4bn, 2029 $1.2bn, 2030 $1.3bn. Liquidity $6.9bn (cash + undrawn $6.6bn revolver, increased by $250m in July 2026).
- **Capital return**: $199m of buybacks + $404m dividends in Q2 2026 alone (>$600m/quarter); $805m remaining under the current repurchase authorization; share count down from 274m (FY2025 average) to 268m (Q2 2026).
- **Pending M&A — the key new fact (2026-09-23 8-K, checked to cutoff)**: Royal Caribbean Group will acquire a 50% equity interest in Sandals and Beaches Resorts (Caribbean all-inclusive land resorts) for **approximately $3.0bn at ~10x forward EBITDA**, funded entirely by **committed debt financing from Morgan Stanley** — no equity issuance disclosed. Expected to close early 2027, "accretive to earnings next year." This is unaudited, self-reported deal economics (no independent fairness opinion or financing terms disclosed yet) and will push net debt/EBITDA higher just as the newbuild capex cycle is supposed to ease — the single biggest incremental balance-sheet risk in this dossier.

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse DCF

NTM P/E ~12.4x (triage_cards.csv), FCF yield not meaningful this year (§6). No V1 systematic valuation row exists for RCL in `v1_valuation_table.csv` — **v1_verdict is null**; this section is this dossier's own reverse DCF. Method: two-stage DCF (10-year explicit growth + 3% terminal growth), owner-earnings base = TTM Adjusted Net Income (~$4.38bn = $16.36 TTM adjusted EPS × 268m diluted shares) **substituted for FCF because FCF is temporarily negative from the newbuild cycle (§6) — a deliberate, disclosed methodology choice, not a mix of GAAP and adjusted figures** (the FCF proxy itself is the adjusted metric throughout). Cost of capital: CAPM cost of equity = 5.17% (10yr UST) + 1.75 (sourced beta, FMP profile) × 4.14% ERP = 12.4%; after-tax cost of debt 4.74% (6% pretax, 21.9% tax rate); weights from EV (25.7% debt / 74.3% equity) → **WACC ≈ 10.4%**.

**Implied 10-year owner-earnings growth at the 2026-09-25 close ($242.70): ≈ 8.0%** (sensitivity: 6.1% at WACC 9.5%, to 10.1% at WACC 11.5% — the estimate is meaningfully WACC-sensitive since beta is sourced but the debt/equity mix will move once the Sandals deal closes).

**My base case: ~7%/yr** — supported by guided capacity growth (2026 +6.6%, 2027 +4%, 2028 +6%, 2029 +7%, averaging ~5.9%), continued modest net-yield/pricing growth, and buyback-driven per-share accretion (~3–4%/yr at the current repurchase pace), partially offset by rising interest expense once the Sandals debt is drawn and a deceleration in fleet growth versus the 2024–2027 "Perfecta" push. **This is roughly in line with, not comfortably below, the ~8% implied at the central WACC estimate** — hence "in_line," not "below," and the INCLUDE-SMALL (not full INCLUDE) sizing.

Bear/base/bull (3-year annualized total return, including reinvested dividends/buyback yield in the scenario, WACC/terminal assumptions held constant):
- **Bear** (g = −2%: demand shock, e.g. a recession or a broader geopolitical disruption than Q1 2026's, combined with no capex relief): **−28%/yr**
- **Base** (g = 7%): **+9%/yr**
- **Bull** (g = 12%: Perfecta-style double-digit compounding sustained, Sandals JV accretive as promised, buybacks accelerate): **+33%/yr**

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:**
1. Booked position and pricing power are real and current, not backward-looking: record forward bookings, 110% load factor, Net Yields still guided up in FY2026 despite the Q1 disruption.
2. Newest, most differentiated fleet in the industry (Icon-class) supports a structural pricing premium over Carnival/NCLH that shows up directly in the 33%+ operating margins in seasonally strong quarters.
3. Aggressive, sustained capital return (>$2bn/yr net of newbuild capex once the current ship-delivery peak passes) directly compounds per-share value.

**Bear:**
1. Net debt/EBITDA (~3.3x) is already elevated by cruise-industry standards and about to rise further from a $3bn, fully debt-financed acquisition into an adjacent business (land resorts) where RCL has no operating track record.
2. FCF has been negative for four straight quarters; if newbuild capex does not taper as guided (2027 capacity growth of "only" 4% implies a pause, but Icon VI/VII orders show the pipeline is not closed), leverage could stay elevated for longer than the base case assumes.
3. Demand is not immune to shocks: the company itself cut FY2026 guidance in April 2026 citing "prolonged geopolitical activity" affecting Mediterranean and West-Coast-Mexico itineraries — a real, disclosed, quantified (−$0.60/share-ish combined with fuel) demand/cost event within the trailing four quarters.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria

1. Net debt/EBITDA exceeds **4.0x** at any quarter-end after the Sandals deal closes (vs. ~3.26x now).
2. FCF (OCF − capex) remains negative for **two consecutive fiscal years** (i.e., still negative at FY2027 year-end) — would indicate the newbuild cycle is not tapering as guided.
3. FY Adjusted EPS guidance is cut in **two consecutive quarterly releases** (it has been cut once, in Q1 2026, and then raised back — a second consecutive cut would signal a demand trend, not a one-off).
4. Net Yield growth (as-reported) turns **negative** for two consecutive quarters.
5. The Sandals/Beaches JV fails to close as expected in early 2027, or closes with materially different financing terms (e.g., higher-than-disclosed interest cost) than announced 2026-09-23.

# Broadcom Inc. (AVGO) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — 12–36 month thesis horizon.
One-sentence reason: co-designed custom AI chips (XPUs) for the largest hyperscalers plus a dominant AI-networking franchise are producing genuine, filed operating-margin expansion (24%→54% in two years), and the reverse DCF says the price is roughly in line with a reasonable base case — but revenue is dangerously concentrated in a handful of customers, so it is investable, not a full-conviction position.

## 1. Business in plain English
Broadcom sells two things that matter here: (1) custom AI accelerators ("XPUs") that it co-designs with hyperscalers and AI labs — Google's TPU, OpenAI's new "Jalapeño" inference chip, and others — where Broadcom does the physical design/verification/packaging and the customer owns the architecture; and (2) AI networking silicon (Tomahawk Ethernet switches, Jericho fabric chips) that connects those chips into giant clusters. Layered on top is a large, slower-growing infrastructure-software business (VMware, acquired 2023) and legacy broadband/storage/wireless semiconductors. AI semiconductors are now the growth engine; software and legacy semis are the cash-generative, low-growth base.

## 5. Moat and competition
- **Design-win moat:** OpenAI's "Jalapeño" — its first custom Intelligence Processor — was co-developed with Broadcom from design to tape-out in 9 months, "the fastest ASIC development cycle ever achieved" per the companies' joint announcement; this is a real switching-cost moat (multi-year co-design relationships), not marketing. [4]
- **Google:** delivered Ironwood TPU v7 in high volume in Q3 FY26 and began production shipments of the next-gen TPU v8i — an established, multi-generation relationship. [1]
- **Customer concentration — the central risk:** six core custom-chip customers (reported to include Google, Meta, Anthropic, OpenAI) are expected to drive essentially all of the >$100B FY2027 AI-semiconductor revenue target, with four of the six "particularly large" — a $100B target spread six ways implies >$16B/customer on average. OpenAI is reportedly on track to become the second-largest XPU customer in FY2027. [5][6] Any one hyperscaler pausing or re-architecting its own silicon roadmap is a material single-name risk to the whole AI segment.
- **Networking:** Tomahawk 6 (102.4 Tbps Ethernet) entered volume production March-2026, the industry's first at that speed; Jericho4 fabric chip (51.2 Tbps, shipping since Aug-2025) interconnects over 1M XPUs across data centers — genuine technical leadership in the scale-out layer that AI clusters need regardless of whose chip sits inside them (a partial hedge against XPU customer concentration, since Broadcom sells networking silicon to NVIDIA-based clusters too). [1]

## 8. Bull case
1. Operating margin has more than doubled in two years (23.7%→53.9%) on filed numbers — genuine, not adjusted-metric, operating leverage.
2. $73B AI backlog gives multi-quarter revenue visibility rare among semiconductor companies.
3. Networking silicon (Tomahawk/Jericho) sells into AI clusters regardless of accelerator vendor — a real hedge against XPU customer concentration.

## 9. Bear case
1. Six customers drive substantially all AI-segment growth; four are "particularly large" — a single large customer's pause is a company-moving event.
2. SBC at 7–10% of revenue is a real, ongoing dilution/earnings-quality drag versus NVDA/AAPL.
3. The $230B FY2028 figure is "outlined," not firmly guided, and implies AI-segment revenue alone approaching Broadcom's entire current company size — a base-rate-defying multi-year target that the market may already be crediting.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria — thesis-invalidation triggers (what would change the verdict / break the thesis)
1. Any of the four largest XPU customers publicly cancels or materially shrinks its custom-silicon program.
2. AI-semiconductor revenue growth decelerates below 40% YoY for two consecutive quarters (vs. currently >100%).
3. Net debt/EBITDA (adjusted) rises above 2.5x (from ≈1.1x) on further debt-funded M&A. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: baseline mislabeled: ≈1.1x is gross debt/EBITDA (1.06x at May 2026); net debt/EBITDA was 0.74x in May and lower at Aug]**
4. VMware/infrastructure-software organic growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters.
5. SBC exceeds 12% of revenue for two consecutive quarters.

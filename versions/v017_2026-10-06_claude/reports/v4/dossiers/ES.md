# ES — Eversource Energy

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — thesis horizon 12–36 months, reservation: **the FERC transmission-ROE reduction and a recurring, still-growing offshore-wind exit liability are genuine, evidenced earnings drags that the "cheap, de-risked, pure-play T&D" triage framing understates.** The valuation (13.1x NTM P/E, positive FCF yield, 1.47x book) is the cheapest of the batch and reflects a real simplification (Aquarion Water sold, offshore wind fully exited), but FY2026 guidance was **cut**, not raised, this year, and the cheapness partly reflects that cut and unresolved regulatory risk rather than pure market mispricing.

## 2. Business in plain English
Eversource is a New England energy-delivery utility — regulated electric transmission and distribution in Connecticut, Massachusetts and New Hampshire, plus regulated natural-gas distribution — serving over 4 million electric and gas customers. It earns a regulator-approved return on its invested capital (rate base); revenue grows mainly by winning approval for new grid and gas-infrastructure investment, not by selling more units. It has spent the last two years simplifying: fully exiting offshore wind (2024) and, on 30 June 2026, completing the sale of its water utility (Aquarion), leaving a "pure-play regulated electric and natural gas delivery company" per management's own description.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1 factor scores (2026-09-25): composite percentile 80.6% (high, driven by value: ep pct 85%, fcfp pct 84%), but roe pct only 18% (bottom quartile) and sue (earnings-surprise measure) is sharply negative (−0.55, among the worst in the batch). **This is a value/cash-flow signal, not a quality or momentum signal** — durable to the extent the simplified, regulated-only structure genuinely commands a cleaner multiple, but the low ROE percentile and negative SUE independently corroborate the primary-source finding below (a real earnings miss driven by one-off charges and an adverse regulatory order) rather than conflicting with it.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FERC transmission ROE litigation is live and adverse so far.** FERC's 19 March 2026 order cut the allowed base ROE for New England transmission owners from 10.57% to 9.57%, and required refunds retroactive to two historical complaint periods (from Oct 2011–Dec 2012, and from Oct 2014 forward, with interest). Eversource booked a $43.9m after-tax charge in Q1 2026 for the first complaint period. The company has filed a rehearing request, sought a stay, and filed a Section 205 request proposing a prospective ROE of 11.39%; that filing's outcome is unresolved as of this review. **This is an open, adverse regulatory proceeding materially affecting the transmission segment (the single largest earnings segment), not a closed item.**
- **Recurring offshore-wind exit liability.** Since completing its 2024 exit from offshore wind (sale of South Fork Wind and Revolution Wind to Global Infrastructure Partners), Eversource has booked *repeated* incremental after-tax charges tied to that same transaction: ≈$524.0m (FY2024, on completing the sales), $75.0m further (FY2025), and $164.0m more (Q2 2026) — a cumulative ≈$763m in charges across three periods for a deal management describes as a completed clean exit. This recurring pattern is itself a red flag on earnings-quality/disclosure grounds: an "exited" liability that keeps growing.
- **Balance sheet.** Long-term debt (noncurrent) $26.6bn at 2026-06-30 (roughly flat vs $26.9bn at 2025-12-31); stockholders' equity $16.3bn. Total debt per the live snapshot (2026-09-25): $29.8bn; cash $1.8bn; EV $52.2bn vs market cap $24.0bn — debt-heavy, as expected for a rate-regulated network (per the sector playbook, D/E screens are not meaningful here; FFO/Debt-style metrics were not independently rebuilt in this pass — disclosed limitation).
- **Cash flow.** OCF $2.4bn YTD through Q2 2026 (vs $2.1bn YTD Q2 2025); capex running ~$1.0–1.1bn/quarter. FCF (per live snapshot) modestly **positive** (+$0.81bn TTM, +3.4% yield) — genuinely rare for a utility mid rate-base build, and consistent with the Aquarion/offshore-wind capital recycling reducing the funding gap versus peers still building.
- **Shares outstanding**: ≈377m (per the Q2 2026 release).

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse-DCF sense
Price 2026-09-25 close: **$63.67**. NTM P/E 13.1x (fy1 12.95x); P/B 1.47x; ROE (trailing) ≈9.0%; dividend yield ≈4.8% ($3.15/share indicated); consensus NTM EPS growth ≈+5.7%. No V1 systematic-valuation row exists for ES.

Applying the sector playbook's justified-P/B framework (justified P/B ≈ (earned ROE − g)/(Ke − g)): with Ke via CAPM (rf 4.16%, ERP 5.0%, beta 0.698 → Ke≈7.65%) and earned ROE ≈9.0%, solving 1.47 = (0.09 − g)/(0.0765 − g) gives **g ≈ 3.7%** implied — close to, and slightly below, the company's own guided 5–7% long-term EPS CAGR (using the low end as the more conservative comparison point given the FERC and Aquarion uncertainty). That reads as **fair to modestly cheap**: the market is not paying up for the top of management's own growth guidance, and a regulated utility trading below the low end of its guided compounding rate, with a rate base still growing from MA/NH/CT base-rate increases, has some margin of safety — but not so much that a further adverse FERC outcome (a genuine, open risk) would be a surprise to the market.

**Bear case:** the FERC Section 205 request for an 11.39% ROE is denied or delayed for years, the current 9.57% rate (with additional retroactive refund periods) becomes durable, and a fourth consecutive year of incremental offshore-wind-related charges erodes confidence in management's "one-off" framing → 3yr annualised return ≈ **+1%** (near dividend yield only, limited price appreciation).
**Base case:** FERC litigation resolves close to the current 9.57%–11.39% range (say ~10.3% blended outcome), no further offshore-wind surprises, 5–7% EPS CAGR delivered as guided off the $4.65 2026 midpoint → 3yr annualised return ≈ **+9%** (dividend yield + guided EPS growth + modest multiple recovery toward 14–15x as regulatory clarity improves).
**Bull case:** the 11.39% ROE request is granted, New England transmission investment (grid hardening, offshore-wind-adjacent interconnection, electrification) accelerates rate-base growth toward the top of the 5–7% range, and Aquarion/offshore-wind charges are now genuinely finished → 3yr annualised return ≈ **+15%**.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Genuinely simplified structure — offshore wind and now the water utility both fully exited — leaves a lower-risk, pure-play regulated T&D story that is easier to value and to underwrite than two years ago.
2. Cheapest valuation in the batch (13x NTM P/E, sub-1.5x book) with a rare-for-the-sector *positive* free cash flow yield, giving some cushion if the regulatory outcomes disappoint.
3. Rate base still growing from approved MA/NH base-distribution-rate increases and continued gas-infrastructure investment, funding a still-guided 5–7% long-term EPS CAGR.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. FERC cut the allowed transmission ROE by 100bp (10.57%→9.57%) with retroactive refund exposure, and the company's own request to reverse this (to 11.39%) is unresolved — this is the single largest segment and the outcome is genuinely two-sided.
2. The 2024 "completed" offshore-wind divestiture has generated incremental charges in three straight periods (FY2024, FY2025, Q2 2026) totalling roughly $763m after tax — a real pattern of understated exit costs, not a one-time event.
3. FY2026 guidance was cut this year (from $4.80–$4.95 to $4.57–$4.72), the opposite of a "raised guidance" story; ROE (trailing ~9%) sits in the bottom quartile of the peer batch per the b1 data.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable) -- what would invalidate this thesis
1. FERC's Section 205 replacement-ROE request (targeting 11.39%) is denied, or a further retroactive refund period is added, resulting in another quarter with a transmission-related charge exceeding $0.10/share.
2. FY2026 non-GAAP recurring EPS finishes below the low end of the reaffirmed $4.57–$4.72 range.
3. A fourth consecutive period (beyond FY2024, FY2025, Q2 2026) books an incremental offshore-wind-related charge under the GIP agreement.
4. Long-term debt (noncurrent) grows more than 10% year-over-year without a matching, regulator-approved rate-base increase disclosed in the same filing.
5. Dividend growth rate falls below the historical ~3–4%/year cadence (a signal management itself sees compounding pressure).

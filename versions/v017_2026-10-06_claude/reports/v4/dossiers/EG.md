## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 18–30 months. Reason: the market is pricing continued, permanent earnings decline (implied 10-year growth is deeply negative — see §7) **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the -11.9% figure came from treating EPS as distributable cash with a 4.2% risk-free rate; on the corrected P/B-vs-ROE method (see Re-assessment RA12) the price implies a sustainable ROE of about 9.4%, close to the 10-11% base case, so the market is not pricing a permanent decline]** despite two consecutive clean quarters of results since the company capped its worst known casualty-reserve exposure with a $1.2bn reinsurance cover; the reservation is that reserve adequacy is unproven beyond two quarters and premium is still shrinking sharply, so this is a statistically cheap, evidence-supported turnaround, not a proven one.

**Thesis (≤25 words):** Global reinsurer that twice surprised the market with US casualty reserve charges, now capped its worst legacy exposure with a $1.2bn cover, priced at 6.4x earnings.

## 2. Business in plain English
Everest is a Bermuda-based global reinsurer and specialty insurer: it takes on underwriting risk from other insurers (reinsurance) and directly insures specialty commercial risks, collecting premiums and paying claims, and investing the "float" between the two. It makes money when premiums plus investment income exceed claims and expenses (a "combined ratio" below 100%). Its competitive position is mid-tier-large among global reinsurers (peers: RenaissanceRe, Arch, Aracle), respected in property-catastrophe reinsurance, but its US casualty/liability insurance book has been the source of repeated, market-moving reserve problems.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) the $1.2bn ADC caps the single largest known tail risk (2024-and-prior US casualty), and two subsequent quarters (Q1/Q2-26) show clean, even favorable, development; (2) trading at 0.93x book value with a 6.4x P/E, any evidence of stabilization is a large re-rating opportunity — even partial re-rating to 9–10x (still below high-quality reinsurance peers) implies large upside; (3) management is deliberately shrinking the worst-margin business (Global Wholesale & Specialty combined ratio ~95–97%) which should raise the blended margin over 2026–27 even as premium shrinks.
**Bear:** (1) Everest has now had **two distinct adverse-reserve or loss events in five quarters** (Q4-24, Q3-25) — a base rate of one surprise roughly every 12–15 months is not disproven by two clean quarters; (2) net premiums written down 26.3% YoY (Q2-26) is a large, ongoing top-line contraction that could pressure future scale/fixed-cost absorption even if margins improve; (3) the ADC does not cover 2025-and-later accident years, so if current-accident-year casualty pricing is still inadequate industry-wide (a live industry debate), Everest could face a *new* reserve problem the ADC does nothing to protect against.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. Any new adverse prior-year development charge on North America casualty exceeding $150m in a single quarter (a signal the ADC's 2024-and-prior fix was insufficient or a new-year problem has emerged).
2. Group combined ratio above 100% for two consecutive quarters (last two: 91.2%, 92.0%).
3. Net premiums written declines exceeding −15% YoY for three consecutive quarters (currently 2 of the last 2 quarters at −14.7% and −26.3%; a third would signal the shrinkage is not stabilizing on schedule) **[Corrected 2026-10-06: only Q2-26 (-26.3%) is worse than -15%; Q1-26 (-14.7%) is not, so the count is 1 of 2, not 2 of 2]**.
4. Book value per share (ex-AOCI) declines quarter-over-quarter for two consecutive quarters.
5. Rating-agency downgrade (A.M. Best/S&P) of Everest Re's financial-strength rating.

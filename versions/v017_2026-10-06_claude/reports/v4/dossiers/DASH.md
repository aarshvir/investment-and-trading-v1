## 2. Business in plain English
DoorDash runs a three-sided marketplace app connecting consumers, local restaurants/retailers and independent delivery workers ("Dashers"). It earns commissions/fees from merchants and consumers on each order, plus a growing advertising business selling merchants placement in the app, and has expanded into grocery/retail/new-verticals delivery and — via the 2025 Deliveroo acquisition — international markets. Its competitive position is the largest US food-delivery marketplace by order volume, ahead of Uber Eats and Grubhub.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1 factor scores show a strong earnings-momentum (SUE) signal (96th pct) and improving quality trend (leverage 97th pct favourable — low net debt), but weaker raw value scores (bp/ep low percentile, as expected for a still-scaling, historically loss-making business). This is a **genuine inflection, not an artefact**: DASH only turned sustainably GAAP-profitable in 2025 (net income $123M in FY2024 → $935M in FY2025 — 10-K, filed 2026-02-18) and operating income went from –$38M (FY2024) to $723M (FY2025), a real structural improvement in unit economics (Dasher pay/incentive efficiency, ads ramp, take-rate) rather than one-off items.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Entity scope: all figures below are consolidated (DoorDash, Inc., 10-Q/10-K).** DASH has no separately regulated financial subsidiary.
- **FCF conversion:** TTM OCF $2,830M vs TTM NI $841M → OCF/NI ≈ 3.4x, reflecting float from marketplace payment timing (DASH collects from consumers faster than it pays merchants/Dashers) — a durable structural feature of the model, not a one-off working-capital swing, though it does mean **GAAP earnings understate the cash-generative power of the business** and also that a sudden change in payment terms/timing (e.g., regulatory mandate on faster Dasher payout) could compress this gap.
- **SBC:** material — DASH is a heavy stock-comp payer (typical of recently-IPO'd tech); GAAP operating margin (3.5–7.5% in recent quarters) is well below any adjusted-EBITDA-margin framing management uses (Adjusted EBITDA $914M in Q2-26 vs GAAP operating income $156M — a ~$758M GAAP-vs-adjusted gap in one quarter, driven mainly by SBC and D&A/amortization add-backs). This is a **real, large, and persistent** GAAP/adjusted gap that investors relying on the "Adjusted EBITDA" headline should discount.
- **Balance sheet:** cash $4,424M + short-term investments $923M + long-term investments $869M = **$6,216M in cash/investments** vs convertible notes (2030 Notes) $2,727M (10-Q, 2026-06-30) → **net cash ≈ $3.49B** including all investments (≈$1.70B using cash-only vs debt). No leverage concern either way.
- **Buybacks:** DASH only began meaningful repurchases in 2026 — $162M (Q1-26) then $1,049M (Q2-26), a sharp step-up (FY2025 buybacks were $0). **[Corrected 2026-10-06: $1,049M is the six-month total; Q2-2026 alone was $887M ($1,049M less $162M). Net diluted shares were flat at 438-443M over the last five quarters]** Shares outstanding still rising slowly on net (SBC dilution has historically outpaced the pre-2026 buyback program), so this is a recent and not-yet-proven capital-return commitment.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3):** (1) Adjusted EBITDA has beaten the top of guidance for at least one of the last three quarters and management raises the following quarter's range each time — genuine, not promotional, operating momentum. (2) Advertising and new-verticals (grocery/retail) revenue are high-incremental-margin and still early, giving a credible path to margin expansion beyond delivery commissions alone. (3) International expansion via Deliveroo materially increases the addressable market without DASH having to build local density from scratch.

**Bear (3):** (1) Active Dasher-misclassification litigation and regulatory inquiries in multiple states (California EDD audit/assessment, per the Q2-2026 10-Q's own legal-proceedings note) plus a reported (secondary-source) ~$131.5M multi-state worker-payment settlement create both a cash-cost and business-model risk (reclassification to employees would materially raise costs). (2) GAAP profitability is thin and volatile relative to the "Adjusted EBITDA" headline (§6) — a large, persistent GAAP/adjusted gap driven by SBC that could compress reported per-share value even if Adjusted EBITDA keeps growing. (3) Intensifying competition (Uber Eats bundling with rides/Instacart-style grocery entrants) in a business with limited switching costs for either merchants or consumers.

# CEG — Constellation Energy Corporation

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** — thesis horizon 24–36 months. The nuclear-plus-gas platform and the Microsoft/Meta/CyrusOne contracted-power story are real and evidenced in primary filings, but the price already embeds a meaningful chunk of the Calpine-integration and Crane (Three Mile Island) restart upside, leverage rose sharply with the Calpine deal, and near-term GAAP earnings are noisy (integration costs, PTC swings, a large regulatory-driven divestiture still to close). This is a "wait for the DOJ divestitures to close and the first clean post-merger quarter" name, not a "buy the current print" name.

## 2. Business in plain English
Constellation is the largest US generator of carbon-free electricity — mostly nuclear (~30+ reactors after the 2022 Exelon spin-off) — plus, since 7 Jan 2026, Calpine's natural-gas, geothermal and battery-storage fleet and Calpine's competitive retail electricity supply business (~62 TWh/yr of retail load). It sells power both into wholesale markets and under long-term contracts (including 20-year deals with Microsoft — the Three Mile Island Unit 1 restart, now renamed the Crane Clean Energy Center — and Meta at the Clinton plant) and supplies retail electricity to businesses and utilities. It makes money from the spread between its (largely fixed) generation cost and the market/contract price it is paid, plus retail margin. Competitive position: unmatched scale in dispatchable, carbon-free baseload power at a moment when AI/data-center demand is bidding up long-term power prices — a genuine, evidenced moat, not marketing.

b1 factor scores (2026-09-25): composite percentile 1.1% (very low — bottom of the batch), roe pct 63%, fcfp pct 78%, but ep pct 1.6% (very expensive on trailing earnings yield) and mom_12_1 pct 8% (share price down 16% over the trailing year net of the most recent month). ## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **Merger accounting.** On 7 Jan 2026 CEG acquired all outstanding Calpine equity for total consideration of **$21,835m** ($17,603m in ~50m newly issued CEG shares valued at the $354.58 close on 6 Jan 2026, plus $4,342m cash), and assumed ~$12.6bn of Calpine debt (10-Q Note 2, filed 2026-08-06). Goodwill recognised: $11,527m (up from $420m pre-deal) — a material intangible-asset bet on the deal's synergies.
- **DOJ divestitures (regulatory condition of closing).** The DOJ required divestiture of 5 PJM gas plants (~4.4 GW), 1 ERCOT plant and a minority Gregory Power Plant stake, with definitive agreements due within 240 days of close (4 Sep 2026). Status as of the Q2 2026 10-Q: Gregory stake sold Jan 2026; PJM assets under agreement with LS Power for $5.0bn (March 2026); the ERCOT plant (Brazos Valley Energy Center, f/k/a Jack A. Fusco) under agreement with LS Power for $860m (**signed August 2026**, the last piece). All three agreements are now signed (meeting the regulatory deadline), but **none of the sales has closed yet** — closing is targeted "by the end of this year" and remains subject to DOJ/FERC review. This is a real, dated, unresolved item, not a closed risk.
- **Leverage rose sharply.** Long-term debt (noncurrent) went from $7,250m (2025-12-31) to $19,111m (2026-06-30); short-term borrowings from $1,650m to $5,226m. Total debt per the live snapshot (2026-09-25): **$24.70bn**; cash only $697m; net debt ≈ $24.0bn against a $93.3bn market cap and $117.0bn enterprise value. Stockholders' equity roughly doubled too (stock-funded deal), so leverage on an equity basis looks less dramatic than the debt figures alone suggest, but **the triage note's "lowest leverage (3.0x) in the batch" claim does not reconcile with these post-merger balance-sheet figures and should be treated as stale/pre-merger** (data_conflict, flagged below).
- **Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was negative in three of the four quarters straddling the 2024–25 nuclear PTC ramp and working-capital swings (e.g., 2024 full year OCF –$2,464m), turned positive from 2025 ($4,237m FY2025 OCF), and continues positive post-merger ($1,128m in Q2 2026 alone, on a YTD basis). Capex is running $1.2–1.3bn/quarter post-merger (fleet maintenance, Crane restart, new PPA-backed builds) — FCF is negative on the live snapshot (–$6.6bn TTM) largely because of the acquisition-year capex/financing mix, consistent with a name mid-integration rather than a red flag on its own. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: TTM operating cash flow of about $4.2bn less TTM capex of about $3.9bn is roughly +$0.3bn (10-K and 10-Q XBRL); the -$6.6bn vendor figure is not explained by capex and appears to include the ~$4.3bn cash paid for Calpine and other investing items]**
- **Share count**: 355m shares outstanding at 30 Jun 2026 (362m at 31 Mar 2026 — buybacks reducing the post-merger share count).
- **Dividend**: raised 10% for 2026 (to $0.4265/quarter, i.e. $1.706 annualised); management "expects to grow the dividend per share by another 10% in 2026" onward per the Q4 2025 release — modest current yield (~0.65%) reflecting a growth, not income, thesis.

**Bear case (own-history/evidence-based):** integration execution risk (a genuinely large, complex M&A with a hard regulatory divestiture deadline already passed for the definitive-agreement stage but not for closing); nuclear PTC volatility already visible in two of the last five quarters as an earnings headwind; a re-rating back toward historical merchant-generator multiples (6–9x EV/EBITDA) if data-center demand growth disappoints or gas/power spreads compress → 3yr annualised return ≈ **−4%**.
**Base case:** Calpine integrates on plan, the three DOJ divestitures close by year-end 2026 for combined proceeds of ≈$5.9bn (used to delever), Crane restarts on schedule in 2027, guidance is met at the raised $11.50–$12.50 range and grows roughly with the ~9–10% consensus for 2–3 years before decelerating toward mid-single digits as contracted volumes mature → 3yr annualised return ≈ **+7%**.
**Bull case:** additional hyperscaler PPAs (following Microsoft/Meta/CyrusOne) re-rate the whole nuclear fleet as bond-like contracted cash flow, Crane and license-renewal capacity (two NY units filed for renewal in 2026) all clear regulatory hurdles ahead of schedule → 3yr annualised return ≈ **+18%**.

`valuation_view_vs_v1`: no V1 row exists; **dossier_view = full** (price already assumes execution goes right; growth priced in via the Gordon-growth read is *below* consensus near-term growth but the EV/EBITDA read is rich versus sector bands) — net view: **fair-to-full, not cheap**, which under the program's quality-growth-lane rule (growth priced in must be ≤ the analyst's base case) argues for **WATCH rather than INCLUDE** until the divestitures close and one clean post-merger quarter is reported.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Contracted, 20-year, investment-grade-counterparty PPAs (Microsoft/Crane, Meta/Clinton, CyrusOne) lock in premium pricing for decades, de-risking a large and growing share of nuclear output just as AI/data-center demand for firm carbon-free power accelerates.
2. Calpine adds a large, flexible, dispatchable gas/geothermal/storage fleet and the nation's largest competitive retail electric supply platform (~62 TWh/yr), diversifying away from pure nuclear-merchant exposure and creating commercial cross-sell.
3. A demonstrated four-year record of beating the midpoint of its own guidance, now backed by a raised FY2026 range after only two quarters on the combined basis.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Leverage roughly tripled in gross terms with the Calpine deal; the DOJ-mandated divestitures (worth an estimated ~$5.9bn combined) have not yet closed, so both the deleveraging plan and the "clean" post-divestiture earnings base are still forecasts, not facts.
2. GAAP earnings are genuinely volatile quarter to quarter (Q1 2026 GAAP EPS $4.49 vs Q2 2026 GAAP EPS $1.42) on integration and PTC items; investors are relying heavily on the company's own adjusted "Operating Earnings" framing.
3. At ~20x NTM P/E and ~14.7x trailing EV/EBITDA, the stock is not obviously cheap for a business still mid-integration with unresolved regulatory divestiture risk.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable) -- what would invalidate this thesis
1. Any one of the three DOJ-mandated divestitures (LS Power PJM $5.0bn deal, LS Power ERCOT/Brazos Valley $860m deal, Gregory stake) fails to close by the extended internal target ("end of this year," i.e. by 2026-12-31) or is renegotiated down in price.
2. FY2026 Adjusted Operating EPS comes in below the low end of the raised $11.50–$12.50 guidance range at year-end.
3. Net debt/EBITDA (on a clean, full-year-Calpine basis, once reportable) exceeds 4.0x with no credible deleveraging path.
4. Crane Clean Energy Center restart slips past 2027 or the NRC/FERC approvals already obtained are challenged/reversed.
5. Two consecutive quarters of GAAP-to-adjusted reconciling items exceeding 30% of adjusted operating earnings, signalling the "adjusted" framing is masking a structurally worse GAAP business.

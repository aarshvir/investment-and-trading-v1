# TSLA - Tesla, Inc. (research only; not personal investment advice)

**1. Verdict: WATCH.** A real, profitable, net-cash vehicle, energy and services company whose 25 Sep 2026 close ($372.11, market cap about $1.47 trillion on the 3,949.5m cover-page shares) already prices roughly 39% a year of free-cash-flow growth for ten years; my evidence-based base case is about 24% a year (2036 free cash flow $50bn), so the price sits at my bull case, not my base case. Triage "do not advance" is confirmed on price, not on business quality. Horizon: re-underwrite every 12 months; no position until price or evidence closes the gap (section 7 and 9 give the conditions).

**2. Business in plain English.** Tesla sells electric cars (Model 3/Y, Cybertruck, a new purpose-built Cybercab), grid batteries (Megapack, Powerwall), and a growing services line (charging, service, insurance, used cars, FSD subscriptions). It earns money from vehicle margin (16.9% automotive gross margin in Q2 2026, GAAP), battery storage and software subscriptions, and is now spending heavily (capex above $25bn in 2026 per the filing) on AI compute, cell and chip factories, robotaxis and the Optimus humanoid robot. Its edge is manufacturing cost, charging network (8,704 Supercharger stations) and fleet data; its weakness is that valuation depends on autonomy products that are not yet earning material profit.

**8. Bull and bear.** Bull: (1) record Q2 deliveries (480,126), FSD active subscriptions 1.48m (+56%) with over 55% attach on new North American deliveries, and Services and Other gross profit $648m (14% margin); (2) unsupervised Robotaxi "Ramping Unsupervised" in Austin, Dallas, Houston, Miami, Orlando and Tampa, with Cybercab in production; (3) net cash $34bn funds the build-out without equity. Bear: (1) operating margin 1.4% and free cash flow negative while capex doubles; the equity value rests on products with no disclosed profit; (2) energy gross margin fell about 990 bps on a vendor-cell warranty issue, regulatory credits are fading ($146m vs $439m), and average selling prices are lower; (3) governance and dilution: the 2025 CEO award, dependence on one executive, and the $2.0bn SpaceX stake (equity-method, fair-valued) showing related-party entanglement.

## 2. Business in plain English
Bristol-Myers Squibb is a global biopharmaceutical company that discovers, develops and sells prescription medicines, mainly in oncology, hematology, cardiovascular disease, immunology and neuroscience, to patients via physicians, hospitals, pharmacies and payers (Medicare/Medicaid, commercial insurers, and international health systems). It earns money by selling patent-protected branded drugs at a large gross margin (~71% GAAP) while losing that margin rapidly once patents/exclusivity expire and generics enter. Its historically dominant products — the anticoagulant **Eliquis** and the multiple-myeloma drug **Revlimid** — are both now past peak and in structural decline (Revlimid) or newly subject to U.S. government price-setting (Eliquis), while a second wave of newer drugs (**Opdivo/Opdivo Qvantig, Breyanzi, Reblozyl, Camzyos, Opdualag, Cobenfy, Krazati**) is scaling to replace that lost revenue. Competitive position: top-5 global oncology/immunology franchise with real depth (not a single-product story), but facing the same IRA Medicare price-negotiation and patent-cliff dynamics as every large-cap innovator.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **OCF/FCF — the clearest adverse fact in this file:** FY2025 OCF $14.156bn vs FY2024 $15.190bn (−6.8%, 10-K MD&A: "higher GTN [gross-to-net] payments"). It has **worsened materially since**: H1 2026 OCF **$4.497bn vs H1 2025 $5.871bn (−23.4%, −$1.4bn)**, and the Q2 2026 10-Q states plainly: *"The $1.4 billion decrease in cash provided by operating activities compared to 2025 was driven by lower net customer receipts, **primarily due to a list price reduction for Eliquis**, partially offset by lower expenses due to the ongoing strategic productivity initiative."* TTM FCF is **$11.44bn vs a prior-TTM $14.58bn (−21.5%)**. This is BMS's own quantification that the Eliquis IRA Medicare price cut (see below) is landing at the cash-flow line even while headline Eliquis *revenue* is still growing — a real tension the market does not appear to be pricing separately from the revenue growth.
- **Eliquis IRA Maximum Fair Price (verified against CMS's primary published price list):** effective 2026-01-01, CMS's negotiated Medicare Part D price is **$231/30-day supply vs a 2023 list price of $521 (a 56% discount)** (CMS, "Negotiated Prices for Initial Price Applicability Year 2026," Aug 2024). Despite this, **reported Eliquis WW revenue grew +16% YoY (Q1'26) and +22% YoY (Q2'26)**, and FY2026 Eliquis growth guidance was *raised* from 10–15% to 20–25%. BMS's press releases attribute this only to generic "demand" and do not publish a volume/price bridge. Both things are simultaneously true and both are primary-sourced: revenue is growing, and BMS's own 10-Q says the Eliquis price cut is the primary driver of a $1.4bn cash-flow decline. **This is a watch item, not a resolved question** — gross-to-net/rebate accounting can lag, and a reader relying only on the revenue line would miss the cash-flow deterioration entirely.
- **GAAP vs. non-GAAP gap and its drivers:** the gap is large and volatile — driven mainly by (i) Amortization of acquired intangibles ($3.3bn FY25, $8.9bn FY24 — declining as Revlimid-related intangibles fully amortize) and (ii) **Acquired IPR&D charges, which BMS's non-GAAP explicitly does NOT exclude** ("GAAP and non-GAAP EPS include the impacts of Acquired IPRD charges and licensing income," repeated in every release) — a genuine mitigant vs. peers who strip M&A charges from non-GAAP entirely. Other specified items (restructuring, litigation/settlement charges, contingent-consideration fair-value marks, priority-review-voucher purchases) are smaller and disclosed line-by-line in each release's Specified Items schedule.
- **SBC:** $553M FY2025 / ~$562M annualized (H1'26 ×2) ≈ **1.1–1.2% of revenue** — low, not a dilution concern.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Growth Portfolio is real and broad-based, not one drug: Breyanzi +82% FY25, Camzyos +77%, Reblozyl +31%, Opdualag +28%, Opdivo Qvantig (subcutaneous, LOE-defense reformulation) scaled from ~$0 to $238M FY2025 in one year — this is genuine multi-product execution, not a single bet. (2) Balance sheet is genuinely deleveraging (LT debt −$7.5bn since FY2023) while still funding a growing, well-covered dividend (4% yield, 17 consecutive annual increases). (3) Despite a 56% Medicare price cut taking effect Jan 2026, Eliquis revenue is still growing double digits and FY2026 growth guidance was raised, not cut — the single biggest headline policy risk has not (yet) broken the franchise.
**Bear:** (1) FY2025 non-GAAP EPS missed BMS's own most-recently-reissued guidance range once a Q4 M&A charge landed — the guidance "raises" investors cite structurally exclude the next dilutive deal, which recurred once already. (2) OCF is down 23% H1-over-H1 and BMS's own 10-Q says this is "primarily" the Eliquis price cut — a real, quantified, ongoing cash-flow headwind that the revenue-growth headline obscures. (3) The single largest near-term de-risking catalyst for the growth portfolio's terminal narrative — Cobenfy's Phase 3 ADEPT-2 readout in Alzheimer's-disease psychosis — has been delayed from a planned H2 2026 readout to "beginning early 2027" after the company disclosed clinical-trial-execution irregularities at a small number of sites and re-opened enrollment (BMS press release, 2025-12-03; corroborated by trade press).

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (thesis-invalidation triggers)
The INCLUDE-SMALL thesis above is invalidated if any of the following observable, time-bound conditions are met:
1. **Legacy-drag overtakes Growth-Portfolio offset again:** consolidated quarterly revenue growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters (it was +5.7% in Q2'26; watch this reverse).
2. **OCF decline persists into H2 2026:** if Q3/Q4 2026 OCF is also down YoY, the Eliquis cash-flow impact is structural, not a one-time H1 dip.
3. **FY2026 non-GAAP EPS misses the raised $6.75–$7.00 range** when reported (~Feb 2027) — would confirm a second consecutive year of the "guidance excludes the next deal charge" pattern.
4. **Cobenfy ADEPT (Alzheimer's-psychosis) data, now expected "beginning early 2027," reads out negative or slips again** — removes the clearest near-term catalyst for the growth-portfolio bull case.
5. **Net debt/EBITDA (GAAP) rises back above ~3x** (from ~1.9x now), e.g., from a large new debt-funded deal — would reverse the one clearly positive, unambiguous trend in this file.
6. **Dividend held flat or cut**, breaking the 17-year increase streak — payout ratio (58–79%) leaves some room but is not undemanding against a shrinking Legacy base.

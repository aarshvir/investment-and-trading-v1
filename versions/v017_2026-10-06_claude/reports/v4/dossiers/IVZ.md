# IVZ — Invesco Ltd. (global asset manager, ETFs incl. QQQ) — diligence dossier F121

**1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL.** The quick triage rejected Invesco for "negative ROE" and "secular decline", but both come from one non-cash $1.8bn impairment in Q4-25. On the filings the business is at a record: net long-term inflows of $45.1bn in Q2-26 (8.5% annualised organic growth), adjusted operating margin 37.5%, adjusted EPS $0.71 (+97% year on year), and Invesco consolidated parent net debt (debt less cash) cut to $709m. The stock ($30.92) sits at about 11x Q2 run-rate adjusted EPS and the price implies only about 2.5% a year of long-run earnings growth against my base of about 7%. Named reservation: earnings are levered to equity-market levels at a record $2.47tn AUM (raw beta about 1.4), and the flows are heavily low-fee passive and China-joint-venture money while active equity still bleeds. Horizon 12–36 months; half weight.

**2. Business in plain English.** Invesco manages about $2.5 trillion for retail and institutional clients and charges a fee on assets: ETFs and index products (including the QQQ Nasdaq-100 fund, converted to an open-end ETF on 20 Dec 2025, which now books management fees), active equity and bond funds, private markets, and a China joint venture. Profit is fee-paying AUM x net revenue yield (about 22 bps on average AUM in Q2-26, my calculation) x operating margin, minus flow risk. Competitive position: a large ETF franchise (QQQ plus the Invesco ETF line-up) alongside an active business facing fee pressure.

**3. Why the model likes it / durability.** The quant screens (b1 composite rank 78, V1) show a cheap stock: trailing GAAP earnings yield is -2.5% (distorted by the impairment), but V1's SBC-adjusted FCF yield is 12.9% and the d4 NTM P/E is 9.8x, with strong recent price momentum. The value reading is partly real (cheap on adjusted earnings), but the quality reading is polluted by the impairment and preferred-share repurchase cost in trailing GAAP numbers. Durable part: ETF/QQQ scale and operating leverage; cyclical part: market-driven AUM (Q2-26 net market gains +$256.8bn versus net long-term flows +$45.1bn).

**8. Bull case (3) and bear case with risks (3).** Bull: (1) record inflows and QQQ fee conversion lifted adjusted margin to 37.5% with headcount down 12%; (2) ending AUM $2.47tn is 4% above Q2 average AUM of $2.37tn, so Q3 revenue has a built-in lift; (3) deleveraging and buybacks rising 80% year to date while leverage falls. Bear: (1) a 25% equity drawdown would plausibly cut earnings by about a third (my estimate, given largely fixed costs) and the stock is near its 52-week high ($33.31 on 27 Aug); (2) active equity keeps leaking (-$7.7bn in Q2) and the $1.795bn impairment of US retail mutual-fund contracts says the old franchise is shrinking; net revenue yield is slipping; (3) key-person and mix risk: China JV ($6.9bn of Q2 inflows) is run by Andrew Lo, who retires effective 31 Mar 2027 (8-K 0000914208-26-000347), and MassMutual holds $2.5bn of preferred with board and consent rights.

## 1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL (half weight) — thesis horizon 12–36 months
Uber's underlying operating business is genuinely strong and improving on primary-source numbers: Gross Bookings +22–24% YoY, Adjusted EBITDA margin expanding from 4.1% to 4.9% of Gross Bookings over the trailing eight quarters, guidance beaten in every quarter sampled, trailing-twelve-month free cash flow exceeding $10bn for the first time in company history, and a shrinking share count. The named reservation, and the reason this is sized small rather than full: **GAAP net income is dominated by large, volatile, non-operating items** (a $4.9bn one-off tax-valuation-allowance release in Q3 2025; $1.5–1.6bn swings each quarter since from mark-to-market revaluations of minority equity stakes in Aurora, Didi and Delivery Hero) that must not be read as operating profit, and **legal/regulatory/safety exposure is real and currently expanding, not just legacy**: a ~$966m Dutch data-protection fine (under appeal, announced after the 10-Q), a June 2026 shareholder derivative suit alleging the board is a "serial compliance offender," and a federal sexual-assault multidistrict litigation whose pending-case count grew from "over 3,000" (as of 1 June 2026) to 4,588 (as reported in September 2026) in three months, against a next trial date of 7 October 2026.

## 2. Business in plain English
Uber runs a two-sided marketplace connecting riders/consumers with independent drivers (Mobility) and connecting consumers with restaurants/retailers via couriers (Delivery), plus a freight-brokerage business (Uber Freight) and a growing advertising business layered on top. It earns a "take rate" — the difference between what riders/consumers pay and what it pays out to drivers/couriers/merchants — on Gross Bookings, at large and improving scale (208 million Monthly Active Platform Consumers in Q2 2026, up 16% YoY). Its competitive position is as the largest global rideshare/delivery platform by Gross Bookings, now also positioning itself as an aggregation layer for third-party autonomous-vehicle (AV) fleets (Waymo, Nvidia-backed partners, WeRide) rather than only building its own AV technology.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **The GAAP-vs-operating gap described in §4 is this dossier's central earnings-quality finding.** Non-GAAP (Adjusted EBITDA, Adjusted Free Cash Flow) is the right lens for trend analysis here; GAAP net income should not be used for YoY comparisons without stripping the equity-revaluation and tax items, which the company itself flags in every release.
- **Free cash flow is genuinely strong and growing:** six-months-2026 OCF $5.21bn (vs $4.89bn H1 2025, +7%) and FCF $5.08bn (vs $4.73bn, +7%) per the 10-Q; the Q2 2026 release states trailing-twelve-month FCF **exceeded $10bn for the first time in company history**.
- **Balance sheet:** unrestricted cash, cash equivalents and short-term investments were $5.4bn at Q2 2026 quarter-end per the release; the 10-Q separately shows total cash+restricted cash of $7.2bn and marketable debt securities/restricted investments of $10.0bn (Q2 2026) vs $9.6bn/$9.4bn at FY2025 — a strong liquidity position. Long-term debt (XBRL) rose from $10.6bn (FY2025) to $12.7bn (Q2 2026), reflecting a $2.0bn drawdown under a variable-rate Term Loan Credit Agreement (10-Q, Item 3) with $1.0bn of remaining committed capacity — modest incremental leverage, not a balance-sheet concern given the cash position (liquid assets roughly matching or exceeding total debt).
- **SBC is a real, larger dilution item than the other two names in this batch:** approximately $500m/quarter (Q1 2026 $473m; six-months-2026 $1,023m) against $13.2–14.2bn quarterly revenue ≈ **~3.5% of revenue** — materially higher than STLD (~0.2%) or TMUS (~1%). This is being offset, not fully absorbed, by buybacks: weighted-average diluted shares fell from 2,120m (FY2025) to 2,050m (Q2 2026 quarter), a ~3.4% reduction, with the Q2 2026 release specifically citing "continuing to reduce our share count."
- **Accrued legal/regulatory liability:** $1.8bn as of Q2 2026 (down from $2.1bn at FY2025), per Note 11 of the 10-Q, covering "alleged independent contractor misclassification claims… FCRA claims… data and privacy claims, securities claims, antitrust claims" and other matters — this is the company's own recorded (probable-and-estimable) reserve; the items in §11 below that are not yet reflected in a filing (the Dutch fine, the June 2026 shareholder suit, the growing MDL) sit outside this $1.8bn figure and represent unquantified incremental risk.
- **Business-model changes affecting comparability:** effective January 2026, a UK regulatory/tax-driven business-model change removed Uber's responsibility for Mobility services in certain UK markets, reducing reported revenue (not Gross Bookings) by roughly $1.1bn in Q2 2026 alone and by 8–9 percentage points of YoY revenue growth in both Q1 and Q2 2026 — a real, disclosed, non-operating driver of the gap between Gross Bookings growth (22–25% YoY) and revenue growth (12–14% YoY) in recent quarters.

## 8. Bull case and bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Guidance beaten in all four quarters sampled, with Adjusted EBITDA margin expanding every year-over-year comparison shown (4.1%→4.9% of Gross Bookings) — durable, filed operating improvement, not a one-off.
2. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow exceeded $10bn for the first time in company history (Q2 2026 release), funding continued buybacks (share count down ~3.4% in two quarters) without balance-sheet strain (liquid assets roughly cover total debt).
3. The AV strategy is explicitly a "capital-efficient" aggregation/partnership model (Waymo, Nvidia-linked partners, WeRide) rather than a capital-intensive proprietary build — a genuine hedge against the single biggest bear-case risk (robotaxi disintermediation) rather than a denial of it.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Legal/regulatory/safety exposure is expanding, not just a legacy overhang: a ~$966m Dutch privacy fine (appealing, announced 23 Aug 2026, after the last 10-Q), a June 2026 shareholder derivative suit calling Uber a "serial compliance offender," and a sexual-assault MDL whose case count grew from 3,000+ to 4,588 in three months (next trial 7 Oct 2026) — none of this is yet reflected in the $1.8bn accrued liability on the balance sheet.
2. GAAP net income is not a usable trend metric on its own: every one of the last four quarters shown carried a $1.5–4.9bn non-operating swing (tax valuation release, or equity-stake mark-to-market), meaning headline "GAAP EPS growth" figures reported in the press (e.g., +77% YoY in Q2 2026) substantially overstate underlying operating improvement.
3. SBC (~3.5% of revenue) is a real, larger dilution drag than peers in this batch, and driver-classification litigation (California AG suit, Swiss Federal Tribunal rulings classifying drivers/couriers as employees in Geneva) remains open and could, in an adverse outcome in a major market, force a structural change to the cost model.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Adjusted EBITDA margin (% of Gross Bookings) declines for two consecutive quarters (currently expanding every quarter shown, 4.1%→4.9%).
2. Any single new legal/regulatory settlement or fine (net of insurance/existing accrual) exceeds $1 billion — e.g., the Dutch fine being upheld on appeal, or a comparable new finding.
3. Quarterly Gross Bookings guidance is missed (below the low end of the company's own guided range) — has not occurred in the four quarters reviewed.
4. A major AV/robotaxi partner (Waymo, an Nvidia-linked platform partner, or WeRide) terminates or materially narrows its Uber platform agreement.
5. A court or regulator in a top-5-revenue market (US federal, California, or a major EU member state) legally mandates employee (not independent-contractor) classification for drivers, forcing a structural change to the cost model.

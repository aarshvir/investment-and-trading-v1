# VRSN — Verisign, Inc. — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — 24–36 month horizon. A durable, contractually-protected monopoly on .com/.net domain registry with real, exercised pricing power and an aggressive buyback program, but the current 27.3x NTM P/E is pricing in roughly 14.5%/yr growth for a decade against a delivered 5-year CAGR of just 5.8% — even generous credit for the newly-resumed price increases doesn't fully close that gap. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the 14.5% is the year-1 growth rate of a ten-year linear fade to 3% (average about 8.7%), not a constant rate, and it is not comparable with the 5.8% delivered CAGR; on free cash flow after SBC and a 9.24% WACC the constant-growth equivalent is 9.5% a year versus about 9-10% in the evidence-based base (price steps of up to 7% a year on .com to Nov 2029 plus 5% domain-base growth), so implied_vs_base is in_line; verdict unchanged]** Named reservation: hold at reduced weight versus a full-conviction name; do not size this as if the bull case (25.8%/yr) were the base case.

## 2. Business in plain English
Verisign operates the authoritative registry for the .com and .net top-level internet domains under an exclusive agreement with ICANN (renewed in November 2024 through November 30, 2030) and a related Cooperative Agreement with the U.S. Department of Commerce. It sells wholesale registrations and renewals to domain registrars (GoDaddy, Namecheap, etc.), who in turn sell to end customers; Verisign itself does not sell to consumers. Because the marginal cost of adding or renewing a domain in an existing registry database is close to zero, incremental revenue converts to profit at very high margins (68.2% GAAP operating margin, Q2 2026). Its competitive position is not "market share" in the normal sense — it is a government-linked, contractually exclusive registry operator, with pricing capped by that same contract.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
B1 ranks VRSN #53 (composite 89.4th percentile), driven by exceptionally strong **Quality** (gross-profit/assets 98.8th percentile — reflecting the registry's near-zero marginal cost — OCF/assets 96.9th percentile) and strong **Value** (FCF yield 79.4th percentile, EBIT/EV 64.8th percentile), with average Momentum and Earnings-momentum. ## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- Extremely capital-light: capex is consistently **under 2% of revenue** ($26.0M in H1 2026 vs. $434.6M Q2 revenue alone), so OCF converts to FCF almost fully.
- OCF growing: FY2025 $1,091.1M vs. FY2024 $902.6M (+20.9%) vs. FY2023 $853.8M; H1 2026 $504.0M vs. H1 2025 $493.8M (+2.1% — a visible deceleration from the FY2025 pace worth watching, though could be timing/working-capital).
- **Negative equity from buybacks (use net debt/EBITDA, not ROE)**: stockholders' equity is **−$2,254.1M** at Q2 2026, deepening from −$1,977.0M at Q1 2025 — buybacks have exceeded net income in most recent years (FY2025 buybacks $881.6M vs. net income $825.7M = 107%; FY2024 buybacks $1,225.6M vs. NI $785.7M = 156%). On an OCF basis the coverage is healthier (FY2025 buybacks were 81% of OCF, not underwater). Debt: ~$1.785–1.79bn of senior notes, essentially flat for years. **Net debt/EBITDA ≈1.1x** (V1 net debt $1,306.2M ÷ TTM EBITDA ≈$1,186M) — modest leverage despite the negative book-equity headline.
- **June 2026 debt refinancing, fully closed before this review's cutoff**: on 2026-06-26 Verisign issued $550M of 5.10% Senior Notes due 2031; on 2026-07-20 it used the proceeds to redeem the existing $550M 4.75% Senior Notes due 2027. Net effect: maturity extended from 2027 to 2031, coupon up ~35bps, no increase in principal — a maturity-extension refinancing, not incremental leverage for buybacks.
- Share count down from 107.1M (Q3'22) to 91.1M (Q2'26), −14.9% over ~3.75 years (~3.9%/yr).

**My view: the "fair" verdict is right, and I would not push back on the base case (+7.8%/yr) — but the "implied 14.5%/yr growth" framing overstates how aggressive the pricing really is.** VRSN can raise .com prices up to 7%/yr in years 3–6 of each 6-year ICANN term (no increases allowed in years 1–2) — it just exercised this, raising the wholesale price from $10.26 to $10.97 (+6.9%) effective November 1, 2026 — and domain-base growth guidance has just been raised to 5.2–6.0%. Price (~+7%) × base (~+5.5%) is a plausible ~12%/yr *revenue* tailwind in peak-pricing years, before the buyback-driven EPS uplift (~4%/yr from share-count reduction) — i.e., something in the low-to-mid-teens EPS growth is achievable in good years, which is close to, but still below, the 14.5%/yr the reverse-DCF requires *every year for ten years*. The **bull case (25.8%/yr, requiring 34.9x exit multiple)** is the scenario that needs the most scepticism, not the base case.
**dossier_view: fair; consistent with V1: yes** (I agree with the verdict and the base case; I flag the "implied growth" framing and the thin peer set as nuances, not disagreements with the number itself).

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:**
1. Contractually exclusive registry through Nov 2030 with demonstrated, exercised pricing power (7% max annual increase, just taken) flowing through at near-100% incremental margin.
2. Domain-base growth reaccelerating and guided up twice in 2026 (3.1–4.3% → 5.2–6.0%), with record new registrations, management attributing part of the strength to AI-related tailwinds rather than headwinds.
3. Extremely capital-light (capex <2% of revenue), funding a large, sustained buyback program (14.9% share-count reduction in ~4 years) against only ~1.1x net debt/EBITDA.

**Bear:**
1. The reverse-DCF's implied 14.5%/yr decade-long growth rate is well above anything VRSN has ever delivered (5-year CAGR 5.8%, 10-year CAGR 4.4%), and rests on a thin, imperfect 2-name peer comparison; the model's own base-case value already exceeds the Street's most bullish 12-month target.
2. Pricing power is not unlimited: no increases are allowed in years 1–2 of each 6-year ICANN cycle, and the whole framework is a negotiated, government-linked agreement (ICANN / U.S. Dept. of Commerce) that is renewed, not permanent.
3. Negative and deepening equity (−$2,254.1M, worsening ~$277M in 15 months) now funded partly by debt refinanced at a higher coupon (5.10% vs. 4.75%) — a modest but real increase in the cost of capital.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
1. ICANN/.com pricing authority is capped, revoked or renegotiated below the current "7%/yr in years 3–6" framework before the Nov 2030 expiry.
2. Domain-base growth falls back below **3% YoY for two consecutive quarters** (current guide 5.2–6.0%).
3. Net debt/EBITDA rises above **2.0x** (currently ≈1.1x).
4. Quarterly revenue growth decelerates below **4% YoY for two consecutive quarters** (currently +6.0% Q2'26, +6.6% Q1'26).
5. A materially adverse outcome in any future .com/.net registry-agreement renewal or a DOJ/ICANN antitrust or regulatory action against the arrangement.

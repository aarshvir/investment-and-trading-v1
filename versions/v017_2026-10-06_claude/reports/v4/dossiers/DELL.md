# DELL — Dell Technologies Inc. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F60, Wave 3)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (12–36 month horizon), with a named reservation: DELL is now, in effect, an AI-server-cycle stock (revenue and backlog concentrated in Nvidia-platform rack integration for a handful of hyperscale/AI customers) wrapped around a legacy, low-margin PC/traditional-server business and a still-large (if improving) legacy-LBO debt load. No V1 systematic valuation row exists for DELL (`v1_verdict` = null); this verdict rests on my own primary-source work below.

## 2. Business in plain English
Dell designs, builds and sells servers, storage, PCs and related services to enterprises, governments and consumers, largely through its own direct sales/channel model. Its fastest-growing segment (Infrastructure Solutions Group, ISG) integrates and ships AI-optimized server racks (built around Nvidia GPUs) to hyperscalers and large enterprises; its larger but slower-growing Client Solutions Group (CSG) sells PCs/laptops. It also runs Dell Financial Services (DFS), a captive leasing/financing arm that funds equipment purchases for customers.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1 factor scores show strong quality (asset growth 88th pct — balance sheet expanding to fund the AI buildout — and strong earnings momentum, SUE 85th pct), with ROE not computed (negative/undefined equity base, a legacy artefact of the 2016 EMC-merger LBO capital structure, not a current red flag by itself). The growth is **real and accelerating, not an artefact**: revenue, operating income and — critically — the AI-server order backlog have all inflected together across three consecutive quarters of results and two consecutive guidance raises (§5), each corroborated by a specific, disclosed backlog dollar figure rather than vague commentary.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3):** (1) $95B AI-server backlog (disclosed at the Q2 FY2027 earnings call, 2026-09-01) provides multi-quarter revenue visibility well beyond the current guide period. (2) Two consecutive large guidance raises in one fiscal year, each beating the prior raise, show genuine operating momentum rather than a one-quarter pop. (3) The GAAP-vs-non-GAAP EPS gap is comparatively narrow for a large-cap tech name, meaning the market isn't being asked to look past heavy stock-comp adjustments to believe the growth story.

**Bear (3):** (1) DELL's AI-server business is a rack-integration/assembly margin on top of Nvidia's GPUs for a concentrated set of hyperscale/AI customers — a single large customer loss, a shift to buying direct, or an Nvidia platform-availability shock would hit disproportionately. (2) $9.6B of the $34.5B total debt is DFS financing debt tied to customer leases — if AI-server demand reverses, DFS receivable credit quality (customer concentration again) becomes a live balance-sheet risk, not just a revenue one. (3) The scale of the last two guidance raises (revenue guide midpoint jumping ~15% and EPS guide ~42% within one quarter) signals genuinely lumpy, hard-to-forecast order timing — the same dynamic that produces upside surprises can produce downside ones just as fast.

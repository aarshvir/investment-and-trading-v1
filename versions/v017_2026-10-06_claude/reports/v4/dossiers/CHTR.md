# CHTR — Charter Communications (Spectrum cable / broadband) — diligence dossier F121

**1. Verdict: WATCH.** The equity looks very cheap on cash flow (about 27% pro forma free-cash-flow yield on my estimate) but it is now a thin slice (about 14% of enterprise value) on top of roughly $110bn of debt, revenue and EBITDA are shrinking at an accelerating rate, the first combined Charter+Cox quarter has not been reported, and the CFO resigns on 15 Oct 2026. Horizon 12–36 months. Condition to upgrade to INCLUDE-SMALL: Q3-2026 results (expected late Oct, date not yet announced) show legacy-Charter Adjusted EBITDA decline no worse than 3% year on year, combined free cash flow at or above $1.0bn per quarter, and a 2027 capex guide that steps down from $11.4bn. This confirms the quick triage (not advanced) but for different reasons: the triage used a pre-Cox share count and a "2.7x P/E" that no longer describes the company.

**2. Business in plain English.** Charter sells broadband internet, mobile phone lines, TV and phone to about 31.5m US homes and businesses under the Spectrum brand, mostly on its own cable network. Revenue is subscriptions (Internet $5.8bn and Video $3.1bn of Q2-26 revenue of $13.5bn) plus business services and advertising. Its moat is a local-monopoly-like cable plant passing 59m premises, scale in mobile (12.5m lines via Verizon's network) and bundling; the threat is fixed-wireless and fibre overbuild taking broadband customers and video cord-cutting. On 19 Aug 2026 it closed the Liberty Broadband merger and the Cox Communications deal (8-K, accession 0001140361-26-033909, filed 2026-08-20), adding Cox's roughly $12bn revenue cable business.

**3. Why the model likes it / durability.** b1 factor families favour value (earnings yield 0.29, FCF yield 0.11 on the data card) and quality (ROE 0.54 from a tiny book equity). Value is real but not a clean artefact-free signal: the data card uses about 121m Class A shares (market cap $13.7bn) whereas the economic share count after Cox is about 163m (Class A about 114.6m, Advance/Newhouse units about 14.7m, Cox units 33.6m; my derivation from 10-Q accession 0001091667-26-000052 and 8-K 0001140361-26-033909), i.e. about $18.4bn at the $112.91 close. The ROE factor is leverage-inflated. Price momentum is deeply negative (closing price down from $427 in May 2025 to $113).

**6. Earnings quality and balance sheet.**
- Scope is critical. Charter consolidated principal debt was $93.8bn at 30 Jun 2026 ("total principal amount of debt was $93.8 billion", Q2 release), of which $12.5bn credit facility, $54.7bn investment-grade senior secured notes and $26.7bn high-yield senior unsecured notes (10-Q 0001091667-26-000052, Liquidity section), plus a $1.6bn equipment-installment-plan financing facility (separate bankruptcy-remote subsidiary). Consolidated cash $509m. Charter-defined leverage was "4.15 times Adjusted EBITDA as of December 31, 2025" against a target range of 4.0–4.5x (10-K 0001091667-26-000017).
- Pro forma for Cox (unaudited pro forma combined balance sheet at 30 Jun 2026, 8-K 0001104659-26-089522; Charter historical consolidated noncurrent long-term debt was $92,960m per the same table): pro forma combined long-term debt $107.2bn, pro forma combined current debt $2.0bn, EIP facility $1.6bn, cash $0.43bn, so pro forma consolidated net debt about $110bn, about 4.0x my pro forma EBITDA of about $27.3bn (derived as pro forma revenue less operating costs; not company-reported Adjusted EBITDA). Cox's debt and finance leases of about $12bn stay at Cox subsidiaries and are now guarantors and collateral-sharing with Charter secured debt (8-K 0001104659-26-100972). New money since 6 Aug: $4.75bn of senior secured notes at 6.05%, 6.60%, 6.95% and 7.85% (8-K 0001104659-26-098363), and exchange-offer notes at 7.087% and 7.337% (8-K 0001104659-26-100328). Pro forma interest expense about $6.06bn a year (pro forma statement of operations, six months x2).
- Equity claims ahead of common: $6.0bn convertible preferred units at 6.875% (conversion price $477.41, far above the market), Cox holds 33.6m common units, and Liberty's preferred converts to Charter Series A preferred (about 7.2m shares).
- Cash conversion: TTM FCF/net income to Charter = $4.7bn / $4.9bn, about 96%; SBC about 1.5% of Q1-26 revenue ($203m). Non-GAAP: Adjusted EBITDA and FCF are company-defined; GAAP net income includes debt-extinguishment gains (Q2-26) and $5.6bn Cox intangible impairment appears in Cox's 2025 pro forma column (not Charter's).
- Share count: basic weighted shares fell 13.1% year on year in Q2-26 (buybacks of 4.0m shares for $838m in Q2; Liberty merger retired 38.6m shares and issued 33.9m).

**7. Valuation and reconciliation with V1.** No V1 row exists for CHTR (v1_verdict null). Economic equity value $18.4bn (163m shares at $112.91); pro forma net debt about $110bn (consolidated) plus $6.2bn preferred gives enterprise value about $135bn, 4.9x EBITDA. My pro forma equity FCF is about $5.05bn (Charter TTM FCF $4.74bn + Cox about $1.29bn (2 x 6M operating cash flow less capex, Cox standalone 8-K 0001104659-26-089522) - $0.56bn incremental interest (pro forma adjustment 2e) - $0.41bn preferred coupon), about $31 a share, a 27.5% yield. **Reverse DCF:** at a 14% cost of equity the price implies equity FCF shrinking about 12% a year for 10 years (terminal growth 0%); at enterprise level (WACC 8%, unlevered FCF about $11.5bn) it implies about -0.9% a year. Evidence-based cases (3-year, equity is levered about 7x so outcomes fan out): bear EBITDA -4% a year, capex stuck at $13bn, exit 4.5x EBITDA, equity nearly wiped (-36% a year); base EBITDA -2% a year, capex falls to $11bn by 2029 (my assumption; company outlook is only the quoted "approximately $11.4 billion" for 2026), exit 4.9x: +14% a year; bull EBITDA flat, capex $10bn, exit 5.6x: +48% a year. Implied growth is below my base, but my base rests on an unquantified capex step-down; if capex does not fall and EBITDA keeps falling 3-4% a year, the market price is about right.

**8. Bull case (3) and bear case with risks (3).** Bull: (1) Mobile lines +15.5% and video losses narrowing (-21k vs -80k) cushion EBITDA; (2) 2027 network-evolution completion should cut capex toward $10bn and lift FCF; (3) Cox adds scale and cost takeout (quantified synergies are not in the filings I opened), buybacks at a 27% FCF yield are hugely accretive if leverage holds 4.0–4.5x. Bear: (1) EBITDA falling faster each quarter, Internet customers -1.7% year on year with fixed-wireless and fibre competition; (2) equity is about 14% of EV, so a 15% EV decline erases it, and new debt prices at 6–7.85%; (3) CFO leaving mid-integration, Cox revenue was already -4.0% (six months 2026 $6,067m vs $6,323m) and integration risk is untested.

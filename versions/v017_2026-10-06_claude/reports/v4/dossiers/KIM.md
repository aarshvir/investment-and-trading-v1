# KIM — Kimco Realty (grocery-anchored shopping-center REIT) — diligence dossier F121

**1. Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL.** A high-quality, A-rated landlord growing funds from operations (FFO) per share about 4–5% with record occupancy, priced at about 12.2x 2026 FFO and a 5.0% dividend yield; the price implies about 2.75% a year of long-run cash-flow growth against my 4% base. Named reservation: the 10-year Treasury yield is about 5.2% (V1 input), the growth ceiling is mid-single-digit, and consolidated leverage is about 5.7x, so this is a defensive income compounder with limited upside, not a high-conviction winner. Horizon 12–36 months. This overturns the lukewarm triage (not advanced, "26x P/E"): GAAP P/E is the wrong yardstick for a REIT.

**2. Business in plain English.** Kimco owns and operates open-air shopping centers, mostly anchored by grocers, in first-ring suburbs of big coastal and Sun Belt metros, leasing space to grocers, off-price retailers, restaurants and service tenants. It earns rent (minimum rent, recoveries of taxes and maintenance), has joint-venture interests, a structured-investment lending book and a small mixed-use/residential pipeline. Moat: scarce well-located land with little new supply, tenants selling everyday necessities, and A-range credit ratings (Moody's A3, S&P A-) that give a funding-cost edge over smaller REITs.

**3. Why the model likes it / durability.** b1 shows earnings yield 0.059 and FCF yield 0.059 with a mid-pack composite (live rank 76); the lift comes from quality/stability and value families rather than momentum. The reason is durable for a rent-roll business, but the GAAP earnings yield understates cash earnings (depreciation is large) while the triage's 26x GAAP P/E overstates expensiveness. Growth in 2026 is partly embedded: the release says "The spread between the company's pro-rata leased versus economic occupancy rates was 400 basis points ... representing $75 million in future rents from signed leases that have not yet commenced" (Q2-26 release, 8-K accession 0001193125-26-331498).

**8. Bull case (3) and bear case with risks (3).** Bull: (1) record occupancy with a 400 bps signed-not-open gap means visible rent growth; (2) A-range credit lets Kimco recycle capital (Costco-anchored sales about $127m, acquisitions via 1031 and structured investments) and fund buybacks below NAV; (3) 12% dividend raise signals cash-flow confidence. Bear: (1) higher-for-longer rates: 10-year yield about 5.2% pressures REIT multiples (stock down from $26.38 in July to $22.44); (2) consolidated leverage about 5.7x and $773m of 2026 maturities refinance above legacy coupons; (3) small-shop occupancy at a record 92.9% leaves little room for further organic gains, and a consumer downturn hits credit loss (outlook "(55bps) to (75bps)" of rental revenues).

**9. Key risks: kill criteria and thesis-invalidation triggers (measurable).**
1. Pro-rata leased occupancy below 95.0% in two consecutive quarters (Q2-26: 96.4%).
2. Same-property NOI growth below 2.0% in two consecutive quarters (YTD "+2.6%"; outlook "+3.0% to +3.5%").
3. Full-year FFO per diluted share outlook midpoint cut below $1.80 (current "$1.83 to $1.84").
4. Consolidated net debt to annualised EBITDA above 6.5x (my basis, currently about 5.7x), or a downgrade of Moody's A3 or S&P A-.
5. Dividend payout above 90% of AFFO or no dividend increase in the 2027 cycle.

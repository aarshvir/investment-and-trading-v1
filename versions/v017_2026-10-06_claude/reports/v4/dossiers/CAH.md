## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE.** Thesis horizon **18–36 months**. One-sentence reason: a thin-margin, oligopoly drug distributor with a four-quarter, no-cuts, beat-and-raise guidance record, a real (if early) higher-margin diversification (10.4% segment margin in "Other" vs 1.2% in core Pharma), and a reverse DCF that prices in roughly flat-to-negative 10-year growth — well below what the evidence supports — against real, disclosed opioid-liability and goodwill-impairment red flags that are sized and monitorable, not thesis-breaking. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: verdict kept, but the reverse DCF is restated: on free cash flow after SBC and with the opioid payments deducted, at CAPM 8.6% the price implies about 2.8% a year (range 1.2-4.6%), not -2.5% to +0.7%; see Re-assessment (RA8)]**

## 2. Business in plain English

Cardinal Health is one of three companies (with Cencora/COR and McKesson) that distribute the large majority of branded and generic prescription drugs to US pharmacies, hospitals and clinics — an oligopoly with structurally thin (~1–2%) distribution margins earned on volume and working-capital efficiency, not pricing power. Alongside that core Pharmaceutical and Specialty Solutions business, it runs Global Medical Products and Distribution (GMPD: medical/surgical products, own-brand Cardinal Health products) and a faster-growing, higher-margin "Other" segment built via acquisition (Nuclear & Precision Health Solutions/oncology and urology physician-practice platforms, at-Home Solutions medical supplies, OptiFreight Logistics freight brokerage). Competitive position rests on scale, distribution infrastructure and long-term contracts with drug manufacturers and providers.

## 3. Why the model likes it, and whether that's durable

Triage flagged 23% street-target upside, buyback-driven EPS growth and steady volume growth, with the historical opioid litigation as the only red flag (already reflected in financials). That is largely confirmed and is **durable**: adjusted diluted EPS guidance for FY2026 was raised at all four quarterly releases in the year (see §5), and the raises were **not** driven only by the one-off IEEPA tariff-refund benefit recognized in Q4 (ex that benefit, FY2026 non-GAAP EPS still grew 33%). The "Other" segment's 10.4% margin (vs 1.19% Pharma, 2.03% GMPD) is a genuine mix shift, not an accounting artefact, funded by disclosed cash M&A (Solaris Health $1.9bn in FY2026; GI Alliance, ADS, Specialty Alliance, ION and Urology America named in the 10-K's intangible-asset note). **Less durable / worth discounting**: revenue growth decelerated every quarter through FY2026 (+22%, +19%, +11%, +6% YoY in Q1–Q4) — largely a base-effect and GLP-1/drug-mix arithmetic (revenue scales with drug list price, gross profit does not), so it is not a demand signal either way, but it means the market's revenue-growth read should not be over-weighted relative to segment profit and cash generation.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **Entity scope**: all figures below are **consolidated** Cardinal Health, Inc. (10-K balance sheet and cash-flow statement), not a segment or subsidiary figure.
- **FCF conversion**: FY2026 OCF $5,174m vs GAAP net income $1,714m — OCF/NI 3.0x, but this is inflated by a large working-capital swing (accounts-payable timing typical of a distributor building GLP-1 inventory/AP) and includes the effect of $417m opioid-settlement cash payments already made. FCF (OCF − capex $649m) = $4,525m. Management's own FY2027 guide for "non-GAAP adjusted free cash flow" is **$3.5bn–$4.0bn**, materially below the FY2026 OCF-capex figure — treat FY2026 FCF as boosted by working-capital timing, not the run rate.
- **GAAP vs adjusted gap** (FY2026, per the 10-K's own bridge, consolidated): GAAP diluted EPS $7.23 → non-GAAP $11.26. Reconciling items include: amortization and acquisition-related costs ($1.47/sh), acquisition-related cash/share-based comp ($1.16/sh), restructuring/severance ($0.34/sh), litigation recoveries net (−$0.04/sh net favorable), impairments/loss on asset disposal ($0.56/sh — this is where the Navista & ION goodwill impairment sits), and a state-opioid-assessment prior-year true-up (−$0.05/sh). The gap is large (56%) but each line is itemized and traceable to the 10-K table, not a single opaque "other" bucket.
- **Balance sheet leverage**: total debt $8,886m (current $1,882m + long-term $7,004m, 10-K FY2026 balance sheet) vs cash $4,856m → **net debt ≈ $4.0bn**. Non-GAAP operating earnings $3,624m plus D&A gives approximate EBITDA in the $4.0–4.3bn range → **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 1x**, low leverage for the sector. **Stockholders' equity is negative (−$2,883m at 2026-06-30)**, the mechanical result of buybacks exceeding retained cumulative earnings over many years — a known, disclosed feature of this business model (also true of peer COR), not a going-concern signal given the low net leverage and IG-typical debt profile.
- **Opioid litigation (the disclosed, quantified red flag)**: $4.3bn accrued at 2026-06-30 for the National Opioid Settlement Agreement and related claims; the company expects to pay the majority of the remainder through **2038**; FY2026 cash payments were $417m, including the fifth annual $366m NOSA installment. This is a known, scheduled, already-accrued cash drag — not an open-ended contingency — but it is real and multi-decade.
- **Other legal/governance items**: a Department of Justice Civil Investigative Demand (received Nov 2023, still open per the FY2026 10-K) concerning Anti-Kickback Statute/False Claims Act questions on a 2022 GPO and rheumatology-MSO transaction; company states it is cooperating. A shareholder Cooperation Agreement with Elliott Associates/Elliott International (Business Review Committee tasked with reviewing strategy, portfolio and capital allocation) **expired in Q2 FY2025** — no ongoing activist standstill/board-seat overhang disclosed as of the FY2026 10-K.
- **Share count / capital return**: FY2026 buybacks $1,358m (vs $765m FY25), dividends $491m with the per-share rate raised each quarter ($0.5107 vs $0.5056 a year earlier); weighted diluted shares fell from 242m (FY25) to 237m (FY26).

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull**: (1) Four-for-four guidance raises in FY2026 and an FY2027 initial guide (+13–15%) already above the company's own long-term algorithm, with net debt/EBITDA near 1x giving ample buyback/M&A capacity. (2) The "Other" segment (10.4% margin, +26% revenue, +37% profit FY2026) is a real, executing diversification away from thin-margin core distribution, funded by disclosed, named acquisitions. (3) The reverse DCF shows the market paying for almost no growth in a business with a demonstrated growth and margin-mix track record — a real margin of safety if the growth continues even at a much slower pace than FY2026's.

**Bear**: (1) $4.3bn of opioid-settlement payments still owed through 2038 is a permanent claim on cash flow, and the DOJ CID (open since Nov 2023) could still produce an adverse outcome not yet accrued for. (2) The Navista & ION goodwill impairment ($184m, Q3 FY26) shows the M&A-funded diversification carries real execution/valuation risk, not a guaranteed payoff. (3) FY2026's cash generation was flattered by working-capital timing (OCF $5.17bn vs a guided FY2027 FCF of only $3.5–4.0bn) and a one-off tariff refund inflated the reported non-GAAP EPS growth rate — the "beat" narrative should be read on the ex-one-off, normalized-working-capital basis, which is still good but less dramatic than the headline.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. Non-GAAP diluted EPS guidance is cut (vs. raised or maintained) at any quarterly release — breaks the core track record this thesis rests on.
2. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.5x (from ≈1x today) without a value-accretive reason disclosed.
3. A second goodwill/intangible impairment >$100m in the Other segment (Navista/ION/Solaris/GI Alliance/ADS platforms) within any four-quarter window.
4. The DOJ Civil Investigative Demand (Anti-Kickback/False Claims Act, opened Nov 2023) converts to a filed complaint or a settlement exceeding $250m not previously accrued.
5. "Other" segment margin falls below 8% for two consecutive quarters (loss of the diversification thesis's economics).

## Re-assessment (RA8, 2026-10-06)
Research only; not personal advice. Data cutoff 25 Sep 2026 close ($220.22); shares 232.58m (10-K cover, 31 Jul 2026), market cap $51.2bn; 10-year Treasury 5.17% on 25 Sep (FRED DGS10).

**Recency (EDGAR to 6 Oct).** 8-K 5 Aug (Item 5.02: Anita Zielinski to succeed Mary Scherer as Chief Accounting Officer on 5 Nov 2026); 8-K filed 11 Aug (Items 1.01/1.02/2.03, acc. 0000721371-26-000036): new unsecured $4.0bn revolver to 7 Aug 2031 (net leverage covenant 4.00x) replacing the $2.0bn five-year, $1.0bn 364-day and the $1.0bn receivables program, no penalties; DEF 14A and ARS 21 Sep; 8-K 1 Oct 2026 (acc. 0000721371-26-000041): "binding Letter of Intent to extend its existing distribution agreement with CVS Health through June 30, 2032, reflecting the continuation of Cardinal Health's current scope", FY2027 non-GAAP EPS growth of 13-15% ($12.40-$12.60) and long-term growth of 12-14% reaffirmed; Q1 FY2027 call confirmed for 5 Nov 2026. No merger, rating action (none found) or guidance cut. CVS was 28% of FY2026 revenue and 22% of gross receivables, top five customers 43% (10-K, acc. 0000721371-26-000038); the dossier omitted the concentration. The LOI is binding but not a definitive agreement.

**Method errors corrected**
1. *EPS used as cash.* The reverse DCF started from non-GAAP EPS $12.50, which excludes acquisition-related cash and share-based compensation ($1.16 a share in FY2026), restructuring ($0.34), impairments and litigation, and the stated -2.5% to +0.7% range does not reproduce (same inputs: +0.7% at 7.5%, +4.6% at 9.0%).
2. *Cash flow.* FY2026: OCF $5,174m, capex $649m, reported FCF $4,525m, but accounts payable rose $3,463m (receivables -$408m, inventories -$488m, net trade working-capital inflow about $2.6bn), SBC was $367m (FY2025 $244m) and opioid payments were $417m. Company "adjusted FCF" of $4,971m adds back $401m of litigation payments and $45m of Specialty Alliance unit repurchases (Schedule 6). FY2027 adjusted FCF is guided at $3.5-4.0bn with capex about $700m. The dossier's 9.3% "FCF yield" is not a run rate; it is stated before SBC and flattered by working capital.
3. *Owner cash flow used here (before M&A, SBC as a cost):* guide midpoint $3.75bn less SBC about $0.35bn = $3.40bn; a bottom-up build (non-GAAP net income $2.91bn less real excluded costs about $0.35bn, plus depreciation about $0.55bn, less capex $0.70bn, plus about $0.3bn structural working-capital inflow) gives $2.7bn; central **$3.05bn**. Opioid payments are valued separately: the $374m sixth NOSA instalment was paid in July 2026, $4.3bn is accrued through 2038, present value $2.7bn at 8.6% (no tax shield taken). Acquisitions ($1,991m FY2026, $5,250m FY2025) are discretionary and not in the cash flow, so growth is organic only. Net debt $4.03bn is on the consolidated entity at 30 Jun 2026 (debt $8,886m less cash $4,856m); equity-level cash flow is valued against market cap, so there is no net-debt or interest-income double count.
4. *Discount rate.* CAPM with ERP 4.14%: Blume beta 0.655 (5 years) gives 7.88%, 0.828 (10 years) gives **8.6%**; the higher is used. Sensitivity 9.0% and 10.0%.
5. *Base.* Evidence: FY2027 segment profit guide Pharma +8-11%, Other +15-18%; long-term EPS growth of 12-14% includes about 2% a year of buyback (about $1bn on $51bn) and M&A. The dossier's 6-8% was a per-share figure compared with an aggregate; organic aggregate base restated to **5%**.

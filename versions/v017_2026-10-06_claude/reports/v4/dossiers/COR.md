## 1. Verdict

## 2. Business in plain English

Cencora is one of the three dominant US pharmaceutical wholesalers (with Cardinal Health and McKesson), distributing branded and generic drugs to retail and hospital pharmacies on thin (~1.5–2%) gross margins earned on volume and working-capital efficiency. It has been building a specialty-oncology and now urology physician-services platform (RCA — Retina Consultants of America, acquired Jan 2025; OneOncology, equity stake stepped up to full consolidation in Feb 2026) inside its larger U.S. Healthcare Solutions segment, plus an International Healthcare Solutions segment (mainly World Courier and European distribution) and an "Other" bucket (MWI Animal Health, Profarma, U.S. Consulting Services, remaining PharmaLex units) that management is now **exploring strategic alternatives for** — i.e., a likely partial divestiture. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: not just a likely divestiture: U.S. Consulting Services was sold in April 2026 and an agreement to sell MWI Animal Health (merger with Covetrus) was signed in Q2 FY26, with assets held for sale of $3,836.1m and liabilities of $869.7m at 30 Jun 2026 and an FTC second request received 20 May 2026 (10-Q 0001140859-26-000034, Notes 3 and 6)]]**

## 3. Why the model likes it, and whether that's durable

Triage's framing (same oligopoly economics as peers, slightly better specialty mix, weak recent momentum despite 20% street upside) holds up. The **durable** part: adjusted diluted EPS grew 16.3% in FY2025 ($16.00 vs $13.76) and FY2026 adjusted-EPS guidance was raised at every quarterly release reviewed (see §5), driven by real segment operating-income growth, not one-off items — GLP-1 volume and the OneOncology/RCA specialty build-out are named, structural drivers in the company's own release language. The **less durable / genuinely mixed** part: FY2026 **revenue-growth guidance was cut**, not raised, over the same year — from "7% to 9%" (Q1 FY26 release) to "**4% to 6%**" (Q3 FY26 release) for both total company and the U.S. Healthcare Solutions segment — even as EPS guidance kept rising. That combination (revenue guide down, EPS guide up) is consistent with margin/mix improvement and a smaller "Other" segment base (MWI Animal Health reclassified toward held-for-sale), but it means the "growth" the model is scoring should be read as *margin-driven*, not *volume-driven*, and the two should not be conflated.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **Entity scope**: all figures are **consolidated** Cencora, Inc. (10-Q balance sheet/cash-flow statement, 2026-06-30), not segment or subsidiary figures unless stated.
- **FCF conversion**: nine-month FY2026 OCF was **$1,687.5m** vs nine-month FY2025 OCF of $741.7m — but quarterly OCF is extremely lumpy (Q1 FY26 alone was **−$2,305m**, Q2 FY26 **−$967m**, before a large positive swing) driven by working-capital timing around drug-price cycles and the OneOncology consolidation; nine-month capex was $511.0m. Full-year FCF should be read off the eventual FY2026 10-K, not annualized from any single quarter.
- **GAAP vs adjusted gap**: FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS $7.96 vs adjusted $16.00 — roughly a 2x gap, the widest of the three companies in this batch, driven by acquisition-intangible amortization, the PharmaLex goodwill impairments (§3), litigation/opioid items, and (in FY2026) the OneOncology remeasurement gain. Every reconciling line is itemized in the company's own non-GAAP tables (10-Q Exhibit 99.1), not a black box.
- **Leverage**: total debt at 2026-06-30 = **$11.72bn** (long-term $11.44bn + current $0.28bn), up sharply from $7.92bn at 2025-12-31 — the increase is the debt-financed OneOncology step-up acquisition (closed Feb 2026), confirmed in the cash-flow statement ("Cost of acquired companies, net of cash acquired" $4.97bn nine-month FY26 vs $4.00bn prior year). Cash $2.82bn → **net debt ≈ $8.9bn**. FY2026 guided adjusted operating income ≈$4.2bn base × 1.13–1.14 growth ≈ $4.7–4.8bn, plus run-rate D&A (~$1.0–1.1bn annualized from the nine-month $779m figure) → approximate EBITDA **$5.7–5.9bn**, putting **net debt/EBITDA at roughly 1.5x** — moderate, and elevated versus FY2025 levels specifically because of the OneOncology deal financing.
- **Stockholders' equity is thin** ($3.05bn at 2026-06-30 vs total assets $83.8bn) — the same buyback-driven capital structure as CAH; not itself a distress signal given the leverage above, but it means small absolute swings in retained earnings move book equity a lot.
- **Opioid litigation**: accrued liability for the Distributor Settlement Agreement and related opioid litigation was **$4.2bn at 2026-06-30** (down from $4.3bn at 2025-09-30), expected to be paid over the **next 13 years**, with $396.2m expected in the next twelve months. In Q3 FY26 the company recorded a **net credit (income)** of $88.6m from litigation/opioid items (a favorable accrual revision or settlement-cost reduction), a data point worth noting as the mirror image of CAH's ongoing net cash outflow.
- **Recurring impairments as an earnings-quality flag**: a further $249.5m "impairment of assets, including goodwill" was booked in Q1 FY2026 (nine-month total, per the 10-Q income statement) — on top of the $723.9m PharmaLex impairment in Q4 FY2025 and $418.0m in Q4 FY2024. Three consecutive fiscal years (FY2024, FY2025, FY2026-to-date) with a material goodwill/asset impairment is a pattern, not a one-off, concentrated in the "Other" segment now under strategic review.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull**: (1) Adjusted EPS guidance raised at every checkpoint in FY2026, on top of 16.3% FY2025 adjusted-EPS growth, evidencing durable execution even as the revenue guide was trimmed. (2) OneOncology/RCA give Cencora a specialty-oncology and retina-care physician-platform business with materially better economics than core distribution, now fully consolidated and contributing to segment profit. (3) The reverse DCF shows the market paying for only low-single-digit growth in a company that has consistently delivered double-digit adjusted EPS growth.

**Bear**: (1) Three consecutive fiscal years of goodwill/asset impairments (PharmaLex primarily) is a genuine capital-allocation track record concern, not a one-off; the businesses now under "strategic alternatives" review (MWI Animal Health, Profarma, U.S. Consulting Services) could realize further losses on sale. (2) Debt nearly doubled in two quarters to fund the OneOncology step-up ($7.9bn → $11.7bn), and net debt/EBITDA at ~1.5x, while not high in absolute terms, is a genuine increase in financial risk versus a year ago. (3) Revenue-growth guidance was cut from 7–9% to 4–6% during FY2026 — the "raise" story is entirely a margin/mix and buyback story, not a volume story, and that is a narrower, more fragile basis for continued EPS beats if margin gains stall.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. Adjusted diluted EPS guidance is cut at any quarterly release (breaking the current raise streak).
2. A further goodwill/asset impairment exceeding $150m is booked in any single quarter (a fourth consecutive year of the PharmaLex-style pattern).
3. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above 2.5x without a disclosed, value-accretive reason.
4. U.S. Healthcare Solutions segment revenue growth falls below 3% for two consecutive quarters (below even the cut 4-6% FY26 guide).
5. The "Other" segment strategic-alternatives review results in a divestiture loss exceeding $200m versus carrying value.

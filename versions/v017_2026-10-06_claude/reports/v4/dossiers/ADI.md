# ADI — Analog Devices, Inc. (Semiconductors, Information Technology)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — 12–36 month horizon. Analog Devices is executing exceptionally (three straight quarters of beat-and-raise, a genuinely new secular AI-data-center leg alongside a normal industrial/auto cyclical recovery) and its balance sheet is sound. Sized small, not full weight, because the reverse DCF shows the market has already priced in 10-year FCF growth (~9–14% depending on discount-rate assumption) that is at the upper edge of — and by some readings modestly above — a prudent through-cycle base case for an analog semiconductor company, and the stock is trading near its highest-ever revenue run-rate, which the sector's own playbook flags as exactly the point multiples look least trustworthy.

## 2. Business in plain English
Analog Devices designs and sells analog, mixed-signal and DSP chips — the components that convert real-world signals (voltage, temperature, motion, radio-frequency) into digital data and back — into industrial automation, automotive (EV powertrain, ADAS, in-cabin), communications infrastructure (including AI data-center power and connectivity) and consumer electronics. It sells through direct enterprise relationships and distributors, with long (5–10+ year) product design cycles once a chip is "designed in" to a customer's product, which makes revenue sticky but also slow to re-accelerate after a design loss. It earns money on high gross margins (~65–67%) typical of analog IP with long product lifecycles and low process-node intensity relative to logic/memory chips.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
ADI's composite_qvm score (0.503, decile 7, rank 173) is unremarkable overall, but the composition matters: **momentum (fam_M 0.683, 69th pct) and earnings-surprise/sentiment (fam_S 0.723, 72nd pct) are doing the work**, while quality (fam_Q 0.399 — gross-profit/assets only 29th pct, ROE 25th pct) and value (fam_V 0.419, FCF/price 40th pct) are middling. This is the mirror image of ADBE: ADI is being re-rated on **execution momentum during a cyclical recovery**, not because it screens as statistically cheap or exceptionally profitable on trailing numbers. That is a **durable but cycle-dependent** reason — real (three consecutive quarters of guide-and-beat, an AI-data-center growth leg that did not exist at the prior cycle peak), but it will fade automatically once the base effect from the FY24 trough laps, so the momentum score should not be read as a standing quality signal.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF conversion:** per the company's own Q3 FY26 release, TTM operating cash flow ≈ $5.5B and TTM free cash flow ≈ $4.9B — a strong ~90% OCF-to-FCF conversion (capex 9-mo FY26 $392.7M, ≈2.8% of TTM revenue, modest for a semiconductor IDM but rising from $318.4M in the year-ago period as the company invests into demand).
- **SBC:** a standalone `ShareBasedCompensation` XBRL tag was not separately reported by ADI in the periods pulled; R&D expense (a proxy ceiling that includes SBC) runs ~11% of TTM revenue. This is a data gap, disclosed rather than estimated.
- **GAAP vs. adjusted gap:** Q3 FY26 guide shows reported EPS $3.14 vs. adjusted EPS $3.86 (≈23% gap), and reported vs. adjusted operating margin 42.6% vs. 52.0% (≈9.4pp gap) — mostly acquisition-related intangible amortization from the 2021 Maxim Integrated deal, a known and stable adjustment, not a new distortion.
- **One-off flag:** Q3 FY25 GAAP EPS of $1.04 was held down by an income-tax expense of $244.9M against pre-tax income that would otherwise imply a ~13–15% effective rate (Q1 FY26's tax expense of $115.0M on a larger base implies a materially lower rate) — a real, filed number, but the swing looks like a discrete tax item rather than an operating change; flagged for the valuation agent rather than adjusted away here.
- **Balance sheet:** cash $2.17B; total debt (current $1.34B + long-term $6.77B) = $8.11B; **net debt ≈ $5.95B**. Against TTM EBITDA of ~$6.9B (vendor-sourced), **net debt/EBITDA ≈ 0.86x** — low-moderate leverage, comfortable, largely a legacy of the debt-financed Maxim/Linear Technology acquisitions, being paid down (long-term debt fell from $8.15B at FYE25 to $6.77B by Q3 FY26).
- **Buybacks/dividends:** 9-mo FY26 buybacks $2.45B, dividends paid $1.56B (implied annualized dividend ≈ $3.20–3.30/share, yield ≈ 0.8–1.0% at $393.60) — balanced capital return, not aggressive relative to cash generation.

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse DCF
No V1 systematic-model row exists for ADI in `v1_valuation_table.csv` — **v1_verdict set to null**, this section is my own work. Vendor NTM P/E ≈ 24.4–24.6x (forward PE 24.1x per d4 snapshot), against a semiconductor/analog peer set (Texas Instruments typically 30x+, Microchip, NXP, ON Semiconductor in the high teens to low 20s) — **roughly in line with, to a modest discount versus, its highest-quality direct peer (TI)**, and above the broader analog/industrial peer group, consistent with its recent execution premium.

**10-year reverse DCF** (EV ≈ $196.7B [$190.7B market cap + $5.95B net debt]; TTM FCF $4.9B): solving for the uniform 10-year FCF growth rate that justifies the current EV, holding terminal growth at 3–4%:
- At WACC 7.5%: implied growth 7.6–10.0%/yr.
- At WACC 8.0–8.5% (my central estimate given ADI's low beta and investment-grade balance sheet): implied growth **9.3–12.8%/yr**.
- At WACC 9.0%: implied growth 12.4–14.1%/yr.

My evidence-based **base case** is company-level FCF compounding at **~9–11%/yr** through a full cycle — built from ADI's own 15-year organic-plus-M&A revenue CAGR (~9–10%, 2010–2025), a few points of structural upside from the new AI-data-center leg (Communications +84% y/y this quarter did not exist as a growth driver at the FY23 peak), offset by the industry's well-documented capex/inventory cyclicality (the FY24 trough is only two years behind us). **The market-implied range (9–14%) straddles this base case rather than sitting clearly below it** — this is the honest, sensitivity-tested conclusion, not a forced one. I classify this as `implied_vs_base = "in_line"`.

- **Bear case:** AI-data-center demand normalizes and the industrial/auto recovery stalls; FCF growth ~2%/yr for 3 years, multiple compresses from ~40x EV/FCF to ~30x → **~ −6%/yr** 3-year annualized return net of dividend.
- **Base case:** FCF growth ~11%/yr, multiple roughly flat (already fair) → **~+12%/yr** including dividend.
- **Bull case:** the AI-data-center leg proves structural and industrial/auto keep compounding; FCF growth ~18%/yr, multiple re-rates modestly toward TI's premium (~45x) → **~+23–24%/yr**.

## 8. Bull case
1. Three consecutive quarters of beat-and-raise with broad-based growth (industrial, comms, auto all up double-digits) — this is not a single-end-market or single-customer story.
2. A genuinely new, structural AI-data-center demand leg (Communications segment +84% y/y) layered on top of the normal cyclical recovery, which the market has not fully distinguished from ordinary cyclicality.
3. Investment-grade balance sheet with net debt/EBITDA ≈ 0.86x and falling as Maxim-era debt is retired, leaving capacity for further buybacks or M&A.

## 9. Bear case
1. Revenue is at a fresh all-time-high run-rate (TTM ~13% above the FY23 cyclical peak); the sector's own playbook explicitly warns that record-quarter P/E multiples are the classic way to buy an analog/semis cycle top, and my reverse DCF shows growth priced in is at the upper edge of (not clearly below) a fair base case.
2. Two disclosed 2026 cybersecurity incidents (data exfiltration confirmed 23 Jun 2026; a second, unrelated matter under assessment as of 26 Jul 2026) carry unquantified financial/reputational/litigation tail risk (a class-action solicitation was already reported).
3. The Q3 FY25 tax-rate swing and the general reliance on adjusted (ex-Maxim-amortization) EPS mean the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge needs to be re-checked every quarter rather than assumed stable.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Sequential revenue turns negative for **two consecutive quarters** after the current record run.
2. Communications (AI data-center) segment growth decelerates to **below 20% y/y for two consecutive quarters**.
3. Net debt/EBITDA rises above **2.0x** (from M&A or leveraged buybacks).
4. Either 2026 cybersecurity incident is confirmed to have caused a **quantified customer, contractual, or regulatory financial impact**.
5. Adjusted operating margin falls below **48% for two consecutive quarters** (a utilisation/pricing-driven margin-compression signal that the cycle has turned).

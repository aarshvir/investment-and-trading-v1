# Intel Corporation (NASDAQ: INTC) — Diligence Dossier

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (12–36 month horizon). Confirms the triage's own hesitation rather than overturning it. Q2 2026 revenue growth (+25% YoY, "fastest in more than fifteen years" per CEO Lip-Bu Tan) and Products segment operating income ($4.82B) are genuinely strong, but (a) Intel Foundry lost $2,089M in the quarter despite 31% segment revenue growth, confirming the triage's red flag verbatim, (b) the eye-catching $(11.0)B GAAP net loss and $(2.16) GAAP EPS is driven by a non-operating, non-cash derivative-liability revaluation on shares owed to the U.S. government — a fact the triage's cited news sources do not mention — and (c) at the 2026-09-25 close, the stock prices in a 10-year FCF growth rate far beyond what any base case in this filing set supports.

## 2. Business in plain English
Intel designs and (increasingly) contract-manufactures semiconductors: x86 CPUs for PCs and servers (Client Computing and Physical AI Group; Data Center and AI), and, through Intel Foundry, wafer fabrication capacity it is trying to sell to external chip customers to fill its own leading-edge fabs — a capital-intensive turnaround now financially backstopped by direct equity/warrant stakes from the U.S. government, Nvidia and SoftBank.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Triage's own words: *"Q2'26 showed the fastest revenue growth in 15 years and AI/data-center revenue +59%, a real inflection worth full diligence, but the business is still lossmaking and foundry execution risk is high, so this is a turnaround call, not a screen-and-buy."* I verify DCAI (Data Center and AI) revenue was $6.26B in Q2 2026, **up 59% YoY** — confirmed exactly against the primary release (*"Data Center and AI (DCAI) $6.3 billion up 59%"*). Total Intel Products (CCPG + DCAI) operating income was $4.82B on $15.1B revenue — a healthy ~32% segment operating margin — genuinely durable demand strength on the product side. The Foundry loss and the escrow-related GAAP loss are separate, real issues layered on top; none of this is an artefact of the factor model being fooled by a one-off — the growth is real, but so are the underlying losses.

**Why the GAAP net loss is so much larger than the operating loss implies:** the income statement shows Operating income $1,796M, but then "Interest and other, net" of **$(12,576)M**, driving Income before taxes to $(10,819)M and Net income (loss) attributable to Intel to $(11,033)M. Per Intel's 10-Q (period ended June 27, 2026), Note on Escrowed Shares: *"During the three and six months ended June 27, 2026, we recognized $12.5 billion and $13.6 billion, respectively, of losses related to the net change in fair value of both Escrowed Shares released and Escrowed Shares still held in escrow at June 27, 2026. The fair value of the Escrowed Shares derivative liability was $15.6 billion at June 27, 2026 and $2.7 billion at December 27, 2025."* This liability is tied to shares Intel owes the U.S. Department of Commerce under the CHIPS Act "Secure Enclave" agreement; **as Intel's stock price rose, the fair value of the shares Intel must deliver rose too, producing a large non-cash accounting loss** — the opposite intuition from a normal write-down. Entity: consolidated; source: the Note 4 "Escrowed Shares Issued to the U.S. Government" table in Intel's Form 10-Q, accession 0000050863-26-000157.

## 5. Guidance track record (quote-verified)
- **Q1 2026 release (Apr 23, 2026)**: *"Forecasting second-quarter 2026 revenue of $13.8 billion to $14.8 billion; expecting second-quarter EPS attributable to Intel of $0.08 and non-GAAP EPS attributable to Intel of $0.20."*
- **Actual Q2 2026 result vs. that guide:** revenue $16.1B **beat the high end of the $13.8-14.8B range by ~$1.3B**; non-GAAP EPS $0.42 beat the $0.20 guide; GAAP EPS $(2.16) missed the $0.08 guide badly, but purely because of the escrow revaluation described above, not an operating miss.
- **Q2 2026 release (Jul 23, 2026), guidance for Q3 2026**: *"Forecasting third-quarter 2026 revenue of $15.8 billion to $16.8 billion; expecting third-quarter EPS attributable to Intel of $0.31 and non-GAAP EPS attributable to Intel of $0.38."* This is guidance **raised** versus the trailing quarter's actual ($16.1B) at the midpoint ($16.3B), continuing the upward revenue trend, though the GAAP EPS guide of $0.31 assumes no further escrow-liability swing (a real forecasting risk given Intel's own stock-price sensitivity).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF / cash generation:** Q2 2026 operating cash flow $7.0B (per the release's news summary: *"In the second quarter, the company generated $7.0 billion in cash from operations"*) — strong, and not distorted by the escrow non-cash loss (which does not touch cash flow). Intel's capex remains very heavy (fab buildout), so free cash flow after capex is a materially smaller, and this pass did not isolate a precise capex figure for Q2 2026 — flagged as a gap; H1 2026 operating cash flow across both quarters was approximately $8.1B (Q1 $1.1B + Q2 $7.0B).
- **Balance sheet (consolidated, per 10-Q as of June 27, 2026 vs. December 27, 2025):** cash and cash equivalents $12,874M (down from $14,265M); short-term investments $16,853M (down from $23,151M) — combined liquid assets fell ~$7.7B in six months. Short-term debt $1,988M; long-term debt $48,549M (up from $44,086M) → total debt (short-term + long-term, summed directly from the 10-Q consolidated balance sheet, not a single XBRL "total debt" tag) ≈ $50.5B. Net debt ≈ $50.5B − $29.7B (cash+STI) ≈ **$20.8B**. Total Intel stockholders' equity fell from $114,281M to $87,542M in six months — a $26.7B equity decline, driven by the GAAP net losses (including the escrow revaluation) flowing through retained earnings, which dropped from $48,983M to $33,842M.
- **Government/strategic equity stakes:** the U.S. government holds warrants for up to 241 million shares at $20.00/share, exercisable only if Intel's ownership of its foundry business falls below 51% — a structural constraint on any future foundry divestiture or JV that reduces Intel's majority stake. Non-controlling interests on the balance sheet rose from $12,079M to $15,601M, consistent with minority stakes taken in Intel subsidiaries (Altera 49% sale in 2025, and other JV-type structures).
- **Litigation-related accruals (per web search cross-check of the 10-Q, to be verified against the primary document before final sizing):** approximately $1.0B accrued for VLSI-related litigation and $308M for an EC-imposed antitrust fine (originally €376M, reduced to €237M on appeal in December 2025). A Delaware Court of Chancery shareholder derivative suit was filed March 5, 2026 alleging the board breached fiduciary duty in approving the $11B U.S. government equity stake — this is a governance red flag tied directly to the same escrow mechanism driving the GAAP losses above.

## 7. Valuation

Reverse DCF (10-yr FCF CAGR *g*, WACC 10% given elevated execution and dilution risk, 2.5% terminal growth), close **$123.00** (Sept 25, 2026, secondary-source cross-check), ~5,043M shares issued and outstanding (consolidated 10-Q balance-sheet note) → market cap ≈ **$620B**; consolidated net debt ≈ $20.8B → **EV ≈ $641B**. Even generously assuming a normalized forward FCF base of $3.0B/yr (op. cash flow is strong but capex is enormous and true sustainable FCF is likely lower, possibly near breakeven given the Foundry losses), the required 10-year FCF CAGR to justify this EV **exceeds the 30% upper bound I tested and could not be bracketed** — i.e., the market is pricing an extraordinarily aggressive, multi-decade compounding turnaround from a currently small/uncertain FCF base. My bear/base/bull FCF-CAGR framing (base level, not the unresolved reverse-DCF rate): bear — Foundry losses persist, 18A external yield/ramp disappoints, FCF stays near zero for years; base — Products keep growing at a Client/DCAI blended high-single-digit to low-teens rate, Foundry loss narrows but doesn't turn a profit within the horizon; bull — 18A/14A yields ramp successfully, external foundry customers materialize at scale, Foundry breaks even by 2028. **Under any of these three scenarios, current price requires a base case far more optimistic than the "base" scenario above** — the valuation is not reconcilable with a base-case view, which is the addendum's explicit trigger for a WATCH rather than INCLUDE verdict regardless of how real the Q2 revenue inflection is.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) DCAI revenue +59% YoY and total Products operating margin ~32% show the core chip business is genuinely re-accelerating; (2) government, Nvidia and SoftBank equity/warrant commitments materially de-risk Intel's near-term funding for fab buildout; (3) Q3 2026 guidance of $15.8-16.8B revenue, if hit, would be a seventh consecutive quarter of beating or meeting raised expectations per management's own characterization.
**Bear:** (1) Intel Foundry lost $2.089B in Q2 2026 alone despite 31% revenue growth — the core turnaround thesis (external foundry customers at scale) remains unproven; (2) the escrowed-share derivative liability mechanically produces enormous, unpredictable non-cash GAAP losses as Intel's own stock price moves, adding a strange, reflexive volatility source to reported earnings that most factor models will not correctly price; (3) at ~$620B market cap the implied 10-year FCF growth requirement is far outside any evidence-based base case built from primary filings in this review.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Intel Foundry quarterly operating loss exceeds $2.5 billion for two consecutive quarters (vs. $2.089B in Q2 2026).
2. Consolidated net debt exceeds $30 billion at any quarter-end (vs. ≈$20.8B at Jun-26).
3. Q3 2026 revenue comes in below the guided $15.8B low end, i.e., a guidance miss rather than the pattern of beats/raises seen through Q2 2026.
4. The escrowed-share derivative liability produces a further single-quarter loss exceeding $5 billion, indicating the government-stake mechanism remains a large, uncontrolled source of reported-earnings volatility.
5. External (non-Intel) 18A/14A foundry customer revenue fails to appear as a disclosed, named line item within the next four quarters — the triage's own stated unproven-yield/ramp concern.

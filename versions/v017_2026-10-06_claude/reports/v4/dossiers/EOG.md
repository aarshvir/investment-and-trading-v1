# EOG Resources, Inc. (EOG) — Diligence Dossier

**Agent:** F15 (replaces F9, which hung before writing anything — this is a fresh review) | **As of:** 2026-09-25 close | **Price:** $140.35

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — thesis horizon **12–24 months** (shorter than the other two names in this batch because the near-term thesis is entangled with an unresolved geopolitical oil-price event).

EOG is a genuinely low-cost, disciplined operator (company-disclosed sub-$50/bbl WTI breakeven covering both its full capital program *and* its regular dividend) with a successfully integrating Encino/Utica acquisition. The named reservation, and the reason this is not a plain INCLUDE: **current TTM and even NTM earnings are elevated by a live, unresolved Iran/Strait-of-Hormuz war oil-price spike** (WTI briefly exceeded $110–112/bbl in April 2026 and was still ~$92–93/bbl at this review's cutoff, versus EOG's own realized ~$65–73/bbl in the year before the conflict began). "Cheap on 9x NTM P/E" is a fair headline but should not be read as cheap on a normalized, through-cycle basis. EOG is also now a named defendant in a fresh, unquantified shale-antitrust class action that survived a motion to dismiss three weeks before this review's cutoff.

## 2. Business in plain English

EOG Resources explores for and produces crude oil, natural gas liquids and natural gas from US shale plays (Delaware Basin/Permian, Eagle Ford, Utica, plus international exploration), selling hydrocarbons at prevailing market/benchmark-linked prices. It makes money on the spread between realized commodity prices and its (low) all-in cost to find, develop and produce a barrel. Its competitive position is cost leadership: EOG has spent over a decade emphasizing "premium" drilling returns and capital discipline over pure production growth, which shows up in its dividend/breakeven framework (section 7).

## 3. Why the model likes it

B1 live_rank 43 — the weakest quant rank of this batch's three names — reflects a much more modest V1 conviction score (0.64 vs ~0.94 for CVS/DVA) and a base case of only +9.4%/yr (vs +36% and +20%). This is the most "ordinary," least stretched of the three cases on its face. The quant signal here is standard value/quality (cheap multiple, strong balance sheet, resilient cash generation), not a turnaround story — durability depends almost entirely on where oil settles once the current geopolitical event resolves (section 7).

## 5. Guidance track record

EOG's practice is to give full-year capital and production guidance at the Q4/FY release and update pricing/operational detail each quarter; the specific FY2026 capex $-range and production-growth % were **not captured within this review's time box** (the Q4-2025/Q1-2026/Q3-2025 earnings-release exhibits reviewed contained detailed actuals but the forward capex table was in a section of the release not fully extracted). What is confirmed: the regular quarterly dividend was raised from **$0.9750/share (Q3 2025) to $1.0200/share**, and has been held at $1.0200 through Q1 and Q2 2026 (no cut, no special dividend disclosed in the releases reviewed). Buybacks stepped up sharply with the cash-flow windfall: $479M (Q3-25) → $418M (Q1-26) → **$1,299M (Q2-26)** — a disciplined choice to return the price-spike windfall to shareholders rather than accelerate drilling into a cost-inflated, event-driven price spike. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the 10-Q also shows the Board raised the repurchase authorization from $10bn to $20bn effective 20 May 2026, with $11.65bn remaining at 30 Jun 2026]**

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet

- **FCF:** Q2 2026 OCF $4,669M, FCF (non-GAAP, company-reported) $2,799M. TTM OCF ≈ $13.36bn (D3/V1).
- **Capex / reinvestment rate:** V1 flagged TTM capex as "missing/zero in D3, proxied with D&A" — resolved here with primary-source figures: FY2025 capex ≈ **$6.3bn**; Q2 2026 capex **$1,638M** **[Corrected 2026-10-06: $1,638M is additions to oil and gas properties; total capital expenditures (non-GAAP) were $1,587M]**. Using a ~$6.3–6.6bn TTM capex estimate against ~$13.4bn TTM OCF implies a **reinvestment rate in the high-40s-to-50% area**, consistent with EOG's own disclosed 45–70% framework across $55–80 WTI scenarios (source 6).
- **Balance sheet:** Total debt **$7,926M**, cash **$4,907M** (net debt ≈ $3.0bn per V1/D3) at Q2-2026 — a conservative balance sheet for the cash flow generated. New debt issued 2025-11-24: $750M of 4.400% notes due 2031 + $250M of 5.950% notes due 2055 (fungible with $500M issued July-2025), explicitly to **repay/redeem the 4.15% notes due 2026** — routine maturity management, not incremental leverage (source 7).
- **Capital return framework (company-disclosed):** WTI breakeven **below $50/bbl** covers the full capital program *and* the regular dividend; reinvestment rate 45–70% across a $55–80 WTI range; projected 12–20% ROCE and $10–18bn cumulative FCF 2026–2028 at $55–70 WTI (source 6).
- **Encino/Utica integration:** the ~$5.6bn acquisition (closed 2025) appears to be integrating well — well costs reported down ~20% from Encino's pre-acquisition run-rate, drilling/completion speed up double digits, activity levels "more than tripled" vs pre-acquisition, targeting ~$150M of synergies, and management/analyst commentary describes the asset as "outperforming the pre-acquisition playbook" (source 8, 9). This is a secondary-source characterization (trade press, analyst commentary), not independently verified against EOG's own quantified synergy disclosure.
- **Leadership:** CFO Ann Janssen (30-year company veteran, CFO since Jan-2024) is retiring; Jeff Hibbard (ex-Morgan Stanley energy banker, joined EOG Aug-2025) succeeds her **effective 2027-01-01** — an orderly, well-telegraphed transition, disclosed 2026-09-24 (source 10).

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull:**
1. Disciplined, self-disclosed sub-$50/bbl WTI breakeven covering the full capital program *and* dividend — real resilience if oil reverts to (or below) the pre-war $60s.
2. Encino/Utica integration evidence (well costs down ~20%, activity tripled, "outperforming the pre-acquisition playbook") suggests the ~$5.6bn deal is working, not a value-destroying scale-chase.
3. Very low beta (0.27) and modest V1 base case (+9.4%/yr) mean this name is not priced for perfection — less downside to a "disappointing but not disastrous" outcome than CVS or DVA.

**Bear:**
1. Current TTM/NTM earnings are inflated by an unresolved geopolitical event (Iran/Hormuz conflict, began 2026-02-28); a durable resolution that returns WTI to the $60s would mechanically compress reported earnings and could make the stock look "expensive" on the very same trailing multiple that looks cheap today.
2. EOG is now a named defendant (with Permian Resources, Expand Energy/Chesapeake, Continental Resources, Diamondback Energy, Hess, Occidental and Pioneer, plus former CEOs Scott Sheffield and John Hess) in a consolidated 54-count antitrust class action alleging coordinated production restraint; a New Mexico federal judge (Hon. Matthew L. Garcia) denied most of the defendants' motion to dismiss on 2026-08-31, allowing the core conspiracy claims to proceed (source 13). Exposure is entirely unquantified at this stage.
3. CFO transition (effective Jan-2027), while orderly, removes a long-tenured (30-year) finance leader during a period when commodity-price and capital-allocation judgment calls matter more than usual.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)

1. WTI averages **below $50/bbl for two consecutive quarters** *and* the regular dividend is cut (the direct test of the company's own disclosed breakeven claim).
2. Full-year capex guidance is **raised without a corresponding production-guidance increase**, or the reinvestment rate (capex/OCF) exceeds **70%** outside a low-price justification.
3. The shale-antitrust litigation (*In re Shale Oil Antitrust Litigation*, D.N.M.) reaches **class certification** or a quantified potential-damages disclosure appears in a 10-Q/10-K.
4. Encino/Utica well costs stop improving versus the pre-acquisition run-rate, or the ~$150M disclosed synergy target is abandoned/missed.
5. Total debt rises **above $12bn** without a corresponding production or proved-reserve increase (a sign buybacks are being funded by leverage rather than cash flow, unlike the current conservative balance sheet).

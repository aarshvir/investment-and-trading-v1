# Apple Inc. (AAPL) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Verdict: INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight) — 12–36 month thesis horizon. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: verdict changes to WATCH: implied growth is above the base case, which fails the INCLUDE/INCLUDE-SMALL rule; see Correction DV01]**
One-sentence reason: a 2.5-billion-device installed base is monetizing better than ever (Services at a record run-rate, gross margin at an 8-quarter high), but the current iPhone-17 supercycle plus Services re-acceleration has pushed the price to imply more growth than this analyst's steady-state base case, so it is a hold-with-a-reservation, not a full-conviction add.

## 1. Business in plain English
Apple sells premium hardware (iPhone ~50% of revenue, Mac/iPad/Wearables the rest) to a large, loyal, high-income-skewed installed base, then monetizes that base through Services (App Store, subscriptions, advertising, Apple Care, licensing — notably Google's payment for default search placement). The moat is the hardware-software-services ecosystem and switching costs, not any single product's technical superiority. Growth increasingly comes from Services (higher margin, more durable) rather than hardware unit growth.

## 5. Moat and competition
- **Installed base:** >2.5 billion active devices, up from 2.35 billion a year earlier (≈6.4% YoY growth) — the base that Services monetizes; management called it "a new all-time high...across all major product categories and geographic segments." [1]
- **Services:** FY2025 Services revenue $109.16B — the first fiscal year Services exceeded $100B; Q1 FY26 Services hit a record $30.0B, +14% YoY. Services carries materially higher gross margin than products and is the more durable growth engine.
- **China — share recovering, but competitively contested:** Apple held 18.1% of China's smartphone market in Q2 2026 (up from 13.9% a year earlier, +24.9% YoY shipment growth — one of only two top-10 vendors growing), but **Huawei leads with 22.6% share**, "the highest in five years." Apple used early price-hike warnings to pull forward iPhone-17 upgrades while holding its own price, a tactic that will not repeat indefinitely. [4]
- **Regulatory:** Apple opened to third-party payments/app stores in the EU under the DMA; in China, 48 iOS developers petitioned SAMR for an antitrust investigation into App Store commissions (Jun-2026), and Apple pre-emptively cut its standard China App Store commission from 30% to 25% (Mar-2026) — a real, filed reduction in take-rate in at least one major market, with more regulatory pressure visible elsewhere. [4]
- **Apple Intelligence / AI features:** sources reference "a more personalized Siri coming this year" — i.e., Apple's flagship on-device AI feature was still pending/rolling out as of the cutoff, later than competitors' generative-AI assistants; this is a genuine execution-timeline risk to the "AI upgrade cycle" bull thesis, not yet a proof point. [1]

## 8. Bull case
1. Gross margin at an 8-quarter high (50.0%) and rising, on filed numbers, not adjusted metrics.
2. Installed base still growing (~6.4% YoY) at 2.5B+ devices — the monetization base for Services keeps expanding even if hardware unit growth is modest.
3. Services just crossed $100B/year (FY2025) and continues compounding at double digits, a higher-margin, more durable revenue stream than hardware.

## 9. Bear case
1. Huawei leads China smartphone share (22.6% vs. Apple's 18.1%) and Apple's recent China growth was partly a price-hike-avoidance pull-forward, not necessarily sustainable.
2. Regulatory pressure on App Store take-rates is spreading (EU DMA already binding; China commission already cut 30%→25%; a SAMR antitrust probe has been requested) — a structural risk to Services' superior margin.
3. Apple Intelligence/Siri AI features were still described as "coming" as of the cutoff — behind several competitors' shipped generative-AI assistants, a real execution risk to the AI-upgrade-cycle bull case.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria — thesis-invalidation triggers (what would change the verdict / break the thesis)
1. China smartphone market share falls back below 15% for two consecutive quarters.
2. Services revenue growth decelerates below 8% YoY for two consecutive quarters.
3. Gross margin falls below 45% for two consecutive quarters.
4. A regulatory action cuts Apple's global (not just EU/China) App Store take-rate by more than 5 points.
5. Installed-base active-device growth decelerates below 4% YoY.

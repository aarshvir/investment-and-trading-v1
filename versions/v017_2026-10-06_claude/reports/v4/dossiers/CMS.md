# CMS — CMS Energy Corp. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F40, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight), reservation stated explicitly: Moody's moved the outlook on CMS/Consumers Energy to **negative** (from stable) over the size of a $24bn five-year capital plan relative to regulatory cost-recovery timing, and the balance sheet is securitization-heavy, complicating a clean leverage read **[Corrected 2026-10-06: securitization debt is only $525M of about $19.3bn total debt (2.7%); consolidated leverage is about 6.2x net debt/EBITDA]**. Valuation is not demanding and the earnings-guidance track record is strong. Horizon: 24–48 months (regulated utility — this is a rate-of-return/dividend-growth thesis, not a re-rating trade).

## 2. Business in plain English
CMS Energy is the holding company for Consumers Energy, Michigan's largest combined electric-and-gas regulated utility, serving ~6.7M of the state's residents. It earns a regulated return on the capital it invests in generation, transmission and distribution, set by the Michigan Public Service Commission (MPSC). It is one of the more purely "regulated-utility" names in its peer set (minimal unregulated/merchant exposure since NorthStar Clean Energy is a small renewables developer segment).

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Triage flagged CMS for a "cheap NTM P/E (15.4x) for above-average guided growth" and a multi-year 6–8% EPS-growth record. That growth record is **durable and verified**: 2024 adjusted EPS $3.34, 2025 adjusted EPS **$3.61** (beat the $3.54–3.60 guide range — the 22nd/23rd consecutive year of meeting-or-beating guidance per company messaging), 2026 guide **$3.83–3.90** (raised twice from an initial $3.80–3.87) **[Corrected 2026-10-06: raised once (5 Feb 2026), then reaffirmed in April and July]**, and a newly introduced 2027 guide of **$4.08–4.17** (+6.7% at the midpoint) — all consistent with the reaffirmed 6–8% long-term algorithm. The one thing that looks like an artefact at first glance is the **Q2-2026 earnings "miss"**: adjusted EPS of $0.37 vs. $0.71 a year earlier and vs. a ~$0.76 consensus (a >50% shortfall). Management's own bridge (Q2-26 earnings call) attributes it to items **already contemplated in the 2026 plan**: an $0.08 unfavourable weather comparison, $0.19 of storm-related O&M, and $0.23 from non-repeating 2025 liability-management benefits — and the company **reaffirmed** full-year guidance the same day. I read this as a real quarterly print, correctly explained, not a data conflict with the multi-year growth thesis — but it is a reminder that CMS's quarters are lumpy and headline "misses" will recur.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) 20-year unbroken dividend-growth streak and a guidance record that has never been cut in the window checked; (2) FY27 guide (+6.7% at midpoint) shows the growth algorithm rolling forward intact; (3) valuation (16.3x NTM P/E, ~5.2% implied growth) does not demand anything beyond the guided range.
**Bear:** (1) Moody's negative outlook is a genuine forward-looking risk — if the $24bn capital plan outruns rate-case recovery, the credit profile (and eventually the equity story) could deteriorate; (2) securitization-heavy leverage makes headline debt ratios hard to compare to peers and could mask true balance-sheet risk; (3) Q2-26 shows how volatile quarterly prints can be (weather/storm/comp timing) even when the full-year plan is intact — a headline-risk name for anyone who reacts to single quarters.

## 9. Kill criteria
1. Moody's or S&P actually downgrades CMS Energy or Consumers Energy (current status: negative outlook only, ratings affirmed).
2. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($3.83–3.90) is cut mid-year (reaffirmed twice through Q2-26 to date).
3. An MPSC rate-case order disallows a material (>10%) share of pending capital-recovery requests.
4. Net debt continues rising without a matching credit-rating cushion or explicit securitization pass-through disclosure.
5. Adjusted EPS growth falls outside the reaffirmed 6–8% long-term range for two consecutive fiscal years.

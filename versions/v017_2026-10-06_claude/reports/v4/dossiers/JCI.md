# JCI — Johnson Controls International — Diligence Dossier (Agent F87, Wave 5)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL.** Real, broad-based demand acceleration (not just data centers) with a clean balance sheet and
strong cash conversion; reservation: the current price already needs several more years of above-trend growth to
look cheap, and roughly a third of the recent EPS growth reflects margin/mix and a divestiture-driven prior-year
comp rather than pure volume. Horizon: 12–36 months.

## 2. Business in plain English
Johnson Controls makes and services the systems that heat, cool, ventilate, secure and fire-protect buildings —
HVAC equipment, building-automation software, fire/security systems — sold to commercial building owners,
industrial customers and increasingly data-center operators who need precision cooling for AI server racks. It
earns money on equipment sales plus a growing recurring-service/software tail, competing with Carrier, Trane and
Honeywell.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Data-center thermal management is a genuine structural tailwind layered on a normal-cycle building
products business, evidenced by a record backlog (~$20–21B) and +27% orders. (2) Balance sheet is clean
(consolidated net debt/EBITDA ~2.2x) with FCF funding buybacks, not leverage. (3) Portfolio simplification (residential/
fire-and-security exit) should raise the remaining business's margin profile and reduce complexity.

**Bear:** (1) FY25's reported EPS growth was flattered by a one-time divestiture gain — investors extrapolating
the headline number risk overpaying for growth that won't repeat. (2) Data-center HVAC is now a crowded,
capital-intensive race (Carrier, Trane, Vertiv, Schneider all chasing the same demand) — backlog could convert at
lower margins than hoped if pricing competition intensifies. (3) A slowdown in AI-data-center capex (the same
cyclical risk flagged for KEYS) would hit the fastest-growing part of JCI's order book simultaneously.

## 9. Kill criteria (measurable)
1. Quarterly net sales growth decelerates below **+5% YoY** for two consecutive quarters.
2. Backlog growth turns negative YoY (vs. the current record ~$20–21B level) for two consecutive quarters.
3. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above **3.0x** (current estimate ~2.2x).
4. Continuing-operations diluted EPS growth YoY falls below **+15%** for two consecutive quarters.
5. FCF/NI (continuing operations) conversion falls below **90%** for two consecutive quarters (signals
   working-capital deterioration behind the growth).

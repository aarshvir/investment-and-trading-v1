# ICE — Intercontinental Exchange, Inc.

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (12–36 month horizon). Reservation: leverage from the Black Knight deal is still elevated and management just announced a second large, debt-funded acquisition (MarketAxess, $5.7bn) — integration/deleveraging execution, not the underlying exchange/data franchise, is the swing factor.

## 2. Business in plain English
ICE runs financial exchanges (ICE futures/energy/ags, and NYSE — the world's largest listing venue), sells fixed-income pricing/reference data and analytics, and — since the 2023 Black Knight deal — operates a mortgage-technology platform (MERS, servicing/origination software) tied to the US mortgage industry. It earns a mix of transaction fees (cyclical, tied to trading volumes/volatility) and subscription/recurring data and software revenue (sticky, contracted).

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
b1 shows solid quality/value blend (ROE 13.7%, EBIT/EV percentile 43%) with a still-attractive multiple versus the exchange peer group per the original triage (17.9x forward at the time). The durable driver is the recurring data/fixed-income and mortgage-tech subscription base (management raised FY26 recurring-revenue guidance for Fixed Income & Data Services to 7–8% growth); the less durable/cyclical driver is transaction revenue, which moves with volatility and mortgage origination volumes.

2Q26 release: management raised full-year recurring revenue guidance for Fixed Income & Data Services to 7–8% (up from a prior, lower range per the transcript — exact prior figures not independently re-verified from the press release PDF in the time box; flagged as a to-verify item). ## 6. Balance sheet, leverage, and pending deal
Consolidated basis (10-Q Jun-30-26): cash $1.07bn; stockholders' equity $29.55bn; long-term debt (noncurrent) $18.63bn — up from $17.36bn a year earlier, consistent with the triage's flagged 2.9x net debt/EBITDA from the 2023 Black Knight acquisition. **New and material:** in the Q2 2026 release/call, ICE announced a **$5.7bn acquisition of MarketAxess**, described as immediately EPS-accretive with ~$100m of targeted synergies within three years — this is a pending deal not reflected in the balance-sheet figures above and adds further leverage/integration risk on top of Black Knight, which is still being digested.

Implied (5.3%) sits **below** my base case → dossier view: **cheap-to-fair**, consistent with INCLUDE-SMALL, but the reservation is that two large, debt-funded deals stacked on top of each other raise the odds of an integration stumble or a leverage-driven de-rating before that growth shows up.

## 8. Bull case
1. Recurring Fixed Income & Data Services growth guided up to 7–8%, showing the subscription base is accelerating, not just holding.
2. NYSE listings franchise and futures/energy complex give a durable, high-margin transaction-revenue base with structural growth (energy transition hedging, index/derivatives volumes).
3. Black Knight (mortgage tech) is showing its best pro forma performance since H1 2022 — the acquisition thesis (digitizing mortgage origination/servicing) is starting to show in the numbers rather than remaining a promise.

## 9. Bear case
1. Two large debt-funded deals in three years (Black Knight 2023, MarketAxess 2026 pending) keep consolidated leverage structurally elevated; consolidated net debt/EBITDA was already ~2.9x before MarketAxess.
2. Transaction revenue (a meaningful share of the mix) is volatility- and volume-dependent; a low-volatility regime would show up quickly in quarterly results.
3. MarketAxess is a regulatory-approval-dependent pending deal — antitrust or timing risk is real given ICE's scale in fixed-income trading infrastructure.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated) rises above 3.5x following the MarketAxess close.
2. Fixed Income & Data Services recurring revenue growth falls below 5% for two consecutive quarters (vs the 7–8% FY26 guide).
3. MarketAxess deal fails to close, is renegotiated at worse terms, or faces a material regulatory objection within 12 months of announcement.
4. GAAP diluted EPS ex-one-offs declines YoY for two consecutive quarters.
5. Mortgage-tech (Black Knight) segment net revenue growth turns negative for two consecutive quarters (a proxy for a mortgage-origination-cycle downturn hitting the newest, most leveraged part of the business).

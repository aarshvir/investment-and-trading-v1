## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 24–36 months. Named reservation: consolidated leverage is
meaningfully higher than the regulated-utility parts of the business alone would justify, and the core regulated
electric segment has twice in a row recovered materially less than it asked for from its regulator — real execution
risk sits underneath the headline growth story. The offsetting case: DTE has reaffirmed (not cut) its 6–8%
long-term operating-EPS growth framework, has 2.4GW+ of *executed*, MPSC-approved data-center supply contracts with
strong take-or-pay protection (not just a pipeline announcement), and the current price does not appear to be
paying for the top of that growth range.

## 2. Business in plain English
DTE Energy is a Michigan-based utility holding company: **DTE Electric** (regulated, ~2.3 million customers,
Southeast Michigan) and **DTE Gas** (regulated, ~1.4 million customers) earn a regulator-set return on their asset
base; **DTE Vantage** (non-utility gas storage, pipelines, renewable natural gas, industrial energy) and **Energy
Trading** are unregulated, market-exposed businesses layered on top (source: Q2 2026 earnings release, 8-K Ex.99.1,
filed 2026-07-28, "About DTE Energy"). The two halves have structurally different risk/return profiles and must not
be blended into one consolidated ratio (utilities sector playbook: regulated vs merchant/non-utility split).

## 8. Bull case
1. **2.4GW of executed, MPSC-approved data-center supply agreements** (Oracle ~1.4GW + Google, per WebSearch of
   MPSC/trade-press coverage) structured as long-term (19-year) primary supply agreements with the customer paying
   for **at least 80%** of contracted demand regardless of actual usage — real downside/take-or-pay protection
   against the classic utility risk of building capacity for a load that doesn't show up.
2. Management is confident enough in the plan to offer a **two-year rate-increase freeze** (no new DTE Electric
   rate case filing until at least 2028) conditional on the first data-center project coming online as planned by
   end-2027 — a credible, falsifiable commitment tied to a specific near-term date.
3. Reaffirmed (not cut) FY2026 operating EPS guidance and 6–8%/yr long-term framework despite a below-ask 2025 rate
   case outcome — management is not walking back the growth story even after a real regulatory setback.

## 9. Bear case
1. DTE Electric — the core, supposedly-stable regulated segment — posted **declining** operating EPS YoY in Q2 2026
   ($1.30 vs $1.53, −15%), the direct result of the MPSC approving only 42% of the requested 2025 rate increase and
   holding ROE flat; the pending 2026 rate case (filed Feb 2026, requesting $474m) **[Corrected 2026-10-06: filed 28 Apr 2026]** carries real execution risk that
   it repeats this pattern.
2. Consolidated leverage is concentrated outside the regulated utility (≈$12.2bn of $25.9bn consolidated debt sits
   at DTE Gas/Vantage/Trading/parent, per the entity-scope breakdown in §6) — this is a materially riskier capital
   structure than "DTE Electric's own 13.7bn" would suggest, and the triage-flagged 7.7x consolidated net-debt/
   EBITDA figure, while not independently re-derived here in full, is directionally corroborated.
3. The data-center growth story carries real regulatory/political risk: press coverage found in this review
   (Engineering News-Record, "Power Contracts Face Appeals"; Planet Detroit, "Nessel calls it 'ransom note'")
   indicates the MPSC-approved contracts and DTE's rate-freeze offer are contested and under appeal — the growth
   catalyst is not yet fully de-risked.

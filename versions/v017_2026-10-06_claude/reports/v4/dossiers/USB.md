# U.S. Bancorp (NYSE: USB) — Diligence Dossier
Agent F71 | Standard depth | As of 2026-09-25 close ($59.33/share, mkt cap ≈ $92.3bn, consolidated)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** — 24–36 month horizon. A diversified super-regional bank (parent of U.S. Bank National Association)
with a genuinely strong and *improving* Q2 2026 quarter — record net revenue, positive operating leverage of 400
bps, rising ROTCE, improving efficiency ratio, improving credit quality, and stable capital — trading at a trailing
P/E and P/TBV that price in essentially no further growth. This is the strongest of the three names in this batch:
the fundamentals are getting better each quarter, not just cheap on a stale multiple.

## 2. Business in plain English
U.S. Bancorp is the parent of U.S. Bank, a super-regional commercial and consumer bank (deposits, commercial and
consumer lending, wealth management/trust), plus a large payments franchise (Elavon merchant acquiring/processing)
and, since 1-Jun-2026, an equity capital-markets and institutional brokerage arm (BTIG, newly acquired). It earns
net interest income on the spread between loans/securities and deposits, plus a large and growing stream of fee
income from payments, wealth management, capital markets, mortgage banking and other services — a more
fee-diversified model than a typical regional bank, which the model's quality score picks up as scale/moat in
payments (Elavon).

## 7. Valuation snapshot (no V1 row exists for USB; v1_verdict = null)
Price $59.33 (2026-09-25 close, matches b1_live_scores.csv). Diluted shares ≈1,555m (Q2 2026 10-Q) ⇒ market cap
≈$92.3bn (b1 `mktcap` $92.44bn — cross-checked, matches). TTM diluted EPS (Q3'25 $1.22 + Q4'25 $1.26 + Q1'26 $1.18
+ Q2'26 $1.35) = **$5.01** → **trailing P/E ≈11.8x**. Book value/share $38.91 ⇒ **P/B ≈1.53x**; tangible book
value/share $30.04 ⇒ **P/TBV ≈1.98x**.

**Reverse valuation** (Gordon-growth-on-excess-returns, the bank-appropriate method per the sector playbook — a DCF
on levered bank cash flow is not meaningful): P/TBV = 1 + (ROTCE − r)/(r − g). Solving for the growth rate g the
current P/TBV (1.98x) implies, using the reported Q2 2026 ROTCE (18.7%) and a cost of equity r = 9.5%–10.0% (typical
super-regional-bank estimate; not independently re-derived via CAPM at Standard depth): **implied long-run growth
g ≈ 0%–2.3%**. My evidence-based base case — ROTCE sustaining in the high-teens (per the bank's own medium-term
target, already being met/exceeded) with roughly 40–50% of earnings retained after dividends/buybacks — implies
**tangible-book-value-per-share growth capacity of roughly 6%–9%/year**, materially **above** the ≈0–2% the price
implies. **implied_vs_base = "below"** — the market is pricing USB as close to a no-growth bank despite
demonstrably improving returns, which is the core of the INCLUDE case. **[Corrected 6 Oct 2026: the 0-2.3% implied growth used a cost of equity of 9.5-10.0% that does not match a 10-year Treasury of 5.17% (FRED DGS10, 25 Sep 2026; 4.44% on 30 Jun 2026), and the 6-9% "base case" is not a perpetual rate (it exceeds long-run nominal growth and the loan growth that keeps CET1 near the ~10% target). Recomputed: cost of equity 10.4% (5.17% + beta 1.05 x ERP 5.0%; beta assumed), P/TBV 1.975x. Implied g is 1.9% at ROTCE 18.7%, 3.1% at 17.5% and 4.7% at 16.0%; against a base of ROTCE 17.5% and g 4%, fair P/TBV is 2.11x (+7% versus price; 1.93x, -2%, at a cost of equity of 11%). Implied vs base is in line, not below, and the verdict is now INCLUDE-SMALL. The 10-year move since 30 Jun (+73 bp) also lowers AOCI on the $89.1bn available-for-sale book; see the Re-assessment (RA5) section]**

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull (3 points):**
1. Five straight quarters of improving NIM, efficiency ratio and (with one Q1-2026 dip) ROTCE — a genuine,
   multi-quarter positive-operating-leverage trend, not a single good print.
2. Credit quality is improving (NCO ratio down to 0.53%) even as loan growth accelerates (+7.1% YoY) — the bank is
   growing without visibly loosening underwriting on this metric.
3. Priced at ~11.8x trailing EPS and ~2.0x tangible book for an 18%+ ROTCE bank — cheap on both an absolute and a
   sector-relative basis, per the reverse-valuation read above.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Average loan growth (+7.1% YoY) is outpacing average deposit growth (+2.4% YoY); if this gap widens, funding
   costs could pressure the NIM expansion that is currently driving the earnings beat.
2. The BTIG acquisition (effective 1-Jun-2026) adds capital-markets/brokerage revenue that is more cyclical and
   market-dependent than core banking fee income — a first strong quarter is not proof of a full-cycle track
   record.
3. Historical CFPB consent orders (sham-accounts practices, $37.5m penalty, and COVID-era ReliaCard unemployment-
   benefit freezes, $20.7m penalty) — both were only terminated by the regulator in Aug/Sep 2025 — indicate a
   pattern of consumer-compliance lapses in the recent past, even though both matters are now formally closed.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Basel III standardized CET1 ratio falling below 9.5% (from 10.8% currently; per quarterly earnings releases /
   10-Q regulatory-capital disclosures, consolidated).
2. Net charge-off ratio rising above 0.75% for two consecutive quarters (from 0.53% currently; per earnings
   releases, consolidated).
3. Efficiency ratio rising back above 60% for two consecutive quarters (from 57.1% currently; per earnings
   releases, consolidated, non-GAAP as defined by the company).
4. Net interest margin compressing more than 15 bps YoY in any single quarter (currently +13 bps YoY; per earnings
   releases, taxable-equivalent basis, consolidated).
5. A new material regulatory enforcement action (CFPB, OCC or Federal Reserve) carrying a fine or consent order
   exceeding $50m.

Global insurance broker/HR-consultant delivering genuine, primary-source-confirmed margin expansion and mid-teens adjusted EPS growth, still priced for roughly 4%/yr — well below even a conservative base case. One correction to the triage: the "41.5% EPS growth" claim in the triage card was **not found in any primary source reviewed**; actual adjusted-EPS growth has run +19% (Q1'26), +17% (Q2'26) and +5% (FY2025) — strong, but not 41.5% (see §5, data conflict).

## 2. Business in plain English
WTW advises and brokers insurance/risk (Risk & Broking segment) and runs employee-benefits, retirement, and HR-consulting services (Health, Wealth & Career segment) for corporate clients worldwide. It earns commissions/fees, not underwriting risk itself (it is a broker/advisor, not an insurer). Its 2020–21 attempted $30bn merger with Aon collapsed under antitrust pressure; WTW has spent the years since on margin-improvement and capital return, and recently kicked off a new multi-year AI/automation program ("Propel," 2026–2028).

## 8. Bull case
1. Real, multi-quarter margin expansion confirmed in primary filings: adjusted EBITDA margin 20.8% (Q2'25) → 21.5% (Q2'26); operating margin +30bps in the Risk & Broking segment even as consolidated GAAP margin dipped (§6). **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the +30bps belongs to Health, Wealth & Career; Risk & Broking margin rose +100bps to 22.2%]**
2. A specific, capital-committed, dated efficiency program (Propel, ~$625M invested 2026–2028) targets ~30% adjusted operating margin by 2028, up from ~21–22% today — if even half-delivered, this is a large EPS tailwind on top of organic growth.
3. Capital return is accelerating, not just maintained: buyback authority increased by $1.5bn on 2026-07-30, with 2026 guidance of "$1.0B or greater" in repurchases.

## 9. Bear case
1. GAAP diluted EPS fell 27% YoY in Q2 2026 even as adjusted EPS rose 17% — a widening GAAP/adjusted gap that was not fully explained at standard depth; if the excluded items recur or reflect a real deterioration (e.g., higher restructuring or Propel-related charges becoming a permanent feature), the "adjusted" growth story is less trustworthy than the headline suggests.
2. Propel is a large, multi-year cash commitment (~$625M through 2028) with execution risk typical of "transformation program" plans — WTW's own prior "multi-year operational transformation program" is referenced as "recently-completed" in risk-factor language, i.e., this would be at least the second such program in a few years, raising the question of whether promised savings from the first one fully materialized.
3. Total debt rose from ~$4.76bn (Sep 2025) to ~$6.53bn (Jun 2026), a jump largely concentrated in Q4 2025 — a new bond issuance not yet fully explained at standard depth; net debt/EBITDA at ~2.2x is still reasonable, but the trend (rising, not falling) is the opposite of WDC's and WFC's deleveraging stories in this batch.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Consolidated organic revenue growth (as reported, quarterly) falls below **3%** for two consecutive quarters (vs. 4–6% in the last four quarters).
2. Adjusted EBITDA margin (consolidated, quarterly) fails to expand YoY for two consecutive quarters (vs. +30–70bps in each of the last four quarters) — Propel thesis breaking.
3. Net debt/EBITDA (consolidated) rises above **3.0x** (vs. ~2.2x now) without a specific, disclosed acquisition rationale.
4. Propel cumulative cash cost (disclosed each quarter going forward) exceeds the original ~$625M budget by more than 25% before FY2027, without a commensurate upgrade to the $350M net-savings target.
5. GAAP-vs-adjusted diluted EPS gap (quarterly) widens for two more consecutive quarters beyond the -27%/+17% divergence seen in Q2 2026, without clear one-time-item disclosure.

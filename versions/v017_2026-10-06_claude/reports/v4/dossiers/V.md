# Visa Inc. (V) — Fundamental Diligence Dossier

**Verdict: INCLUDE** — 24–48 month thesis horizon.
One-sentence reason: the highest-quality payments network in the batch (51% net margin, net-cash-like leverage, mid-teens EPS growth reaffirmed and raised twice this fiscal year) trades at a price that a reverse DCF says requires only ~9% 10-year FCF growth — below this analyst's 13% base case — even after netting out a large, real, recurring interchange-litigation drag.

## 1. Business in plain English
Visa operates the world's largest card-payment network (VisaNet), connecting banks, merchants and consumers; it does not lend or take credit risk itself (that sits with issuing banks). Revenue is a small, growing take-rate on global consumer/commercial spend (service and data-processing fees) plus incentive-netted assessments, so nominal GDP + card-share-of-spend + cross-border travel/e-commerce are the underlying volume drivers. The moat is network effects (merchants accept Visa because consumers carry it; consumers carry it because merchants accept it) plus decades of processing-security infrastructure that is expensive to replicate.

## 5. Moat, competitive position and secular drivers
- Non-lending network model: Visa carries no consumer credit risk, so credit-cycle stress hits issuer banks, not Visa's income statement directly (though it can slow volume growth in a downturn).
- Cross-border volume (ex.-intra-Europe) grew 11–12% in each of the last three quarters — a higher-take-rate segment than domestic, and the main reason margin has stayed >50%.
- New growth vectors disclosed in filings/calls: Visa Stablecoin Platform (stablecoin minting/movement/management), an "Open Standard" partnership for OpenUSD issuance, tokenized-deposit work with Pismo, and an OpenAI partnership to enable Visa payments inside agentic-commerce flows. These are optionality, not yet revenue lines — treated as bull-case items, not base-case drivers.
- Competitive risk: Mastercard is the direct scaled competitor; real-time/account-to-account rails (FedNow, Pix-style schemes, stablecoins) and large-merchant surcharging (post-MDL settlement, see §6) are the structural threats to take-rate over a 10-year horizon.

## 8. Bull case
1. Two consecutive guidance raises within FY2026 (Apr and Jul 2026 calls) on real, filed acceleration in quarterly revenue growth (12%→17%).
2. Reverse-DCF implied growth (8.9%) sits below both management's own guide and this analyst's base case (13%) — the stock is not priced for its own guidance to hold.
3. Net-debt/EBITDA under 0.4x with $28.4B of buyback authorization remaining — capital return is a durable, self-funded EPS tailwind independent of volume growth.

## 9. Bear case
1. Interchange MDL litigation costs have been large and recurring: $2.5B provisioned in FY2025 alone, plus $707M (FQ1 FY26), $311M (FQ2 FY26) and $237M (FQ3 FY26) — over $3.7B added across the last five reported quarters **[Corrected 2026-10-06: the $3.7B is FQ1 FY25 to FQ3 FY26, seven quarters; the last four quarters total about $1.7B (FQ4 FY25 $434M + $707M + $311M + $237M)]**, a real and growing (not one-off) drag on reported margins.
2. Real-time account-to-account rails and stablecoin payment rails are a genuine long-run disintermediation threat to card interchange economics — the same secular risk the company itself is now hedging via its own Stablecoin Platform.
3. FQ3 FY26 revenue growth (14%) decelerated from FQ2's 17% — a single data point, but worth watching for whether the FQ2 print (aided by FIFA World Cup-related value-added-services demand per management) was a temporary pull-forward.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria
1. Net revenue growth falls below 8% YoY (constant-currency) for two consecutive fiscal quarters.
2. Quarterly interchange-litigation provisions exceed $500M in any single quarter (vs. $237–707M range seen FQ1–FQ3 FY26), signalling the MDL settlement is deteriorating rather than resolving.
3. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 1.5x (from ~0.4x today) without a offsetting acquisition-driven earnings step-up.
4. Non-GAAP diluted EPS growth guidance is cut (vs. today's "low end of mid-teens" for FY2026) at any of the next two quarterly calls.
5. Cross-border volume growth (ex.-intra-Europe) falls below 6% YoY for two consecutive quarters (from 11–12% today) — the highest-margin volume line decelerating structurally.

## 11. Catalysts & calendar
- Next earnings: fiscal Q4/full-year FY2026, **estimated late October 2026 (not yet confirmed as of this cutoff)** — FY2025's equivalent release was 28-Oct-2025.
- Interchange MDL: the district court granted **preliminary approval of the amended/superseding settlement agreement in June 2026** (the agreement itself is dated 10-Nov-2025); final approval and the 90-day implementation window for merchant-surcharging changes are the next milestones to watch — a real catalyst/risk date, not yet reached as of 25-Sep-2026.

## 12. Red-flag scan
- **Litigation (the primary red flag):** the interchange multidistrict litigation (In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation, E.D.N.Y.) is decades-old; Visa entered a superseding amended settlement agreement dated **10-Nov-2025**, which received **preliminary court approval in June 2026** (10bps reduction in the US combined average effective credit interchange rate for five years, expanded merchant surcharging rights). This is disclosed and provisioned for in the earnings releases cited in §9 — a known, sized, recurring cost, not a surprise, but large enough (>$3.7B across five quarters) to matter to margin trend.
- No auditor changes, restatements, going-concern language, or SEC/DOJ investigations beyond the above found in sources reviewed.
- Insider-selling pattern not independently re-checked at Form-4 level this pass (time-boxed); flagged as unverified rather than asserted clean.
- **Data conflict:** `b1_live_scores.csv` shows V with a blank `mktcap`/`composite`/`live_rank` despite being a >$650B constituent — a data-pipeline gap, reported to the quant team, not evidence of any fundamental issue.

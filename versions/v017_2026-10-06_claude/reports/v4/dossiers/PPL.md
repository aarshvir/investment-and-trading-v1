# PPL — PPL Corporation

## 1. Verdict

**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon: 12–36 months. Reservation: the growth thesis is real, primary-source-verified, and currently ahead of schedule (Section 4–5), but it is financed by a rapid, debt-funded capex ramp (long-term debt +$1.8bn in one quarter) against structurally negative free cash flow; the multi-gigawatt data-center pipeline is mostly signed-agreement-stage rather than in-service, and the largest incremental opportunity (the Invitium Energy generation JV) is explicitly excluded from current guidance and is a 2029–2032 story, not a near-term one. Full weight is not warranted until the financing plan and at least one more data-center in-service milestone are confirmed.

## 2. Business in plain English

PPL Corporation owns three regulated, wires-and-pipes utilities: PPL Electric Utilities (Pennsylvania transmission and distribution), Louisville Gas & Electric and Kentucky Utilities (Kentucky regulated electric and gas, including generation), and Rhode Island Energy (electric and gas, acquired 2024). All three earn a regulator-approved return on their asset base; PPL does not sell into a competitive retail market. The current investment story is a wave of data-center and other large-load demand in Pennsylvania and Kentucky that is driving a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar transmission/generation build-out, funded through customer-protected tariffs designed so new large loads — not existing residential/commercial customers — pay for the infrastructure built to serve them.

## 8. Bull case / bear case

**Bull (3 points):**
1. Results are beating the "reaffirmed" framing: GAAP EPS grew 20% YoY in Q2 2026 even as guidance language stayed at "reaffirmed," and management explicitly expects "stronger earnings growth in the second half of 2026" and "beginning in 2027" — a self-described acceleration, not a plateau.
2. The signed (not merely pipeline) data-center backlog — >11 GW in Pennsylvania, 1.3 GW in Kentucky — is large relative to PPL's existing ~46B-asset base and is protected by regulator-approved large-load tariffs that shift infrastructure cost to the new customers, reducing the risk that existing ratepayers (and therefore regulators) push back.
3. The Invitium Energy generation JV (up to $12.5–15bn of turbine-related investment opportunity at the JV level alone) is a real option not yet priced into the 6–8% base guidance, i.e., asymmetric upside if even a subset of the reservation agreements convert to signed ESSAs.

**Bear (3 points):**
1. Financing intensity is rising quickly — H1 2026 capex +36% YoY, long-term debt +$1.8bn in a single quarter — and if bond markets reprice utility credit (or PPL's own ratings drift), the growth plan becomes more expensive exactly when it needs the most capital.
2. A large share of the "growth" story is signed agreements and advanced-stage pipeline, not in-service load; data-center projects nationally have a documented history of delay, resizing, or cancellation, and PPL's own numbers (31.8 GW "advanced planning" vs >11 GW "signed") show the gap between hope and contract.
3. Two different state regulators (Pennsylvania PUC and Kentucky PSC) both have to keep approving cost recovery for this to work; the August 2026 KPSC rehearing order was constructive this time, but it is evidence the regulatory process is actively contested, not a settled matter.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable) — thesis-invalidation triggers / what would change our mind

1. Annual ongoing EPS growth falls below 6% (bottom of the guided range) for two consecutive fiscal years.
2. Signed data-center service agreements (currently >11 GW PA + 1.3 GW KY) are reduced by more than 20% through cancellation or resizing within any 12-month window.
3. Total debt/EBITDA proxy rises above 6.5x for two consecutive quarters (from ≈5.4x currently), or a credit-rating agency downgrades PPL/LG&E/KU or moves the outlook to negative.
4. Pennsylvania PUC or Kentucky PSC issues a rate order disallowing more than 10% of a requested revenue increase in the companies' next base rate case.
5. Invitium Energy fails to sign a single commercial ESSA by the company's own stated end-of-2026 target (a management-set, checkable milestone from the Q2 2026 release).

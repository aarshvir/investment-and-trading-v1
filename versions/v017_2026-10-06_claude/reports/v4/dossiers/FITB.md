# FITB — Fifth Third Bancorp — Diligence Dossier (Agent F44, wave 2, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (reservation: Comerica integration is ~60% through a multi-quarter systems/synergy conversion, and the market has already re-rated the stock for a good chunk of the expected outcome). Thesis horizon: 12–24 months (integration completion + synergy realization).

## 2. Business in plain English
Fifth Third is a Cincinnati-based regional bank that, on 2 Feb 2026, completed an all-stock merger with Comerica, becoming the ~9th-largest US bank by assets (~$294B, per opening-balance disclosure of $86B of Comerica assets added to Fifth Third's prior ~$208B base). It makes money the way any bank does — taking in deposits cheaply and lending them out at a spread (commercial & industrial, commercial real estate, auto, mortgage, and Comerica's historically strong middle-market and wealth franchise) — plus fee income from wealth/asset management, commercial payments, and capital markets. Competitive position: a top-10 US regional with a legacy Midwest/Southeast retail franchise now layered with Comerica's Texas/California/Michigan commercial-banking strength.

## 3. Why the model likes it / durability
Triage (Q06) flagged +67% forward EPS growth and +52% revenue growth from the deal and an 11.8x NTM P/E. That growth is **not organic** — it is the arithmetic effect of consolidating a similarly-sized bank via an all-stock deal (diluted share count rose from 669.2M avg (Q4 2025) to 916.0M avg (Q2 2026), +37%). The durable part of the thesis is real: NIM has expanded for two straight quarters (3.13%→3.30%→3.36%) on repricing and deposit-cost discipline, and credit quality improved through the deal (NCO ratio 0.45%→0.37%→0.30%, the best since 2Q23). The artefact risk is real too: ~65% of expected full-year merger charges were already booked through Q2 2026, and the "growth" figures triage cited will normalize to flat/low-single-digit once the merger comparison rolls off.

## 5. Guidance track record
Fifth Third's earnings press releases do not carry quantitative full-year EPS/revenue guidance (typical for large US banks — guidance is qualitative and given on the earnings call, which was not transcribed within this time-box). Qualitative commentary across the three reviewed releases has been consistently constructive and specific: Q4 2025 emphasized standalone momentum ahead of the deal; Q1 2026 said integration was "progressing as we expected"; Q2 2026 said the Comerica systems conversion was on track for Labor Day 2026 (the last major integration milestone) and reiterated the "on-track" synergy language without a dollar figure change. No guidance was cut in the reviewed window. **This is a gap, not a clean pass**: I could not verify a specific cost-synergy target ($ millions) against delivery because it was never quantified in the press releases I read; flagged as a confidence limiter.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **GAAP vs adjusted:** Q1 2026 GAAP EPS $0.15 vs an implied ~$0.83 adjusted (reported as "a net negative $0.68 impact from certain items"); Q2 2026 GAAP $0.83 vs adjusted $1.02 (0.19 of certain items). The gap is real and merger-related — not a case of recurring non-GAAP add-backs masking weak organic results — but the size of it (over 5x reported EPS in Q1 2026) means **GAAP EPS is not usable as a run-rate** for at least two more quarters.
- **Capital:** CET1 fell from 10.81% (Q4 2025) to 9.96% (Q1 2026, deal close: $12.3B of common stock issued as merger consideration, $6.2B of goodwill/intangibles created, $73B of RWA added) and has been roughly stable since (9.93% at Q2 2026). No share repurchases in 1H26 — capital is being conserved to rebuild the ratio, a sensible but confidence-limiting signal (management is not yet confident enough in excess capital to resume buybacks).
- **Credit:** NPA ratio actually rose slightly (0.57%→0.60% Q1 2026→Q2 2026) even as NCOs fell — a mild, disclosed divergence ("modest growth in consumer and commercial NPAs") worth watching, though ACL coverage remains strong (303% of NPLs).
- **Funding:** Average deposits rose $209.4B→$231.5B q/q (Comerica full-quarter effect); consumer deposits +$4.6B, interest-bearing deposit costs down 2bp to 2.13% — funding cost discipline is intact.
- **Debt/capital markets:** Routine debt management only in the red-flag window — a senior-notes exchange-offer registration completed 23 Sep 2026 and an early redemption notice (24 Sep 2026) for $500mm of 1.707% notes due Nov 2027, called one year early. Both are ordinary treasury operations, not distress signals (SEC 8-Ks, filed 2026-09-23 and 2026-09-24).
- **Share count:** diluted shares +37% (669.2M→916.0M) from the all-stock deal — durably dilutive unless synergies close the gap, which is exactly the open question this thesis rests on.

## 7. Valuation
No V1 systematic valuation row exists for FITB (not yet run by the valuation agent) — `v1_verdict` is null in the summary JSON; nothing to reconcile against.
Own reverse-engineered view: banks are valued on P/TBV, not FCFF DCF (P/TBV = (ROE−g)/(COE−g) is the standard identity; a mechanical FCFF reverse-DCF is inappropriate for a lender per sector convention). At the 25-Sep-2026 close of **$52.10** and TBVPS of $23.15, FITB trades at **2.25x tangible book**. Solving the identity with an assumed long-run adjusted ROTCE of ~15.5% (between the Q2 2026 adjusted 15.6% and a haircut for execution risk) and a 10% cost of equity implies the market is pricing in roughly **5–6% perpetual growth** in tangible book/earnings power. My own base case (mid-single-digit organic loan/fee growth once integration normalizes, plus buyback resumption funded by strong capital generation) is **~5–6%** — essentially the same number. **Implied vs base: in_line.** Peers: HBAN trades far cheaper on the same framework (see HBAN dossier) — FITB has already been given credit for successful integration that hasn't fully happened yet.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) Comerica's Texas/Southwest franchise plus Fifth Third's Southeast branch build-out compounds into multi-year above-peer loan and deposit growth. (2) Systems conversion completing on schedule (Labor Day 2026) unlocks the back-half of cost synergies, which should show up cleanly in 3Q/Q4 2026 efficiency ratios. (3) NIM has expanded for two straight quarters into a more balanced funding mix — further Fed easing would help asset-sensitive repricing less than deposit-cost relief helps the liability side.
**Bear:** (1) CET1 at 9.93% with no buybacks is a tell that management isn't yet confident in the combined balance sheet; a credit surprise (NPAs already ticking up) would force a capital raise or prolonged buyback pause. (2) Integration execution risk is not hypothetical — 65% of a nearly $1bn charge pool has already been spent and the "last major milestone" hasn't happened yet as of this writing. (3) The stock already prices in successful execution (2.25x TBV, above HBAN's 1.62x for a comparable ROTCE) — the easy re-rating may be behind, not ahead.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria
1. CET1 ratio falls below **9.5%** for two consecutive quarters without an explicit, disclosed capital plan.
2. Net charge-off ratio rises above **0.55%** for two consecutive quarters (vs 0.30% at Q2 2026).
3. Adjusted efficiency ratio fails to improve below **60%** by Q4 2026 (vs 57.1% at Q2 2026, after the Labor Day systems conversion is supposed to unlock synergies).
4. Nonperforming asset ratio rises above **0.75%** (vs 0.60% at Q2 2026) for two consecutive quarters.
5. Share buybacks are not resumed within four quarters of deal close (by Q1 2027) — a signal capital generation is weaker than modeled.

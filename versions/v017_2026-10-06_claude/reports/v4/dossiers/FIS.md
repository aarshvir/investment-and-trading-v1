# Fidelity National Information Services, Inc. (FIS) — Diligence Dossier (F34, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight). Thesis horizon 12–36 months. One-sentence reason: FIS just completed a genuinely transformative portfolio swap (bought Global Payments' Issuer Solutions business, sold its Worldpay stake) that upgrades its earnings quality at a statistically very cheap multiple, but it did so by taking leverage to 3.5x (above its own 2.8x target, forcing a buyback pause) and cut its adjusted-EPS guidance one quarter later — real, current risk that argues for a starter position, not full weight.

**Thesis (≤25 words):** Bank core-processing giant swapped a non-cash-generating Worldpay stake for a big, high-margin issuer-processing business — cheap, but now more levered and integrating.

## 2. Business in plain English
FIS sells the core software and processing infrastructure banks and credit unions use to run accounts, cards and payments — the plumbing behind everyday banking, not a consumer brand. On January 9, 2026, FIS completed a transformative transaction: it acquired Global Payments' Issuer Solutions business (formerly TSYS, enterprise value $13.5bn) — a large, high-margin, recurring-revenue card-issuer-processing franchise — in exchange for FIS's 45% minority stake in merchant acquirer Worldpay (valued at $24.25bn) plus roughly $7.7bn of cash. The strategic logic: replace a passive, non-cash-generating equity stake with a large operating business that adds scale to FIS's core banking-technology franchise. Competitive position: one of the largest bank-core and issuer-processing technology providers globally, alongside Fiserv and Jack Henry, with particular strength serving large financial institutions (management cites an "85% win rate" there).

## 7. Valuation snapshot & reverse DCF
Price (2026-09-25 close) $35.41; market cap ≈$18.3bn (d4); EV ≈$38.6bn (net debt ≈$20.5bn). FY2026 P/E on the just-revised guided adjusted EPS midpoint ($6.20) ≈**5.7x** — very cheap on an adjusted basis, though the wide GAAP-to-adjusted gap (§4) means this multiple should be read with the earnings-quality caveat in mind, not as a clean P/E. **No V1 row exists for FIS** (checked `v1_valuation_table.csv` — no row) → v1_verdict = null; valuation view is independently derived.

**Reverse DCF:** EV = FCF₁×(1+g)/(WACC−g). Using FCF₁ = $2.2bn (FY26 guide midpoint) and WACC ≈8.5% (a CAPM-mechanical WACC using FIS's pre-deal beta of 0.80 would understate the risk of the new, much higher post-deal leverage, so a judgmentally higher 8.5% is used — cost of equity ≈8.2% via CAPM plus a leverage-risk add-on, blended with a ≈4.3% post-tax cost of debt at a ≈53% debt weight), solving for g against EV $38.6bn gives **implied perpetual FCF growth ≈2.6–2.7%/yr**. **[Corrected 2026-10-06: FCF of $2.2bn is after about $0.8bn of interest yet is set against EV, and stock-based compensation ($101M in H1) is added back; unlevered, after SBC (about $2.64bn) the same formula gives about 1.5% a year; still far below the 8-10% base, implied_vs_base stays below, verdict unchanged (DVH5)]** My evidence-based base case is **~8–10%/yr** over the medium term (synergy capture toward the disclosed $150M+ 2028 EBITDA target, mid-single-digit organic bank-tech growth once Capital Markets stabilizes, and deleveraging-driven interest-expense relief) — well short of management's own implied ~15%/yr FCF path to "$3bn+ by 2028," but comfortably **above** the ~2.6% the price implies. Implied growth is **below** base case: this reads cheap even after discounting management's own target for the usual optimism. Bear case (integration stumbles, Capital Markets stays soft, leverage stays near 3.5x for the full window, buybacks stay paused, EPS grows only ~3%/yr, multiple stays at a distressed-adjacent ~4.5–5.7x): **3-yr annualized return ≈ +1–2%** (dividend yield roughly offsets a modest de-rating). Base case (EPS compounds ~8–9%/yr as the 2028 targets are partly delivered, multiple re-rates from 5.7x toward 7.5x as leverage nears target and buybacks resume): **≈ +20–21%/yr**. Bull case (synergies exceed target, leverage falls faster, multiple re-rates toward bank-tech-peer levels of ~10–11x): **≈ +34–36%/yr**.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) The Issuer Solutions/Worldpay swap is a real strategic upgrade — a large, high-margin recurring-revenue processing business replacing a passive minority stake — with management guiding to $150M+ of EBITDA synergies and $3bn+ FCF by 2028, and synergy capture already tracking "ahead of plan"; (2) at ~5.7x guided adjusted EPS and a ~5.7% FCF yield on EV, the market prices in almost no execution success from a deal management itself frames as transformative; (3) FIS's core large-bank issuer-processing franchise shows an "85% win rate" against newer entrants (e.g., Visa's Pismo), evidence the moat (deep, sticky bank-core integrations) is intact even as smaller/fintech-focused rivals target the low end of the market.
**Bear:** (1) Gross leverage of 3.5x (vs. a 2.8x target) is real and current, not hypothetical — it has already forced a buyback and tuck-in-M&A pause, and adjusted-EPS guidance was cut (from 8–10% growth to 7.0–8.5%) just one quarter after the deal closed; (2) disclosed organic softness is current-quarter, not distant risk: pro forma revenue/EBITDA growth guidance was cut 60–150bps, Capital Markets professional-services revenue fell 17%, and UBS/Credit Suisse-related client attrition is cutting ~1 point off 2026 revenue growth; (3) GAAP earnings are dominated by non-recurring items (a $2.2bn one-time gain inflated Q1'26 GAAP EPS to $4.58, while Q2'25 was a GAAP loss of −$0.90/share) — "adjusted EPS" is doing most of the work in the cheap-valuation case, and a resolved-but-relevant $210m securities-fraud settlement (§11) shows the company has previously been found to have over-stated integration/synergy disclosures around a prior (Worldpay) acquisition.

## 9. Key risks & measurable kill criteria
Any of the following would break the thesis; each is a specific, observable, time-bound signpost to monitor at each earnings release, not a vague worry:
1. Adjusted-EPS guidance is cut again at the Q3'26 or Q4'26 release (any reduction to the low end of the current $6.15–6.24 range).
2. Gross leverage fails to decline toward the 2.8x target — stays at or above 3.5x for two consecutive quarters after Q2'26.
3. Client attrition (UBS/Credit-Suisse-style losses) widens beyond the currently disclosed ~1-point drag on 2026 revenue growth.
4. Capital Markets segment professional-services revenue declines for a third consecutive quarter (currently −17% in the most recent disclosed quarter).
5. Share buybacks remain fully paused beyond FY2027 — i.e., management fails to resume "meaningful" repurchases once leverage nears its own 2.8x target, contradicting its own stated plan.

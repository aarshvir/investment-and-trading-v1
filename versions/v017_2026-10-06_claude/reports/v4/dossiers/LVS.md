# LVS — Las Vegas Sands Corp. — F20 diligence dossier

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE** — thesis horizon 12–36 months. This is the cheapest name in the F20 batch by a wide margin on a systematic reverse-DCF basis (the price implies FCF *shrinking* more than 11%/yr for a decade against a 5-year delivered CAGR of +27%), the business is genuinely improving (Macau mass-market share gains, a monopoly Singapore asset, an investment-grade upgrade), and management is returning capital aggressively and consistently. ## 2. Business in plain English
Las Vegas Sands owns and operates large integrated resorts (casino, hotel, retail, convention/MICE space) — today essentially Macau (through ~70%-owned Sands China: The Venetian Macao, Londoner, Parisian, Plaza) and Singapore (Marina Bay Sands, 100%-owned). It sold its US Las Vegas properties in 2022 and is no longer a US casino operator in the traditional sense; earnings come almost entirely from Asian gaming and premium-mass tourism. It makes money on gaming win (mass-market table games and slots, plus VIP/junket-driven rolling chip volume), room/retail/MICE revenue, and — for Macau specifically — is subject to a government concession that sets the terms under which it may operate.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1_live_scores.csv ranks LVS moderately (composite percentile 56th, decile 6/quintile 3, live_rank 219 — not a top-70 name; it reached full diligence via the triage/quality-growth route after Q02 flagged it). The valuation gap the model is really responding to is in V1's own systematic reverse-DCF (see §7), not the factor composite. The underlying growth is durable, not a sugar-high: Sands China mass GGR +8% YoY in Q2-26 (vs the overall Macau market's +4%, i.e. genuine share gain, not just market recovery), and Marina Bay Sands mass gaming revenue +5% YoY in a quarter where overall visitation was depressed by the football World Cup — both are volume/share stories, not one-off VIP-hold luck.

## 5. Guidance track record
Las Vegas Sands, like most casino operators, **does not issue formal quantitative revenue/EPS guidance**. The closest analogue is management's informally stated Macau EBITDA aspiration of ~$700m/quarter, which the company missed in Q2-2026 ($430m actual, ~$517m hold-normalised) — disclosed above as a genuine, measurable shortfall against management's own stated bar, not glossed over. No formal guidance-track-record table applies; this absence itself is stated rather than treated as a pass (per the addendum's "missing evidence is not a pass" instruction).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- OCF (TTM through Q2-26, sum of last 4 quarters): 567+... — quarterly OCF only reliably available at Q1-tagged (~90-day) contexts in this dataset for several years plus the two most recent 10-Qs; FY2025 OCF was strong and full-year metrics (below) are the more reliable read.
- FY2025: net revenue $13.02bn, operating income $2.82bn (21.7% margin), net income (consolidated) $1.63bn, diluted EPS $2.35, adjusted property EBITDA $5.23bn (vs $4.38bn FY2024, +19.4%).
- **Net debt (30-Jun-2026): long-term debt & capital leases $13,694m + current portion $1,568m − cash $3,376m = $11,886m** (matches V1's independently sourced figure to the dollar). Against TTM EBITDA of roughly $4.4–4.9bn, **net debt/EBITDA is approximately 2.4–2.7x** — S&P's own stated expectation is leverage sustained "around 2.5x" through the Singapore expansion, with a 3.0x downgrade threshold; **S&P upgraded LVS/Sands China from BBB- to BBB in 2026** on disciplined financial policy, and LVS raised $1bn of bonds at the improved rating. This is a genuinely improving credit story, not a deteriorating one.
- **Capital return is aggressive and shrinking the equity base:** $740m of buybacks in Q1-2026 and $787m in Q2-2026 (H1-2026 total $1,542m) plus $400m of dividends YTD; diluted share count fell from 675m (Q4-25) to 648m (Q2-26), a ~4% reduction in two quarters. Stockholders' equity attributable to LVS fell from $1,198m (Q1-26) to $581m (Q2-26) — this looks alarming in isolation but is mechanically explained by buybacks retiring equity faster than retained earnings accrete it, not by asset impairment or losses; still worth monitoring since it leaves a thin common-equity cushion on a historical-cost balance sheet that materially understates the properties' market value.
- SBC is small (~$13–24m/quarter, under 1% of revenue) — not a distortive factor here.
- **Governance/control:** the Adelson family (Dr. Miriam Adelson, Irwin Chafetz and related trusts) beneficially owns/controls **~59.7% of voting power** — LVS is a controlled company. This concentrates capital-allocation authority in one family (a genuine governance risk for minority holders) but that family has run the business consistently and, on the evidence above, is currently allocating capital toward buybacks at a statistically cheap multiple — a reasonable use of control, not an obvious expropriation risk, but disclosed as a structural fact.

## 8. Bull case
1. Genuine, share-gaining mass-market recovery in both Macau (Sands China mass GGR +8% vs market +4%) and Singapore (MBS mass +5% in a soft-visitation quarter) — the durable part of the business is accelerating, while the volatile part (VIP hold) is what is currently depressing headline EBITDA.
2. Credit quality is improving, not deteriorating: S&P upgrade to BBB, leverage target ~2.5x, and a successful $1bn bond raise at the better rating — the balance-sheet risk the market may be pricing (via the thin post-buyback common-equity base) is not corroborated by the rating agencies actually holding the debt.
3. Marina Bay Sands is a structurally scarce, effectively monopoly asset (Singapore issues a small, fixed number of casino licences) now being expanded at scale ($8bn), and the concession renewal cycle for Macau just completed in 2023 with a full 10-year grant, i.e. the closest major renewal risk (2032) is still six years away.

## 9. Bear case
1. The **2032 Macau concession renewal** sits inside any decade-long valuation horizon and is a real, if not immediate, source of uncertainty on operating terms, tax/gaming-levy rates, and required non-gaming capex commitments (VML has already committed ≥35.84bn patacas / ~$4.47bn under the current concession).
2. Singapore capex has already **doubled from original estimates to ~$8bn** — a live execution-risk pattern (cost overruns on large gaming-resort construction projects are common in the industry) that could pressure free cash flow and the capital-return program if it recurs.
3. VIP-hold volatility (the Q2-2026 Macau EBITDA miss) demonstrates that quarterly headline numbers can swing by ~20% on pure statistical variance in a business line management does not fully control — a reminder that near-term print risk is real even if the multi-quarter trend (mass-market share gains) is sound; combined with majority family control (59.7% voting power), minority shareholders have limited recourse if capital-allocation priorities were ever to shift away from buybacks.

## 10. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Sands China mass-market GGR growth falls below the overall Macau market's growth rate for two consecutive quarters (loses the share-gain thesis).
2. Consolidated net debt/EBITDA rises above 3.0x (S&P's own stated downgrade threshold) for two consecutive quarters.
3. Marina Bay Sands expansion capex guidance is revised upward again beyond the current ~$8bn, or the 2031 opening date slips by more than two quarters.
4. Any formal notice or public commentary from the Macau government casting doubt on standard renewal terms ahead of the December-2032 concession expiry.
5. Quarterly dividend or buyback pace is cut (a reversal of the current aggressive capital-return program) absent a stated, value-accretive reinvestment alternative.

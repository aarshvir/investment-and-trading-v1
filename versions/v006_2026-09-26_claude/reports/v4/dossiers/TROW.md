## 1. Verdict
**WATCH.** T. Rowe Price is a well-capitalized, genuinely free-cash-flow-generative active manager trading close to fair value on a normalized P/B-vs-ROE framework, with a real (if decelerating) structural net-outflow problem in its core active-equity franchise and sell-side sentiment that has turned more negative in the last several weeks, including two price-target cuts (one below the current share price). **Thesis horizon:** 12–24 months — shorter than a typical multi-year compounder call, because the near-term question (does the Q4'25→Q2'26 outflow deceleration continue, or reverse) is resolvable within 2–3 quarters and should be watched before sizing a position.

## 2. The Business
T. Rowe Price is a pure-play active investment manager: it earns fees (predominantly a percentage of assets under management, "investment advisory fees," $1,744.8M of Q2 2026's $1,907.4M net revenues [Q2 2026 earnings release, 8-K Ex-99.1, 2026-07-31], plus smaller administrative/distribution/servicing fees) for managing money across equity, fixed income, multi-asset and alternatives strategies for retail investors (largely US retirement accounts, where it is a major target-date-fund manager) and institutions. Revenue is a direct function of average AUM × blended fee rate, so it moves with both markets (beta) and the firm's own net flows. Its competitive position is a large, diversified, historically strong-performing active shop increasingly squeezed by the decades-long structural shift toward passive and lower-fee vehicles — the single dominant theme in this dossier.

This is a very thin guidance regime — investors get almost no forward operating signal from management each quarter, which is itself worth noting as a transparency limitation (though not unusual for the traditional asset-management sector).

## 6. Earnings Quality & Balance Sheet
**FCF conversion:** FCF (CFO − capex) was $911.2M / $1,262.2M / $1,479.2M in FY2023 / FY2024 / FY2025 [XBRL `NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities` less `PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment`], i.e. FCF/NI-to-common of 52.2% → 61.7% → 72.6% — a genuinely improving cash-conversion trend, confirmed by `ratios.py` (FCF/EBITDA cash conversion 45.9% → 54.1% → 67.6%; Sloan accrual ratio −1.7% in FY2025, i.e. **no** accrual red flag). **SBC**: $265.6M / $247.3M / $216.9M in FY2023/24/25 [XBRL `ShareBasedCompensation`], a declining 3.6%/3.5%/3.0% of revenue.

**GAAP-vs-adjusted gap and its driver:** in Q2 2026, GAAP diluted EPS ($2.88) *exceeded* adjusted EPS ($2.57) by 12%, because Q2's $369.1M of non-operating income — seed-capital market gains $66.1M, deferred-compensation-hedge gains $133.7M, and consolidated-investment-product gains $132.6M [Q2 2026 earnings release] — is stripped out of the adjusted figure. One quarter earlier (Q1 2026), the relationship **flipped**: GAAP EPS ($2.23) was *below* adjusted ($2.52) because Q1 carried a $48.3M non-operating **loss** (weak investment-portfolio and hedge marks) [Q1 2026 earnings release, XBRL `NonoperatingIncomeExpense`]. In Q4 2025, GAAP EPS ($1.99) was well below adjusted ($2.44) mainly because of a **$148.8M restructuring charge**, of which $127.3M was accelerated depreciation/impairment on owned real estate, under a "broad and ongoing expense management program" that has also produced smaller charges every quarter since ($28.5M Q3'25, $10.0M Q1'26, $6.7M Q2'26) [respective 8-K Ex-99.1 releases]. **Read together: reported GAAP EPS growth (Q2'26 +28.6% YoY) substantially overstates core earnings momentum; adjusted EPS growth (Q2'26 +14.7% YoY, $2.57 vs $2.24) is the more honest, still-respectable, comparison.**

**Balance sheet / capital metrics (asset-manager appropriate, not bank/insurer ones):** stockholders' equity $11,002.5M at Q2 2026 [10-Q, XBRL], up from $10,860.1M at FY2025-end and $9,505.1M at FY2023-end. Cash $3,234.3M at Q2 2026 [10-Q, XBRL]. **T. Rowe carries essentially no true corporate debt**: a Q2 2026 10-Q review found the only debt on the consolidated balance sheet relates to *consolidated investment products* — a CLO warehousing facility (~$220.1M as of the most recent lag-reported date, floating rate SOFR+1.65%, non-recourse to the parent, **already repaid at securitization close in Q2 2026**) and small CLO repurchase agreements (~€18.6M / ~$21.2M, various maturities through 2035) [Q2 2026 10-Q, filed 2026-07-31]. This is a consolidation accounting artifact (per the holdco/asset-manager sector playbook's explicit warning about CLO/VIE consolidation), **not** parent-company leverage — net cash position is effectively the full ~$2.78–3.2B, not reduced by meaningful real debt. **Buybacks accelerated sharply**: $500.8M repurchased in H1 2026 vs $328.3M in H1 2025 (+52.6% YoY) [XBRL `PaymentsForRepurchaseOfCommonStock`]; diluted share count fell from 223.3M (FY2024) to 220.3M (FY2025) to a weighted-average 213.3M more recently. **Dividend**: quarterly dividend has been raised every year for the last four years shown ($1.22 → $1.24 → $1.27 → $1.30 per quarter, each a Q1 increase) [XBRL `CommonStockDividendsPerShareDeclared`], confirming the increase streak remains intact through Q1 2026 — this was verified directly rather than assumed.

## 7. Valuation Snapshot
TTM (thru Q2 2026): P/E on GAAP EPS ($9.97) = **10.6x**; on adjusted TTM EPS ($10.34) = **10.2x** (both consistent with Yahoo's reported trailing P/E of 10.59x [`d4_live_snapshot.parquet`]). `ratios.py --sector holdco-assetmgr` on FY2025 figures: P/E 14.8x (FY2025-only, depressed by the Q4 restructuring charge), EV/EBITDA 8.7x, P/B 2.07x, dividend yield 5.1%. `valuation.py` on a TTM/current basis: EV/EBITDA 8.4x, **reverse-DCF: the current $105.51 price already assumes only ~3.5%/yr FCF growth for 10 years fading to 2% terminal**, at a 10.5% WACC — a modest, plausibly achievable bar rather than an aggressive one, *provided* net outflows keep decelerating as they have for three quarters. A conservative forward DCF at a flat 2% FCF growth assumption gives **$96.30/share (−8.8% vs. spot)**; the probability-weighted scenario table (Bear 30% $76.50/−27.5%, Base 45% $113.30/+7.4%, Bull 25% $149.50/+41.7%) gives an expected value of **$111.30 (+5.5%)**.
**Primary method per the sector playbook — P/E on core (adjusted) earnings cross-checked against P/B-vs-sustainable-ROE:** using TTM ROE (~19.4–20.8% per Yahoo/pipeline, both close) and an assumed 10.5% cost of equity and ~2% long-run growth, justified P/B = (ROE−g)/(COE−g) = (0.198−0.02)/(0.105−0.02) ≈ **2.1x — almost exactly the actual 2.0–2.05x P/B**, i.e. **the stock looks close to fair value on this framework using current-run-rate ROE**, not cheap. It looks statistically cheaper only if one uses the FY2025-full-year (restructuring-suppressed) ROE of 14.4%, which understates the current run rate. Peer context (traditional active managers, dividend yield only, price-sourced 2026-09-04): Franklin Resources (BEN) yield 3.80%, Invesco (IVZ) yield 3.07%, Janus Henderson (JHG) yield 3.34% [WebSearch, aggregated pricing sites, 2026-09-26] — TROW's 4.99% yield is meaningfully higher than all three, consistent with either superior capital-return discipline or the market pricing more risk into TROW specifically. ## 8. Bull Case and Bear Case
### Bull case
1. Net outflows have decelerated for three straight quarters (−$25.5B → −$13.7B → −$6.5B) [8-K Ex-99.1 releases]; if this continues toward flat/positive, the stock is priced for a permanent decline that may not materialize, and the reverse-DCF bar (~3.5%/yr FCF growth) is low.
2. Genuinely clean balance sheet — no real corporate debt (Section 6), improving FCF conversion (72.6% of NI in FY2025), a dividend raised every year including through Q1 2026, and buybacks up 52.6% YoY, all self-funded from a business generating $1.48B of FY2025 FCF.
3. On current-run-rate ROE (~20%), the stock trades close to its own justified P/B (2.1x model vs ~2.0x actual) — it is not obviously overpriced even before crediting any flow-stabilization upside, and the 4.99% dividend yield provides a real cash return while waiting.

### Bear case
1. Every single quarter in the 8-quarter table shows net outflows, concentrated in active equity (−$74.9B of the −$56.9B FY2025 total came from equity alone, more than offsetting positive fixed-income/multi-asset/alternatives flows) [Q4/FY2025 earnings release] — this is the industry's dominant secular trend (active-to-passive), not a cyclical dip, and TROW's own Item 1A risk factors name it as a top-three risk [FY2025 10-K].
2. Sell-side sentiment has gotten *more* negative in the last several weeks, not less: Morgan Stanley cut its target to $97 (below the current $105.51 price) explicitly citing flow and NAV pressure, Barclays cut to $103 with an "underweight," and the broader analyst consensus was reported as "Reduce" in mid-September 2026 [MarketBeat 2026-08-03; TipRanks/"the Fly"; stockanalysis.com accessed 2026-09-26] — professional analysts covering the name see less upside than either the price or the quant composite implies.
3. Reported GAAP earnings growth is partly a function of volatile non-operating investment/hedge marks (Section 6) that can reverse quarter to quarter (Q1 2026 was a $48.3M non-operating *loss*); a run of "ongoing" restructuring charges every quarter for a year raises the question of whether the cost base is durably reset or merely being trimmed reactively each quarter as flows disappoint.

**Strongest counter to the bear case:** the outflow-deceleration trend is real and three-quarters-long, not a one-off, and it is happening *while* the stock already prices in only very modest (~3.5%/yr) forward growth — so the bear thesis is largely already reflected in the valuation, which is why this is a WATCH, not a REJECT.

## 9. Key Risks & Kill Criteria — What Would Invalidate This Thesis
1. **Outflow re-acceleration**: net outflows exceed $20B in any single quarter, or the three-quarter deceleration trend reverses for two consecutive quarters — observable in each quarterly earnings release (8-K Ex-99.1). By: next two quarterly releases (est. 2026-10-30 and Q4 2026 release, early Feb 2027).
2. **Dividend increase streak breaks** or the quarterly dividend is cut/frozen at a Q1 reset date — observable in the Q1 2027 10-Q/dividend declaration (expected ~April 2027).
3. **Adjusted operating margin falls below ~26%** for two consecutive quarters (below the low end of the range shown in Section 4) — observable each quarter's earnings release.
4. **A fifth consecutive quarter of "ongoing" restructuring charges** without a stated end date or a disclosed run-rate expense-growth target — would suggest the cost program is reactive rather than a genuine structural reset. Observable each quarter's 8-K Ex-99.1.
5. **A major sell-side price target moves below the DCF bear case (~$76.50)**, or two or more of the covering banks currently at Reduce/Underweight downgrade further — would indicate the professional consensus sees a worse outcome than modeled here.

# CMI — Cummins Inc. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F40, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**WATCH** (not REJECT — quality/moat are real; not INCLUDE — price already discounts more growth than the evidence supports). Thesis horizon: 12–36 months, revisit on a pullback toward a 13–15x NTM P/E or on two consecutive quarters of EBITDA-margin expansion into the guided range.

## 2. Business in plain English
Cummins designs and makes diesel/natural-gas engines, powertrains, and — increasingly — large stationary generators ("Power Systems") used for data-center backup and continuous power, plus filtration, emission-control and electrified components. Customers are truck OEMs (PACCAR, Daimler, Navistar), construction/mining equipment makers, and, in the fastest-growing segment, hyperscale data-center developers who need standby/prime power while grid capacity catches up to AI-driven demand. It earns money on engine/parts sales, aftermarket service, and a growing share of high-margin large-genset systems.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
b1 composite flags CMI on quality (low leverage, strong ROE/ROIC) and momentum (+36% trailing). The **durable** part is real: Power Systems' large-genset backlog is capacity-constrained into late 2026/2027, with orders on the newest platform (QSK95) being taken through H2 2028 (Cummins CEO J. Rumsey, Q2-26 call). The **artefact** risk is that revenue guidance has been raised three releases running mostly on volume/mix (data-center gensets, NA on-highway recovery, China construction), while EBITDA **margin actually fell YoY in Q2-26** (17.5% vs 18.4%) on higher incentive comp and R&D — i.e., the market is paying up for a top-line story whose margin delivery hasn't yet shown through.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
FCF/NI ~1.24x (strong). SBC only ~$93M TTM (~0.27% of revenue, per V1/D3 — immaterial). Net debt $3.16bn / TTM EBITDA (~$5.01bn) ≈ **0.6x** — low leverage, matches triage's characterisation. No pending M&A disclosed. Share count essentially flat (138.5–142.1M, buybacks roughly offsetting modest issuance). Two large one-offs bracket the two-year window in opposite directions (Atmus gain, Q1-24; Electrolyzer charge, Q4-25) — both are flagged above so GAAP and "clean" trends aren't conflated.

## 7. Valuation snapshot — reconciled with V1
V1 (systematic reverse DCF, FCFF method, SBC not added back, 10y fade to 3% terminal, WACC 8.74%) verdict: **"excessive."** NTM P/E 16.19x is only the 48.6th percentile of CMI's own 177-month history (i.e., not rich on a simple multiple basis) and is a **discount** to peer median 20.06x (CAT/PCAR/WAB) — but the reverse DCF says the $525.06 close implies **15.66%/yr** FCF growth for 10 years, versus consensus FY1 growth of 8.95%, 5y delivered CAGR 8.44%, and 10y delivered CAGR 6.67%. V1 base-case 3y annualised return is **−3.9%** (bear −25.1%, bull +19.4%). **I agree with V1's excessive call**: the structural data-center tailwind is real, but it would have to compound at nearly double its own trailing rate, for a decade, with margin expansion that hasn't shown up yet, to earn today's price. **Unresolved conflict to disclose:** sell-side consensus price targets (mean $746, low $530) imply the stock is already *below* the Street's most bearish target — i.e., analysts are more bullish than either V1 or this dossier. I read that gap as sell-side targets embedding continued guidance raises and multiple re-rating that the reverse DCF treats as already-priced.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) data-center standby-power backlog is capacity-constrained into 2027–28, a multi-year, non-discretionary buildout; (2) balance sheet (0.6x net debt/EBITDA) funds it without dilution; (3) three consecutive guidance raises show real operating momentum, not just estimate resets.
**Bear:** (1) EBITDA margin fell YoY in the most recent quarter despite the raises — the market is paying for margin that hasn't arrived; (2) on-highway engines remain cyclical (Class 8 truck demand swings), a large share of revenue outside Power Systems; (3) the price already implies growth well above anything CMI has delivered in 5 or 10 years.

## 9. Kill criteria
1. EBITDA margin stays below 18.0% for two consecutive quarters despite reiterated FY26 guidance (would break the "raises are backed by margin" thesis).
2. Power Systems / large-genset book-to-bill falls below 1.0x for two consecutive quarters (backlog thesis breaking).
3. Net debt/EBITDA rises above 1.5x (from ~0.6x) without offsetting FCF growth.
4. NTM P/E re-rates above the 20x peer median without a corresponding upward revision to consensus 10-year growth.
5. Follow-on shareholder/consumer litigation tied to the 2023–24 DOJ/EPA/CARB Clean Air Act settlement produces a material (>$200M) incremental charge beyond current accruals.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **Regulatory:** $1.675bn DOJ/EPA/California Clean Air Act settlement (largest-ever CAA penalty), finalized April 2024, for defeat-device/emissions-certification violations in pick-up-truck engines; >$325M in required remediation spend; ongoing shareholder/consumer follow-on litigation (immaterial accruals disclosed to date, but unresolved).
- **One-offs:** Q1-24 $1.3bn Atmus gain (positive); Q4-25 $458M Electrolyzer/Accelera charge (negative) — both must be excluded from any "clean" growth read.
- **Insider activity:** routine, small (<$0.6M each) open-market sales by VP-level executives through 2026 (CIO, Treasury VP, HR/CAO); no CEO/CFO sales found this session — no unusual pattern identified.
- No going-concern language, no auditor change, no disclosed pending acquisition.

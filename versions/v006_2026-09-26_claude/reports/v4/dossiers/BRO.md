# BRO — Brown & Brown, Inc. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F40, standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** (half weight), reservation stated explicitly: organic revenue growth just turned **negative** (Q2-26: −0.7%) after decelerating for five straight quarters, and leverage roughly tripled to fund the Accession deal. Valuation is not demanding, but the growth thesis triage relied on ("32% revenue growth, 54% EPS growth") does not survive a GAAP/organic check. Horizon: 12–24 months; revisit for full weight once organic growth re-stabilises at ≥3%.

## 2. Business in plain English
Brown & Brown is an insurance broker/wholesaler — it doesn't take underwriting risk, it earns commissions and fees placing property-casualty, employee-benefits and specialty (E&S) insurance for businesses and individuals through four segments (Retail, Programs, Wholesale Brokerage/Specialty Distribution, and now the newly-acquired Accession/National Programs businesses). It grows by winning new accounts, raising rates/exposure with existing clients ("organic"), and buying smaller brokers ("bolt-on M&A") — plus, since August 2025, one very large acquisition.

## 3. Why the model likes it — and why that's now an artefact, not durable
b1/triage liked BRO for "32% revenue growth and 54% EPS growth at 12.8x forward." That headline growth is **not organic** — it is the consolidation of **Accession Risk Management Group** (parent of Risk Strategies), acquired for a **$9.825bn** gross, cash-and-debt-free price, closed August 1, 2025 (Brown & Brown 8-K/press release; Holland & Knight deal summary). Financed with ~$4.3bn of new equity (42.16M shares at $102, June 2025) and ~$4.2bn of senior notes. Stripping the deal out: **organic revenue growth has decelerated every quarter since Q1-25** — +6.5% (Q1-25) → +3.6% (Q2-25) → +3.5% (Q3-25) → flat (Q1-26) → **−0.7% (Q2-26)**, per Brown & Brown's own press releases (investor.bbrown.com). GAAP diluted EPS has been **flat-to-down** YoY in three of the last five quarters (Q2-25 −10%, Q3-25 −16%, Q2-26 +7.7% but off a much larger diluted share base) because acquisition financing (interest expense, intangible amortization, +18% more diluted shares) outran GAAP net income growth; only the company's own **adjusted** EPS shows consistent growth (+15% Q3-25, +7.8% Q1-26, +3.9% Q2-26 — itself decelerating). The triage's "54% EPS growth" figure is not supported by either GAAP or adjusted quarterly EPS in the SEC filings checked this session — treat it as an error or a stale/mismatched comparison.

## 5. Guidance track record
Brown & Brown does **not** issue formal quantitative revenue/EPS guidance — only qualitative commentary, which I track like guidance: at Q4-25 (Feb-26) management said it "expects improved organic performance in 2026" and flagged a 2026 headwind from lower investment income. That expectation was **not met** — Q1-26 organic was flat and Q2-26 organic went negative. At Q3-25 (Oct-25), management guided Q4 Specialty Distribution organic to "decline mid-single digits" and Retail organic "similar to 2.7%"; full FY25 organic came in at 2.8%, roughly as flagged. Net: the company's near-term qualitative calls have been reasonably accurate on the segment mechanics but the "improved 2026" framing has so far been wrong.

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
FCF (OCF − capex, TTM Q3'25–Q2'26) ≈ $1.45bn vs GAAP NI $1.2bn (≈1.2x conversion) — but BRO's OCF includes swings in fiduciary/premium funds held on behalf of insurers and clients, which can inflate or deflate reported OCF independent of underlying economics; treat this FCF figure as directional, not precise. SBC ≈$49M in H1-26 (~1.4% of revenue) — modest. **Leverage jumped materially**: long-term debt $3.60bn (year-end 2024) → $7.35bn (Q2-26); net debt (LT+ST debt − cash) ≈ **$6.84bn**, versus TTM adjusted EBITDAC roughly $2.5bn → **net debt/EBITDAC ≈ 2.8x**, up from well under 1x pre-deal. Diluted share count rose from ~284M (2024) to ~335M (Q2-26), an ~18% increase, funding part of the Accession purchase price. This is the central balance-sheet fact of the thesis: BRO took on real financial risk to buy growth that has not yet shown up organically.

## 7. Valuation snapshot (no V1 row exists for BRO — independent reverse DCF/cross-checks)
No systematic V1 valuation exists for BRO this cycle (not in `v1_valuation_table.csv`/`v1_valuation.json`); `v1_verdict` is therefore `null` and the check below is this dossier's own.
- **FCFF reverse DCF** (EV0 = mkt cap $20.35bn + net debt $6.84bn = $27.19bn; TTM FCF $1.45bn; WACC ≈7.4% [beta ~0.8, rf 5.17%, ERP 4.14%, post-tax cost of debt ~4.0%, D/EV 25%]; 10y fade to 3% terminal) implies **≈0.3%/yr FCF growth** priced in — i.e., the market is assuming almost no growth. Caveat: FCF0 is noisy (fiduciary-cash swings, and only ~11 months of Accession contribution at a still-ramping run-rate), so this likely understates true normalized FCF and therefore **overstates** how cheap BRO looks; treat as a soft lower bound, not a precise number.
- **Cross-check, multiples:** TTM adjusted P/E ≈13.8x vs BRO's own trailing-10-year average of ~24x (SimplyWallSt secondary-source estimate) and vs peer Arthur J. Gallagher's ~28.5x forward P/E (Q1-26). Zacks consensus has BRO's 2026 EPS growth at +16.4% YoY, well above what either cross-check says the market is paying for.
- **Read:** both methods agree the stock is **not priced for the deceleration risk in §3** — it is priced for far less growth than sell-side consensus or BRO's own acquisition algorithm assumes. That is either a real margin of safety or the market correctly discounting execution risk; the recent organic prints argue the market may be right to be cautious, hence INCLUDE-SMALL rather than full INCLUDE.

## 8. Bull case / Bear case
**Bull:** (1) $9.8bn Accession deal materially expands scale and diversifies into new specialty/programs niches; management reaffirms $30–40M of FY26 synergies on track; (2) valuation (13–14x adjusted TTM P/E) is a large discount to its own history and to AJG, pricing in almost no growth; (3) 20+ year record of disciplined bolt-on M&A and margin management before this deal.
**Bear:** (1) organic revenue is now negative — the "compounder" story requires this to reverse, and two straight quarters (flat, then −0.7%) argue the deceleration is not noise; (2) net debt/EBITDAC near 2.8x (from <1x) removes balance-sheet flexibility right as organic growth weakens; (3) $18M of Q2-26 revenue was lost to litigation tied to producers departing for a competing startup — a people-based business's key risk (retention) made visible.

## 9. Kill criteria
1. Organic revenue growth negative for 3+ consecutive quarters (1 of 3 so far: Q2-26).
2. Net debt / adjusted EBITDAC exceeds 3.5x (currently ≈2.8x).
3. Adjusted EBITDAC margin contracts >150bps YoY for two consecutive quarters (Q2-26 was already −100bps YoY).
4. Accession-segment synergies fall short of the disclosed $30–40M FY26 target.
5. Additional material producer/team departures or adverse non-compete litigation rulings beyond the $18M already disclosed.

## 11. Red-flag scan
- **M&A/financing:** $9.825bn acquisition financed with $4.3bn new equity + $4.2bn senior notes (Jun-2025) plus a $300M revolver draw (Q3-25) — the single largest capital event in company history; integration risk is real and not yet fully proven.
- **Litigation:** producers/staff departing for a startup competitor caused an $18M Q2-26 revenue reduction — an unusual, named litigation-driven revenue impact.
- **Insider activity:** the one Form 4 found this session (director J. Hunt, Sept 2026) was a **gift** transaction (5,000 shares to a family trust), not an open-market sale — no material insider-selling pattern identified in the time available; this is a limited scan and should not be read as a clean bill of health.
- No auditor change, no going-concern language, no disclosed pending additional acquisitions of similar scale.

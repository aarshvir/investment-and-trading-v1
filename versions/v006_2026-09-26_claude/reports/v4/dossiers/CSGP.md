# CSGP — CoStar Group, Inc. — Diligence Dossier (Agent F41, Wave 2, Standard depth)

## 1. Verdict
**INCLUDE-SMALL** — a near-monopoly commercial real-estate data/analytics moat funding a large, multi-year residential ("Homes.com") build-out that has just turned an EBITDA-positive corner; named reservation: GAAP earnings are still near breakeven, full-year revenue guidance was actually **cut** even as EBITDA guidance was raised, and capital-allocation credibility is still being rebuilt under activist (Third Point) pressure. Thesis horizon: 24–48 months (the company's own Homes.com breakeven timeline runs to 2029–2030).

## 2. Business in plain English
CoStar Group sells subscription data, analytics and marketing services to the commercial real-estate industry (CoStar, LoopNet, Apartments.com, Ten-X, STR, Matterport 3D-capture, and others) — a business with a genuine, decades-old data moat (CoStar's owned research/photography/leasing-comp database is very hard to replicate) and ~47% margins in its core segments per the triage note. Since 2021 it has plowed several billion dollars into building "Homes.com," a residential listing portal, to compete with Zillow — a large, still-loss-making bet that has drawn activist pressure (Third Point) to cut spending.

## 3. Why the model likes it — durable or artefact?
Triage: quality 4/5, growth 4/5, price_vs_growth 4/5; b1 factor read shows very strong momentum-percentile inputs but weak-to-negative quality percentiles on ROE/OCF-to-assets (consistent with the depressed consolidated GAAP profitability below) — i.e., the model likes CSGP mostly on **growth/momentum**, not on quality ratios, which are genuinely poor at the consolidated level right now. The core CRE-data moat (durable) is being partly offset in the reported numbers by the Homes.com investment phase (a real, disclosed, temporary drag, not an accounting artefact) — durability judgment: **durable moat, temporarily depressed consolidated profitability**, and the Q2 2026 print is the first primary-source evidence the drag is easing.

## 5. Guidance track record (last 2 full-year guides + Q3 commentary)
- **Initial FY2026 guide (8-K, 7 Jan 2026):** revenue $3.78–3.82bn (≈18% YoY growth); adjusted EBITDA $740–800m (≈20% margin at midpoint); Homes.com net investment to fall by "more than $300m in 2026, down from $850m in 2025," with $100m+ further annual reductions through 2030; Homes.com projected to reach revenue-exceeds-expenses by 2029 and positive Adjusted EBITDA by 2030.
- **Q2 2026 guide (28 Jul 2026):** revenue **cut** to $3.715–3.755bn; adjusted EBITDA **raised** to $780–820m (midpoint +$30m vs. January). **This is a mixed signal, not a clean "raise":** the top line was guided down by roughly $65m at the midpoint even as profitability guidance improved — consistent with contemporaneous press coverage ("weak Q3 guide sends shares down"). We report this precisely rather than characterising it as an unambiguous raise, per the addendum-2 lesson on guidance claims.
- Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA guide: $190–210m (per company commentary).

## 6. Earnings quality & balance sheet
- **FCF (FY, OCF−capex, $m):** FY2022 478.7−35.2=443.5; FY2023 490.0−118.0=372.0; FY2024 393.0−579.0=**−186.0** (capex spike, likely Matterport/data-center and Homes.com infrastructure build plus M&A-related items); FY2025 430.0−307.0=123.0. FCF has been volatile and, in FY2024, negative — a real, disclosed feature of the Homes.com investment phase rather than a one-off.
- **GAAP-to-adjusted gap:** wide and persistent — GAAP diluted EPS $0.02 for all of FY2025 vs. an adjusted EBITDA run-rate implying a much larger non-GAAP profit; Q2 2026 adjusted EPS ($0.32) is more than double GAAP ($0.14). SBC, D&A on acquired intangibles (Matterport, Visual Lease, Domain) and Homes.com-related opex are the main reconciling items (not separately itemized here at standard depth — a limitation).
- **Balance sheet:** cash fell from $5.2bn (FY23) to $4.68bn (FY24) to **$1.27bn** (Q2 2026) — a ~$4bn reduction over roughly 18 months, funding the Matterport (Feb 2025, ~$1.6bn stated deal value per prior program note), Visual Lease and Domain acquisitions, continued Homes.com losses, and a new $1.5bn buyback authorization (Dec 2025). Long-term debt is stable at ~$0.99–1.0bn (2029 convertible-type notes per the LongTermDebt XBRL tag); net cash position remains positive (~$270m) — **no leverage concern**, but the cash-burn rate on the M&A + Homes.com combination bears watching against the $1.5bn buyback authorization.
- Diluted share count: ~404–424m, modestly volatile quarter to quarter (convertible-note dilution effects), not a a clean trend either way.
- No dividend.

## 7. Valuation snapshot and reverse DCF
No V1 systematic-valuation row exists for CSGP (`v1_valuation_table.csv` has no CSGP line) → `v1_verdict = null`; this section is the analyst's own reverse DCF, with an explicit methodology caveat.
- Price (25-Sep-2026 close) $28.09; market cap ≈$11.38bn; EV ≈$11.11bn (market cap less ~$270m net cash). NTM P/E per triage card 16.9x (on consensus/adjusted EPS); trailing P/E 156x (GAAP, essentially meaningless given the near-zero FY2025 GAAP EPS base — **do not use trailing GAAP P/E for this name**).
- **Mechanical reverse DCF on trailing (depressed) FY2025 FCF ($123m):** implies the market is pricing in an implausible **≈27%/yr** 10-year FCF growth rate. This number is **not meaningful** — it is an artefact of using a capex-inflated, transitional FCF base, not evidence the market is irrationally paying up.
- **More meaningful cross-check — EV/forward-EBITDA:** EV $11.1bn ÷ FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA midpoint $800m ≈ **13.9x forward**. Using a normalized/maintenance-capex FCF proxy (≈55% of adjusted EBITDA, a rough analyst estimate, not company-disclosed) of ≈$440m, the same reverse-DCF method (r=9.5%, terminal g=3%) implies **≈9.5%/yr** 10-year growth — close to a plausible base case of ~10%/yr (moderating from today's high-teens growth as the CRE-data business matures and Homes.com's own growth normalizes).
- **Analyst view: fair (in_line).** The normalized-proxy implied growth (~9.5%) is close to, and very slightly below, a defensible base case (~10%) → **implied_vs_base = "in_line."** This is a genuinely uncertain estimate (the normalization assumption is a judgement call, not a disclosed figure) — confidence on this section is **low-to-medium**, not the "cheap at a 7-year low" framing the initial triage note suggested; our own analysis lands closer to fair value than to a clear bargain.

## 8. Bull case / bear case
**Bull:** (1) Core CRE-data business is a genuine, multi-decade moat with ~47%-margin economics (per triage) that keeps compounding regardless of the Homes.com outcome; (2) Q2 2026 was the **first quarter the Residential segment (including Homes.com) was Adjusted-EBITDA-positive**, a real, primary-source-verified inflection, with EBITDA guidance raised in the same release; (3) net cash balance sheet gives real optionality (buyback authorization, further bolt-ons) without leverage risk.
**Bear:** (1) GAAP profitability is essentially zero (FY2025 net income $7m on $4.3bn+ of revenue) and has been declining for three straight years as Homes.com spend ramped — the "inflection" is one quarter of data; (2) full-year **revenue guidance was cut** in the same release that raised EBITDA guidance, a genuinely mixed signal that the market (correctly, per stock-price reaction) treated as a caution flag, not an unambiguous positive; (3) management's capital-allocation credibility is still being actively rebuilt under Third Point activist pressure — the $300m 2026 spending cut was a response to investor pressure, not a self-initiated pivot, and the company does not reach sustained positive Homes.com profitability until 2029–2030 on its own guidance.

## 9. Key risks & kill criteria (measurable)
1. Full-year revenue guidance is **cut again** at the next quarterly release (already cut once, Jan→Jul 2026).
2. Residential-segment Adjusted EBITDA reverts to **negative for two consecutive quarters** after the Q2 2026 inflection.
3. Cash and equivalents fall below **$750m** (from $1.27bn at Q2 2026) without a corresponding new debt raise, signalling liquidity stress from the M&A + buyback + Homes.com combination.
4. GAAP net income is **negative for the full FY2026** (it was only marginally positive, $7m, in FY2025).
5. Homes.com net 2026 investment reduction (guided at "more than $300m" cut vs. 2025's $850m) is **not delivered** by year-end 2026 (i.e., actual 2026 Homes.com net spend comes in above the ~$550m implied level).

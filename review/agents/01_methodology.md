# Independent methodology and code audit

**Verdict: the supplied 14-stock allocation is a research screen, not a validated investment model. Do not rely on its 11.4% return forecast, 90% five-year profit claim, or 92/100 self-grade when allocating capital.** This conclusion does not mean the companies are bad investments. It means the supplied evidence does not establish the claims made for them.

Scope: inspected the supplied Python source, saved tables and final JSON; independently reproduced key arithmetic, missing-data counts and bootstrap sensitivities. Sources were not modified. This audit does **not** independently authenticate the market prices or company fundamentals in the supplied bundle. File/line references below refer to `source_bundle/equity_project/` in the workspace. Two external primary sources were checked: Damodaran's valuation methodology and McLean–Pontiff's original paper.

## Reproduced sensitivity: the 90% result rests on its drift

Reused original historical monthly returns, original weights and three-month block method; 20,000 paths, seed5. These numbers deliberately retain the original missing-history flaw to isolate drift sensitivity. For the 50/50 mix the original claimed stock-over-index annual drift spread was held fixed at about1.36 percentage points. This is not a corrected forecast.

| Assumed index annual drift | Index five-year probability of positive nominal return | 50/50 mix five-year probability |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 43.59% | 47.49% |
| 4% | 69.30% | 69.50% |
| 6% | 79.21% | 78.04% |
| 8% | 86.64% | 84.84% |
| 10% | 91.72% | 89.85% |

The original ~90% is reproducible under its assumptions. That establishes implementation reproducibility, not forecast validity. The sampling uncertainty of 20,000 simulated outcomes is much smaller than the structural uncertainty illustrated here.

The saved conviction proxy test also fails to establish excess return: Sep2024 picks averaged10.34% against14.47% for its own survivor universe, and Sep2025 averaged6.99% against17.91%. The 2023 filter selected zero stocks due to coverage. These are weak, biased, two-window results; they cannot prove that 90% confidence is impossible for every individual stock, nor that the proposed portfolio earns it.

## Replacement methodology for the actual mandate

1. **Mandate first:** USD50,000–100,000, weekly review, variable eventual holding periods, 15–20% drawdown tolerance. Weekly review frequency is not a one-week forecast horizon. Each position must declare its thesis horizon and the events that can shorten it. A business-value thesis can require years; a short-lived earnings catalyst is a separate trade with a different risk budget.
2. **Evidence ledger:** snapshot timestamp, source URL, filing accession/publication time, accounting period, unit/currency, GAAP versus adjusted definition and split basis for every material number. Reconcile price×diluted shares to market value; EPS×P/E to price; CFO-capex to FCF; enterprise/equity bridges. All prices and key outputs need independent second-source checks with defined tolerance.
3. **Screen all companies consistently:** stable point-in-time universe; sector/business-specific metrics; unknown evidence remains unknown. Score quality, valuation, momentum and risk as descriptive variables. Do not convert ranks to odds. Keep explanations and disqualifying evidence. New names may only enter after the same diligence checklist.
4. **Company underwriting:** two years of filings and earnings discussions; five-year historical operating bridge where available; revenue/margins/reinvestment/stock compensation/share count; competitive durability; customer and supplier concentration; debt covenants/maturities; company-specific bear thesis and falsification event. Independent bull and bear analysts must cite primary evidence.
5. **Coherent valuation:** FCFF/WACC and peer sanity checks for operating companies; justified P/B or excess returns with normalized capital requirements for banks/insurers; AFFO and property leverage for REITs. Forecast per-share economics. State bear/base/bull values and the assumptions needed for today's price. Scenario weights must be labeled judgments unless actually calibrated.
6. **Validation:** as-filed point-in-time data, historical membership and delistings; a development window, untouched holdout and forward paper record; net transaction costs, spreads, taxes and turnover; comparisons with broad index, simple equal weight and simple quality/value alternatives. Measure alpha, drawdown, turnover, calibration and sensitivity; uncertainty intervals, not just one number.
7. **Portfolio construction:** choose allocation from acceptable loss and liquidity needs, not desired return. Joint optimization with hard limits and an explicit cash asset; assert all limits after optimization; inspect sector/factor/correlation and look-through index overlap. Evaluate historical stress, synthetic earnings recession, multiple compression and correlation spikes. A15–20% drawdown tolerance is a planning objective, never a guaranteed ceiling.
8. **Weekly buy/hold/reduce/exit review:** refresh prices, filing/event calendar, thesis milestones, valuation ranges and total-portfolio risk. Buy only when verified evidence, valuation margin and risk capacity align. Hold while thesis remains intact and expected reward compensates risk. Reduce for excess position/factor exposure or diminished valuation margin. Exit for thesis falsification, governance/accounting evidence or a predefined risk-budget breach; distinguish confirmed deterioration from normal price noise. Every decision records date, evidence, reason, position impact and next trigger.
9. **Monitoring quality:** report realized drawdown, thesis progress, changes in assumptions and decision errors. Price moves alone do not validate or invalidate a thesis. Avoid weekly churn merely because a ranking changes by one place. A weekly portfolio update can be “no trade.”

**Release gate:** the research is ready for a capital-allocation decision only after price/fundamental reconciliation, corrected valuation, primary-source finalist diligence and feasible portfolio risk limits are complete. A prettier dashboard or a higher self-grade cannot substitute for those gates.
